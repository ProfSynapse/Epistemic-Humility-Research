## Summary

Zhang and Nanda conduct the first systematic study of how methodological choices in activation patching affect interpretability conclusions in language models. The paper evaluates three degrees of freedom: corruption method (Gaussian noise, GN, vs. symmetric token replacement, STR), evaluation metric (probability vs. logit difference), and patching granularity (single-layer vs. sliding window). All experiments are run on GPT-2 XL for factual recall (145 PairedFacts prompt pairs) and GPT-2 small for IOI circuit discovery (500 prompts). The core finding is that these choices are not interchangeable and can yield qualitatively different or even contradictory conclusions. GN produces MLP localization peaks for factual recall that are 2x-5x larger than STR across window sizes, a difference robust to metric choice. On the IOI circuit, GN with probability fails to detect any of the 3 Name Mover heads, while STR with probability detects 1 of 3 and both detect at least 1 with logit difference. The authors argue the source of the gap is that GN puts the model off-distribution: NM attention patterns collapse from a clean average of 0.58 to IO down to 0.26 IO / 0.21 S1 under GN, while STR preserves role structure with IO and S1 switched. Restoring S-Inhibition Head values recovers a logit difference of 1.04 under STR but only 0.49 under GN, indicating the GN-corrupted forward pass is not repairable by single-component patching. On metrics, probability is shown to be incapable of detecting negative model components (those that hurt performance) when corruption drives IO probability near zero (roughly 1e-4 under fully random name replacement). Logit difference detects Negative Name Movers in this regime while probability does not. On sliding window patching, simultaneously restoring adjacent layers inflates peak localization by 1.40x, 1.75x, and 1.59x over the sum of single-layer effects for window sizes 3, 5, and 10, a non-linear amplification the authors attribute to joint suppression of corrupted information flow and cooperative layer computation. Recommendations: prefer STR over GN, prefer logit difference over probability, start with single-layer patching before sliding windows, and predeclare all three choices before interpreting any patching result.

## Relevance to experiment

Three direct implications for mechanism activation-patching pilots. First, corruption method and metric must be preregistered before any pilot is run and interpreted. The paper shows that switching GN for STR, or probability for logit difference, can change a finding from a clear peak to a near-flat curve, or change a positive component detection into a negative one. Treating these as post-hoc tuning knobs undermines any causal claim. Second, the authors' preferred choices point in specific directions: STR over GN (in-distribution counterfactuals preserve mechanism integrity), logit difference over probability (detects both positive and negative components, controls for components that boost all names equally), and single-layer patching before sliding window (sliding windows amplify weak single-layer signal through non-linear joint effects and should be interpreted as joint-window attribution, not single-layer). mechinterp sweep candidates should adopt these defaults unless there is a specific reason not to, and the reason should be recorded. Third, cross-arm comparability requires identical method choices across all locked training-regimen arms; otherwise a difference in patching effect between a SFT arm and a DPO arm could reflect metric sensitivity rather than a genuine mechanistic change. The PairedFacts dataset (145 factual recall prompt pairs) is a self-contained evaluation set that could be reused if mechanism program extends to factual-recall localization on Qwen3, as GPT-2 XL architecture is different but the dataset construction method is portable.
