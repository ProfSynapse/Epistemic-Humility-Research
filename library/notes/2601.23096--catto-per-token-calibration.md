## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) often make accurate next token predictions but their confidence in these predictions can be poorly calibrated: high-confidence predictions are frequently wrong, and low-confidence predictions may be correct. This miscalibration is exacerbated by preference-based alignment methods breaking the link between predictive probability and correctness. We introduce a Calibration Aware Token-level Training Objective (CATTO), a calibration-aware objective that aligns predicted confidence with empirical prediction correctness, which can be combined with the original preference optimization objectives. Empirically, CATTO reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by 2.22%-7.61% in-distribution and 1.46%-10.44% out-of-distribution compared to direct preference optimization (DPO), and by 0.22%-1.24% in-distribution and 1.23%-5.07% out-of-distribution compared to the strongest DPO baseline. This improvement in confidence does not come at a cost of losing task accuracy, where CATTO maintains or slightly improves multiple-choice question-answering accuracy on five datasets. We also introduce Confidence@k, a test-time scaling mechanism leveraging calibrated token probabilities for Bayes-optimal selection of output tokens.

## Summary

CATTO (Calibration Aware Token-level Training Objective) addresses a structural problem in preference alignment: DPO and related objectives constrain only the relative likelihood ratio between preferred and dispreferred outputs, leaving absolute token probabilities unconstrained. This allows logit magnitudes to drift during training, decoupling confidence from correctness. CATTO introduces a differentiable per-token calibration loss built on a probability-margin correctness surrogate: for each token position it computes sigma(p_correct - p_best_wrong), uses that smooth signal as a correctness target, and minimizes the L1 deviation between predicted confidence and this target. This loss is added linearly to the DPO objective and applied to both preferred and dispreferred sequences. The result is an end-to-end training pipeline that simultaneously performs preference alignment and confidence calibration with no additional model parameters and negligible computational overhead relative to DPO alone. A companion inference mechanism, Confidence@k, selects among k generated candidates by picking the one with the highest token-level calibrated confidence, and the paper proves this rule is Bayes-optimal under well-calibrated confidence. All experiments use Qwen3-4B with DeepSeek-R1 generated chain-of-thought traces for preference pairs, evaluated across five classification and multiple-choice tasks (Reward Bench 2, SNLI, ANLI, TLDR, CommonsenseQA) in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution (trained on MMLU) regimes. RCFT, the strongest prior baseline, costs 18-39 times more compute than DPO and still leaves a calibration gap that CATTO closes at training time rather than through a post-hoc supervised phase.

## Relevance to experiment

CATTO is directly applicable to the Phase 1 DPO arm as a calibration-aware augmentation at negligible extra cost. It uses Qwen3-4B, matching the Phase 1 model family, and its synthetic CoT traces from DeepSeek-R1 mirror the kind of reasoning data the experiment already considers. The per-token probability-margin surrogate (sigma applied to the gap between ground-truth token probability and the highest competing token probability) is structurally related to the correctness signal Grad Detect uses in gradient space: both attempt to estimate token-level correctness without access to ground-truth labels at inference time. Whether the two signals correlate or dissociate across Phase 1 training arms is a tractable Phase 3 experiment. The Confidence@k mechanism is a clean inference-time read-out for well-calibrated checkpoints that requires no additional training and works directly on token probabilities already available in the experiment harness. Three caveats bound the transfer. First, CATTO is evaluated only on fixed-choice MCQ and binary classification tasks with synthetically generated CoT; the paper explicitly defers open-ended generation and iterative alignment to future work, so calibration numbers should not be assumed to hold on abstention-style free-form tasks. Second, the OOD setting trains on MMLU and tests on other tasks; this is a domain-shift regime, which limits how directly the OOD ECE numbers transfer to Phase 1 abstention regimes. Third, the probability-margin surrogate and Grad Detect probes operate in different spaces (logit-probability vs gradient), so a meaningful comparison would require bridging experiments not described in either paper.

## Claims

- CATTO reduces in-distribution ECE by 2.22%-7.61% relative to DPO across five benchmarks while preserving or improving task accuracy, with no additional model parameters or substantial compute overhead. (Table 1; Section 4.2) [[catto]]
- DPO generally improves task accuracy (+0.88% to +2.92%) but degrades confidence calibration on most benchmarks, and post-hoc methods like RCFT only partially correct this at 18-39x the computational cost of DPO. (Table 1; Section 4.2; Section 1) [[preference-collapse-causes-alignment-overconfidence]]
- Out-of-distribution (trained on MMLU, tested on held-out tasks), DPO+BCE and RCFT exhibit severe calibration failures on SNLI (ECE 11.02-14.40 points worse than CATTO) and Reward Bench 2 (3.57-4.82 points worse), while CATTO remains consistently calibrated. (Table 2; Section 4.2) [[calibration-aware-training-prevents-confidence-drift]]
- Confidence@k with CATTO at k=8 reverses DPO degradation on ANLI and SNLI: where DPO underperforms unaligned SFT by 3.87% and 3.96%, CATTO surpasses SFT by +9.07% and +10.35% absolute accuracy. (Table 3; Section 4.2) [[confidence-at-k]]
- The per-token probability-margin surrogate sigma(p_correct - p_best_wrong) is directionally consistent with true correctness and provides an upper bound on ECE via the population L1 calibration risk, enabling stable gradient-based training without hard binning. (Section 3.1; Appendix A and E) [[catto]]
