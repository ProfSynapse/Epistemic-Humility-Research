## Summary

Guo et al. (ICML 2017) document that modern high-capacity neural networks are systematically miscalibrated in a way that earlier, shallower architectures were not. The central empirical finding is that ECE grows with model depth and width even as classification error falls, and that the mechanism is NLL overfitting: after test accuracy plateaus, continued training minimizes cross-entropy further by inflating confidence without improving classification. On CIFAR-100, test error drops from 29% to 27% in the NLL-overfitting regime while calibration worsens. Batch Normalization and reduced weight decay, both hallmarks of modern training, each independently increase ECE. Against this backdrop, the paper evaluates six post-processing calibration methods (histogram binning, isotonic regression, BBQ, temperature scaling, vector scaling, matrix scaling) across 15 image and NLP benchmarks. Temperature scaling, a single-parameter logit divisor, is the standout: it outperforms all other methods on most vision tasks and performs comparably on NLP tasks. The paper's own Table 1 shows one counterexample where vector scaling edges out temperature scaling (CIFAR-100 ResNet-110 SD: 0.90% vs 0.96%), so the dominance is strong but not absolute. That vector scaling recovers nearly the same scalar solution as temperature scaling is the paper's evidence that miscalibration is intrinsically low-dimensional. Matrix scaling, by contrast, overfits whenever classes number in the hundreds. The paper concludes with a practical recommendation: temperature scaling should be the default post-hoc calibration step for any high-capacity classifier.

## Extracted numbers

Numbers extracted from arXiv HTML render at `library/fulltext/1706.04599.html`; key tables and figures: Table 1 (ECE before and after calibration, M=15 bins, all datasets and models), Figure 2 (effect of depth/width/BN/weight-decay on ECE), Figure 3 (NLL-overfitting training curves for 110-layer ResNet SD on CIFAR-100), Figure 4 (reliability diagrams for CIFAR-100 ResNet-110 SD), and Section 3 prose (lines 326-438 on architectural factors). Verified against the source on 2026-06-24. This paper reports miscalibration levels and post-hoc ECE reductions, not a training intervention before/after delta on an abstention or humility metric, so it does not map directly onto the effects.csv intervention schema; the numbers serve as baseline ECE benchmarks and motivational evidence for the calibration term.

- Uncalibrated ECE on CIFAR-100, ResNet-110 with stochastic depth (SD): 12.67%; after temperature scaling: 0.96% (Table 1; Figure 4 caption, labels ECE=12.67 and ECE=0.96).
- Uncalibrated ECE on CIFAR-10, plain ResNet-110: 4.6%; after temperature scaling: 0.83% (Table 1, CIFAR-10 ResNet-110 row; note: the 0.83% figure is for the plain model, not the SD variant).
- Uncalibrated ECE on ImageNet, DenseNet-161: 6.28%; after temperature scaling: 1.99% (Table 1, ImageNet DenseNet 161 row).
- NLL overfitting regime on CIFAR-100 (110-layer ResNet SD): test error drops from 29% to 27% while NLL continues to fall after test-accuracy plateaus, worsening calibration (Section 3, lines 426-430; Figure 3).
- Effect of depth: ECE grows substantially as depth increases from shallow to 110+ layers on ResNet CIFAR-100, even as classification error falls (Section 3, lines 326-336; Figure 2, Varying Depth panel).
- Effect of width: ECE grows with filters per layer on a 14-layer ResNet on CIFAR-100, with depth fixed (Section 3, lines 331-332; Figure 2, Varying Width panel).
- Effect of Batch Normalization: a 6-layer ConvNet on CIFAR-100 has higher ECE with BN than without, even though classification accuracy improves slightly (Section 3, lines 399-404; Figure 2, Using Normalization panel).
- Effect of weight decay: calibration continues to improve as weight decay increases well past the accuracy-optimal setting; lower weight decay consistently worsens ECE (Section 3, lines 374-383; Figure 2, Varying Weight Decay panel).
- Typical uncalibrated ECE across most vision and NLP datasets: 4 to 10%; SVHN (0.44%) and Reuters (0.85%) are exceptions, both at very low error rates below 3% (Section 5 prose, lines 927-933; Table 1). [Note: Reuters is an NLP dataset; the paper groups it with SVHN as a joint exception regardless of modality.]
- Temperature scaling outperforms all other calibration methods on most vision tasks; vector scaling occasionally edges it out (e.g., CIFAR-100 ResNet-110 SD: VS 0.90% vs TS 0.96% per Table 1), confirming dominance is strong but not absolute. Vector scaling learns near-constant vector entries, recovering essentially a scalar solution, which the authors interpret as evidence that miscalibration is intrinsically low-dimensional (Section 5, lines 934-939; Table 1).

## Relevance to experiment

This paper is the canonical reference establishing that high-capacity training degrades ECE and that temperature scaling is the standard post-hoc remedy. Three connections to the epistemic-humility experiment are direct. First, the NLL-overfitting mechanism is the pre-training analogue of the post-training calibration degradation seen after RLHF (see [[rlhf-degrades-conditional-calibration]] and [[rlhf-policy-miscalibration-temperature]]): both phenomena share the same structure where a loss objective decouples from 0/1 accuracy and inflates confidence. Second, temperature scaling, as documented here, is the baseline post-hoc calibration fix against which any training-time intervention (SFT, DPO, KTO) should be compared: a Phase 1 arm that degrades ECE as much as uncalibrated modern nets should be flagged, and temperature rescaling is the cheapest recovery option. Third, the paper's evidence that model capacity (depth and width) is a primary driver of miscalibration is the backdrop for [[model-size-improves-calibration]], which documents the opposite sign at LLM scale, creating an open empirical question about where the crossover lies. The paper does not study LLMs, does not report abstention or verbalized-confidence metrics, and does not populate effects.csv directly.

## Claims

- High-capacity ResNets are substantially more miscalibrated than shallower architectures: a 110-layer ResNet with stochastic depth reaches uncalibrated ECE of 12.67% on CIFAR-100 while a 5-layer LeNet is close to the diagonal, because increased depth and width drive NLL overfitting that inflates confidence without improving accuracy. (Table 1; Figure 1; Section 3) [[nll-overfitting-degrades-calibration]]
- Temperature scaling, a single-parameter logit divisor, reduces ECE from 12.67% to 0.96% on CIFAR-100 ResNet-110 SD and is the most effective calibration method on most vision tasks, outperforming strictly more general methods (vector scaling, matrix scaling) because miscalibration is intrinsically low-dimensional: vector scaling learns near-constant entries and recovers essentially the same scalar solution. (Table 1; Section 5) [[temperature-scaling]]
- Batch Normalization worsens calibration even when it improves accuracy: a 6-layer ConvNet on CIFAR-100 has higher ECE with BN, an effect that persists across hyperparameter settings. (Figure 2 Using Normalization panel; Section 3) [[high-capacity-training-degrades-calibration]]
- Weight decay is a calibration-accuracy decoupled regularizer: calibration improves with increasing weight decay well past the point of optimal classification accuracy, meaning the accuracy-optimal and calibration-optimal weight-decay settings differ systematically. (Figure 2 Varying Weight Decay panel; Section 3) [[high-capacity-training-degrades-calibration]]
- NLL overfitting is the mechanistic explanation for miscalibration: after test accuracy plateaus on CIFAR-100, continued training drives test NLL lower while test error continues to fall modestly from 29% to 27%, meaning the network sacrifices probability calibration for marginal accuracy gains. (Figure 3; Section 3) [[nll-overfitting-degrades-calibration]]
