## Abstract

Activation engineering is becoming increasingly popular as a means of online control of large language models (LLMs). In this work, we extend the idea of inference-time steering with vectors that represent a behavioral direction of interest to tuning those vectors directly into the model, obviating the need for online control. First, we identify activation vectors related to honesty in an open-source LLM (Llama-2-13b-chat). Next, we demonstrate that model output can be made more or less honest by adding positive or negative multiples of these vectors to residual stream activations during generation. Then, we show that a similar effect can be achieved by fine-tuning the vectors directly into the model, by use of a dual loss function based on the cosine similarity of residual stream activations to the vectors combined with a standard token-based loss ("representation tuning"). Finally, we compare the generations in response to honesty-probing prompts from the resulting models to those from models fine-tuned with a token-based loss alone, and to those from the untuned model subjected to online steering. Overall, fine-tuning the vectors into the models using the cosine similarity plus token loss showed a stronger effect than online steering, and generalized better than using the standard loss, suggesting the potential utility of this approach as a safety measure. ## Summary

The paper introduces representation tuning, which adds a cosine activation-alignment loss to token cross-entropy. It derives honesty and dishonesty directions from contrastive residual-stream activations, selects layers and positions through activation analysis and steering, then tunes selected attention modules so their activations align with the chosen direction. The tuned Llama-2-13B-Chat models express the target behavior without online activation injection and generalize better than token-only fine-tuning on the tested open-ended prompts.

## Extracted numbers

- On morally ambiguous questions, the honesty-tuned model was judged more honest than the base model on 65% of responses by Claude 3.5 Sonnet and 60% by GPT-4, with p=0.0290 and p=0.0413 respectively (Table 1).
- On instrumental-lying prompts, the honesty-tuned model was judged more honest on 71.4% of responses by Claude 3.5 Sonnet and 76.2% by GPT-4, with p=0.0258 and p=0.0023 respectively (Table 2).
- Token-only truth tuning showed no significant difference from the base model on either open-ended evaluation, while the representation-tuned models differed significantly from token-only models in the paper's pairwise tests (Section 3, Tables 1 through 6).
- Representation-tuned model perplexity on WikiText was similar to the untuned model, with paired-bootstrap p-values reported as approximately 1 (Section 3).

## Relevance to experiment

The study supplies a direct objective for coupling a learned internal direction to model behavior at the weights level. It does not add a conditional gate: the learned direction is applied through ordinary model computation for all inputs. Any answerability study that adapts this objective should therefore test whether gating or negative controls prevent indiscriminate behavioral shifts.

## Claims

- Evidence label: intervention study. A joint cosine activation-alignment and token loss internalizes honesty and dishonesty directions into model weights, producing behavior changes without online steering (Sections 2 and 3).
- Evidence label: controlled comparison. Representation-tuned models generalized to two small open-ended honesty evaluations, while token-only tuned models did not significantly differ from the untuned model (Section 3, Tables 1 through 6).
- Evidence label: utility check. Representation tuning left WikiText perplexity statistically similar to the untuned model in paired-bootstrap comparisons (Section 3).
