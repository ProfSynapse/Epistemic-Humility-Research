## Abstract

Can large language models introspect, that is, accurately detect perturbations to their own internal states? We systematically investigate this question using activation steering in Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. First, we show that the binary detection paradigm used in prior work conflates introspection with a methodological artifact: apparent detection accuracy is entirely explained by global logit shifts that bias models toward affirmative responses regardless of question content. However, on tasks requiring differential sensitivity, we find robust evidence for partial introspection: models localize which of 10 sentences received an injection at up to 88\% accuracy (vs.\ 10\% chance) and discriminate relative injection strengths at 83\% accuracy (vs.\ 50\% chance). These capabilities are confined to early-layer injections and collapse to chance thereafter -- a pattern we explain mechanistically through attention-based signal routing and residual stream recovery dynamics. Our findings demonstrate that LLMs can compute meaningful functions over perturbations to their internal states, establishing introspection as a real but layer-dependent phenomenon that merits further investigation. ## Summary

The paper tests whether Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct can report controlled
concept-vector perturbations to its residual stream. It finds that a binary
yes-or-no detection prompt is fully confounded by a content-independent shift
toward affirmative logits. It then introduces two relative tasks that cannot be
solved by that uniform shift. The model identifies which sentence received an
injection and which of two sentences received the stronger injection well above
chance, but only when the injection occurs in early layers. Attention tracking,
logit-lens projections, and residual-stream trajectories motivate an account in
which attention routes a localized anomaly, later computation integrates it,
and concurrent recovery attenuates the intervention.

## Extracted numbers

- Binary detection and factual-no control shifts correlate at r = 0.999 across
  40 layer-strength configurations. The mean detection-minus-control signal is
  -0.01 +/- 0.03 logits (Table 2; Section 4.2).
- Sentence localization reaches 88 percent at layer 2 and injection strength 5,
  compared with 10 percent chance. Performance falls to chance beyond layer 8
  (Figure 2; Section 5.2).
- Strength comparison reaches 83 percent at layer 3 for strengths 3 versus 7,
  compared with 50 percent chance. Layers 15 through 30 average 47 percent
  (Figure 1; Section 5.1).
- With injection at layer 2, all 32 attention heads at layer 3 localize the
  affected sentence in the five-way analysis. Mean attention localization
  remains 37 percent across layers 20 through 31, above 20 percent chance
  (Figure 3; Section 6).
- The logit-lens localization result rises from 28 percent at layer 4 to 60
  percent at layer 12 and plateaus at 72 percent by layer 20 (Figure 4; Section
  6.1).

## Relevance to experiment

The paper supplies relative, position-counterbalanced tests for a model's use of
an internal intervention. These tests separate localized or graded sensitivity
from a uniform answer-token shift. Its layer result also shows that an internal
signal can be detectable by attention while still requiring substantial later
computation before it controls an explicit report. The evidence is limited to
one 8B model, synthetic concept directions, and deliberately injected states.
It does not show that a model consults a naturally occurring answerability
readout during ordinary generation.

## Claims

- Evidence label: causal diagnostic. In the tested binary task, the apparent
  detection signal is fully accounted for by the same affirmative-logit shift
  measured on factual questions whose correct answer is no (Tables 1 and 2;
  Section 4).
- Evidence label: controlled intervention. Early-layer injections support
  sentence localization up to 88 percent and injection-strength comparison up
  to 83 percent, while late-layer performance falls to chance (Figures 1 and 2;
  Section 5).
- Evidence label: mechanistic correlation. Attention heads localize an injected
  anomaly soon after layer-2 injection, while the explicit sentence prediction
  develops gradually in later layers (Figures 3 and 4; Sections 6 and 6.1).
- Evidence label: mechanistic correlation. Perturbed residual streams return
  toward baseline and their projection onto the injection direction decays
  across later layers (Figure 5; Section 6.2).
- Evidence label: scoped interpretation. The authors describe the result as
  partial, layer-dependent introspection implemented through general-purpose
  anomaly detection and signal integration, not evidence for a specialized
  introspection circuit (Sections 6.3 and 7).
