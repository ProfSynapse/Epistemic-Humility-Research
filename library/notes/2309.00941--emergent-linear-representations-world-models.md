## Abstract

How do sequence models represent their decision-making process? Prior work suggests that Othello-playing neural network learned nonlinear models of the board state (Li et al., 2023). In this work, we provide evidence of a closely related linear representation of the board. In particular, we show that probing for "my colour" vs. "opponent's colour" may be a simple yet powerful way to interpret the model's internal state. This precise understanding of the internal representations allows us to control the model's behaviour with simple vector arithmetic. Linear representations enable significant interpretability progress, which we demonstrate with further exploration of how the world model is computed.

## Claims

- Linear probes on player-relative (Mine/Yours/Empty) board representations achieve 99.4% accuracy at layer 6 of OthelloGPT, versus only ~75% for probes trained on absolute (Black/White/Empty) colour labels, demonstrating that the emergent world model is linearly encoded once the correct feature frame is identified. (Table 1) [[relative-board-encoding]]
- Causal interventions by adding linear probe direction vectors to the residual stream yield an average of 0.10 move-prediction errors for colour-flip tasks and 0.02 for tile-erasure tasks, compared to a null baseline of 2.723 and 2.73 respectively, confirming that the linear board-state representations are causally upstream of move predictions. (Table 2) [[activation-intervention]]
- OthelloGPT attention heads in the first layer specialise to attend either to 'My' moves or to 'Your' moves (never both), providing a mechanistic account of how the model constructs its player-relative board representation. (Figure 4, Section 5.2) [[emergent-world-model]]
- In end-game positions (after move ~30), OthelloGPT frequently computes valid legal moves before completing a full board-state representation, suggesting multiple co-existing circuits rather than a single board-then-moves pipeline. (Figure 5, Section 5.4) [[emergent-world-model]]
