## Abstract

A safe and trustworthy use of Large Language Models (LLMs) requires an accurate expression of confidence in their answers. We propose a novel Reinforcement Learning approach that allows to directly fine-tune LLMs to express calibrated confidence estimates alongside their answers to factual questions. Our method optimizes a reward based on the logarithmic scoring rule, explicitly penalizing both over- and under-confidence. This encourages the model to align its confidence estimates with the actual predictive accuracy. The optimal policy under our reward design would result in perfectly calibrated confidence expressions. Unlike prior approaches that decouple confidence estimation from response generation, our method integrates confidence calibration seamlessly into the generative process of the LLM. Empirically, we demonstrate that models trained with our approach exhibit substantially improved calibration and generalize to unseen tasks without further fine-tuning, suggesting the emergence of general confidence awareness.

## Summary

Rewarding Doubt trains LLMs to output factual answers together with calibrated confidence estimates. The method uses a logarithmic scoring-rule reward: confident correct answers are rewarded, confident errors are penalized, and the reward is maximized when stated confidence matches empirical correctness probability. The authors frame this as integrating calibration directly into generation rather than bolting on a separate probe or post-hoc estimator.

Empirically, the paper shows strong ECE and AUROC gains on TriviaQA and QAMPARI, with additional transfer tests on CommonsenseQA and MedQA. It is a direct predecessor for the RLMF paper: both use RL to improve confidence expression, but Rewarding Doubt focuses on factual correctness calibration whereas RLMF targets faithful alignment between expressed and intrinsic uncertainty.

## Relevance to experiment

Rewarding Doubt is relevant as a factual-calibration RL baseline for later abstention and confidence-expression work. It supports the broader design claim that RL objectives can directly shape confidence expression, but it does not by itself establish faithful calibration in the RLMF sense because its target is correctness-calibrated stated probability, not alignment between expressed and intrinsic uncertainty.

This should not change the locked locked protocol. It is most useful as a future comparison point for confidence-reward arms: a model could become well calibrated to empirical correctness while still learning a policy surface that is decoupled from its internal uncertainty signal.

## Claims

- Method claim: A logarithmic scoring-rule RL reward gives the model a direct gradient for calibrated confidence expression alongside factual answers.
- Empirical claim: On TriviaQA and QAMPARI, Rewarding Doubt substantially reduces ECE and improves AUROC relative to plain verbalized confidence while keeping task accuracy in the same range.
- Transfer claim: The TriviaQA-trained policy transfers imperfectly but usefully to CommonsenseQA and MedQA, suggesting some general confidence-awareness behavior rather than pure dataset memorization.
