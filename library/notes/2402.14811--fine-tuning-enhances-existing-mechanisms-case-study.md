## Abstract

Fine-tuning on generalized tasks such as instruction following, code generation, and mathematics has been shown to enhance language models' performance on a range of tasks. Nevertheless, explanations of how such fine-tuning influences the internal computations in these models remain elusive. We study how fine-tuning affects the internal mechanisms implemented in language models. As a case study, we explore the property of entity tracking, a crucial facet of language comprehension, where models fine-tuned on mathematics have substantial performance gains. We identify the mechanism that enables entity tracking and show that (i) in both the original model and its fine-tuned versions primarily the same circuit implements entity tracking. In fact, the entity tracking circuit of the original model on the fine-tuned versions performs better than the full original model. (ii) The circuits of all the models implement roughly the same functionality: Entity tracking is performed by tracking the position of the correct entity in both the original model and its fine-tuned versions. (iii) Performance boost in the fine-tuned models is primarily attributed to its improved ability to handle the augmented positional information. To uncover these findings, we employ: Patch Patching, DCM, which automatically detects model components responsible for specific semantics, and CMAP, a new approach for patching activations across models to reveal improved mechanisms. Our findings suggest that fine-tuning enhances, rather than fundamentally alters, the mechanistic operation of the model.

## Summary

The paper compares an entity-tracking circuit in LLaMA-7B with the corresponding circuits in three descendants: Vicuna-7B, Goat-7B, and FLoat-7B. Path patching and minimality pruning identify a 72-head base circuit organized into four functional groups. The same heads retain high faithfulness after conversational or arithmetic fine-tuning, while the arithmetic-tuned models use larger 175-head circuits that approximately contain the base circuit. Desiderata-based Component Masking attributes entity tracking to detection, transmission, and use of the queried object's position. Cross-model activation patching shows that transplanting the fine-tuned Position Transmitter and Value Fetcher activations into the base model improves its accuracy, with Value Fetcher patching recovering the fine-tuned performance.

## Extracted numbers

- Entity-tracking evaluation uses 500 tasks with seven boxes and single-token objects. Circuit discovery uses 300 examples (Sections 3 and 4.1).
- The initial LLaMA-7B circuit has 90 heads. Minimality pruning removes 20%, leaving 72 heads (Section 4.2).
- Full-model accuracies are 0.66 for LLaMA-7B, 0.67 for Vicuna-7B, and 0.82 for both Goat-7B and FLoat-7B (Table 1).
- The fixed 72-head LLaMA circuit achieves circuit accuracies of 0.66, 0.65, 0.73, and 0.72 in those models. Its corresponding faithfulness scores are 1.00, 0.97, 0.89, and 0.88 (Table 1).
- Ten matched random circuits have accuracy at or near zero in all four models (Section 4.3, Table 1).
- Goat-7B and FLoat-7B each have a discovered 175-head circuit that approximately contains the 72-head base circuit (Section 4.3, Appendices E and F).
- DCM assigns positional-information functionality to 71.43% of Group B heads and 70.0% of Group C heads. Functionality evaluation uses 500 samples repeated ten times (Section 5.2).
- Goat-7B Value Fetcher patching improves target accuracy by 20% relative to LLaMA-7B in the reported within-model functional intervention (Section 5.3).

## Relevance to experiment

The paper provides a method for asking whether fine-tuning creates a new computation or strengthens an existing one. Cross-model activation patching is especially relevant when base and fine-tuned models share an architecture, because it tests whether a fine-tuned component causally improves the base computation. The study does not concern answerability, conditional generation, or weight-level consultation of a readout.

## Claims

- Evidence label: circuit intervention. The 72-head LLaMA-7B entity-tracking circuit preserves 88% to 97% faithfulness when evaluated unchanged in three fine-tuned descendants, while matched random circuits have near-zero accuracy (Section 4.3, Table 1).
- Evidence label: circuit discovery. Arithmetic-tuned Goat-7B and FLoat-7B use larger 175-head circuits that approximately contain the base circuit, so full fine-tuned performance includes additional components (Section 4.3, Appendices E and F).
- Evidence label: functional intervention. DCM and activation patching identify positional detection, transmission, and value fetching as the same group functions across the four models (Section 5, Figure 3, Appendices G to I).
- Evidence label: cross-model intervention. Patching Position Transmitter or Value Fetcher outputs from arithmetic-tuned models into LLaMA-7B improves base-model performance. Value Fetcher patching recovers the fine-tuned-model performance (Section 6, Figure 4).
- Evidence label: limitation. The conclusions cover one synthetic entity-tracking task and three fine-tuned descendants of one base architecture. The paper does not analyze training dynamics or establish that mechanism invariance is typical across tasks and model families (Section 7).
