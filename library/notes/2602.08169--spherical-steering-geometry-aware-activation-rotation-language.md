## Abstract

Inference-time steering offers a promising way to control language models (LMs) without retraining. However, standard approaches typically rely on activation addition, which inevitably alters the hidden-state magnitudes raising concerns about representation collapse and degraded open-ended generation. In this work, we explore Spherical Steering, a training-free primitive that resolves this trade-off through activation rotation. Rather than shifting activations with a fixed vector, our method rotates them along a geodesic toward a target direction, preserving signal integrity while steering toward the target concept. To further enhance adaptivity, we incorporate a confidence gate that dynamically modulates steering strength based on input uncertainty. Extensive experiments across multiple-choice benchmarks demonstrate that Spherical Steering significantly outperforms addition-based baselines (notably by +10% on TruthfulQA, COPA, and Storycloze), while simultaneously maintaining the model's general open-ended generation quality. This work highlights the value of geometric consistency, suggesting that norm-preserving rotation is a robust and effective primitive for precise inference-time control. The code is available at: https://github.com/chili-lab/Spherical-Steering.

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment (Table 1, Section 4.2). Spherical Steering achieves a Pareto improvement over additive baselines on TruthfulQA: MC average +11.09% (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct) and +5.31% (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct) over the best baseline, while also posting the highest TRUE x INFO score (+3.45% and +0.90% respectively); additive baselines that raise MC accuracy (ITI, CAA) simultaneously drop TRUE x INFO (e.g., ITI: 48.24 -> 40.31 on LLaMA).
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 2, Section 4.3). Spherical Steering achieves the best result on all six multiple-choice benchmarks tested on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (TruthfulQA, COPA, StoryCloze, MMLU, WinoGrande, BoolQ), with the largest gains on commonsense-discrimination tasks: +11.00% on COPA and +10.06% on StoryCloze over baseline.
- Evidence label: analysis (Figure 3, Section 4.4.1). Activation-norm magnitude is a weak discriminator of truthfulness: mean L2 norms of truthful vs. hallucinated last-token activations differ by <1% relative deviation across all 32 layers of LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct on TruthfulQA, motivating direction-only (norm-preserving) intervention over magnitude-altering addition.
- Evidence label: experiment (Figure 4, Section 4.4.2). Norm-preserving rotation is more "collapse-efficient" than activation addition: at matched effective-rank drop (Δrank≈50), rotation delivers 8-10% higher multiple-choice accuracy, and while additive steering's TRUE x INFO stays flat or degrades as intervention strength increases, rotation's TRUE x INFO improves across a broad range of rank drops.
