## Abstract

We introduce Concept Bottleneck Large Language Models (CB-LLMs), a novel framework for building inherently interpretable Large Language Models (LLMs). In contrast to traditional black-box LLMs that rely on limited post-hoc interpretations, CB-LLMs integrate intrinsic interpretability directly into the LLMs -- allowing accurate explanations with scalability and transparency. We build CB-LLMs for two essential NLP tasks: text classification and text generation. In text classification, CB-LLMs is competitive with, and at times outperforms, traditional black-box models while providing explicit and interpretable reasoning. For the more challenging task of text generation, interpretable neurons in CB-LLMs enable precise concept detection, controlled generation, and safer outputs. The embedded interpretability empowers users to transparently identify harmful content, steer model behavior, and unlearn undesired concepts -- significantly enhancing the safety, reliability, and trustworthiness of LLMs, which are critical capabilities notably absent in existing models. Our code is available at this https URL .

## Summary

The paper introduces Concept Bottleneck Large Language Models for classification and autoregressive generation. Named concept neurons connect linearly to class or token logits, which exposes their contribution and permits direct intervention. Because generation also needs broad unsupervised features, the generation architecture adds a parallel unsupervised path and adversarially removes labeled concept information from it. This discourages the token head from bypassing the interpretable bottleneck. The paper evaluates classification accuracy and explanation faithfulness, generation concept detection and steering, concept unlearning, and toxicity control.

## Extracted numbers

- Automatic Concept Correction improved classification accuracy by 3.5 percentage points on average, bringing CB-LLM classification performance to the fine-tuned black-box level (Section 3.1, Step 3).
- Deactivating the “overpriced” concept shifted 2,726 Yelp test predictions from negative to positive. An NLI model found that 2,162 of those samples, or 79%, entailed that concept (Section 3.3, Figure 2).
- With adversarial training, generation steerability was 0.82 on SST2, 0.95 on Yelp Polarity, 0.85 on AG News, and 0.76 on DBpedia. Without it, the scores were 0.57, 0.69, 0.52, and 0.21 (Section 4.2, Table 5).
- Concept-detection accuracy stayed within one percentage point of the direct fine-tuned classifier, while generated-text perplexity remained in a similar range to the black-box model (Section 4.2, Table 5).
- The toxicity chatbot reached 0.9996 harmful-query detection accuracy and 0.9137 response steerability (Section 4.3, Figure 5).

## Relevance to experiment

The generation architecture is an example in which supervised internal concept values sit directly on the token-prediction path and can be intervened on during decoding. Its adversarial module addresses a specific bypass risk by stripping the same concept from the parallel unsupervised path. The trained concept neurons are new supervised representations, so the study does not establish reuse of a pretrained model's native answerability readout.

## Claims

- Evidence label: architectural intervention. Named concept neurons feed token logits and can steer autoregressive samples toward a selected concept (Section 4.1 and Section 4.2, Table 5).
- Evidence label: ablation. Removing adversarial disentangling sharply reduces steerability while leaving concept-detection accuracy similar, supporting the claim that the unsupervised path can otherwise bypass the bottleneck (Section 4.2, Table 5).
- Evidence label: task comparison. CB-LLMs match black-box classification accuracy after Automatic Concept Correction and provide human-rated concept explanations (Section 3.2, Tables 2 through 4).
- Evidence label: case studies. Direct concept intervention supports targeted concept unlearning and toxicity-response control (Sections 3.3 and 4.3, Figures 2 and 5).
