## Abstract

While large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable abilities across various fields, hallucination remains a significant challenge. Recent studies have explored hallucinations through the lens of internal representations, proposing mechanisms to decipher LLMs' adherence to facts. However, these approaches often fail to generalize to out-of-distribution data, leading to concerns about whether internal representation patterns reflect fundamental factual awareness, or only overfit spurious correlations on the specific datasets. In this work, we investigate whether a universal truthfulness hyperplane that distinguishes the model's factually correct and incorrect outputs exists within the model. To this end, we scale up the number of training datasets and conduct an extensive evaluation -- we train the truthfulness hyperplane on a diverse collection of over 40 datasets and examine its cross-task, cross-domain, and in-domain generalization. Our results indicate that increasing the diversity of the training datasets significantly enhances the performance in all scenarios, while the volume of data samples plays a less critical role. This finding supports the optimistic hypothesis that a universal truthfulness hyperplane may indeed exist within the model, offering promising directions for future research.

## Summary

Liu et al. ask whether a single linear hyperplane in an LLM's hidden-state space can classify factual correctness across tasks it was never trained on. The motivation is a well-known failure mode: probes trained on one dataset (commonly TruthfulQA) overfit its spurious surface features and collapse near chance on out-of-distribution (OOD) data. The paper's core move is to scale training diversity rather than training volume, assembling 49 datasets across 17 task categories and training two standard linear probes, logistic regression (Probe-LR) and mass mean (Probe-MM), on concatenated attention-head outputs from a sparse subset of transformer positions selected by a held-out validation procedure. The attention-head position selection trains 1,024 preliminary probes (32 layers x 32 heads on LLaMA2-7b-chat) and keeps the top 1 or 2 positions per validation split. This selection step keeps the final feature vector compact while concentrating truthfulness signal. Across three evaluation regimes (cross-task, cross-domain, in-domain) on LLaMA2-7b-chat, Mistral-7b, and LLaMA2-13b-chat, the diverse-dataset probes beat both the Self-Eval prompting baseline and token-probability baseline by 5-7 absolute points on average, and outperform single-dataset probes by roughly 14 points in the cross-task setting. A key practical finding is data efficiency: matching the probe trained on 800 samples per dataset requires only 10 samples per dataset, consistent with the linear nature of the probes. Attention-head outputs outperform residual-stream activations by at least 3 accuracy points throughout. Cross-task accuracy rises with the number of training tasks, though the improvement is a general trend rather than strict monotonicity (the paper hedges with "general corresponding enhancement"). A positive model-size trend appears: Mistral-7b reaches 77.11% and LLaMA2-13b-chat 73.88% cross-task accuracy, both above LLaMA2-7b-chat's 70.47%, suggesting the universal truthfulness hyperplane becomes more geometrically pronounced in stronger models. The paper stops short of causal claims: the Limitations section explicitly notes that the probe may reflect what the model knows (i.e., recall familiarity) rather than a ground-truth-aligned truth concept, and that causal intervention experiments are left for future work.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper bears on locked training-regimen and mechanism program of the Synaptic Tuner experiment in three ways. First, the cross-task vs. in-domain accuracy gap (roughly 10 points for diverse probes, roughly 28 points for single-dataset probes) is a direct empirical risk for any evaluation design that trains and evaluates probes on one benchmark. If locked training-regimen uses a single benchmark to measure truthfulness probing before and after SFT or DPO arms, single-dataset OOD collapse (Table 1) is the failure mode to guard against. The 49-dataset diverse-probe approach is the mitigation. Second, the attention-head localization finding (1-2 head positions carry most truthfulness signal) is a specific, actionable hypothesis for the mechanistic interpretability sweeps: it narrows the candidate inventory from the full residual stream to sparse attention-head outputs and predicts that 1-2 top-k positions will dominate. Comparing this localization before and after each training arm could test whether fine-tuning sharpens or disperses the truthfulness signal. Third, the Limitations section's caution that the probe may reflect the model's own knowledge boundary rather than ground truth connects to the existing graph nodes around p-ik-ood-generalization-gap and verbalized-prob-generalizes-logit-overfits-distribution-shift: even a diverse, well-generalizing probe may confound factual correctness with model familiarity. That caveat motivates pairing activation probes with output-level calibration signals in the locked training-regimen study's eval stack rather than treating activation-space accuracy as a standalone measure. The data efficiency result (10 samples per dataset matches 800) is also practical: probe evaluation does not require large annotated sets for each new post-training checkpoint.

## Claims

- Probes trained on a single dataset (TruthfulQA) achieve high in-distribution accuracy (82.28% for Probe-LR) but collapse to near-chance OOD accuracy (54.44%), trailing the prompting-based Self-Eval baseline (63.31%) by roughly 9 points, confirming that single-dataset truthfulness probes overfit spurious distribution-specific features rather than a universal truthfulness signal. (Table 1) [[estimator-divergence-invalidates-single-probe-faithfulness]]
- Training linear probes on 49 diverse datasets across 17 task categories yields approximately 70% cross-task accuracy on LLaMA2-7b-chat (Probe-MM 70.47%, Probe-LR 69.24%), outperforming Self-Eval by 5.10 absolute points on average and prior single-dataset probes by roughly 14 points, providing positive evidence for the existence of a universal truthfulness hyperplane in LLM hidden states. (Table 2, §3.3) [[universal-truthfulness-hyperplane]]
- Attention-head outputs outperform layer residual activations by at least 3 accuracy points across all probe types and training-task counts in cross-task experiments, and selecting only 1-2 attention-head positions from 1,024 candidates (32 layers x 32 heads) achieves optimal performance, indicating that truthfulness signal is sparse and concentrated in a small fraction of attention-head positions rather than distributed across the residual stream. (Figure 4a, §2.4, §3.5) [[attention-head-truthfulness-concentration]]
- Cross-task accuracy increases with model capability: Mistral-7b (Probe-MM 77.11%) and LLaMA2-13b-chat (73.88%) both exceed LLaMA2-7b-chat (70.47%), and the paper explicitly attributes this to the universal truthfulness hyperplane becoming more geometrically pronounced in stronger models, consistent with the hypothesis that scale amplifies linear separability of truthfulness in hidden states. (Table 3, §3.4) [[universal-truthfulness-hyperplane]]
- Training with as few as 10 data points per dataset matches the cross-task performance of 800 samples per dataset, a result the paper attributes to the linear nature of the probes, demonstrating that diverse-dataset coverage matters far more than per-dataset sample volume for generalizing truthfulness detection. (Figure 4c, §3.5) [[diverse-training-enables-universal-probe-generalization]]
