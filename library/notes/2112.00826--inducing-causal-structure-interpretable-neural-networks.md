## Abstract

In many areas, we have well-founded insights about causal structure that would be useful to bring into our trained models while still allowing them to learn in a data-driven fashion. To achieve this, we present the new method of interchange intervention training (IIT). In IIT, we (1) align variables in a causal model (e.g., a deterministic program or Bayesian network) with representations in a neural model and (2) train the neural model to match the counterfactual behavior of the causal model on a base input when aligned representations in both models are set to be the value they would be for a source input. IIT is fully differentiable, flexibly combines with other objectives, and guarantees that the target causal model is a causal abstraction of the neural model when its loss is zero. We evaluate IIT on a structural vision task (MNIST-PVR), a navigational language task (ReaSCAN), and a natural language inference task (MQNLI). We compare IIT against multi-task training objectives and data augmentation. In all our experiments, IIT achieves the best results and produces neural models that are more interpretable in the sense that they more successfully realize the target causal model.

## Summary

The paper introduces interchange intervention training (IIT), which aligns variables in a high-level causal model with neural representations. During training, IIT swaps aligned internal states between source and base inputs and trains the neural model against the causal model's counterfactual output. The method is evaluated on MNIST-PVR, ReaSCAN, and MQNLI, where it improves both behavioral generalization and agreement with the target causal model. The paper also defines typed IIT, which permits swaps across aligned variables that share a value type.

## Extracted numbers

- On MNIST-PVR, IIT reached 93.93% behavioral test accuracy and 94.85% interchange-intervention test accuracy. IIT plus multi-task learning reached 96.01% and 96.64%, respectively. Standard training reached 0.00% behavioral and 20.60% interchange-intervention test accuracy (Table 1).
- Removing typed interventions reduced MNIST-PVR behavioral test accuracy from 93.93% to 0.09% and interchange-intervention test accuracy from 94.85% to 16.88% (Table 1).
- On ReaSCAN, IIT plus multi-task learning gave the best behavioral exact match across the four systematic-generalization splits: 80.37% novel color, 74.84% novel size, 14.72% novel direction, and 25.82% novel length (Table 2 in the HTML rendering).
- On MQNLI, IIT aligned at BERT layer 6 or later produced approximately 100% behavioral and interchange-intervention accuracy (Section 6, Figure 4).

## Relevance to experiment

IIT is a direct example of a differentiable objective that trains model behavior to depend causally on a designated internal representation. Its formal guarantee applies only when the IIT loss is zero and the alignment and output mapping satisfy the stated assumptions. The experiments use task-semantic variables rather than answerability, so they establish a method pattern rather than evidence about epistemic readouts.

## Claims

- Evidence label: theorem. If the IIT loss is zero for all base and source inputs, the aligned high-level causal model is a causal abstraction of the neural model (Section 3 and Appendix A).
- Evidence label: controlled experiment. IIT greatly improved behavioral and interchange-intervention generalization on MNIST-PVR compared with standard and multi-task training (Table 1).
- Evidence label: controlled experiment. Combining IIT with multi-task learning gave the strongest results across the ReaSCAN splits (Section 5, Table 2 in the HTML rendering).
- Evidence label: controlled experiment. IIT produced nearly perfect MQNLI behavior and causal-structure agreement when the aligned representation was at BERT layer 6 or later (Section 6, Figure 4).
