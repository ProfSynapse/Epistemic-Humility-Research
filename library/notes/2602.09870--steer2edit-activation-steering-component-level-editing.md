## Abstract

Steering methods influence Large Language Model behavior by identifying semantic directions in hidden representations, but are typically realized through inference-time activation interventions that apply a fixed, global modification to the model's internal states. While effective, such interventions often induce unfavorable attribute-utility trade-offs under strong control, as they ignore the fact that many behaviors are governed by a small and heterogeneous subset of model components. We propose Steer2Edit, a theoretically grounded, training-free framework that transforms steering vectors from inference-time control signals into diagnostic signals for component-level rank-1 weight editing. Instead of uniformly injecting a steering direction during generation, Steer2Edit selectively redistributes behavioral influence across individual attention heads and MLP neurons, yielding interpretable edits that preserve the standard forward pass and remain compatible with optimized parallel inference. Across safety alignment, hallucination mitigation, and reasoning efficiency, Steer2Edit consistently achieves more favorable attribute-utility trade-offs: at matched downstream performance, it improves safety by up to 17.2%, increases truthfulness by 9.8%, and reduces reasoning length by 12.2% on average. Overall, Steer2Edit provides a principled bridge between representation steering and weight editing by translating steering signals into interpretable, training-free parameter updates. ## Summary

Steer2Edit converts contrastive activation-steering directions into component-level rank-1 weight updates. It edits attention-head output projections and individual MLP down-projection neurons, with edit directions and magnitudes chosen from semantic alignment scores and an Elastic-Net objective. The resulting parameter edits preserve the standard forward pass. Evaluations cover jailbreak refusal, TruthfulQA preference, and reasoning length, with unrelated task accuracy used as utility.

## Extracted numbers

- At matched downstream performance, the paper reports safety gains up to 17.2%, truthfulness gains of 9.8%, and an average reasoning-length reduction of 12.2% relative to activation steering (Section 1, Contributions).
- Safety-oriented edits are sparse and concentrated in later-layer attention heads, with near-zero MLP coefficients (Section 4.2.1, Figure 3).
- Truthfulness-oriented edits remain sparse and attention-dominated, but span early and late layers. Gemma-2-2B-IT uses mainly negative coefficients (Section 4.2.2, Figure 5).
- Reasoning-efficiency edits are dense and distributed across MLP neurons, while attention heads play a smaller role (Section 4.2.3, Figure 7).

## Relevance to experiment

The paper provides a direct example of using an internal behavioral direction to compute persistent weight edits. Its component-specific results also warn that the useful parameter class depends on the target behavior and model, so a weights-level answerability intervention should compare attention and MLP edit surfaces instead of assuming one shared site.

## Claims

- Evidence label: controlled empirical comparison. Steer2Edit improves safety, truthfulness, and reasoning-efficiency trade-offs over activation steering at matched downstream utility (Section 4.2, Figures 2, 4, and 6).
- Evidence label: component analysis. Sparse attention-head edits dominate safety and truthfulness control, while distributed MLP-neuron edits dominate reasoning-efficiency control (Section 4.2, Figures 3, 5, and 7).
- Evidence label: ablation. Dense truthfulness edits and unnormalized reasoning edit scores can collapse utility, supporting the method's sparsity and normalization choices (Appendix E, Tables 5 and 6).
