## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in high-stakes domains such as science, law, and healthcare, where accurate expressions of uncertainty are essential for reliability and trust. However, current LLMs are often observed to generate incorrect answers with high confidence, a phenomenon known as "overconfidence". Recent efforts have focused on calibrating LLMs' verbalized confidence: i.e., their expressions of confidence in text form, such as "I am 80% confident that...". Existing approaches either rely on prompt engineering or fine-tuning with heuristically generated uncertainty estimates, both of which have limited effectiveness and generalizability. Motivated by the notion of proper scoring rules for calibration in classical machine learning models, we introduce ConfTuner, a simple and efficient fine-tuning method that introduces minimal overhead and does not require ground-truth confidence scores or proxy confidence estimates. ConfTuner relies on a new loss function, tokenized Brier score, which we theoretically prove to be a proper scoring rule, intuitively meaning that it "correctly incentivizes the model to report its true probability of being correct". ConfTuner improves calibration across diverse reasoning tasks and generalizes to black-box models such as GPT-4o. Our results further show that better-calibrated confidence enables downstream gains in self-correction and model cascade, advancing the development of trustworthy LLM systems. The code is available at https://github.com/liushiliushi/ConfTuner.

## Summary

ConfTuner (Li et al., NUS 2025) introduces a supervised fine-tuning method that trains LLMs to express calibrated verbalized confidence without requiring ground-truth confidence scores, proxy estimates, or repeated sampling. The core contribution is the tokenized Brier score, a loss function that operates on the model's probability distribution over a discrete set of confidence tokens (integers 0-100, or a coarser set) extracted from logits at the token before the confidence expression is generated. Theorem 1 proves the tokenized Brier score is a proper scoring rule for verbalized confidence: fine-tuning on it drives the model to place all probability mass on the token closest to the true conditional correctness probability. This extends classical calibration theory (Brier score as proper scoring rule for classifiers) into the verbalized-token setting, filling a gap that proxy-estimate methods like SaySelf (sampling 100 responses per example across 90,000 examples) and LACIE (model-judgment labels over 10,000 examples) leave open. Experiments on three base LLMs (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Ministral-8B-Instruct) trained on HotpotQA and evaluated across four out-of-distribution datasets (TriviaQA, StrategyQA, GSM8K, TruthfulQA) show ConfTuner achieves best ECE and AUROC in nearly all conditions. ConfTuner generalizes to linguistic confidence expressions (high/medium/low) it was never trained on, enables proxy calibration of GPT-4o treated as a black box, and yields downstream gains in model cascade of up to 9.3% accuracy on HotpotQA. Training takes 4 minutes on 4 A40 GPUs with 2,000 examples, versus 26 minutes for LACIE and 120 minutes for SaySelf.

## Relevance to experiment

ConfTuner is direct prior art for the locked training-regimen SFT verbalized-calibration arm. The Epistemic Humility experiment trains LLMs to express calibrated uncertainty under SFT, DPO, and KTO; ConfTuner shows a principled SFT-only approach grounded in proper scoring theory can match or exceed proxy-labeling baselines at a fraction of the data and compute cost (2,000 samples, 4 minutes vs. 9,000,000 total sampling draws). The critical contrast is with RLCR (2507.16806), which realizes the same Brier-score proper-scoring guarantee inside an RL reward rather than an SFT loss: the two papers bracket the training-regime axis and together predict that locked training-regimen should replicate this calibration gain under at least one arm without costly label generation. The tokenized Brier score mechanism also sharpens the locked training-regimen measurement question: if DPO or KTO outperform SFT on abstention, does that come at a cost to verbalized ECE and AUROC? The three confirmed OOD datasets here (TriviaQA, StrategyQA, TruthfulQA) overlap directly with locked training-regimen evaluation sets. For mechinterp: ConfTuner operates exclusively on output confidence-token logits, not on hidden-state geometry, so it is a narrower target than the gradient-based (Grad Detect) or activation-based (ITI) probes in the sweep. That narrowness is itself informative: good output-level calibration does not require uncovering a truth direction in residual streams, which raises the question of whether ConfTuner-tuned checkpoints show the same hidden-state calibration sharpening that activation probes would detect.

## Claims

- The tokenized Brier score is a proper scoring rule for verbalized confidence (Theorem 1): fine-tuning on it drives the LLM to assign all probability mass to the confidence token closest to the true conditional correctness probability, yielding calibration incentives equivalent to those that make binary classifiers well-calibrated when trained with squared error loss. (Section 3.3, Theorem 1) [[tokenized-brier-score-is-proper-scoring-rule-for-verbalized-calibration]]
- ConfTuner achieves average ECE 0.1082 on LLaMA across five datasets, a 54.7% reduction relative to best baseline LACIE (0.2387), using only 2,000 training examples and 4 minutes of training on 4 A40 GPUs, demonstrating that a proxy-label-free proper-scoring loss is more data- and compute-efficient than sampling-based calibration methods. (Table 1, Table 6) [[conftuner]]
- ConfTuner generalizes to linguistic high/medium/low confidence expressions it was never trained on, achieving average AUROC 0.6511 vs. base 0.5718 and LACIE 0.5126 on LLaMA, showing that proper-scoring-rule SFT on numerical confidence induces a transferable calibration representation rather than format-specific memorization. (Table 3) [[proper-scoring-sft-calibration-transfers-to-unseen-confidence-formats]]
- ConfTuner acts as a surrogate calibrator for black-box GPT-4o, reducing GPT-4o average ECE from 0.1640 to 0.0970 and raising average AUROC from 0.5945 to 0.6275 by having an open-weight ConfTuner model assess the black-box model's outputs, demonstrating cross-model generalization of verbalized calibration training. (Table 5, Section 4.2) [[conftuner]]
- Well-calibrated verbalized confidence from ConfTuner enables model cascade accuracy gains of up to 9.3% on HotpotQA and 5.5% on TruthfulQA over LLaMA base at equal GPT-4o revision budget (100-400 low-confidence samples routed for revision), showing downstream utility beyond the calibration metric itself. (Section 4.3, Figure 5) [[calibrated-verbalized-confidence-enables-model-cascade-gains]]
