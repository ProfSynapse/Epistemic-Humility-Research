## Abstract

LLM reasoning transparency is a critical affordance for understanding model decisions, mitigating misuse and misalignment, and debugging surprising model behaviors. However, DiffusionGemma performs a larger fraction of its computation in a continuous latent space; does this make its reasoning less transparent? We study this question by decomposing transparency into two components: variable transparency, whether we understand intermediate snapshots of a model's computational state; and algorithmic transparency, whether we can use these snapshots to reconstruct the process by which the model arrived at its outputs. Naively, DiffusionGemma has poor variable transparency: its opaque serial depth, the amount of serial computation that occurs in between interpretable model states, seems at first 28.6X higher than the corresponding autoregressive Gemma 4 model. However, we show that we can map the information flowing between denoising steps through an interpretable token bottleneck with no decrease in downstream performance. Treating these intermediate states as interpretable reduces the opaque serial depth to just 1.1X that of Gemma 4. Algorithmic transparency is harder for diffusion models than for autoregressive models because all token predictions in the canvas can change at every denoising step, giving the model the power to implement complicated distributed algorithms during the denoising process. To begin bridging this gap, we conduct a suite of interpretability case studies, uncovering initial evidence of novel diffusion-specific phenomena such as non-chronological reasoning, token and sequence smearing, and intermediate-context reasoning. Finally, we test monitorability, a key application of transparency that measures whether model outputs are useful for downstream tasks. We find that DiffusionGemma is similarly monitorable to Gemma 4.

## Summary

This paper audits whether a text-diffusion reasoning model preserves the transparency properties usually associated with autoregressive chain-of-thought models. It separates variable transparency from algorithmic transparency, compares DiffusionGemma against Gemma 4, and treats monitorability as a practical downstream test of whether model outputs remain useful for detecting properties of the model's reasoning or behavior.

The main result is conditional. If the intermediate denoising-step token bottleneck is treated as interpretable, DiffusionGemma's opaque serial depth is close to Gemma 4; if not, the model has much more opaque serial computation. The paper also identifies diffusion-specific reasoning patterns including early response-length prediction, retroactive self-correction, non-chronological code generation, token and sequence smearing, and intermediate-context reasoning.

## Relevance to experiment

This is context and mechanism evidence rather than a quantitative training-effect row. It informs mechanism program by adding a non-autoregressive latent-reasoning architecture where uncertainty, monitorability, and reasoning transparency depend on intermediate-state access. It suggests that hidden-state and token-trajectory diagnostics should distinguish multi-canvas autoregressive-like behavior from single-canvas denoising behavior.

## Claims

- DiffusionGemma's apparent opacity depends strongly on whether denoising-step bottleneck states are treated as interpretable; Table 1 moves from 28.6x Gemma 4 opaque serial depth to 1.1x under that assumption. [[opaque-serial-depth]] [[token-bottleneck-restores-diffusion-transparency]]
- DiffusionGemma and Gemma 4 are reported as similarly monitorable on the adapted monitorability evaluations, with the caveat that the evaluated rollouts span multiple canvases. [[monitorability]]
- DiffusionGemma exhibits non-autoregressive reasoning patterns in which later or distributed denoising states can influence earlier positions, including retroactive self-correction and skeleton-first code generation. [[denoising-enables-non-chronological-reasoning]]
- The paper flags single-canvas monitorability as an unresolved risk because multi-canvas rollouts behave more autoregressively than a single denoising canvas. [[monitorability]] [[text-diffusion-language-model]]
