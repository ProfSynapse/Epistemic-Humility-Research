## Abstract

Abstention, the refusal of large language models (LLMs) to provide an answer, is increasingly recognized for its potential to mitigate hallucinations and enhance safety in LLM systems. In this survey, we introduce a framework to examine abstention from three perspectives: the query, the model, and human values. We organize the literature on abstention methods, benchmarks, and evaluation metrics using this framework, and discuss merits and limitations of prior work. We further identify and motivate areas for future research, such as whether abstention can be achieved as a meta-capability that transcends specific tasks or domains, and opportunities to optimize abstention abilities in specific contexts. In doing so, we aim to broaden the scope and impact of abstention methodologies in AI systems.

## Claims

- Abstention methods can be organized along three axes of the LLM lifecycle (pretraining, alignment, inference) and three decision perspectives (query answerability, model confidence, human value alignment), providing a unified framework absent from prior work. (§2.1, Fig. 1, Fig. 2) [[abstention-framework-query-model-values]]
- Instruction tuning on abstention-aware data improves abstention ability but can lead to over-abstention; researchers disagree on whether it enables abstention as a meta-capability that generalizes across tasks and domains. (§3.2 Instruction Tuning Summary) [[over-abstention]]
- No single metric captures all aspects of abstention performance; the survey recommends balancing task accuracy, abstention precision/recall/F1, abstention rate, and accuracy-coverage trade-off measures (ER, C@Acc, AUROC, AUACC, AURCC). (§4.2, Table 1) [[effective-reliability]]
- Existing abstention evaluation benchmarks primarily focus on a single perspective (query, model, or human values) rather than providing holistic, system-wide assessment; abstention research has been largely confined to question-answering rather than general chatbot settings. (§4.1, §1 p3) [[abstention]]
