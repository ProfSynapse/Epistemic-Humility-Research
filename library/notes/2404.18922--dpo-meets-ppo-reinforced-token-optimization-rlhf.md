## Abstract

In the classical Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) framework, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed to learn from sparse, sentence-level rewards -- a challenging scenario in traditional deep reinforcement learning. Despite the great successes of PPO in the alignment of large language models, its open-source implementation is still largely sub-optimal. To address these issues, we introduce a framework that models RLHF problems as a Markov decision process (MDP), enabling the capture of fine-grained token-wise information. Under this framework, we introduce an algorithm Reinforced Token Optimization (\texttt{RTO}), which learns the token-wise reward function from preference data and performs policy optimization based on this learned token-wise reward signal. Theoretically, \texttt{RTO} is proven to have the capability of finding the near-optimal policy sample-efficiently. For its practical implementation, \texttt{RTO} innovatively integrates Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and PPO. DPO, originally derived from sparse sentence rewards, surprisingly provides us with a token-wise characterization of response quality, which is seamlessly incorporated into our subsequent PPO training stage. Extensive experiments demonstrate that \texttt{RTO} performs better than PPO and other direct preference learning algorithms. In particular, RTO outperforms PPO by 7.5 points on the AlpacaEval 2 benchmark and by 4.1 points on Arena-Hard. Our code and models are available at \href{https://github.com/zkshan2002/RTO}{https://github.com/zkshan2002/RTO}.

## Summary

Zhong et al. propose Reinforced Token Optimization (RTO), an RLHF method that
recasts response generation as a token-level MDP. The practical algorithm uses
DPO to obtain an implicit token-wise reward signal and then uses PPO-style
policy optimization against that dense reward.

The paper is most relevant to this project as evidence for a distinct
`preference -> RL` design pattern: preference information is not merely another
offline objective, but can be converted into reward shaping for a later RL
optimization stage. That supports prioritizing SFT -> DPO/KTO -> GRPO style
crossings over reciprocal DPO/KTO stacking when compute is constrained.

## Extracted numbers

- Abstract / Section 5.1: RTO is reported to outperform PPO by 7.5 points on
  AlpacaEval 2 and by 4.1 points on Arena-Hard.
- Appendix F.1 / Table 3: on TL;DR summarization, RTO is reported as a 61%
  GPT-4-evaluated win rate over DPO.
- Appendix F.1 / Figure 6: the paper attributes faster reward improvement to
  the implicit DPO-derived reward used during RTO training.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper supports keeping `SFT -> DPO -> GRPO` and `SFT -> KTO -> GRPO` as
the stronger three-step experimental direction: preference-family training can
provide a signal that a later RL/reward optimizer exploits. It does not, by
itself, justify treating `DPO -> KTO` and `KTO -> DPO` as equally important
theoretical crossings, since those remain reciprocal offline preference-family
objectives rather than preference-to-RL or RL-to-preference order tests.

## Claims

- Method claim: RTO combines DPO-derived token-wise reward estimation with
  PPO-style policy optimization, making preference-derived dense rewards the
  bridge between offline preference learning and online policy optimization
  (Sections 3-4).
- Empirical claim: RTO outperforms PPO and direct preference-learning baselines
  on instruction-following benchmarks, including the reported AlpacaEval 2 and
  Arena-Hard deltas in the abstract and Section 5.1.
- Experiment-design claim: the paper gives external support for our
  `preference -> RL` stack comparison, but does not supply a matching rationale
  for reciprocal DPO/KTO ordering as a high-priority matrix arm.
