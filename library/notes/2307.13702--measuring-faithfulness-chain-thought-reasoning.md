## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) perform better when they produce step-by-step, "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) reasoning before answering a question, but it is unclear if the stated reasoning is a faithful explanation of the model's actual reasoning (i.e., its process for answering the question). We investigate hypotheses for how CoT reasoning may be unfaithful, by examining how the model predictions change when we intervene on the CoT (e.g., by adding mistakes or paraphrasing it). Models show large variation across tasks in how strongly they condition on the CoT when predicting their answer, sometimes relying heavily on the CoT and other times primarily ignoring it. CoT's performance boost does not seem to come from CoT's added test-time compute alone or from information encoded via the particular phrasing of the CoT. As models become larger and more capable, they produce less faithful reasoning on most tasks we study. Overall, our results suggest that CoT can be faithful if the circumstances such as the model size and task are carefully chosen.

## Claims

- Task variation in CoT faithfulness is large and not correlated with CoT accuracy gains: AQuA early-answering AOC = 0.44 and adding-mistakes AOC = 0.52 (most post-hoc), while ARC Easy AOC = 0.02 and 0.07 respectively (least post-hoc). HellaSwag shows an accuracy drop of -4.69% with CoT yet low post-hoc reasoning. (Table 2) [[faithfulness-area-over-curve]]
- Replacing the chain-of-thought with filler '...' tokens of equivalent length yields no accuracy improvement over the no-CoT baseline for any of the eight tasks; for TruthfulQA and OpenBookQA, performance drops slightly with longer filler contexts. This rules out test-time compute alone as the driver of CoT performance gains. (Figure 5) [[filler-tokens-test]]
- Paraphrased CoT achieves nearly identical accuracy to original CoT across all tasks and CoT lengths, indicating that phrasing-encoded information (e.g., steganographic cues, punctuation, word choice) is not a major driver of the accuracy gains conferred by chain-of-thought prompting. (Figure 6) [[chain-of-thought-faithfulness]]
- CoT faithfulness decreases monotonically with model size from 13B to 175B parameters for 6 of 8 standard tasks (all but AQuA and LogiQA), with the 13B model showing the most faithful reasoning on those 6 tasks. On addition tasks, faithfulness of CoT increases with model size, suggesting inverse scaling is task-specific. (Figure 7, Figure 8) [[chain-of-thought-faithfulness]]
