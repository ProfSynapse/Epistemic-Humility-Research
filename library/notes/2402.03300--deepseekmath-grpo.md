## Abstract

Mathematical reasoning poses a significant challenge for language models due to its complex and structured nature. In this paper, we introduce DeepSeekMath 7B, which continues pre-training DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B with 120B math-related tokens sourced from Common Crawl, together with natural language and code data. DeepSeekMath 7B has achieved an impressive score of 51.7% on the competition-level MATH benchmark without relying on external toolkits and voting techniques, approaching the performance level of Gemini-Ultra and GPT-4. Self-consistency over 64 samples from DeepSeekMath 7B achieves 60.9% on MATH. The mathematical reasoning capability of DeepSeekMath is attributed to two key factors: First, we harness the significant potential of publicly available web data through a meticulously engineered data selection pipeline. Second, we introduce Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), that enhances mathematical reasoning abilities while concurrently optimizing the memory usage of PPO.

## Summary

DeepSeekMath introduces two related contributions: a large-scale math pre-training corpus and a new RL training algorithm. The DeepSeekMath Corpus is a 120.2B-token dataset mined from Common Crawl through four iterations of fastText-based filtering, seeding from OpenWebMath and expanding via domain annotation to reach 35.5M mathematical web pages. DeepSeekMath-Base 7B is trained for 500B total tokens with the Corpus as the dominant math data source, alongside AlgebraicStack, arXiv, GitHub code, and natural language text. Despite being 77x smaller, DeepSeekMath-Base 7B outperforms Minerva 540B on both GSM8K and MATH, demonstrating that high-quality domain-specific data can substitute for scale. An important pre-training design finding is that code training before math training improves both tool-assisted and tool-free mathematical reasoning, while arXiv-only corpora provide no notable benefit. After supervised fine-tuning to produce DeepSeekMath-Instruct, the paper introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): a PPO variant that discards the critic model and instead normalizes per-response rewards against the mean and standard deviation of a group of G=64 sampled outputs for the same prompt. This group baseline replaces the value network, cutting GPU memory substantially. The policy is optimized with a clipped surrogate objective plus a per-token KL penalty (beta=0.04) against the reference policy, using a 144K-question RL dataset, policy learning rate 1e-6, and max sequence length 1024. GRPO raises GSM8K from 82.9% to 88.2% and MATH from 46.8% to 51.7% over the SFT baseline, with out-of-domain transfer to Chinese benchmarks. A unified paradigm shows that SFT, DPO, PPO, and GRPO all reduce to gradient updates weighted by algorithm-specific coefficients, establishing GRPO as part of a broader RL family rather than a category apart. The paper also finds that RL increases majority-vote accuracy (Maj@K) without improving sample-level diversity (Pass@K), indicating that RL robustifies the output distribution rather than raising the fundamental capability ceiling, a finding with direct implications for understanding what RL training changes in a model's behavior.

Most numbers represent benchmark accuracy deltas from pre-training or RL interventions; they map to the effects.csv intervention schema where training method is the treatment and benchmark accuracy is the outcome, though the paper does not report calibration or abstention metrics directly.

- DeepSeekMath Corpus: 35.5M mathematical web pages totaling 120.2B tokens, mined from Common Crawl via four iterations of fastText-based filtering (Section 2.1; Table 1).
- DeepSeekMath Corpus on DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B: 23.8% GSM8K and 13.6% MATH, vs Proof-Pile-2 (14.3% / 11.2%) and OpenWebMath (11.5% / 8.9%) at the same 1.3B scale (Table 1).
- DeepSeekMath-Base 7B: 64.2% GSM8K and 36.2% MATH (chain-of-thought, no tool use), outperforming Minerva 540B (58.8% GSM8K / 33.6% MATH) by a model 77x smaller (Table 2; Section 2.3).
- Code pre-training before math training improves MATH from 14.4% (general+math path) to 15.3% (code+math path) and MATH+Python from 6.7% to 9.4% on DeepSeek-LLM 1.3B (Table 6).
- Math pre-training generalizes to general reasoning: DeepSeekMath-Base 7B improves BBH from 42.9% to 59.5% and MMLU from 42.9% to 54.9% relative to the DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 pre-decay checkpoint (Table 4).
- ArXiv-only continual training (MathPile or ArXiv-RedPajama) yields no notable improvement or degradation on GSM8K, MATH, and formal proving benchmarks for both 1.3B and 7B models (Tables 8-9; Section 5.1.2).
- GRPO (DeepSeekMath-RL 7B) raises GSM8K from 82.9% to 88.2% (+5.3 pp) and MATH from 46.8% to 51.7% (+4.9 pp) over the SFT baseline (Table 5; Section 4.2).
- GRPO out-of-domain transfer: CMATH 84.6% to 88.8% and MGSM-zh 73.2% to 79.6% (Table 5).
- Self-consistency Maj@64 over DeepSeekMath-RL 7B achieves 60.9% on MATH, exceeding Gemini-Ultra single-sample CoT Top1 (53.2%) and GPT-4 single-sample CoT Top1 (52.9%) -- note: the 60.9% is a majority-vote score while the Gemini/GPT-4 figures are non-majority-vote Top1 scores from Table 5, so the comparison is not apples-to-apples (Abstract; Table 5).
- GRPO hyperparameters: G=64 sampled outputs per question, KL coefficient beta=0.04, policy learning rate 1e-6, max sequence length 1024, RL dataset approximately 144K questions (Section 4.2).
- GRPO+PS (process supervision) outperforms GRPO+OS (outcome supervision), which outperforms Online RFT, in the 1.3B ablation study (Section 5.2.1; Figure 5).
- RL training improves Maj@K (majority-vote accuracy) but not Pass@K (any-sample-correct probability), indicating RL robustifies the output distribution rather than raising the model's fundamental capability ceiling (Figure 7; Section 5.2.2).

## Relevance to experiment

Three connections to the epistemic-humility experiment are direct. First, GRPO is the RL algorithm most likely to appear as a comparison or inspiration for any Phase 1 RL arm: it shares the online, reward-signal-driven update structure but differs from DPO and KTO in that it never forms explicit preference pairs. The unified gradient-coefficient framework (Section 5.3, Table 10) places SFT, DPO, PPO, and GRPO in the same family, which means any Phase 1 finding about DPO or SFT carries over to a GRPO comparison in a principled way. Second, the Maj@K-improves / Pass@K-flat finding is a mechanistic diagnostic for what RL training changes: it makes the output distribution more concentrated around correct answers without increasing the probability that any single sample is correct. This maps directly onto the abstention question: RL may produce models that look more confident (higher Maj@K) without actually knowing more (Pass@K unchanged), a potential source of the over-confidence patterns documented elsewhere in the graph (see reasoning-training-decouples-decisiveness-from-confidence, deeper-reasoning-increases-overconfidence). Third, the process-vs-outcome supervision comparison (GRPO+PS above GRPO+OS) is relevant to reward design in any RL-based abstention training: step-level correctness feedback is harder to obtain but appears to give stronger gradients, suggesting that if the Phase 1 experiment moves toward RL, process-annotated abstention chains would be worth the label cost. The hyperparameter anchors (beta=0.04, G=64, lr=1e-6) give a concrete starting point for any GRPO replication or adaptation.

- GRPO with process supervision (GRPO+PS) outperforms GRPO with outcome supervision (GRPO+OS), indicating that fine-grained step-aware gradient coefficients provide additional benefit. (Section 5.2.1, Figure 5) [[outcome-reward-model]]
