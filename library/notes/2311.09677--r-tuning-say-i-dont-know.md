## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have revolutionized numerous domains with their impressive performance but still face their challenges. A predominant issue is the propensity for these models to generate non-existent facts, a concern termed hallucination. Our research is motivated by the observation that previous instruction tuning methods force the model to complete a sentence no matter whether the model knows the knowledge or not. When the question is out of the parametric knowledge, it will try to make up something and fail to indicate when it lacks knowledge. In this paper, we present a new approach called Refusal-Aware Instruction Tuning (R-Tuning). This approach is formalized by first identifying the disparity in knowledge encompassed by pre-trained parameters compared to that of instruction tuning data. Then, we construct the refusal-aware data based on the knowledge intersection, to tune LLMs to refrain from responding to questions beyond its parametric knowledge. Experimental results demonstrate R-Tuning effectively improves a model's ability to answer known questions and refrain from answering unknown questions. Furthermore, when tested on out-of-domain datasets, the refusal ability was found to be a meta-skill that could be generalized to other tasks. Further analysis surprisingly finds that learning the uncertainty results in better calibration and an improved ability to estimate the uncertainty than uncertainty-based testing. Our code is available at https://github.com/shizhediao/R-Tuning.

## Summary

Zhang et al. introduce Refusal-Aware Instruction Tuning (R-Tuning), a two-step SFT method that teaches LLMs to say "I don't know" precisely where their parametric knowledge is insufficient. The key insight is that standard instruction tuning forces a model to complete answers even for questions outside its knowledge, causing it to hallucinate rather than abstain. R-Tuning first runs the pre-trained model on the training set and splits examples into D1 (certain: prediction matches label) and D0 (uncertain: mismatch). It then fine-tunes on both sets, padding D1 examples with "I am sure" and D0 examples with "I am unsure" after a fixed certainty prompt. At inference, the model first produces an answer and then is prompted to express its confidence; the combined confidence score enables precision-recall tradeoff measurement via Average Precision (AP). An unsupervised variant (R-Tuning-U) replaces label-matching with entropy over k=10 samples for the D0/D1 split. A replacement variant (R-Tuning-R) substitutes uncertainty expressions for the answer on D0, achieving near-100% refusal on unanswerable benchmarks. Single-task experiments run on ParaRel and MMLU; multi-task experiments train on five datasets (ParaRel, MMLU, WiCE, HotpotQA, FEVER) and test on the unseen HaluEval-QA. A key finding is that learning uncertainty during training outperforms applying uncertainty-based filtering at test time (Vanilla-C, k=10), both on AP scores and on answer quality. Perplexity and entropy analyses confirm that D0 questions are systematically harder for the pre-trained model, grounding the partition in internal model state. The refusal ability transfers across task types and datasets, which the authors characterize as a task-agnostic meta-skill.

## Relevance to experiment

R-Tuning is the founding SFT baseline for knowledge-boundary-aware abstention and is directly upstream of Phase 1. Three connections matter. First, the paper's core claim, confirmed across all ten number checks, is that training-time uncertainty labeling beats test-time uncertainty filtering (Vanilla-C) on every split and model size. That is the mechanism the Phase 1 SFT arm operationalizes, making R-Tuning the paper most directly compared against when measuring whether SFT, DPO, or KTO closes more of the gap. Second, the refusal generalization result (Figure 4, Section 4.2) shows that a model trained on five tasks transfers its refusal behavior to unseen HaluEval-QA at inference, which is the same OOD abstention generalization the Phase 1 holdout sets are designed to measure. Third, Tables 6-7 provide perplexity and entropy as interpretability targets: D0 questions have higher model perplexity and higher oracle entropy. These are direct activation-space candidate variables for the mechinterp sweep, linking internal difficulty signals to the known/unknown partition before any fine-tuning occurs. The Vanilla-C vs R-Tuning framing (training-time vs test-time uncertainty) is the cleanest experimental contrast in the R-Tuning paper for mapping onto the Phase 1 study design.

## Claims

- R-Tuning significantly outperforms vanilla instruction tuning on both in-domain and out-of-domain accuracy for answered questions across ParaRel and MMLU, e.g., LLaMA-13B achieves 77.30 vs 64.12 (OOD, ParaRel) AP score. (Table 1) [[refusal-aware-instruction-tuning]]
- Refusal-aware answering ability generalizes as a meta-skill: R-Tuning trained on five tasks achieves higher AP scores than baselines on the unseen out-of-domain HaluEval-QA dataset. (Figure 4, Section 4.2) [[refusal-aware-instruction-tuning]]
- R-Tuning and R-Tuning-R achieve near-100% refusal rate on unanswerable questions in SelfAware (e.g., LLaMA-13B 99.71% and 96.61%), while vanilla fine-tuned baselines and pre-trained models refuse far fewer (12-28%). (Table 5) [[abstention-rate]]
- Learning uncertainty during training (R-Tuning-U) outperforms directly filtering questions by uncertainty at test time, demonstrating that uncertainty learning improves both calibration and answer quality simultaneously. (Table 3, Table 4, Section 5.1) [[average-precision-score]]
