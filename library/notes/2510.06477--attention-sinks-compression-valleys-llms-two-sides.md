## Abstract

Attention sinks and compression valleys have attracted significant attention as two puzzling phenomena in large language models, but have been studied in isolation. In this work, we present a surprising connection between attention sinks and compression valleys, tracing both to the formation of massive activations in the residual stream. We prove theoretically that massive activations necessarily produce representational compression and establish bounds on the resulting entropy reduction. Through experiments across several models (410M-120B parameters), we confirm that when the beginning-of-sequence token develops extreme activation norms in the middle layers, both compression valleys and attention sinks emerge simultaneously. Targeted ablation studies validate our theoretical predictions. This unified view motivates us to propose the Mix-Compress-Refine theory of information flow, as an attempt to explain how LLMs organize their computation in depth by controlling attention and representational compression via massive activations. Specifically, we posit that Transformer-based LLMs process tokens in three distinct phases: (1) broad mixing in the early layers, (2) compressed computation with limited mixing in the middle layers, and (3) selective refinement in the late layers. Our framework helps explain why embedding tasks perform best at intermediate layers, whereas generation tasks benefit from full-depth processing, clarifying differences in task-dependent representations.

## Claims

- Evidence label: theory (Theorem 1, Corollary 2). Massive activations mathematically necessitate representational compression: for a representation matrix X with bos-token squared norm M and remaining-token squared norm R, the leading singular value satisfies sigma_1^2 >= M + alpha*R (Theorem 1), giving closed-form lower bounds on spectral dominance and anisotropy and an upper bound on matrix-based entropy in terms of the norm ratio c=M/R (Corollary 2); these bounds become nearly exact once massive activations emerge in middle layers (Figure 3, Pythia 410M on 100 RedPajama examples).
- Evidence label: experiment (cross-model correlation, Figure 1 / Section 3.1). Across six models (Pythia 410M/6.9B, LLaMA3-8B, Qwen2-7B, Gemma-7B, Bloom-1.7B) evaluated on all 7.5K GSM8K training examples, bos-token norm spikes of 10^3-10^4x coincide precisely with matrix-based entropy dropping below 0.5 bits and sink rate surging to near 1.0 in the same middle layers, with a cross-model Pearson correlation of r=-0.9+/-0.18 between change in bos norm and entropy, and r=0.58+/-0.25 between change in bos norm and sink rate.
- Evidence label: causal ablation (Figure 4, Section 3.3). Zeroing only the layer-0 MLP's contribution to the bos token in LLaMA3-8B (leaving the attention contribution intact) eliminates both downstream phenomena: entropy stays at ~0.4-0.5 bits instead of dropping to 0.02 bits, sink rate drops from 0.85-1.0 to 0.0 throughout depth, and bos norm stays within 2x of other tokens instead of spiking ~100x, confirming a causal (not merely correlational) role for massive activations in producing both compression and sinks; some models (Pythia 410M, Qwen2-7B) develop massive activations in more than one stage, and ablating all stages is needed to eliminate compression entirely.
- Evidence label: experiment (downstream task depth profiles, Figure 8 and Appendix Figure 32). Task type determines which Mix-Compress-Refine phase yields peak performance: linear-probe accuracy on multiple-choice QA (e.g., ARC Easy) and 32 MTEB embedding tasks peak consistently at 25-75% relative depth -- the Phase 2 compression window -- outperforming early/late layers by 10-20%, whereas WikiText-2 perplexity (via LogitLens) and the same multiple-choice QA tasks' zero-shot generation accuracy stay flat until roughly 40-60% depth and then improve through Phase 3, requiring norm equalization and positional/identity-head refinement to peak.
