## Abstract

We provide evidence of performative chain-of-thought (CoT) in reasoning models, where a model becomes strongly confident in its final answer, but continues generating tokens without revealing its internal belief. Our analysis compares activation probing, early forced answering, and a CoT monitor across two large models (DeepSeek-R1 671B & GPT-OSS 120B) and find task difficulty-specific differences: The model's final answer is decodable from activations far earlier in CoT than a monitor is able to say, especially for easy recall-based MMLU questions. We contrast this with genuine reasoning in difficult multihop GPQA-Diamond questions. Despite this, inflection points (e.g., backtracking, 'aha' moments) occur almost exclusively in responses where probes show large belief shifts, suggesting these behaviors track genuine uncertainty rather than learned "reasoning theater." Finally, probe-guided early exit reduces tokens by up to 80% on MMLU and 30% on GPQA-Diamond with similar accuracy, positioning attention probing as an efficient tool for detecting performative reasoning and enabling adaptive computation.

## Claims

- MMLU-Redux shows performativity rate 0.417 for DeepSeek-R1 vs. 0.012 for GPQA-Diamond, measured as the average slope difference between probe/forced-answer accuracy and the CoT monitor accuracy over 5% timestep bins — confirming that recall-based tasks produce far more performative reasoning than multi-hop tasks. (Table 1) [[performative-chain-of-thought]]
- High-confidence responses (high internal probe confidence) contain dramatically fewer inflection points per step (0.020 total) than non-high-confidence responses (0.045 total) for DeepSeek-R1 on MMLU, and this gap holds for GPT-OSS 120B — indicating that backtracking and 'aha' moments track genuine internal uncertainty rather than learned theatrical reasoning. (Table 2) [[performative-chain-of-thought]]
- Probe-guided early exit at 95% confidence threshold on MMLU-Redux retains 97% of original accuracy while saving 68% of generated tokens; at 80% confidence on GPQA-Diamond the same approach retains 97% accuracy while saving 50% of tokens — demonstrating that internal activation signals alone can drive practical adaptive computation without a CoT monitor. (Figure 8) [[probe-guided-early-exit]]
- On MMLU-Redux, attention probes and forced answering achieve substantially higher accuracy than the CoT monitor from the very first reasoning steps (Figure 2, left), while on GPQA-Diamond all three methods begin near chance and improve at comparable rates (Figure 2, right) — establishing a difficulty-dependent split between performative and genuine reasoning. (Figure 2) [[attention-probing]]
