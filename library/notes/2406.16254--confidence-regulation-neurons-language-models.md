## Abstract

Despite their widespread use, the mechanisms by which large language models (LLMs) represent and regulate uncertainty in next-token predictions remain largely unexplored. This study investigates two critical components believed to influence this uncertainty: the recently discovered entropy neurons and a new set of components that we term token frequency neurons. Entropy neurons are characterized by an unusually high weight norm and influence the final layer normalization (LayerNorm) scale to effectively scale down the logits. Our work shows that entropy neurons operate by writing onto an unembedding null space, allowing them to impact the residual stream norm with minimal direct effect on the logits themselves. We observe the presence of entropy neurons across a range of models, up to 7 billion parameters. On the other hand, token frequency neurons, which we discover and describe here for the first time, boost or suppress each token's logit proportionally to its log frequency, thereby shifting the output distribution towards or away from the unigram distribution. Finally, we present a detailed case study where entropy neurons actively manage confidence in the setting of induction, i.e. detecting and continuing repeated subsequences.

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment. Entropy neurons in GPT-2 Small combine high output-weight norm with low direct logit-projection variance (LogitVar); comparing total effect (TE) to LayerNorm-held-constant direct effect (DE) for 6 identified entropy neurons versus 100 random neurons shows a substantial TE-DE gap only for entropy neurons, confirming their effect on loss/entropy is mediated by the final LayerNorm rather than by directly shifting logits (Fig. 2a-c, Sec. 3.1-3.2).
- Evidence label: analysis. The unembedding matrix W_U has an effective low-rank null space (a sharp singular-value drop around index ~755 of 768 in GPT-2 Small); entropy neurons write their output weights almost exclusively into this null space, and the fraction of a neuron's norm projected there (rho) predicts the size of its LayerNorm-mediated effect. Entropy neurons with this same signature are observed across GPT-2 (Small, Medium), Pythia (410M, 1B), Phi-2, Gemma 2B, and LLaMA2 7B -- up to 7B parameters (Fig. 2d-f, Sec. 3.3-3.4, Appendix F).
- Evidence label: experiment. A newly identified class of token frequency neurons in Pythia 410M shifts the model's output distribution toward or away from the empirical token-frequency (unigram) distribution computed on The Pile; output entropy is negatively correlated with KL(P_freq||P_model), and ablating these neurons substantially changes that divergence relative to the token-frequency-direction-held-constant baseline (Fig. 3a-c, Sec. 4).
- Evidence label: experiment. In an induction case study on GPT-2 Small, mean-ablating entropy neuron 11.2378 during the repeated portion of duplicated 200-token sequences reduces output entropy by up to 70%, and BOS-ablating the top 3 induction heads (L5H1, L5H5, L6H9) substantially reduces this neuron's activation -- indicating induction heads causally drive entropy-neuron activation as a hedge against confidently-wrong predictions during induction (Fig. 4-5, Sec. 6).
