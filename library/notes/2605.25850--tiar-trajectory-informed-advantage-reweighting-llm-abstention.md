## Abstract

This paper investigates large language model (LLM) abstention learning, specifically using ternary reward, which incentivize truthfulness in large language models. This paper extends that idea by moving from a ternary reward to a Trajectory-Informed advantage reweighting, dynamically re-weights the abstention reward during Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) training. The objective of this work focuses on abstention learning instead of improving truthfulness, serving as an exploration into hallucination reduction. The novelty of this paper lies in methodological innovation, advantage re-weighting, and benchmark selection. Leveraging GRPO's multiple trajectories as a natural abstention signal, this method uses a reward signal to explore knowledge boundaries and encourage consistency. By demonstrating that trajectories can be used as a confidence indicator of the policy relative to the query, they are then used to dynamically calculate the abstention advantage. AbstentionBench is used as the evaluation benchmark, as this work aims to contribute to the field of abstention learning. All datasets on the benchmark were tested against this method and various baselines. Empirical results demonstrate that TIAR achieves state-of-the-art abstention F1 scores across five of six evaluation categories, outperforming the static ternary baseline on 17 of 31 benchmark datasets while fully preserving baseline accuracy.

## Summary

Pan et al. extend the static ternary-reward GRPO abstention setup (TruthRL: +1 correct, -1 incorrect, 0 abstain) with Trajectory-Informed Advantage Reweighting (TIAR): GRPO already samples multiple trajectories per training query, and the paper treats the consistency of those trajectories as a natural confidence signal, dynamically reweighting the abstention advantage (via a scaling factor lambda) instead of using a fixed abstention reward regardless of query difficulty. Setting lambda=0 reduces exactly to the static ternary-reward baseline, isolating the effect of the reweighting itself. Evaluated on AbstentionBench (20 datasets, 31 subsets across six abstention scenarios) with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B as base checkpoints, TIAR is compared against four training-based baselines, all starting from the same instruct checkpoint: R-Tuning (SFT on relabeled out-of-knowledge questions), Rejection Fine-Tuning (SFT filtered to correct responses only), DPO (preference pairs with "I don't know" preferred for out-of-knowledge questions), and TruthRL (the static ternary-reward GRPO ablation, lambda=0). TIAR achieves state-of-the-art abstention F1 in five of six evaluation categories and outperforms the static ternary baseline on 17 of 31 datasets while fully preserving baseline accuracy.

## Extracted numbers

- Training setup: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B as base checkpoints for all methods; GRPO training uses the TruthRL-CRAG dataset, 656 training samples derived from the CRAG benchmark (Section 4.1)
- Baselines, all on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Section 4.1): R-Tuning (SFT with out-of-knowledge relabeling via 256-sample knowledge-boundary probing); Rejection Fine-Tuning (same probing, fine-tuned only on correctly answered responses); DPO (preference pairs, "I don't know" preferred over incorrect answers); TruthRL (GRPO with static ternary reward, same training configuration as TIAR, serving as the direct ablation)
- Ablation on the inversion weight lambda (Section 5.2, Table 3, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, full 31-dataset AbstentionBench suite): lambda=1.0 achieves the highest average abstention F1 (71.9) and near-highest average accuracy (72.6 vs. 72.7 for lambda=0/TruthRL), outperforming lambda=0 on 17 of 31 datasets for F1 and 11 of 24 for accuracy; lambda=0.3 causes a collapse in both metrics, showing a non-monotonic relationship
- Headline result: state-of-the-art abstention F1 across five of six AbstentionBench evaluation categories, outperforming the static ternary baseline on 17 of 31 benchmark datasets, while fully preserving baseline accuracy (Abstract)
- Evaluation protocol: vLLM inference at temperature 0.8, top-p 0.95, max generation length 4,096 tokens; two-stage LLM-as-Judge (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) pipeline validated against human annotation at 82.3% accuracy by the AbstentionBench authors (Section 4.2)

**Prompted-only baseline check (explicitly requested for this ingest):** TIAR has no prompted-only, zero-shot, or instruction-removed evaluation arm. Every method in the comparison table (R-Tuning, Rejection Fine-Tuning, DPO, TruthRL, TIAR) is a training-time intervention applied on top of the same instruction-tuned checkpoint (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Qwen3-8B); the paper's few mentions of "base model" (Section 4.1, Section 5.1, Section 6) refer to that starting checkpoint each method trains from, never to an untrained or prompt-only condition evaluated on its own. This confirms the prior read: the paper offers no data point on how much of TIAR's abstention gain a prompted-only approach could reach without any of these training interventions.

## Relevance to experiment

TIAR is useful as a state-of-the-art reference point for training-only abstention improvement (GRPO with trajectory-informed reward shaping), and as one more entry in the family of RL-based abstention methods alongside [[truthrl]] and [[r-tuning]] that the locked training-regimen SFT/DPO/KTO comparison sits next to. Because it evaluates no prompted-only condition, it cannot itself narrow the trained-vs-prompted gap question this related-work reframe is built around; it is evidence about which training recipe is best among training recipes, not about how much of the gain training buys over a well-constructed prompt. Its ablation methodology (isolating the reweighting from the base reward via a lambda=0 clean ablation on the same training pipeline) is a reusable pattern for the locked training-regimen arms' own ablations.
