## Abstract

Refusal-Aware Instruction Tuning (RAIT) enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to refuse to answer unknown questions. By modifying responses of unknown questions in the training data to refusal responses such as "I don't know", RAIT enhances the reliability of LLMs and reduces their hallucination. Generally, RAIT modifies training samples based on the correctness of the initial LLM's response. However, this crude approach can cause LLMs to excessively refuse answering questions they could have correctly answered, the problem we call over-refusal. In this paper, we explore two primary causes of over-refusal: Static conflict occurs when similar samples within the LLM's feature space receive differing supervision signals (original vs. modified "I don't know"). Dynamic conflict arises as the LLM's evolving knowledge during SFT enables it to answer previously unanswerable questions, but the now-answerable training samples still retain the original "I don't know" supervision signals from the initial LLM state, leading to inconsistencies. These conflicts cause the trained LLM to misclassify known questions as unknown, resulting in over-refusal. To address this issue, we introduce Certainty Represented Knowledge Flow for Refusal-Aware Instructions Tuning (CRaFT). CRaFT centers on two main contributions: First, we additionally incorporate response certainty to selectively filter and modify data, reducing static conflicts. Second, we implement preliminary rehearsal training to characterize changes in the LLM's knowledge state, which helps mitigate dynamic conflicts during the fine-tuning process. We conducted extensive experiments on open-ended question answering and multiple-choice question task. Experiment results show that CRaFT can improve LLM's overall performance during the RAIT process. Code and data will be released at https://github.com/opendatalab/CRaFT .

## Summary

Zhu et al. diagnose over-refusal in Refusal-Aware Instruction Tuning (RAIT) and propose CRaFT as a two-stage remedy. The paper names two distinct root causes. Static conflict arises because correctness-based data construction (Cor-RAIT) places semantically similar samples on opposite sides of the vanilla/IdK partition in the model's feature space, producing contradictory supervision signals; t-SNE and the Conflict Rate for Similar Samples (CRSS) metric both confirm large overlap between the two subsets. Dynamic conflict arises because the model's knowledge state shifts during supervised fine-tuning: questions that were initially unknown become answerable as training progresses, yet the training targets were fixed at construction time and still say "I don't know," creating a mismatch that compounds the over-refusal tendency. CRaFT addresses static conflict by adding response certainty (negative entropy for MCQA; cross-response semantic similarity for OEQA) alongside correctness as a joint filter, which substantially reduces CRSS. It addresses dynamic conflict through a rehearsal training pass: the initial model is fine-tuned only on its highest-certainty, highest-correctness samples, producing a perturbed model whose knowledge state is then compared to the original. The resulting correctness and certainty deltas characterize the knowledge flow and allow stale IdK labels to be corrected before the final RAIT fine-tune. The paper also argues that every published refusal-aware metric (S_honesty, TRUTHFUL, R-Acc, ER, A-Acc, A-F1, AP) fails to rank at least one pair of model variants correctly in a constructed counterexample, and proposes the Truthful Helpfulness Score (THS) as a replacement. THS is a geometric metric in the (P_correct, P_wrong) space based on the cross-product magnitude, designed to balance reliability and helpfulness simultaneously. The theoretical motivation for certainty as the right filter variable is that the gradient of correctness with respect to the hidden state is non-differentiable at token boundaries (Appendix A.2), making correctness alone a weak, discontinuous signal; certainty is smoother and more coherent across nearby feature-space neighbors. Experiments span TriviaQA and Natural Questions (OEQA) and MMLU and ARC-c (MCQA) on LLaMA-2-7B-Chat and LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, with CRaFT consistently improving THS over Cor-RAIT on in-distribution tasks and providing modest gains on out-of-distribution splits.

## Relevance to experiment

CRaFT bears directly on Phase 1 in three ways. First, THS is a candidate evaluation metric for the SFT-vs-DPO-vs-KTO abstention study: the paper's systematic demonstration that every incumbent refusal-aware metric misfires on at least one model comparison motivates adopting THS as the primary composite score, or at minimum running it alongside existing metrics as a cross-check. Second, the rehearsal training mechanism is a data-construction technique layerable on any SFT pipeline: it does not require preference pairs or a reward model, which means it could be applied inside any of the three Phase 1 training arms before the main fine-tune, potentially reducing the over-abstention cost that the existing node sft-abstention-causes-over-refusal documents. Third, the dynamic conflict finding yields a direct testable prediction for Phase 3 mechanistic interpretability: if 69% of initially-unknown samples become knowable during fine-tuning, and if those samples still carry IdK labels, the model's internal knowledge representation and its output label will be misaligned at those positions. Phase 3 probes targeting the known-unknown direction should show an elevated false-negative rate precisely for samples that would have shifted category under rehearsal training. The OOD THS gains are modest (LLaMA-2 OEQA OOD: CRaFT 6.5 vs Cor-RAIT 4.7), consistent with limited generalization under distribution shift, which is a standing caveat for any Phase 1 training recipe.

- Cor-RAIT produces large overlap between vanilla and IdK subsets in the LLM's feature space (static conflict), confirmed by t-SNE visualization and the CRSS metric, and this overlap degrades abstention calibration by imposing contradictory supervision on semantically similar samples. - Certainty-filtered data construction (CRaFT Stage 1) substantially reduces CRSS and the resulting THS gain over Cor-RAIT is 3.56 points on TriviaQA for LLaMA-2-7B-Chat; removing the certainty filter (w/o Cer ablation) drops THS from 23.29 to 20.11, isolating certainty's independent contribution. (Table 4, Section 6.1) [[craft]]
- Rehearsal training reveals that 69% of MMLU samples initially below correctness 0.5 improve during fine-tuning, validating the dynamic conflict hypothesis and motivating knowledge-flow-aware IdK label correction. (Section 3.4) [[sft-knowledge-state-shifts-during-training]]
- Every one of the seven existing refusal-aware metrics (S_honesty, TRUTHFUL, R-Acc, ER, A-Acc, A-F1, AP) fails to rank at least one pair of model variants correctly in a constructed four-model ordering; THS correctly orders all four by treating the correctness-abstention tradeoff as a geometric area in (P_correct, P_wrong) space. (Table 1, Section 5.2) [[truthful-helpfulness-score]]
