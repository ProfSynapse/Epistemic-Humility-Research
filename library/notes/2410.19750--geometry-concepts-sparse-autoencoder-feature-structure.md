## Abstract

Sparse autoencoders have recently produced dictionaries of high-dimensional vectors corresponding to the universe of concepts represented by large language models. We find that this concept universe has interesting structure at three levels: 1) The "atomic" small-scale structure contains "crystals" whose faces are parallelograms or trapezoids, generalizing well-known examples such as (man-woman-king-queen). We find that the quality of such parallelograms and associated function vectors improves greatly when projecting out global distractor directions such as word length, which is efficiently done with linear discriminant analysis. 2) The "brain" intermediate-scale structure has significant spatial modularity; for example, math and code features form a "lobe" akin to functional lobes seen in neural fMRI images. We quantify the spatial locality of these lobes with multiple metrics and find that clusters of co-occurring features, at coarse enough scale, also cluster together spatially far more than one would expect if feature geometry were random. 3) The "galaxy" scale large-scale structure of the feature point cloud is not isotropic, but instead has a power law of eigenvalues with steepest slope in middle layers. We also quantify how the clustering entropy depends on the layer.

## Claims

- LDA projection of distractor directions dramatically improves parallelogram and trapezoid quality in SAE crystal structures, demonstrating that semantically irrelevant features (e.g., word length) hide existing geometric relational structure. (Figure 1) [[sae-crystal-structure]]
- SAE functional modularity is statistically robust: phi-coefficient co-occurrence clustering rules out spatial randomness at 954 standard deviations (adjusted mutual information) and 74 standard deviations (balanced logistic regression accuracy) for Gemma-2-2b Layer 12. (Figure 4) [[sae-functional-modularity]]
- The eigenvalue spectrum of Gemma-2-2b SAE feature covariance decays as a power law (not Marchenko-Pastur), with the steepest slope in middle layers: Layer 12 slope 0.47 vs. Layers 0 and 24 at 0.24 and 0.25. (Figure 7) [[sae-eigenvalue-power-law]]
- SAE features exhibit significantly steeper eigenvalue power-law decay than raw model activations from the same layers, indicating that the SAE dictionary captures more concentrated geometric structure than the raw activation space. (Figure 7) [[sae-eigenvalue-power-law]]
