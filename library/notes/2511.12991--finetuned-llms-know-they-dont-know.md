## Abstract

The honesty of Large Language Models (LLMs) is increasingly important for safe deployment in high-stakes domains. However, this crucial trait is severely undermined by supervised fine-tuning (SFT), a common technique for model specialization. Existing recovery methods rely on data-intensive global parameter adjustments, implicitly assuming that SFT deeply corrupts the models' ability to recognize their knowledge boundaries. However, we observe that fine-tuned LLMs still preserve this ability; what is damaged is their capacity to faithfully express that awareness. Building on this, we propose Honesty-Critical Neurons Restoration (HCNR) to surgically repair this suppressed capacity. HCNR identifies and restores key expression-governing neurons to their pre-trained state while harmonizing them with task-oriented neurons via Hessian-guided compensation. Experiments on four QA tasks and five LLM families demonstrate that HCNR effectively recovers 33.25% of the compromised honesty while achieving at least 2.23x speedup with over 10x less data compared to baseline methods, offering a practical solution for trustworthy LLM deployment.

## Summary

Shi et al. identify that SFT-induced dishonesty in LLMs is a spurious phenomenon: domain fine-tuning does not destroy knowledge-boundary representations but suppresses the neurons that express that awareness. Linear probes trained on base-model hidden states transfer to fine-tuned representations with preserved high AUROC, confirming the internal geometry of answerable-vs-unanswerable distinctions is stable across LoRA and full fine-tuning. Building on this, the paper proposes Honesty-Critical Neurons Restoration (HCNR), a two-stage parameter-efficient framework. Stage 1 uses diagonal Fisher Information Matrix scores to rank neurons by honesty importance relative to task importance, then identifies layers with the highest SFT-induced perturbation via relative weight displacement; the intersection yields a targeted neuron set covering roughly 20% of parameters. Stage 2 applies a Hessian-guided compensation vector (derived from OBS) to re-align the restored honesty-critical neurons with the downstream task neurons, preventing task-accuracy collapse. Experiments cover five LLM families (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Qwen2-7B, Mistral-7B) fine-tuned on four QA datasets (HotpotQA, Natural Questions, MedMCQA, BioASQ); Table 1 reports detailed per-benchmark results for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on HotpotQA and MedMCQA, with other model families and datasets in Appendix D. HCNR uses only 256 total samples (128 honesty + 128 task) and completes in under 4 minutes on an A800 GPU, achieving at least a 2.23x speedup over RAIT, DPO, and ORPO baselines that consume 3,000-9,000 samples and modify 100% of parameters. On both domain splits HCNR matches or exceeds all baselines on average F1 and RF Delta while maintaining domain accuracy near the fine-tuned ceiling. An ablation confirms that both the Fisher-based neuron selection (Stage 1) and the Hessian compensation (Stage 2) are individually necessary: removing either component drops average F1 and RF Delta substantially.
