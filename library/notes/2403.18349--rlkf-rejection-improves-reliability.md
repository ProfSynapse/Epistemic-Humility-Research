## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) often generate erroneous outputs, known as hallucinations, due to their limitations in discerning questions beyond their knowledge scope. While addressing hallucination has been a focal point in research, previous efforts primarily concentrate on enhancing correctness without giving due consideration to the significance of rejection mechanisms. In this paper, we conduct a comprehensive examination of the role of rejection, introducing the notion of model reliability along with corresponding metrics. These metrics measure the model's ability to provide accurate responses while adeptly rejecting questions exceeding its knowledge boundaries, thereby minimizing hallucinations. To improve the inherent reliability of LLMs, we present a novel alignment framework called Reinforcement Learning from Knowledge Feedback (RLKF). RLKF leverages knowledge feedback to dynamically determine the model's knowledge boundary and trains a reliable reward model to encourage the refusal of out-of-knowledge questions. Experimental results on mathematical questions affirm the substantial efficacy of RLKF in significantly enhancing LLM reliability.

## Summary

Xu et al. introduce Reinforcement Learning from Knowledge Feedback (RLKF), an alignment framework that teaches LLMs to explicitly refuse questions outside their knowledge boundary instead of hallucinating. The method operates in three steps: (1) probe each question's answerability by sampling the policy multiple times and comparing consistencies or using external verifiers, yielding model-specific Reliable Preference Data (RPD) that ranks correct responses above refusals above wrong answers; (2) train a Reliable Reward Model (RelyRM) starting from a helpfulness-trained RM and continually training it on RPD; (3) fine-tune the LLM policy against RelyRM using PPO. The paper also proposes three evaluation metrics: precision (correct / attempts), accuracy (correct / total), and accountability (non-hallucinated / total), combined into a composite reliability score (rely = ans_rate * accountability + (1 - ans_rate) * accuracy) that penalizes both over-answering and over-refusal. Experiments on LLaMA-2-chat 7B show RLKF achieves the highest reliability on both an in-domain arithmetic dataset and the out-of-domain GSM8K benchmark. A critical finding is that SFT on in-domain rejection examples improves in-domain reliability but collapses out-of-domain reliability through near-total refusal on GSM8K. RLKF avoids this collapse by grounding abstention in model-specific competence signals rather than domain surface features. The baseline (rely=46.6) in Table 2 refers to LLaMA-2-chat with its default prudent system prompt, not a bare unaligned model.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper bears on the Phase 1 experiment in three concrete ways. First, it documents the SFT over-refusal failure mode with precise numbers (OOD accuracy drops to 4.7 after SFT abstention training, reliability from 35.2 to 20.8), providing a quantified reference case for what the Phase 1 SFT arm should avoid and what DPO or KTO should improve on. Second, the RPD synthesis recipe (sample multiple times, compare consistency for OOD questions, use external verifiers for in-domain questions, then rank correct over refused over wrong) is a concrete data construction method that could inform how abstention training pairs are built for the Phase 1 and Phase 2 runs. Third, the rely metric (composite of precision, accuracy, and accountability) is a candidate primary metric for Phase 1 results tables: it captures both over-refusal and hallucination in a single number, balancing helpfulness and honesty in a way that accuracy alone does not. A limitation for the present experiment is that the paper tests only RLKF (a PPO-based approach) and SFT, with no DPO or KTO comparisons; the OOD consistency-based boundary detection used for RPD synthesis is analytic rather than trained, which is conceptually related to consistency-based-confidence but is used at training time rather than inference time.

## Claims

- RLKF achieves reliability scores of 56.5 (in-domain arithmetic) and 41.5 (out-of-domain GSM8K), the highest among all methods including RLHF (49.1 / 36.4), rule-based PPO (51.6 / 33.6), and prompt-based baselines. (Table 1, §5.2) [[reinforcement-learning-from-knowledge-feedback]]
- SFT improves in-domain reliability to 61.2 but reduces out-of-domain reliability to 20.8, indicating poor generalization through over-conservative refusal on unseen distributions. (Table 2, §5.2) [[accountability]]
- RLHF decreases model accountability relative to no-training baseline because its helpfulness-focused reward model treats refusals as unhelpful, pushing the model to attempt answers on out-of-knowledge questions. (§5.2, Table 1) [[reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback]]
- RLKF raises precision (correct answers / answers attempted) from 43.7 to 72.8 on arithmetic and from 22.6 to 29.6 on GSM8K, reflecting improved self-knowledge about which questions the model can reliably answer. (Table 1, §5.2) [[llm-reliability-score]]
