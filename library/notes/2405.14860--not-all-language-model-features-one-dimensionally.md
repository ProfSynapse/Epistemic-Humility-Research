## Abstract

Recent work has proposed that language models perform computation by manipulating one-dimensional representations of concepts ("features") in activation space. In contrast, we explore whether some language model representations may be inherently multi-dimensional. We begin by developing a rigorous definition of irreducible multi-dimensional features based on whether they can be decomposed into either independent or non-co-occurring lower-dimensional features. Motivated by these definitions, we design a scalable method that uses sparse autoencoders to automatically find multi-dimensional features in GPT-2 and Mistral 7B. These auto-discovered features include strikingly interpretable examples, e.g. circular features representing days of the week and months of the year. We identify tasks where these exact circles are used to solve computational problems involving modular arithmetic in days of the week and months of the year. Next, we provide evidence that these circular features are indeed the fundamental unit of computation in these tasks with intervention experiments on Mistral 7B and Llama 3 8B, and we examine the continuity of the days of the week feature in Mistral 7B. Overall, our work argues that understanding multi-dimensional features is necessary to mechanistically decompose some model behaviors.

## Summary

Engels et al. refine the linear representation hypothesis by showing some features are irreducibly multi-dimensional: represented in a low-dimensional but not one-dimensional linear subspace. An SAE-clustering method auto-discovers such features, most vividly circular representations of days-of-week, months, and years. Interventions on only the 2D circular subspace nearly match patching the whole layer, showing these rings are causally used for modular arithmetic in GPT-2, Mistral 7B, and Llama 3 8B. Irreducibility is tested via a separability index (min mutual information over rotations) and an epsilon-mixture index.

## Claims

- Evidence label: definitional. A feature is irreducible when no rotation+translation makes its coordinates separable (independent marginals) or a mixture (non-co-occurring lower-dim parts); such multi-dimensional features cannot be decomposed into lower-dim features (Defs 2-3; Sec 3.1). [[multi-dimensional-feature]]
- Evidence label: empirical. An SAE-clustering method auto-discovers circular features for days, months, and years (in PCA dims 2-3, with PC1 a radius/intensity axis) in GPT-2 and Mistral 7B (Sec 4; Fig 1). [[multi-dimensional-feature]]
- Evidence label: intervention. Intervening on only the 2D circular subspace (average-ablating the rest) nearly matches patching the entire layer and beats patching top PCA dims, establishing causal sufficiency for modular arithmetic (Sec 5.1-5.2; Figs 6-8). [[circular-features-causally-compute-modular-arithmetic]]
