## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are known to hallucinate, whereby they generate plausible but inaccurate text. This phenomenon poses significant risks in critical applications, such as medicine or law, necessitating robust hallucination mitigation strategies. While recent works have proposed fine-tuning methods to teach LLMs to abstain from answering questions beyond their knowledge or capabilities, these methods rely on the existence of ground-truth labels or are limited to short-form responses. To address these limitations, we propose fine-tuning using semantic entropy, an uncertainty measure derived from introspection into the model which does not require external labels. We demonstrate that our approach matches or outperforms models fine-tuned using prior work and achieves strong performance for both short and long-form generations on a range of datasets.

## Summary

Tjandra et al. (Oxford OATML, 2024) show that semantic entropy (SE) is a more effective abstention-training signal than either ground-truth correctness labels or classical token-level entropy. The key move is to reuse SE, originally proposed for hallucination detection, as a dataset-construction heuristic: for each training question the model samples M=10 responses at T=1.0, computes SE via bidirectional entailment clustering, partitions questions into high-entropy (abstain) and low-entropy (answer) subsets at a tunable threshold tau, relabels the high-entropy subset with "I don't know the answer", and fine-tunes with cross-entropy loss using LoRA (r=8 on query and value projection matrices). Two SE variants are evaluated: SE (DeBERTa) using DeBERTa-NLI as the entailment model (following Kuhn et al. 2023) and SE (Llama) using Llama-3-70B-Instruct (following Farquhar et al. 2024). The base model throughout is LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct. Baselines are R-Tuning (label-dependent, uses correctness to partition) and R-Tuning-U (label-free, uses classical token-level entropy). The paper introduces Accuracy-Engagement Distance (AED), a normalized Euclidean distance from an ideal (all-correct, all-engaged) model in the (incorrect, correct) response count space, as its primary metric. AED jointly penalizes hallucinations and over-abstention, unlike accuracy-only metrics. Experiments span four datasets (TriviaQA, BioASQ, NQ, SQuAD), two answering settings (Long-QA free-form sentence, Short-QA short phrase), and both in-distribution and out-of-distribution evaluation. SE (Llama) achieves the best aggregate AED across all four conditions in Table 1, with SE (DeBERTa) close behind. R-Tuning and R-Tuning-U are on average worse than the original unfine-tuned model in several settings. The Long-QA adaptation plot shows SE (Llama) forms a Pareto frontier over all other methods across all nine thresholds on four OOD datasets. The Short-QA frontier is less clear, attributed to lesser lexical variance in short-form generation making SE's advantage over token-level entropy smaller. Threshold selection uses 9 equally-spaced values from 0.25 to 2.25, with best threshold chosen on the in-distribution validation split of 500 examples (2000 train, 500 val from each dataset's validation split, closed-book).

## Relevance to experiment

Three direct connections to the training and mechanism programs. First, the paper provides the closest available precedent for the SE-signal SFT arm: it demonstrates that an uncertainty threshold partitions training data into abstain/answer subsets without ground-truth labels, and that the choice of entailment model (DeBERTa vs. Llama-70B) matters substantially for Long-QA generalization. The Mult condition (training on TriviaQA plus BioASQ plus NQ, evaluating OOD on SQuAD) is a direct analogue to the cross-domain generalization tests planned in the locked training-regimen study, and the SE (Llama) Pareto frontier result there suggests that stronger entailment models are particularly important when the training and test distributions diverge. Second, the AED metric is a direct addition to the evaluation stack: it captures over-abstention as well as hallucination in a single number, which the current effects.csv schema's binary abstention-rate and accuracy-rate columns do not. Reporting AED alongside those columns for the locked training-regimen arms would allow apples-to-apples comparison with this paper's results. Third, the failure of R-Tuning-U in Long-QA (average AED 0.521 for Long-QA in-distribution, worse than the unfine-tuned base model at 0.380) confirms the graph's existing mechanism that lexical-entropy-based abstention SFT overfits in-domain. The SE advantage in Long-QA (30.1% AED reduction) being larger than in Short-QA (8.7%) aligns with the mechanism that lexical entropy overestimates uncertainty under paraphrase: the gap closes when paraphrase variation is limited, as in short-form answers. One gap: the base model here is LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, not Qwen3, so hyperparameter transfer is approximate; the LoRA rank and epoch count are calibrated for 8B-scale, and a replication on Qwen3 would need its own sweep.

## Claims

- SE (Llama) achieves lower aggregate AED than R-Tuning and R-Tuning-U across all four evaluation conditions (Long-QA and Short-QA, in-distribution and OOD), with reductions of up to 30.1% for Long-QA in-distribution and up to 25.0% for Long-QA OOD. (Table 1, Section 7) [[semantic-entropy-sft-signal-outperforms-lexical-entropy-on-abstention]]
- Accuracy-Engagement Distance (AED) penalizes both incorrect answers and excessive abstention by computing the normalized Euclidean distance from the ideal (zero incorrect, all-dataset correct) point, giving a single scalar that captures the hallucination-over-abstention tradeoff. (Section 4) [[accuracy-engagement-distance]]
- SE (Llama) forms a Pareto frontier over all other methods in the Long-QA All-Threshold Adaptation Plot across TriviaQA, BioASQ, NQ, and SQuAD OOD: for every uncertainty threshold tau, models fine-tuned with SE (Llama) achieve fewer incorrect responses and more correct responses than models fine-tuned with SE (DeBERTa), R-Tuning, or R-Tuning-U. (Figure 3, Section 7) [[semantic-entropy-sft-signal-outperforms-lexical-entropy-on-abstention]]
- R-Tuning-U (classical token-level entropy) yields average in-distribution Long-QA AED of 0.521, worse than the unfine-tuned base model at 0.380, confirming that lexical entropy is a noisier training signal than semantic entropy and can actively degrade abstention quality. (Table 1, Section 7) [[lexical-entropy-overestimates-uncertainty-under-paraphrase]]
- Using 2000 training examples per dataset with LoRA (r=8), the method closes on but does not always beat the original model in per-dataset single-split experiments, suggesting that low LoRA rank and small dataset size limit fine-tuning capacity and that full fine-tuning or more data would improve results. (Section 7 Limitations) [[low-rank-adaptation]]
