## Abstract

Mitigating hallucinations in Large Language Models (LLMs) is critical for their reliable deployment. Existing methods typically fine-tune LLMs to abstain from answering questions beyond their knowledge scope. However, these methods often rely on coarse-grained signals to guide LLMs to abstain, such as overall confidence or uncertainty scores on multiple sampled answers, which may result in an imprecise awareness of the model's own knowledge boundaries. To this end, we propose a novel reinforcement learning framework built on $\textbf{\underline{Fi}ne-grained \underline{S}emantic \underline{Co}nfidence \underline{Re}ward (\Ours)}$, which guides LLMs to abstain via sample-specific confidence. Specifically, our method operates by sampling multiple candidate answers and conducting semantic clustering, then training the LLM to retain answers within high-confidence clusters and discard those within low-confidence ones, thereby promoting accurate post-hoc abstention. Additionally, we propose a new metric for evaluating the reliability of abstention fine-tuning tasks more comprehensively. Our method significantly enhances reliability in both in-domain and out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Summary

An and Xu (Southern University of Science and Technology) address a structural weakness in supervised abstention fine-tuning: it relies on a single, aggregated uncertainty signal per question (e.g., a binary correctness label or a global semantic entropy score), which provides a coarse and distribution-sensitive knowledge-boundary signal. The authors propose FiSCoRe (Fine-grained Semantic Confidence Reward), a GRPO-based reinforcement learning framework that replaces the coarse aggregate with a per-sample confidence reward. The training loop draws G=10 rollouts per question, clusters them into semantic equivalence classes using bidirectional NLI entailment (DeBERTa-v2-xlarge-mnli), and assigns a reward of 1 to each rollout if the model's verbalized confidence ("sure" or "unsure") aligns with the cluster-size-derived confidence level: large cluster (size >= tau = ceil(G/2) = 5) implies high confidence and should receive "sure"; small cluster implies low confidence and should receive "unsure." This per-sample signal is paired with an auxiliary accuracy reward (checking semantic equivalence of the answer to ground truth) and a format reward, combined in a weighted sum. The paper also proposes F1_rel as the harmonic mean of F1_ans (helpfulness: answer correctly on Known questions) and F1_abs (truthfulness: abstain correctly on Unknown questions), operating on a 2x3 abstention confusion matrix partitioned by pre-training correctness (Known/Unknown) and refined-model outcome (correctly answered, incorrectly answered, abstained). Experiments train on ParaRel (ID) and evaluate on TriviaQA, Natural Questions, and SciQ (all OOD) using Llama3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. The core empirical finding is that SFT-based baselines (R-Tuning, SE-Tuning) achieve competitive ID F1_rel but collapse on OOD benchmarks, while FiSCoRe maintains OOD reliability. An ablation isolates the reward granularity as the causal factor: GRPO-SE, which uses GRPO with a coarse entropy reward over the same training data, consistently underperforms FiSCoRe across all OOD sets. Without the accuracy auxiliary reward (w_a=0), the model learns to abstain universally, confirming that the accuracy component is needed to prevent reward hacking.

## Relevance to experiment

FiSCoRe bears directly on Phase 1 in two ways. First, the core result (GRPO with per-sample confidence reward generalizes OOD while SFT abstention collapses) adds a fourth training-arm data point alongside the SFT, DPO, and KTO arms already in the study design. The Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model is the same family as the Qwen3 base targeted in Phase 1, so the F1_rel numbers are directly relevant as comparison anchors. Second, the 9.1-point GRPO-SE vs FiSCoRe gap on TriviaQA (Qwen) is an isolated reward-granularity comparison holding the backbone and training data constant, which provides the clearest available evidence that replacing a global entropy reward with per-sample cluster-size rewards is the specific intervention driving OOD robustness. This is a design lever that could inform a Phase 1 extension cell. For Phase 3, FiSCoRe training should sharpen the per-sample confidence signal in the model's hidden states: if the model is rewarded for per-sample cluster alignment, the hidden-state abstention signal may become more precise and OOD-robust, which is a testable conjecture for the mech-interp sweep (this is an inference from the training objective, not a claim the paper demonstrates). F1_rel also candidates as the primary composite reliability metric for Phase 1, providing a single score that balances helpfulness and truthfulness without requiring a separate calibration probe. The paper establishes that raw accuracy is an inadequate proxy for abstention quality, directly motivating the two-sided reliability framing in the experiment protocol.

## Claims

- FiSCoRe (Llama3-8B-Instruct) achieves F1_rel 74.3 on ParaRel (ID) and drops only to 70.8 on TriviaQA (OOD), while R-Tuning drops from 79.0 to 49.5 on the same pair; FiSCoRe outperforms all baselines on all three OOD datasets. (Table 3, §4.2) [[fiscore]]
- The GRPO-SE to FiSCoRe gap on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct TriviaQA F1_rel (72.0 vs 81.1, a 9.1-point improvement) isolates the per-sample confidence reward as the causal factor, since both methods share the same GRPO backbone and training data. (Table 3, §4.2) [[fine-grained-semantic-confidence-reward]]
- SE-Tuning (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) achieves F1_rel 76.5 on ParaRel (ID) but collapses to 45.4 on TriviaQA (OOD), illustrating that SFT abstention training overfits to in-domain surface features and fails to generalize. (Table 3, §4.2) [[sft-abstention-overfits-indomain]]
- Setting accuracy reward weight w_a=0 causes the model to achieve near-zero F1_ans by abstaining from almost all questions, confirming that without the accuracy auxiliary reward the agent exploits the confidence reward through universal abstention. (Figure 3b, §4.3) [[fine-grained-semantic-confidence-reward]]
- F1_rel, defined as the harmonic mean of F1_ans (helpfulness on Known questions) and F1_abs (truthfulness on Unknown questions), provides a composite reliability score that monotonically penalizes all four error types (incorrect known, over-abstained known, incorrect unknown, answered unknown) and is the primary evaluation metric across all experiments. (§2.2, Table 3) [[f1-rel-reliability-metric]]
