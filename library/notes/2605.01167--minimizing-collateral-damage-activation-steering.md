## Abstract

Activation steering is a method for controlling Large Language Model (LLM) behavior by intervening in its internal representations to increase the alignment with a specific target feature direction. However, standard interventions, such as vector addition, often cause ``collateral damage", defined as unintended changes in the alignment of activations along other non-target feature directions. This damage occurs because standard methods implicitly assume the isotropy of non-target features. In this work, we provide a mathematical formalization of collateral damage and introduce a principled framework that models steering as a constrained optimization problem. Our method finds a new activation that minimizes the expected squared collateral change weighted by the empirical second-moment matrix of activations. This weighting encodes the nonuniform cost of the perturbation in different feature directions, in contrast to isotropic approaches that penalize changes uniformly in all feature directions. By accounting for the empirical second-moment of activations, our approach achieves more precise control while reducing the degradation of model performance on unrelated tasks.

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment. On Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, average collateral damage across six tinyBenchmarks tasks is strongly negatively correlated with model accuracy (Pearson r < -0.9), supporting collateral damage as a reliable proxy for downstream performance degradation from steering (Fig. 1).
- Evidence label: experiment. Across four instruction-tuned models (Gemma-2-9B-IT, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct), COAST achieves up to a 30% improvement in Attack Success Rate (HarmBench) over Angular Steering and up to a 20% accuracy improvement over ActAdd at matched ASR, while ActAdd shows a steep accuracy-vs-ASR trade-off and Angular Steering fails to reach high ASR (Fig. 3, Sec. 5 'Trade-off between jailbreak success and general capability').
- Evidence label: experiment. Averaged over steering intensities theta in [0deg, 180deg], COAST attains higher average ASR than SLERP on 3 of 4 evaluated models, with any accuracy gap in SLERP's favor being small; on Gemma-2-9B-it and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct COAST improves both ASR and accuracy simultaneously over SLERP (Table 1).
- Evidence label: theory. COAST is proven (Lemma 2, Theorem 1) to converge, via continuous-time Riemannian gradient flow on the alignment-budget manifold from the Euclidean-projection initialization used in Algorithm 1, to the global minimizer of the collateral-damage objective; and it is shown (Sec. 3.4, Def. 1, three isotropic-collateral-damage scenarios in Sec. 3.4/Appendix B) to reduce exactly to the closed-form SLERP solution whenever collateral damage is isotropic, i.e. COAST strictly generalizes SLERP.
- Evidence label: experiment. COAST's inference-time overhead is marginal: 0.9%-4.0% slower than the unsteered baseline and under 1.5% slower than SLERP, measured in tokens/second throughput (Sec. 5 'Computational Efficiency', Appendix D, Table 2).
