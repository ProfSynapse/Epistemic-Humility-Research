## Abstract

In this paper, we identify and characterize the emerging area of representation engineering (RepE), an approach to enhancing the transparency of AI systems that draws on insights from cognitive neuroscience. RepE places population-level representations, rather than neurons or circuits, at the center of analysis, equipping us with novel methods for monitoring and manipulating high-level cognitive phenomena in deep neural networks (DNNs). We provide baselines and an initial analysis of RepE techniques, showing that they offer simple yet effective solutions for improving our understanding and control of large language models. We showcase how these methods can provide traction on a wide range of safety-relevant problems, including honesty, harmlessness, power-seeking, and more, demonstrating the promise of top-down transparency research. We hope that this work catalyzes further exploration of RepE and fosters advancements in the transparency and safety of AI systems.

## Summary

Zou et al. introduce representation engineering (RepE), a top-down transparency framework that places population-level representations rather than neurons or circuits at the center of analysis. The paper proposes two coupled sub-frameworks. Representation Reading uses Linear Artificial Tomography (LAT), a three-step procedure: design a contrastive stimulus set, collect hidden-state activations at relevant token positions, and extract the first principal component of the difference-vector distribution as a reading vector. Representation Control then applies that reading vector to steer model behavior via three baseline transformations: a static reading-vector shift, a stimulus-dependent Contrast Vector computed on-the-fly during inference (at roughly 3x the cost of a single forward pass), and Low-Rank Representation Adaptation (LoRRA), which fine-tunes LoRA weights to align internal representations with contrast-vector targets and can be merged back into model weights for zero-overhead inference. The paper evaluates RepE across honesty/TruthfulQA, ethics and power-seeking (MACHIAVELLI), emotion, harmlessness under adversarial attack, bias, knowledge editing, and memorization suppression, all on LLaMA-2-Chat models. Key result: LAT applied to honesty achieves TruthfulQA MC1 averages of 60.6-60.7% across 7B/13B/70B LLaMA-2-Chat versus 32.3% zero-shot standard, a 28-point gain. The 18.1-point improvement cited in the Introduction refers specifically to the 13B Contrast Vector result (54.0 vs. 35.9% zero-shot), not to an average across model sizes.

## Relevance to experiment

RepE bears on the Epistemic Humility experiment in three overlapping ways. First, the generation-discrimination gap is this paper's empirical starting point: LAT reads truthfulness from LLaMA-2-Chat activations at TruthfulQA MC1 accuracies of 53-70% while zero-shot generation sits at 30-36%, a gap that directly motivates the Phase 3 mech-interp hypothesis that abstention training may not fully express a model's internally represented uncertainty. The reading-vector and contrast-vector results establish that this gap is not just measurable but causally bridgeable through activation-space intervention, setting a bar for what Phase 1 SFT/DPO/KTO arms should aspire to close through weight updates. Second, LoRRA is a lightweight training-time alternative to inference-time steering: it fine-tunes LoRA adapters to align representations with contrast-vector targets, producing zero-overhead inference. This is a direct structural comparison case for the Phase 1 training arms, which also use LoRA-scale parameter counts. Whether LoRRA's representation-alignment objective outperforms or complements standard cross-entropy SFT on abstention and calibration metrics is an open empirical question. Third, the harmfulness robustness findings (over 90% classification accuracy under GCG attack) show that a RepE reading vector can remain stable under adversarial pressure that breaks output-level refusals, which is relevant if post-Phase-1 models are probed for knowledge-boundary stability. The primary caveat is scope: all RepE results are on LLaMA-2-Chat 7B/13B/70B; whether the reading-vector geometry generalizes to Qwen3 after SFT or DPO abstention training is an open question and the right hypothesis for a targeted experiment rather than an assumption.
