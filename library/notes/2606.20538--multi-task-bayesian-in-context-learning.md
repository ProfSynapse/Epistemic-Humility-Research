## Abstract

Bayesian predictive inference gives a principled account of uncertainty
quantification, data efficiency, and robust generalization, but exact inference
is usually intractable and scalable approximations stay expensive or rely on
restrictive modeling assumptions. Prior-Data Fitted and in-context models offer
an amortized alternative by learning to map datasets directly to predictive
distributions, yet existing approaches are tied to the support of the training
prior and have no explicit way to adapt to new priors at test time, which limits
robustness under distribution shift. Zhu, Oermann, and Cho introduce a
multi-task in-context learning framework for amortized hierarchical Bayesian
predictive inference that represents prior information as a prefix of in-context
datasets. A transformer trained on sequences of prior and target tasks learns to
adapt its predictions across families of priors. On evaluations of increasing
difficulty, including out-of-meta-distribution priors and priors with
high-dimensional latent structure, the method matches oracle Bayesian predictors
while being orders of magnitude faster, and it transfers to a real-world
spatiotemporal temperature prediction benchmark.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper sits adjacent to the epistemic-humility focus rather than inside it.
Its interest is a model that reasons explicitly about what it does not yet know:
the predictive distribution is conditioned on a prior supplied at test time, and
the transformer adjusts its own uncertainty as that prior changes, including
priors outside the meta-training distribution. That is the calibration question
posed in a Bayesian regression setting instead of in LLM question answering. It
offers a clean reference point for grounded confidence (matching an oracle
Bayesian posterior) and for the idea that uncertainty should track the regime the
model is actually in, which is the same property the locked training-regimen study wants from a
stated confidence that follows the model-specific known and unknown boundary.
