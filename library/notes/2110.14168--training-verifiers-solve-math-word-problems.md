## Abstract

State-of-the-art language models can match human performance on many tasks, but they still struggle to robustly perform multi-step mathematical reasoning. To diagnose the failures of current models and support research, we introduce GSM8K, a dataset of 8.5K high quality linguistically diverse grade school math word problems. We find that even the largest transformer models fail to achieve high test performance, despite the conceptual simplicity of this problem distribution. To increase performance, we propose training verifiers to judge the correctness of model completions. At test time, we generate many candidate solutions and select the one ranked highest by the verifier. We demonstrate that verification significantly improves performance on GSM8K, and we provide strong empirical evidence that verification scales more effectively with increased data than a finetuning baseline.

## Claims

- Evidence label: benchmark. On the full GSM8K dataset, 6B verification (rank 100
  sampled completions) slightly outperforms a finetuned 175B model - roughly a 30x
  model-size-equivalent boost - and verifiers scale more favorably with data than
  finetuning. (library/pdfs/2110.14168.pdf, Abstract; Sec 6; Fig 5) [[verification-advantage-over-generation]]
- Evidence label: ablation. Token-level value-function verifiers ultimately
  outperform solution-level verifiers and are markedly less prone to overfitting;
  adding a joint language-modeling objective is a strict improvement.
  (library/pdfs/2110.14168.pdf, Sec 4.3; Fig 6a-b) [[value-function-verifier-resists-overfitting]]
- Evidence label: ablation. Generator size matters more than verifier size: a
  large-generator / small-verifier pairing substantially beats the reverse, and
  verification stays effective with a much smaller verifier.
  (library/pdfs/2110.14168.pdf, Sec 4.3; Fig 6c) [[generator-size-dominates-verifier-size]]
- Evidence label: analysis. Test-time search helps up to ~400 completions per
  problem then degrades as adversarial solutions fool the verifier; majority voting
  among top-ranked samples helps further and dropout strongly regularizes.
  (library/pdfs/2110.14168.pdf, Sec 5.1-5.2; Fig 7-8) [[solution-verifier]]
