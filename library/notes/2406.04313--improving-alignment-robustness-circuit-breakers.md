## Abstract

AI systems can take harmful actions and are highly vulnerable to adversarial attacks. We present an approach, inspired by recent advances in representation engineering, that interrupts the models as they respond with harmful outputs with "circuit breakers." Existing techniques aimed at improving alignment, such as refusal training, are often bypassed. Techniques such as adversarial training try to plug these holes by countering specific attacks. As an alternative to refusal training and adversarial training, circuit-breaking directly controls the representations that are responsible for harmful outputs in the first place. Our technique can be applied to both text-only and multimodal language models to prevent the generation of harmful outputs without sacrificing utility -- even in the presence of powerful unseen attacks. Notably, while adversarial robustness in standalone image recognition remains an open challenge, circuit breakers allow the larger multimodal system to reliably withstand image "hijacks" that aim to produce harmful content. Finally, we extend our approach to AI agents, demonstrating considerable reductions in the rate of harmful actions when they are under attack. Our approach represents a significant step forward in the development of reliable safeguards to harmful behavior and adversarial attacks.

## Summary

The paper introduces Representation Rerouting (RR), a circuit-breaking method implemented with low-rank adapters. RR trains harmful-response representations to become orthogonal to their original trajectories while a retain loss keeps benign representations close to the frozen model. Across text-only models, a multimodal model, and a function-calling agent, RR reduces harmful compliance under attacks while largely preserving benchmark utility. The method changes model weights through adapters and does not require an explicit attack detector at inference time.

## Extracted numbers

- On the text HarmBench panel, RR reduced average harmful compliance from 76.7% to 9.8% for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v2 and from 38.1% to 3.8% for Llama-3-8B-Instruct, while MT-Bench moved from 7.60 to 7.53 and from 8.05 to 8.00, respectively (Table 1).
- The paper summarizes these text results as average compliance reductions of 87% for Mistral and 90% for Llama-3, with less than a 1% decrease on its capability evaluations (Section 4.1).
- On LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B under a white-box PGD image attack, RR reduced harmful compliance by 84% relative to the original and 85% relative to a safety prompt. MMMU and LLaVA-Wild performance stayed within 0.5% of the original (Section 4.2, Figure 3).
- On 100 harmful function-calling requests, RR reduced harmful-action compliance by 84% without an attack and by 83% under forced function calling, while retaining Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard performance (Section 4.3, Figure 4).
- Harmfulness probes reduced attack success, but RR had lower average compliance on the tested attacks. The probes were evaluated against attackers that did not know about or optimize against them (Section 4.4, Table 2).

## Relevance to experiment

RR shows that a representation-space objective can be internalized through low-rank weight updates and can generalize across unseen elicitation methods. Its gate is implicit in harmful-generation trajectories rather than a separately validated answerability readout. The circuit-breaker and retain-set split provides a useful pattern for pairing targeted behavior changes with preservation constraints.

## Claims

- Evidence label: intervention. Representation rerouting reduces harmful compliance across black-box, system-level, embedding-space, and representation-space attacks while preserving most tested utility (Section 4.1, Table 1).
- Evidence label: multimodal intervention. RR transfers to a vision-language model and resists white-box image hijacks without the capability loss caused by the safety-prompt baseline (Section 4.2, Figure 3).
- Evidence label: agent intervention. RR reduces harmful function calls under both ordinary and forced-call settings (Section 4.3, Figure 4).
- Evidence label: ablation. The cosine rerouting loss is more stable than the tested random-vector objectives, and removing refusal examples from the retain set harms capabilities (Section 4.4, Table 8).
