## Abstract

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has emerged as a compelling approach for training Large Language Models (LLMs) to adhere to human preferences. However, the performance of DPO is sensitive to the fine-tuning of its trade-off parameter $β$, as well as to the quality of the preference data. We analyze the impact of $β$ and data quality on DPO, uncovering that optimal $β$ values vary with the informativeness of pairwise data. Addressing the limitations of static $β$ values, we introduce a novel framework that dynamically calibrates $β$ at the batch level, informed by data quality considerations. Additionally, our method incorporates $β$-guided data filtering to safeguard against the influence of outliers. Through empirical evaluation, we demonstrate that our dynamic $β$ adjustment technique significantly improves DPO's performance across a range of models and datasets, offering a more robust and adaptable training paradigm for aligning LLMs with human feedback. The code is available at \url{https://github.com/junkangwu/beta-DPO}.

## Summary

Wu et al. analyze why DPO's beta parameter is sensitive to both hyperparameter
choice and preference-pair data quality. They propose beta-DPO, which estimates
a batch-level dynamic beta from pair quality and filters outliers before using
the batch for DPO updates.

The practical point for this project is that static beta can be mismatched when
preference data mixes closely matched informative pairs with easy or noisy
pairs. That is directly relevant to our response-confidence DPO datasets, where
the chosen/rejected pairs include known, unknown, and ambiguous behavior cases.

## Extracted numbers

- Figure 1: the paper defines low-gap pairs as closely matched and usually more
  informative, while high-gap pairs are easier to distinguish and lower quality.
- Figure 1b / Section 1: on Pythia-1.4B with the HH dataset, increasing beta
  from 0.1 to 0.5 reduced win rate on low-gap data from about 42% to 33%, while
  increasing beta helped high-gap data.
- Figure 4 / Section 5.1: beta-DPO outperformed standard DPO across sampling
  temperatures in Anthropic-HH one-step dialogue and TL;DR summarization.
- Section 5.1: at sampling temperature 1.0, standard DPO dropped to about 25%
  win rate while beta-DPO maintained about 54%.

## Relevance to experiment

Our clean response-confidence DPO rows used static beta 0.1. The local
training-exhaust audit shows DPO can optimize its trainer objective while
downstream epistemic-humility behavior moves only modestly. This paper makes a
static-beta sensitivity panel more theoretically grounded than simply adding
more epochs, especially if we can stratify pair quality or at least audit pair
gaps before choosing beta values.

## Claims

- Mechanism: DPO beta should depend on pair quality. Figure 1 and Section 3
  show different beta directions for low-gap versus high-gap preference pairs.
- Method: beta-DPO dynamically calibrates beta at batch level and adds
  beta-guided data filtering to reduce outlier influence.
- Design implication: a DPO sensitivity panel should inspect pair-gap
  distribution and test beta as an objective-shape variable, not merely as a
  generic learning-rate-like knob.
