## Summary

Schulman et al. (2017) introduce Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a family of on-policy policy gradient methods that alternate between sampling environment interactions and maximizing a surrogate objective using minibatch stochastic gradient ascent over multiple epochs. The central contribution is the clipped surrogate objective L^CLIP, which takes the minimum of the unclipped and clipped importance-sampling ratio times the advantage: the clipped version removes the gradient incentive to move the probability ratio r_t outside [1-epsilon, 1+epsilon], so the composite objective forms a pessimistic lower bound on the unclipped objective without requiring constraint optimization. This achieves the stability of TRPO at a fraction of the computational cost, using only first-order methods. The paper also introduces an adaptive KL penalty variant that adjusts the KL coefficient beta upward or downward by a factor of two whenever the observed KL divergence exceeds or falls short of a target dtarg by 1.5x, offering an alternative to clipping. Algorithm 1 specifies the actor-critic implementation: N parallel actors each collect T timesteps, and the combined NT timestep buffer is then consumed for K epochs of minibatch SGD with minibatch size M at most NT. The ablation across four surrogate types (no clipping or penalty, clipping, adaptive KL penalty, fixed KL penalty) on 7 MuJoCo tasks confirms PPO-Clip at epsilon=0.2 as the best variant. On continuous control, PPO outperforms TRPO, CEM, vanilla policy gradient with adaptive stepsize, A2C, and A2C with Trust Region on almost all environments. On 49 Atari games PPO wins more games on the training-period metric while ACER wins more on the final-performance metric.

## Relevance to experiment

PPO is the RL optimizer at the center of RLHF-based alignment pipelines; InstructGPT (Ouyang et al. 2022) adopted it directly as the policy optimizer. That lineage makes this paper the mechanistic starting point for understanding the SFT-vs-DPO-vs-KTO comparison in the locked training-regimen study: DPO and KTO were both motivated in part by the instability and complexity of running PPO at language-model scale (the mechanism dpo-stability-over-ppo captures this explicitly). The clipped surrogate objective establishes the trust-region intuition that the KL-divergence penalty in RLHF later inherits in a different form. For mechinterp work, PPO's actor-critic structure (separate value network, advantage estimation via GAE) contrasts with GRPO's critic-free group-relative reward normalization, which is relevant when interpreting what internal representations are being shaped by each training regime. The PPO-ptx variant (mixing pretraining gradients into updates) directly grounds the ppo-ptx-mitigates-alignment-tax mechanism, which is a reference point for measuring alignment tax across training arms.

## Claims

- PPO with the clipped surrogate objective matches or exceeds TRPO on continuous control benchmarks (MuJoCo) while being simpler to implement and requiring no second-order optimization. (Section 6, Table 1) [[proximal-policy-optimization]]
- The clipped objective outperforms the KL-penalty variant of PPO across most tested environments, making PPO-Clip the recommended default. (Section 5, ablation comparison) [[clipped-surrogate-objective]]
- PPO achieves favorable sample efficiency and wall-clock performance on Atari games relative to A3C and other baseline policy gradient methods. (Section 6, Table 2) [[proximal-policy-optimization]]
