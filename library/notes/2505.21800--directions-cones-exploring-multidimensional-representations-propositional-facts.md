## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit strong conversational abilities but often generate falsehoods. Prior work suggests that the truthfulness of simple propositions can be represented as a single linear direction in a model's internal activations, but this may not fully capture its underlying geometry. In this work, we extend the concept cone framework, recently introduced for modeling refusal, to the domain of truth. We identify multi-dimensional cones that causally mediate truth-related behavior across multiple LLM families. Our results are supported by three lines of evidence: (i) causal interventions reliably flip model responses to factual statements, (ii) learned cones generalize across model architectures, and (iii) cone-based interventions preserve unrelated model behavior. These findings reveal the richer, multidirectional structure governing simple true/false propositions in LLMs and highlight concept cones as a promising tool for probing abstract behaviors.

## Claims

- Evidence label: causal intervention. Concept cones of dimension up to five
  causally mediate truth-related behavior across Qwen2.5 and Gemma-2 model
  families, with larger models supporting more effective dimensions than
  smaller ones (Table 1).
- Evidence label: geometric. The additional cone basis vectors beyond the
  first are near-orthogonal to the classic difference-in-means truth
  direction (cosine similarity on the order of 1e-9 for dimensions 2-5),
  showing DIM captures only one facet of a multi-dimensional truth subspace
  (Table 3, Appendix E).
- Evidence label: fidelity. Truth-direction ablation causes only minimal
  (0.026-0.045) mean KL divergence on unrelated Alpaca instruction prompts,
  so the discovered cones are behaviorally specific rather than a general
  capability-destroying intervention (Table 2).
- Evidence label: null result. The same loss-guided cone-discovery procedure
  failed to find a usable direction for sentiment (Stanford Sentiment
  Treebank) or a valid cone for toxicity (ToxiGen), so multi-dimensional
  cone structure is not guaranteed to transfer to every candidate behavior
  (Appendix B).

## Summary

Yu, Bulusu, Yasunaga et al. extend Wollschläger et al.'s (2025, arXiv
2502.17420) concept-cone framework, originally built for refusal, to
propositional truth. The starting point is the same linear-probe paradigm
Marks and Tegmark (2024, arXiv 2310.06824) used for the single-direction
truth-direction account, but instead of an offline label the model's own
forced binary "Yes"/"No" response to a factual statement is the supervision
signal, letting the same forward pass support both probing and causal
intervention. The paper first localizes truth-related behavior by layer and
token position (Experiment 1): across Qwen2.5 and Gemma-2, single-direction
Answer Switching Rate (ASR) spikes in the 60-75% normalized-depth range and
is strongest at the final token position, so the cone search is restricted to
that region.

The core result (Experiment 2) trains, for each model, an orthonormal basis
of k=1..5 vectors via a three-term loss (induce truth on activation-addition
to false statements, inhibit truth on directional ablation from true
statements, retain unrelated behavior via KL divergence on 30-token Alpaca
continuations), then Monte Carlo samples 64 nonnegative combinations per
dimension to estimate ASR across the resulting cone. Larger models (Qwen-7B,
Gemma-9B) sustain near-100% ASR through all five dimensions; smaller models
(Qwen-3B, Gemma-2B) degrade sharply past dimension 2-3, suggesting the extra
directions start to capture unrelated features once the model's capacity for
independent truth-relevant axes is exhausted. Experiment 3 confirms the
interventions are behaviorally targeted: mean KL divergence on 200 held-out
Alpaca prompts stays at 0.026-0.045 across the tested models. Experiment 4 is
the geometric payoff: cosine similarity between the classic DIM truth
direction and the cone's own basis vectors is non-trivial only for the first
basis vector (order 1e-1) and collapses to noise-floor magnitude (order
1e-9) for every dimension beyond it, in both Gemma-2-9B and Qwen-2.5-9B. The
paper concludes truth is causally multi-dimensional, though it stops short of
claiming the representation is non-linear, since each individual cone axis is
still a linear direction. The datasets used are Marks and Tegmark's cities
statements plus subsets of Azaria and Mitchell's (2304.13734) element_symb and
animals_class true/false statements. Limitations acknowledged by the authors:
models tested span only 1.5B-14B parameters and two families (Qwen, Gemma),
and the truth operationalization is deliberately narrow (simple unambiguous
propositions, not context-dependent or subjective claims).

  sharply between 60-75% of normalized layer depth, in every model tested;
  final token position gives the strongest interventions.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper is the closest published prior art for the claim probed by the
correctness-direction-rotation cell (experiments/correctness-direction-rotation,
resolved as a null result: instrument-resolution-limited). That cell found
the fitted correctness direction was too weakly identified (split-half cosine
noise floor of 0.174) for a cosine-rotation instrument to say anything about
whether the direction rotates across training stages; the mechanism
[[correctness-direction-weakly-identified-defeats-cosine-rotation-probe]]
documents this. This paper suggests a different, complementary path to the
same underlying question: rather than fitting one direction per stage and
comparing its cosine, fit a low-dimensional causal cone at each stage (using
the add/ablate/retain loss and ASR/KL-divergence readouts here) and ask
whether the cone's dimensionality or its causal footprint changes across
stages, which sidesteps the single-direction identifiability problem that
sank the cosine-rotation instrument. It also directly grounds the premise
that a correctness signal being a subspace rather than a single direction is
plausible on priors: an almost mechanically identical construct (propositional
truth) has already been shown to be causally multi-dimensional in exactly this
way, with the multi-dimensionality growing with model scale. New mechanism
[[truth-cone-basis-vectors-causally-mediate-truth-orthogonally-to-dim]]
records this finding; the new atoms [[truth-concept-cone]] and
[[answer-switching-rate]] give the correctness-subspace-overlap experiment
design a named construct and a causal-intervention metric to borrow if a
cosine-only readout proves insufficient again.
