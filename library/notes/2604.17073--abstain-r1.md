## Abstract

Reinforcement fine-tuning improves the reasoning ability of large language models, but it can also encourage them to answer unanswerable queries by guessing or hallucinating missing information. Existing abstention methods either train models to produce generic refusals or encourage follow-up clarifications without verifying whether those clarifications identify the key missing information. We study queries that are clear in meaning but cannot be reliably resolved from the given information, and argue that a reliable model should not only abstain, but also explain what is missing. We propose a clarification-aware RLVR reward that, while rewarding correct answers on answerable queries, jointly optimizes explicit abstention and semantically aligned post-refusal clarification on unanswerable queries. Using this reward, we train Abstain-R1, a 3B model that improves abstention and clarification on unanswerable queries while preserving strong performance on answerable ones. Experiments on Abstain-Test, Abstain-QA, and SelfAware show that Abstain-R1 substantially improves over its base model and achieves unanswerable-query behavior competitive with larger systems including DeepSeek-R1, suggesting that calibrated abstention and clarification can be learned through verifiable rewards rather than emerging from scale alone.

## Summary

Zhai et al. train Abstain-R1, a 3B model derived from Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, to simultaneously abstain on unanswerable queries and produce semantically correct post-refusal clarifications. The target queries are semantically clear but lack a uniquely resolvable answer because of missing or underconstrained conditions, false premises, or known unknowns. The pipeline has three stages. First, a 4.6K SFT cold-start dataset (Abstain-CoT) is built by prompting DeepSeek-V3 to annotate AbstentionBench subsets with structured reasoning traces and target responses that combine explicit abstention with an actionable clarification identifying the missing information. Second, the SFT checkpoint is further trained with GRPO on a mixed corpus drawn from the SUM dataset training split (50K paired instances, sampled 30% unanswerable and 70% answerable). The composite reward combines: a format check (1 if output structure is valid); an answerable-query reward (1 for correct, -1 for false refusal, 0 otherwise); and a refusal-with-clarification reward that awards 0.3 for explicit abstention ("I don't know" in a boxed field) and an additional 0.7 if an xVerify-3B-Ia verifier judges the clarification semantically aligned with the reference. During training the conservative 3B verifier reduces reward hacking; offline evaluation switches to o4-mini for judgments better aligned with human preferences. Third, ablations systematically remove SFT, RL, unanswerable training data, the clarification reward, and the under-abstention penalty to isolate the contribution of each component. The evaluation suite, Abstain-Test (approximately 2.9K instances from AbstentionBench subsets plus the SUM test set), measures six metrics: A-Acc (accuracy on answerable), A-FU (false-unknown rate on answerable, lower is better), A-Acc_c (conditional accuracy on the answered subset), U-Ref (refusal rate on unanswerable), U-Clar (fraction of unanswerable queries that receive both a refusal and a verifier-judged correct clarification), and U-Clar_c (U-Clar conditioned on refusal). The headline finding is that Abstain-R1 reaches U-Ref 68.1% and U-Clar 55.1% on Abstain-Test, versus the base model at 9.4% and 0.6%, while conditional answerable accuracy rises from 60.1% to 71.9%. These figures match or exceed much larger models: Abstain-R1 surpasses 32B Qwen2.5 Instruct with 5-shot ICL on U-Ref (68.1% vs. 66.2%), though it trails on U-Clar (55.1% vs. 60.1%). On SelfAware, Abstain-R1 achieves U-Ref 91.4%, the highest among all evaluated models. On Abstain-QA, its U-Ref 40.1% is the highest, versus 30.0% for the base and 9.1% for DeepSeek-R1. The ablations show that removing SFT cold-start collapses U-Clar to 8.5% while preserving much of U-Ref (65.1%), showing that clarification quality depends on SFT-phase guidance; removing RL drops U-Ref to 51.9% while U-Clar falls to 37.0%. Removing unanswerable training data entirely produces a model that refuses almost never (U-Ref 4.4%, U-Clar 3.1%) while achieving the highest A-Acc (67.5%) and lowest A-FU (0.5%), confirming that abstention behavior is directly learned from the mixed training distribution rather than emerging from scale.

## Relevance to experiment

Three direct connections to the Phase 1 experiment. First, the clarification-aware GRPO reward is a concrete alternative to the binary abstention rewards typical of the RLVR arm: it allocates 0.3 for explicit refusal and 0.7 for correct clarification, with a tunable under-abstention penalty, and this structure is directly substitutable as a comparison condition or baseline in any Phase 1 RLVR cell that currently uses a simpler abstention signal. Second, the Abstain-Test six-metric suite extends binary abstention measurement: U-Clar and U-Clar_c distinguish models that refuse (U-Ref) from models that refuse usefully, a distinction the current Phase 1 metrics do not capture. Adding U-Clar as a read-out would make it possible to see whether Phase 1 arms teach the model when to abstain without teaching it what to say afterward. Third, the ablation evidence (Table 2: U-Clar collapses from 37.0% to 8.5% when RL is run directly from the base without SFT cold-start) is direct empirical support for the hypothesis that clarification quality requires SFT-phase grounding, which bounds the existing rlvr-post-training-degrades-abstention mechanism: standard RLVR degrades abstention when the reward does not include abstention credit, but even targeted RLVR with abstention credit produces near-zero clarification quality without a supervised cold start. That bounding condition has direct design implications for any Phase 1 cell that skips SFT.

## Claims

- A clarification-aware GRPO reward that allocates 0.3 for explicit abstention and 0.7 for verifier-judged correct clarification lifts Abstain-R1 (3B) to U-Ref 68.1% and U-Clar 55.1% on Abstain-Test, versus the base Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct at 9.4% and 0.6%, showing that calibrated abstention and clarification can be acquired through targeted verifiable rewards in a 3B model. (Table 1) [[clarification-aware-rlvr-reward]]
- Running GRPO directly from the base model without SFT cold-start reduces U-Clar from 55.1% to 8.5% while preserving most of U-Ref (65.1%), demonstrating that the SFT phase is the primary locus of clarification learning and that RL alone is insufficient to acquire post-refusal clarification quality. (Table 2) [[rlvr-post-training-degrades-abstention]]
- Abstain-R1 (3B) achieves U-Ref 40.1% on Abstain-QA, the highest among all evaluated models and well above DeepSeek-R1 at 9.1%, and reaches U-Ref 91.4% on SelfAware, the highest evaluated, demonstrating that targeted abstention training at 3B scale outperforms much larger models on unanswerability detection. (Table 1) [[abstain-r1]]
- Setting the under-abstention penalty to 0 (removing it) raises U-Ref to 82.4% and U-Clar to 63.8%, but A-Acc falls to 46.1% and A-FU rises to 36.2%; the default penalty of -1 restores A-Acc to 57.2% and halves A-FU to 20.4%, showing that the penalty controls an explicit abstention-accuracy tradeoff. (Table 3) [[clarification-aware-rlvr-reward]]
- The Abstain-Test six-metric suite measures A-Acc, A-FU, A-Acc_c, U-Ref, U-Clar, and U-Clar_c, covering both the answerable-query penalty and the quality of post-refusal clarification; existing binary abstention metrics such as abstention-rate capture only U-Ref and miss U-Clar, underestimating training success for clarification-capable models. (Section 5.1) [[u-clar]]
