## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are highly effective in various natural language processing (NLP) tasks. However, they are susceptible to producing unreliable conjectures in ambiguous contexts called hallucination. This paper presents a new method for evaluating LLM hallucination in Question Answering (QA) based on the unanswerable math word problem (MWP). To support this approach, we innovatively develop a dataset called Unanswerable Math Word Problem (UMWP) which comprises 5200 questions across five categories. We developed an evaluation methodology combining text similarity and mathematical expression detection to determine whether LLM considers the question unanswerable. The results of extensive experiments conducted on 31 LLMs, including GPT-3, InstructGPT, LLaMA, and Claude, demonstrate that in-context learning and reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) training significantly enhance the model's ability to avoid hallucination. We show that utilizing MWP is a reliable and effective approach to assess hallucination. Our code and data are available at https://github.com/Yuki-Asuuna/UMWP.

## Summary

Sun et al. introduce UMWP (Unanswerable Math Word Problem), a 5,200-question benchmark for evaluating hallucination in LLMs through the lens of mathematical unanswerability. Half the questions are answerable (drawn from GSM8K, SVAMP, MultiArith, and ASDiv); half are unanswerable modifications classified into five types: Key Information Missing (32%), Ambiguous Key Information (49%), Unrealistic Conditions (11%), Unrelated Object (4%), and Question Missing (5%). The evaluation pipeline uses two independent detection branches (Algorithm 1): first, a SimCSE-based similarity check against a curated set of uncertainty-expressing template sentences at a threshold of 0.75 (threshold value sourced from the SelfAware ablation in Yin et al. 2023, not from SimCSE itself); second, a variable-expression detector that labels any output containing a mathematical expression (number, variable) as "answerable." These two branches are independent: a response matching an uncertainty template is labeled unanswerable regardless of whether it also contains an expression, and a response containing an expression is labeled answerable regardless of template similarity. Experiments on 31 LLMs show a consistent pattern: instruction and ICL input forms substantially outperform direct prompting; model size correlates with hallucination avoidance within a model family; and RLHF fine-tuning is the single largest driver, with chat models at 13B competing with base models at 65B. GPT-4 achieves the best F1 at 85.24%, still 7.9 points below the human benchmark of 93.16%. The TemplateRule combined evaluator improves Cohen's kappa over template-only grading by 2.7 to 7.2 kappa units across five models, with all combined scores in the "good match" range above 0.75.

## Relevance to experiment

UMWP provides a structured, knowledge-independent unanswerability signal for measuring abstention behavior: because answering requires computation rather than recalled facts, a model that hallucinates an answer cannot attribute it to memorized world knowledge. This makes UMWP a complementary evaluation surface to factual-QA benchmarks already in the graph. The RLHF finding, that chat-tuned 13B models match base 65B models, is direct prior evidence that alignment training is a larger driver of abstention quality than raw model scale, bearing on Phase 1's SFT-vs-DPO-vs-KTO comparison. The evaluator design (Algorithm 1's two independent OR-branches combining template similarity and expression detection) also illustrates a grading pattern the experiment can adapt for math-domain abstention cells. One caveat for direct reuse: UMWP F1 scores (roughly 30-40% for weak models, 85% for GPT-4) are specific to math-domain unanswerability and may not transfer as reference baselines to Phase 1 factual-QA and calibration metrics without domain-matched replication.

## Claims

- UMWP's five-category unanswerability taxonomy covers 5,200 questions derived from four MWP datasets, with Ambiguous Key Information the most common type at 49% and Question Missing the rarest at 5%. (Table 1, Section 1) [[umwp]]
- The TemplateRule evaluator (combining template similarity and variable-expression detection via two independent OR-branches) improves Cohen's kappa over template-only grading by 2.7 to 7.2 kappa units, with all five tested models landing in the 'good match' range above 0.75. (Table 3, Section 5.5) [[template-rule-evaluator]]
- GPT-4 achieves the best LLM F1 of 85.24% on UMWP under the instruction input form, still 7.9 points below the human benchmark of 93.16%, indicating a persistent human-model gap in recognizing mathematical unanswerability. (Section 5.4) [[self-knowledge-f1]]
- RLHF fine-tuning drives substantially larger gains in unanswerability recognition than model scale: LLaMA-v2-13b-chat competes with LLaMA-65b across all three input forms. (Section 5.4) [[rlhf-improves-unanswerability-recognition]]
- Instruction and ICL input forms substantially outperform direct prompting at recognizing unanswerable math problems, and the F1 gap between instruction and ICL input forms narrows as parameter count increases. (Section 5.4, Figure 3) [[icl-improves-self-knowledge]]
