## Abstract

We show that emotion vectors in LLMs are organized by a two-dimensional valence-arousal (VA) subspace exhibiting circular geometry. Through principal component decomposition and ridge regression, we recover meaningful VA axes underlying emotion steering vectors whose projections correlate with human affect ratings across 44,728 words. Steering along these axes produces monotonic control over the affective properties of generated text, and further affords bidirectional control over multiple downstream behaviors (refusal and sycophancy) from a single subspace. These effects replicate across Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, and Qwen3-14B. We propose lexical mediation to explain why these effects and prior emotionally framed controls work: refusal and compliance tokens occupy distinct VA regions, and VA steering directly modulates their emission probabilities.

## Summary

Sun et al. show that emotion steering vectors in LLMs organize around a two-dimensional valence-arousal (VA) subspace exhibiting circular geometry, then exploit that structure to achieve simultaneous monotonic control over refusal and sycophancy from a single learned subspace. The pipeline has three stages: (1) derive emotion steering vectors from 211,225 GoEmotions-labeled texts via mean-difference contrasts against neutral examples; (2) elicit the model's self-reported VA coordinates for each of the 27 emotion categories; (3) fit VA axes as linear combinations of the top principal components of those steering vectors via ridge regression. The resulting subspace recovers valence with r=0.97 (Llama-3.1-8B), 0.96 (Qwen3-8B), 0.97 (Qwen3-14B) and arousal with r=0.87/0.79/0.81 across the same three models. Projections onto the learned subspace correlate with human NRC-VAD lexicon ratings across 44,728 words (valence r=0.71 at layer 6; arousal r=0.23 at layer 7), confirming semantic alignment. When visualized at the best layer, the emotion vectors arrange themselves into a circle in VA coordinates, peaking at a circularity score of 4.08 (Llama layer 5), 3.13 (Qwen3-8B layer 6), and 2.76 (Qwen3-14B layer 24), directly paralleling Russell's circumplex model of human affect. Critically, steering along the arousal axis controls refusal and sycophancy near-monotonically: on Llama-3.1-8B, lowering arousal raises OKTest refusal from 20% to 86% and XSTest refusal from 8% to 86% (alpha=-0.45), while raising arousal reduces HarmBench refusal from 87% to 5% (alpha=+0.45). Random directions remain within 2-3 percentage points of baseline throughout. Sycophancy on Political Typology drops from 78% to 47% when valence is steered negative (alpha=-0.30), compared to only a 10-point reduction from the best individual emotion vector (disgust). The mechanistic account is lexical mediation: refusal tokens ("I can't," "Sorry") cluster in the low-arousal, negative-valence region of the unembedding space, and VA steering directly modulates their log-odds. Clamping the logits of 21 refusal/compliance tokens to unsteered values collapses the arousal-induced refusal rate from 86.5% to 26.0%, while clamping 21 random tokens leaves it at 86.5%. The refusal contrastive direction is nearly orthogonal to the VA axes (86.5 degrees) yet both yield monotonic behavioral control, with the refusal direction's small overlapping component located at VA coordinates V=-0.058, A=-0.021. Emotional prefixes shift representations toward -V/-A, increasing refusal by up to 30 percentage points for the most potent prefix. Task capabilities are preserved within 1-2 percentage points at moderate alpha. Results replicate across Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, and Qwen3-14B.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper is directly upstream of both the Phase 1 SFT/DPO/KTO sycophancy arms and the Phase 3 mechanistic-interpretability sweeps. Three connections are most concrete. First, the VA subspace provides a training-free behavioral dial: a single arousal-axis steering vector reduces sycophancy on Political Typology by 18 percentage points and refusal rates by up to 66 percentage points, with no weight update. Comparing that magnitude to the Phase 1 fine-tuning deltas on the same sycophancy task puts the training cost in context and motivates a steering-then-fine-tune comparison. Second, the lexical mediation account, supported by logit-clamping ablations, gives a concrete mechanistic hypothesis for Phase 3: if the Qwen3 hidden states encode a VA-like subspace, then probing for arousal-axis alignment before and after each Phase 1 training arm would reveal whether training sharpens or diffuses that structure. The fact that Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B are already replicated models in this paper makes that probe directly applicable. Third, the near-orthogonality result (86.5 degrees) between the VA subspace and the contrastive refusal direction shows that two geometrically distinct paths to behavioral control coexist in the same model, which is relevant for Phase 3 candidate inventory design: activation candidates should include both VA-aligned and task-contrastive directions as separate probes, not conflated. One interpretive note on the relevance claim about annotation cost: the GoEmotions pipeline requires 211k labeled texts and a self-report elicitation step, so it is not cost-free; but it reuses a public dataset and adds no human labeling beyond the existing GoEmotions annotation.

## Claims

- Ridge-regression recovery of valence from PCA components of emotion steering vectors reaches r=0.97 on Llama-3.1-8B and replicates at r=0.96/0.97 on Qwen3-8B/14B, while single-PC recovery reaches r=0.89, showing that a low-dimensional subspace captures most of the valence signal. (Table 1) [[va-subspace-extraction]]
- Arousal-axis steering near-monotonically controls refusal: OKTest refusal moves from 20% to 86% and HarmBench from 87% to 5% on Llama-3.1-8B, while random directions remain within 2-3pp of baseline, establishing arousal as the primary behavioral control axis. (Section 5.1) [[arousal-axis-monotonically-controls-refusal-and-sycophancy]]
- Valence steering outperforms the best individual emotion vector on sycophancy reduction (Political Typology: -31pp vs. -10pp for disgust at alpha=-0.30), demonstrating that decomposing into continuous VA axes exposes control structure that single-category contrastive directions miss. (Section 5.3) [[lexical-mediation-links-va-steering-to-behavioral-control]]
- Clamping the logits of 21 refusal/compliance tokens to their unsteered values collapses arousal-steered refusal from 86.5% to 26.0%, while clamping 21 random tokens has no effect (86.5% retained), constituting mechanistic evidence that VA steering acts primarily through token emission probability rather than upstream reasoning. (Table 14, Section 6.1) [[lexical-mediation-links-va-steering-to-behavioral-control]]
- The refusal contrastive direction lies 86.5 degrees from the VA plane yet yields monotonic behavioral control, showing that geometrically distinct subspaces can converge on the same downstream behavioral effect via the shared lexical-mediation pathway. (Section 6.2) [[refusal-direction-mediates-refusal]]
