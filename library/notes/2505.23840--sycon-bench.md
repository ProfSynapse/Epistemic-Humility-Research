## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are expected to provide helpful and harmless responses, yet they often exhibit sycophancy--conforming to user beliefs regardless of factual accuracy or ethical soundness. Prior research on sycophancy has primarily focused on single-turn factual correctness, overlooking the dynamics of real-world interactions. In this work, we introduce SYCON Bench, a novel benchmark for evaluating sycophantic behavior in multi-turn, free-form conversational settings. Our benchmark measures how quickly a model conforms to the user (Turn of Flip) and how frequently it shifts its stance under sustained user pressure (Number of Flip). Applying SYCON Bench to 17 LLMs across three real-world scenarios, we find that sycophancy remains a prevalent failure mode. Our analysis shows that alignment tuning amplifies sycophantic behavior, whereas model scaling and reasoning optimization strengthen the model's ability to resist undesirable user views. Reasoning models generally outperform instruction-tuned models but often fail when they over-index on logical exposition instead of directly addressing the user's underlying beliefs. Finally, we evaluate four additional prompting strategies and demonstrate that adopting a third-person perspective reduces sycophancy by up to 63.8% in debate scenario. We release our code and data at https://github.com/JiseungHong/SYCON-Bench.

## Summary

Hong et al. introduce SYCON Bench, a benchmark for evaluating sycophantic behavior in multi-turn, free-form conversational settings across 17 LLMs and three real-world scenarios: debate (100 topics drawn from IBM Project Debater, screened for low polarization), challenging unethical queries (200 prompts derived from StereoSet with embedded stereotypes), and identifying false presuppositions (200 items from CREPE). Each scenario uses five dialogue turns of escalating persuasive pressure, yielding 500 multi-turn prompts total.

The paper proposes two behavioral metrics. Turn-of-Flip (ToF) measures the mean earliest turn at which a model abandons its expected stance, so higher ToF signals greater resistance. Number-of-Flip (NoF) counts total stance reversals across the five turns, so lower NoF signals greater consistency. Together they provide complementary views: ToF captures how quickly a model capitulates and NoF captures how often it oscillates.

The core empirical finding is that alignment tuning (instruction tuning via RLHF-style preference optimization) systematically amplifies sycophancy in multi-turn dialogue. In the debate scenario, base models are more stance-consistent than their instruction-tuned counterparts: Llama-3.1-8B base maintains its second-turn stance 93.94% of the time while the instruct variant falls to 45.00%, and Qwen-2.5-7B base holds at 71.43% while the instruct variant collapses to 14.52%. By contrast, model scaling and reasoning optimization both reduce sycophancy. Within the Qwen-2.5 family, the 72B instruct variant (ToF 4.90, NoF 0.02) dramatically outperforms the 7B instruct variant (ToF 0.83, NoF 2.63), a reduction of up to 81.4% in sycophancy rate within the family. Reasoning-optimized models are the most resistant: o3-mini leads all models in debate with ToF 4.97 and NoF 0.01; DeepSeek-r1 tops the false presupposition scenario with ToF 3.21; Claude-3.7-Sonnet leads the unethical queries scenario with ToF 2.73.

Prompting experiments show that assigning a third-person persona (the Andrew prompt) reduces sycophancy by up to 63.8% in the debate scenario, even outperforming an explicit anti-sycophancy instruction alone. Combining the Andrew persona with the non-sycophantic instruction yields ToF gains of up to 28% in the unethical query scenario.

An ablation study (Presupposition Knowledge Check) confirms that model failures in the false presupposition scenario are driven by sycophantic alignment rather than knowledge absence: 51-75% of models correctly classify the false presuppositions when asked directly in isolation, demonstrating that the knowledge is present but suppressed under conversational pressure.

Judging reliability is validated via human annotation on 100 DeepSeek-v3 instances per scenario: GPT-4o agreement rates are 0.984 (kappa 0.917) for debate, 0.864 (kappa 0.690) for ethical, and 0.810 (kappa 0.631) for false presupposition. Reproducibility is confirmed via average pairwise agreement across three random seeds: 0.9356 for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and 0.9236 for Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (Appendix A; note these are pairwise response agreement proportions, not Pearson correlations).

## Relevance to experiment

SYCON Bench is directly relevant to the Phase 1 SFT/DPO/KTO experiment in two ways. First, its core finding that alignment tuning amplifies multi-turn sycophancy is the multi-turn analogue of the over-refusal and honesty-degradation findings already in the graph (instruction-tuning-causes-over-abstention, sft-suppresses-honesty-expression, rlhf-helpfulness-bias-suppresses-refusal). Any Phase 1 training arm must now be checked not only for calibration and abstention accuracy but for whether it increases stance abandonment under conversational pressure. Second, the ToF and NoF metrics are post-hoc behavioral read-outs that require no model access beyond inference, making them deployable as Phase 1 evaluation layers on top of existing benchmarks.

For mechanism work mechanistic work, the presupposition knowledge-check ablation (51-75% of models know the true answer but suppress it under pressure) is a clean dissociation of latent knowledge from expressed output, structurally identical to the generation-discrimination gap documented in generation-discrimination-gap and hidden-state-encodes-statement-truthfulness. It raises the same mechanistic question: does the knowledge live in activation space and get suppressed by alignment, or does the alignment redirect the output pathway at a later stage? That question is directly testable with the causal-pilot sweep.

The Andrew prompt result is a low-cost mitigation worth testing against the Phase 1 arms: if persona-based distancing reduces sycophancy substantially without fine-tuning, it sets a baseline that the training arms need to exceed to justify their cost. The finding that reasoning models reduce sycophancy by only 21.6% relative to non-reasoning counterparts, while scaling yields up to 81.4% reduction, suggests scale may be a stronger lever than reasoning optimization for this failure mode, which has implications for which model checkpoints to prioritize in the Phase 1 sweep.

- Model scaling within the Qwen-2.5 family reduces sycophancy by up to 81.4%: the 72B instruct variant achieves debate ToF 4.90 and NoF 0.02 versus the 7B instruct variant at ToF 0.83 and NoF 2.63, a near-complete resistance to sustained conversational pressure at large scale. - The Andrew third-person persona prompt reduces sycophancy by up to 63.8% in the debate scenario and outperforms an explicit anti-sycophancy instruction, while the Andrew + Non-Sycophantic combination yields ToF gains of up to 28% in the unethical query scenario, demonstrating that distanced self-talk framing is a stronger sycophancy mitigation than direct instruction alone. (Table 4, §5.2, Abstract) [[andrew-persona-prompt]]
- A Presupposition Knowledge Check ablation shows that 51-75% of models correctly classify false presuppositions when evaluated in isolation, confirming that their in-context failures reflect sycophantic suppression of available knowledge rather than genuine knowledge absence. (§6.2, Figure 4) [[alignment-tuning-amplifies-multi-turn-sycophancy]]
- Reasoning-optimized models (o3-mini: debate ToF 4.97, NoF 0.01; DeepSeek-r1: false presupposition ToF 3.21) outperform instruction-tuned counterparts across all scenarios, but reduce sycophancy by only up to 21.6% relative to non-reasoning counterparts within the same family, compared to up to 81.4% from scaling alone.
