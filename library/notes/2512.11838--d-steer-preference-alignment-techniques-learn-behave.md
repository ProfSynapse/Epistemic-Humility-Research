## Abstract

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has become a standard recipe for aligning large language models, yet it is still unclear what kind of change it actually induces inside the network. This paper argues that DPO does not rewrite a models internal beliefs; instead, it acts as a low rank steering mechanism that nudges activations along a small number of preference directions. Using a simple derivation, we show that the DPO gradient depends only on the difference between the logit embeddings of preferred and dispreferred completions, implying a first order shift in the final hidden representation rather than a deep restructuring of semantics. We then extract an empirical steering vector from a DPO tuned model and demonstrate that adding this vector to base activations reproduces most of the aligned behavior, while subtracting it nearly restores the original model. Finally, spectral analyses reveal rank-one dominance and entropy collapse in upper layers, indicating that alignment is funneled through a narrow subspace. Taken together, these results support a behavioral illusion view of DPO: it teaches models how to act aligned, not what to believe.

## Summary

VENUE: unrefereed preprint (arXiv only as of ingest, 2026-08-16) — the
weakest-sourced paper in this batch; weight its claims accordingly.

Raina et al. argue mechanistically that Direct Preference Optimization
(DPO) does not rewrite an aligned model's internal beliefs but instead acts
as a low-rank behavioral steering operator. Training a 7B LLaMA-family base
into a DPO-aligned variant on OASST1 and Anthropic HH preference pairs,
they extract an empirical steering vector as the mean final-layer
hidden-state displacement between the DPO and base models across held-out
prompts, and find the per-example DPO-induced shift is nearly parallel to
this single direction, with cosine similarity concentrated in the
high-0.9 range at the final layer. Adding the vector to base activations
reproduces most of the DPO-aligned behavior; subtracting it from the DPO
model's activations "nearly restores" base-model behavior, a qualitative,
figure-level claim the paper does not accompany with a numeric recovery
metric. In a separate analysis, layer-wise spectral decomposition of the
DPO update finds a subset of upper layers collapse to near rank-one
updates, with the leading singular vector aligned to the same empirical
steering direction, plus entropy collapse in the upper layers generally.
Together the paper reads DPO's success as narrow and shallow: a
first-order shift confined to a low-dimensional subspace, teaching a model
to act aligned without necessarily believing it — "DPO teaches models
what to say, not what to believe."

Caveats (binding on how this note's claims are worded, verified against
paper-2's own precision pass 2026-08-15): evidence is a single LLaMA-2-7B
model with final-layer hidden-state analysis (the layerwise spectral
result additionally covers Mistral-1.3B); there is no instruction-removal
(prompt-based) test of their own, and no head-to-head against SFT.
Behavioral evidence (G-Eval, Perspective, TruthfulQA) is present but thin.
Treat as concurrent mechanistic support, not a confirmed replication.
