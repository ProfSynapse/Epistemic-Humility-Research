## Abstract

Recent work has shown that on-policy distillation can internalize privileged context, such as system prompts or task hints, into a student model so that the context is no longer needed at inference time. However, we identify a counterintuitive and previously unstudied phenomenon: reintroducing the original privileged context to the distilled student often degrades its performance, even on instances it already solves correctly without context. We term this phenomenon context-induced degradation and argue that robust internalization requires not only matching the teacher's context-conditioned behavior, but also remaining stable when the privileged context is reintroduced, a desirable property we call context invariance. To promote this property, we formulate a novel view-robust internalization risk and propose No-Context Anchoring (NCA), a lightweight yet effective consistency regularizer that uses the student's stop-gradient no-context output as an anchor and aligns its context-conditioned output via forward KL divergence. Across 14 configurations spanning diverse domains and model families, NCA improves context-conditioned accuracy in most settings and reduces context harm in 12 out of 14, while preserving or improving no-context performance, demonstrating greater robustness to context reintroduction.

## Summary

Wang et al. study on-policy distillation (OPD), which internalizes privileged context (system prompts, chain-of-thought hints, game-state scaffolds) into a context-free student by matching a context-conditioned teacher's behavior. They identify a counterintuitive failure: reintroducing the very context a student was trained to internalize often degrades its performance, even overturning predictions the student already got correct without the context. They call this context-induced degradation, and trace it to an asymmetry in the standard OPD objective, which optimizes only "privileged fidelity" (matching the teacher without context) and leaves the student's behavior with context present entirely unconstrained. They propose context invariance, the property that a robustly-internalized model's output should be stable whether or not the privileged context is present, as a necessary complement to privileged fidelity. To promote it, they formulate a view-robust internalization risk and introduce No-Context Anchoring (NCA): a lightweight regularizer that treats the student's own stop-gradient no-context output as an anchor and aligns its context-conditioned output to that anchor via forward KL divergence, at the cost of one extra forward pass per training step. Across 14 configurations spanning System-Prompt QA, Text Games, and Mathematical Reasoning, and six model families, NCA reduces context harm in 12 of 14 settings and improves both-view accuracy, without sacrificing no-context performance.

- Most severe case: Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct on MedMCQA under OPD, context-conditioned accuracy (65.3%) falls 9.5 points below context-free accuracy (74.8%), Harm 15.7% (Table 1, Section 5.2.1)
- NCA on the same worst case: Harm drops from 15.7% to 1.6% (a 14.1-point reduction), BothCorrect rises from 63.1% to 73.5% (a 10.4-point gain) (Table 1, Section 5.2.1)
- Even a model with similar context-free and context-conditioned base performance (Qwen3-8B) still shows non-trivial 5.5% harm under OPD on Safety, showing strong base performance alone does not confer robustness to context reintroduction (Section 5.2.1)
- NCA adds only one forward pass per training step relative to standard OPD (Section 1, Section 4)

## Relevance to experiment

This paper generalizes the earlier weights-vs-prompt equivalence question (Askell et al.'s context distillation, arXiv:2112.00861) into a sharper, two-sided test: not just "does the trained model reproduce prompted behavior," but "does the trained model stay correct if the original prompt/context is put back." Context-induced degradation shows the answer can be no, and that this failure is invisible to context-free evaluation alone. Applied to the abstention-training comparison, this motivates evaluating trained SFT/DPO/KTO abstention models both with and without any abstention-relevant system prompt or context they were distilled from (or trained alongside), since a model that abstains well context-free but degrades when reminded of its own training-time framing would be exhibiting exactly this pattern; NCA-style consistency regularization (anchoring context-conditioned behavior to the model's own no-context behavior) is also a directly transferable technique if a future locked training-regimen cell wants a training-time fix for such context sensitivity.
