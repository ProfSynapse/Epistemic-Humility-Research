## Abstract

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) improves large language model reasoning but often suffers from rapid policy-entropy collapse, where the policy prematurely concentrates on narrow high-probability reasoning paths. While global entropy regularization can encourage exploration, uniformly increasing entropy across all token positions is inefficient for long reasoning trajectories, where many tokens are not decision-relevant. We propose Position-Aware Entropy Calibration (PAEC), a token-level entropy-management framework that constructs a soft mask from local top-p entropy and top-two candidate competition, and applies an anchor-based lower-bound penalty to prevent selected-position entropy collapse. Experiments on five mathematical reasoning benchmarks show that PAEC improves macro-average majority-vote performance over strong RLVR baselines, with clear gains on AIME-style tasks. Our results suggest that entropy management in reasoning RL should be formulated as selective exploration allocation over decision-sensitive positions rather than uniform randomness injection.

## Summary

Yang et al. propose Position-Aware Entropy Calibration (PAEC), a token-level entropy management framework for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). The central observation is that standard RLVR training (e.g., GRPO) causes rapid policy-entropy collapse: the policy concentrates probability mass on narrow high-confidence reasoning paths, reducing the diversity of sampled rollouts and ultimately hurting majority-vote accuracy on hard competition math. Global entropy regularization is an inefficient fix because most tokens in a long chain-of-thought are not decision-sensitive; adding noise there wastes capacity.

PAEC constructs a soft position mask from two signals computed at each token position: (1) the local top-p nucleus entropy, which is high when many tokens have comparable probability, and (2) the top-two log-probability gap, which is small when the leading two candidates are competitive. A position is considered decision-sensitive when nucleus entropy is high and the gap is small. The mask is quantile-thresholded (best setting: quantile = 0.8, meaning the top 20% most uncertain positions are selected). An anchor entropy H_0 is established from the first K=4 training steps (Equations 12-13), and a one-sided quadratic penalty fires whenever the entropy at selected positions falls below a lower-bound H_low = rho_min * H_0. This penalty is inactive when entropy is healthy and strengthens as entropy collapses, acting as a floor rather than a constant push.

The backbone is Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B trained with GRPO. Evaluation covers five benchmarks: AIME24, AIME25, AIME26 (Maj@32), MATH500 (Maj@8), and AMC23 (Maj@16). PAEC improves macro-average majority-vote (Avg Maj) by 6.1 points over GRPO (41.6 vs 35.5) and macro-average mean (Avg Avg) by 2.4 points (32.7 vs 30.3). Gains are largest on AIME25 (+16.7 Maj@32). AMC23 is the one benchmark where PAEC trails GRPO by 2.5 points. Supplementary experiments on Qwen3-4B-Base and Llama3.1-8B-Base confirm the pattern. The paper explicitly warns (Section 5.3) that the soft mask is not a causal identification of semantically meaningful decision points; it is a heuristic derived from output-distribution statistics.

## Relevance to experiment

PAEC is a reasoning-accuracy paper, not an epistemic humility paper. It measures majority-vote scores on competition math, not calibration, abstention, or hallucination rates. Three inferential connections to the research program are worth tracking.

First, the mechanism PAEC combats, policy-entropy collapse, is the same process that the graph already documents under rlvr-post-training-degrades-abstention: RLVR optimization drives the policy toward narrow high-confidence paths, and the paper confirms this damages even the accuracy-focused objective. If entropy collapse hurts majority-vote diversity, it almost certainly also reduces the internal uncertainty signal that supports appropriate abstention, reinforcing the mechanism link but not supplying empirical data on abstention.

Second, PAEC's soft position mask is built from token-level entropy and top-two log-probability competition. The authors explicitly caution (Section 5.3) that this is not a causal identification of semantically meaningful decision points. That caveat matters for any attempt to repurpose the mask as an inference-time uncertainty diagnostic: it may correlate with genuine uncertainty, but the paper cannot establish that.

Third, the training backbone is Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B trained with GRPO, with supplementary experiments on Qwen3-4B-Base and Llama3.1-8B-Base. None of these models or training conditions overlap directly with the Phase 1 Synaptic Tuner SFT/DPO/KTO study. PAEC is most relevant as background on RLVR dynamics and as a concrete illustration of what policy-entropy collapse costs in practice.

## Claims

- PAEC's anchor-based one-sided quadratic lower-bound penalty applied to soft-masked decision-sensitive positions raises macro-average Maj@K by 6.1 points over GRPO on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks. (Table 1, Section 4.2) [[paec]]
- Both the lower-bound entropy penalty and the soft position mask are individually necessary: removing the penalty drops Avg Maj from 41.6 to 38.8, and removing the mask drops it to 38.3, confirming that selective-position targeting and floor enforcement are complementary. (Table 2, Section 5.1) [[paec]]
- The soft mask quantile threshold is the most sensitive hyperparameter: rho=0.8 (selecting the top 20% most uncertain positions) yields Avg Maj 41.6 while rho=0.3 yields only 36.6, a 5.0-point gap. (Table 3, Section 5.5) [[policy-entropy-collapse-narrows-rlvr-reasoning-paths]]
- The entropy-collapse problem PAEC targets generalizes across model families: gains of +3.3 points on Qwen3-4B-Base and +3.0 points on Llama3.1-8B-Base over GRPO confirm that uniform entropy regularization leaves diversity gains on the table at multiple scales. (Table 5, Appendix B.3) [[position-aware-entropy-penalty-preserves-exploration]]
- The soft mask is explicitly not a causal identification of semantically meaningful decision positions (Section 5.3); it is a heuristic from output-distribution statistics and should not be treated as a reliable inference-time uncertainty diagnostic without further validation. (Section 5.3) [[paec]]
