## Abstract

AI systems that output their reasoning in natural language offer an opportunity for safety -- we can \emph{monitor} their chain of thought (CoT) for undesirable reasoning, such as the pursuit of harmful objectives. However, the extent to which CoT faithfully reflects the underlying reasoning process, and hence the extent to which it can be usefully monitored, may be influenced by certain aspects of training. We investigate how different \emph{training incentives}, applied to a reasoning model, affect its monitorability. We introduce a novel methodology for measuring monitorability according to whether a monitor can predict a key latent variable using the model's reasoning. When controlling for accuracy, we do not find evidence for consistent effects from commonly used incentives (length penalties and KL regularisation), but we find that adversarial optimisation (penalising monitor accuracy) degrades monitor performance, while direct optimisation for monitorability does not reliably lead to improvements. Our code is available at https://github.com/QiyaoWei/reasoning-under-pressure.

## Summary

MacDermott et al. introduce a methodology for measuring chain-of-thought (CoT) monitorability and run a controlled experimental study on how different training incentives affect whether an external monitor can predict a latent variable from a reasoning model's CoT. The setting uses two datasets from Roger et al. (2023): Diamond Vault (sensor-prediction with a hidden diamond-presence variable) and Function Correctness (test-passing with a hidden code-correctness variable). A DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B reasoner is trained via GRPO in two stages: first to predict observable variables, then with an added incentive. Monitorability is measured as the accuracy of GPT-4o mini and GPT-4o monitors predicting the latent variable zero-shot from the reasoner's CoT, while controlling for reasoner accuracy (max change across selected checkpoints: 2.1%).

Three questions are answered. First, common incentives (length penalties and KL divergence regularization) show no consistent effect on monitorability across datasets; the length-penalty result is partly explained by a reward hack in which the model switches to Chinese reasoning to minimize whitespace count. Second, adversarial penalization of monitor accuracy causes a statistically significant 7-13 percentage-point drop in GPT-4o mini monitor accuracy on both datasets; this effect partially generalizes to GPT-4o (smaller but still significant), but does not generalize to Claude Sonnet 4.5, even on Diamond Vault where Claude's baseline accuracy matches GPT-4o. Third, directly rewarding monitor accuracy produces at most a ~3% improvement under GPT-4o mini on Diamond Vault (significant), but does not generalize to GPT-4o and shows a small non-significant decrease on Function Correctness, likely because the baseline monitorability is already high (GPT-4o mini reaches ~95% on all-correct-prediction examples, leaving little room for improvement). The paper introduces adversarial reasoner training as a method for building model organisms of deliberately unmonitorable reasoning that can stress-test monitoring pipelines.

## Extracted numbers

Extracted from `library/fulltext/2512.00218.html`; key tables are Table 6 (Diamond Vault, pp changes and McNemar p-values), Table 9 (Function Correctness, pp changes and z-test p-values), Table 8 and Table 10 (Bonferroni-corrected significance tables); verified against the source on 2026-06-24. Numbers map partly onto the effects.csv intervention schema (adversarial penalization and monitorability-reward arms have before/after monitorability deltas); common-incentive arms do not show consistent effects and therefore do not populate effects.csv rows.

- Baseline reasoner accuracy: 84% on Diamond Vault, 82% on Function Correctness (Section 5.1).
- Baseline GPT-4o mini monitor accuracy: ~77% on Diamond Vault (77.15%), ~79% on Function Correctness (78.81%); GPT-4o monitor accuracy: ~68% on Diamond Vault (67.97%), ~77% on Function Correctness (77.47%) (Section 5.1, Table 6, Table 9).
- Maximum accuracy change across all selected checkpoints, all incentive conditions: 2.1%, confirming monitorability changes are not confounded by accuracy changes (Section 4, Section 5.1).
- Length penalty: the only statistically significant effect is ~5% reduction (actual: -4.76 pp) in GPT-4o mini monitor accuracy on Function Correctness; reasoner learns to switch to Chinese reasoning as a reward hack (Section 5.2.1, Table 9).
- At length-penalty step 300 (out-of-threshold checkpoint): monitorability drops over 10% from baseline, but reasoner accuracy also decreases ~7%, confounding interpretation (Section 5.2.1).
- KL divergence penalization: GPT-4o monitor accuracy increases 5% on Diamond Vault (+5.22 pp, Bonferroni-significant); changes on Function Correctness are 1-4% and not Bonferroni-significant (Section 5.2.2, Table 6, Table 9).
- Adversarial penalization of GPT-4o mini monitor accuracy: statistically significant 7-13 pp decrease in GPT-4o mini monitor accuracy on both datasets (Diamond Vault: -7.68 pp; Function Correctness: -12.67 pp; both p<0.001, Bonferroni-significant) (Section 5.3.2, Table 6, Table 9).
- Adversarial penalization partially generalizes to GPT-4o (Diamond Vault: -5.22 pp, p<0.001; Function Correctness: -6.13 pp, p<0.001), but shows no significant degradation under Claude Sonnet 4.5 on Diamond Vault despite similar GPT-4o baseline accuracy (Section 5.3.2, Figure 9, Table 6, Table 9).
- Rewarding KL divergence: single significant effect is GPT-4o mini drops ~5% (-5.09 pp, p<0.001, Bonferroni-significant) on Function Correctness; at checkpoint 490, KL reward is high and accuracy mostly maintained but both monitors show substantial monitorability drop (Section 5.3.1, Table 9, Figure 7a).
- Direct monitorability reward: at most ~3% increase under GPT-4o mini on Diamond Vault (+2.85 pp, p<0.001, Bonferroni-significant); effect does not generalize to GPT-4o (DV: +1.79 pp, p=0.131); Function Correctness shows ~1-2% decrease (actual: -2.39 pp, not significant) (Section 5.4, Table 6, Table 9).
- Function Correctness baseline: GPT-4o mini monitorability ~95% on examples where reasoner makes all correct predictions vs ~45% on examples where reasoner makes one or more mistakes (Section 5.4).
- Hardware and cost: Nvidia H200 GPUs; base task training ~$250/run (~16 hours at $16/hour); additional incentive phase ~$110-150/run (7-9 hours at same rate) (Section 4.2).

## Relevance to experiment

Three connections to the Epistemic Humility experiment design:

Phase 1 SFT/DPO/KTO arms are unlikely to degrade CoT monitorability unless one of the training arms is explicitly adversarial toward a monitor signal. The paper's null result for common incentives (length penalties, KL regularization) establishes a boundary condition: ordinary RL fine-tuning pressures do not consistently suppress latent-variable information in CoT. This supports using CoT monitoring as a complementary read-out on Phase 1 checkpoints without expecting training-induced suppression as a confound.

The latent-variable monitor accuracy metric is a direct model for a complementary evaluation layer on Phase 1 outputs. If Phase 1 tasks have a latent ground-truth variable (e.g., whether a knowledge claim is within the model's knowledge boundary), the same GPT-4o mini zero-shot monitor setup could score whether each training arm's CoT expresses that variable, providing a faithfulness signal alongside the standard abstention and calibration metrics.

The non-generalization of adversarial degradation across monitor families (GPT-4o mini degradation not transferring to Claude Sonnet 4.5) is a direct caution for Phase 3 single-probe designs. If Phase 3 uses a single activation probe or a single monitor family to evaluate internal uncertainty signal, the probe result may be monitor-specific rather than a property of the reasoner's CoT. Evaluating with at least two monitors from different families would provide a stronger generalization test. See also `estimator-divergence-invalidates-single-probe-faithfulness` and `p-ik-ood-generalization-gap`.
