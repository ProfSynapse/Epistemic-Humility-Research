## Abstract

Deploying LLMs in real-world applications requires controllable output that satisfies multiple desiderata at the same time. While existing work extensively addresses LLM steering for a single behavior, \textit{compositional steering} -- i.e., steering LLMs simultaneously towards multiple behaviors -- remains an underexplored problem. In this work, we propose \emph{compositional steering tokens} for multi-behavior steering. We first embed individual behaviors, expressed as natural language instructions, into dedicated tokens via self-distillation. Contrary to most prior work, which operates in the activation space, our behavior steers live in the space of input tokens, enabling more effective zero-shot composition. We then train a dedicated \textit{composition token} on pairs of behaviors and show that it successfully captures the notion of composition: it generalizes well to \textit{unseen} compositions, including those with unseen behaviors as well as those with an unseen \textit{number} of behaviors. Our experiments across different LLM architectures show that steering tokens lead to superior multi-behavior steering of verifiable constraints (e.g., length, format, structure, language) compared to competing approaches (instructions, activation steering, and LoRA merging). Moreover, we show that steering tokens complement natural language instructions, with their combination resulting in further gains.

## Claims

- Compositional steering tokens outperform text instructions on unseen behavior compositions by +5.1% for 2-behavior (76.9% vs 71.8%) and +5.5% for 3-behavior (59.5% vs 54.0%) while performing comparably on seen compositions on Qwen3-8B (Table 1 (Qwen3-8B, Composition <and> vs. Instruction Steering rows)) [[compositional-steering-tokens]]
- Orthogonality regularization is critical for compositional generalization: the 'and' embedding initialized without orthogonality achieves only 55.2% on unseen combinations, improving by +15.6% to 70.8% with the constraint; zero-vector initialization with orthogonality provides the best accuracy-variance tradeoff (69.1%, 6.2% order variance) (Table 4 (Qwen3-4B, <and> token ablation study)) [[orthogonality-regularization-steering]]
- The Hybrid combination of compositional token steering and natural language instructions achieves best overall performance across all model families, achieving 62.9% on unseen 3-behavior compositions (Qwen3-8B) and rescuing weaker models with +12.7% gain on Llama3-8B 3-behavior compositions over instruction baseline alone (Tables 1 and 2 (Hybrid rows across model families)) [[compositional-steering-tokens]]
- Activation-space approaches (CAA, LM-Steer) completely collapse on multi-behavior compositional steering, and parameter-space LoRA DARE shows poor compositional generalization (44.8% vs 76.9% for unseen 2-behavior on Qwen3-8B), confirming input-space token steering as the superior compositional paradigm (Table 1 (Qwen3-8B, baseline rows); §5.1) [[activation-steering]]
