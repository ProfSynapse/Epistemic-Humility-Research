## Abstract

Hallucinations in multimodal large language models (MLLMs), where the model
generates content inconsistent with the input image, pose significant risks in
real-world applications, from misinformation in visual question answering to
unsafe errors in decision-making. Existing benchmarks primarily test recognition
accuracy, i.e. whether models can select the correct answer among distractors.
This overlooks an equally critical capability for trustworthy AI: recognizing
when none of the provided options are correct, a behavior reflecting epistemic
humility. We present HumbleBench, a new hallucination benchmark designed to
evaluate MLLMs' ability to reject plausible but incorrect answers across three
hallucination types: object, relation, and attribute. Built from a panoptic
scene graph dataset, we leverage fine-grained scene graph annotations to extract
ground-truth entities and relations, and prompt GPT-4-Turbo to generate
multiple-choice questions, followed by a rigorous manual filtering process. Each
question includes a "None of the above" option, requiring models not only to
recognize correct visual information but also to identify when no provided answer
is valid. We evaluate a variety of state-of-the-art MLLMs, including both
general-purpose and specialized reasoning models, and report findings on their
ability to express epistemic humility.

## Summary

HumbleBench reframes multimodal hallucination evaluation around *abstention under
forced choice*: a model must reject all four plausible-but-wrong options and
select "None of the above" (NOTA) when no listed answer matches the image. The
benchmark holds 22,831 five-way multiple-choice questions spanning three
fine-grained hallucination types (object, relation, attribute), generated from
the Panoptic Scene Graph (PSG) dataset's dense annotations via a GPT-4-Turbo
pipeline (with InstructBLIP in the loop) and then manually filtered. Headline
result: leading MLLMs clear the random-guess baseline on standard items but
collapse on the NOTA-only and pure-noise-image cases, with several models
dropping below chance, exposing a strong bias to always commit to a listed
option. Reasoning tuning does not reliably help, and scale alone is not the fix.

## Relevance to experiment

HumbleBench is the multimodal counterpart to the abstention/NOTA framing the
Phase 1 SFT-vs-DPO-vs-KTO study uses in the text-only setting. Its core finding,
that models overcommit and fail to select NOTA even when every option is wrong,
is the visual analog of the over-confidence / under-abstention failure the
training-method comparison targets. The reasoning-tuning regression it documents
corroborates the existing
[[reasoning-finetuning-degrades-abstention]] mechanism in a new modality, and its
scale-insufficiency result reinforces that abstention behavior is governed by
training design rather than parameter count. Useful as an out-of-domain
robustness reference and as a NOTA-style evaluation template.

## Claims

- Leading MLLMs exceed random-guess accuracy on standard recognition items but
  collapse when the only correct answer is "None of the above," with several
  models falling below chance, revealing a strong bias to commit to a listed
  option. (Abstract; project-page analysis) [[epistemic-humility]]
- Reasoning fine-tuning does not guarantee better epistemic humility: R1-Onevision,
  fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-VL to enhance reasoning, scores 66.89% overall vs 72.20%
  for its base model, and reasoning-tuned variants help inconsistently across the
  board. (Project-page error analysis) [[reasoning-finetuning-degrades-abstention]]
- Model scale alone is insufficient for robustness: a 4B Visionary-R1 outperforms
  several much larger reasoning models, and a 5B Phi-4 surpasses a 12B Pixtral,
  indicating training and adaptation design matters more than parameter count.
  (Project page) [[model-scale-insufficient-for-epistemic-humility]]
- Models overcommit most severely on NOTA-only items and on pure-noise images,
  selecting a confident wrong option rather than abstaining when the image carries
  no supporting evidence. (Project page) [[answer-commitment-bias-undermines-epistemic-humility]]
