## Abstract

Hallucinations in multimodal large language models (MLLMs), where the model
generates content inconsistent with the input image, pose significant risks in
real-world applications, from misinformation in visual question answering to
unsafe errors in decision-making. Existing benchmarks primarily test recognition
accuracy, i.e., evaluating whether models can select the correct answer among
distractors. This overlooks another important capability for trustworthy AI:
recognizing when none of the provided options is supported by the image and
abstaining from committing to a false choice, a humility-related behavior. We
present HumbleBench, a new hallucination benchmark designed to evaluate
false-option rejection in MLLMs under a forced-choice multiple-choice setting
with a "None of the above" option. Built from a panoptic scene graph dataset, we
leverage fine-grained scene graph annotations for objects and relations, use
candidate attribute cues, and prompt GPT-4-Turbo to generate multiple-choice
questions, followed by a rigorous manual filtering process. Each question
includes a "None of the above" option, requiring models not only to recognize
correct visual information but also to identify when no provided answer is valid.
We evaluate a variety of state-of-the-art MLLMs, including general-purpose,
specialized reasoning, and proprietary models, on HumbleBench and report
empirical findings for the community. By incorporating explicit false-option
rejection, HumbleBench fills a key gap in current evaluation suites by assessing
a narrower but important abstention-oriented behavior that is relevant to
trustworthy multimodal reasoning.

## Summary

HumbleBench reframes multimodal hallucination evaluation around *false-option
rejection*: a five-way multiple-choice task in which one option is always "None of
the above" (NOTA), so a model must reject all four plausible-but-wrong options and
pick NOTA when no listed answer is supported by the image. It is deliberately
scoped: the authors stress it is not a measure of calibrated uncertainty or
principled refusal in general, only the one directly observable facet of
[[epistemic-humility]] of rejecting unsupported options. The benchmark holds
22,831 questions over three hallucination types (object, relation, attribute),
built from the [[panoptic-scene-graph]] (PSG) dataset: object/relation labels come
from PSG annotations, attribute cues from InstructBLIP (Vicuna-7B), questions and
distractors from GPT-4-Turbo, then a heavy manual filter (41,843 candidates ->
22,831 final). Twenty-one MLLMs are evaluated across six settings. Headline: even
the best models are far from solved, and standard accuracy hides the abstention
failure, exposed by the novel [[humility-score]] and the NOTA-only stress test.

## Relevance to experiment

HumbleBench is the multimodal, forced-choice counterpart to the text-only
abstention/NOTA framing in the Phase 1 SFT-vs-DPO-vs-KTO study. Its central
finding, that models overcommit and fail to select NOTA even when every option is
wrong (NOTA-only near random while humans hit 92%), is the visual analog of the
under-abstention failure the training-method comparison targets. Two transferable
design ideas: (1) the [[humility-score]] (balanced NOTA-detection accuracy) as a
single number that separates recognition from rejection, since plain accuracy
hides the failure; (2) the cautious-prompt result that prompting trades
recognition accuracy for humility, mirroring the over-abstention tradeoff. The
reasoning-tuning and scale results are cautionary: both are entangled with design,
so neither is a reliable lever for abstention.

## Claims

- Standard accuracy hides false-option-rejection failures: high non-NOTA accuracy
  coexists with low NOTA hit rate (Qwen2.5-VL 78.98% non-NOTA but 32.33% NOTA
  hit), so accuracy alone cannot evaluate abstention-oriented behavior. (Table 4)
  [[answer-commitment-bias-undermines-epistemic-humility]]
- In the NOTA-only stress setting, where NOTA is the correct answer for every
  item, models average only 26.61% (near random) while a human annotator scores
  92.00%, confirming models over-commit to a concrete option rather than
  rejecting. (Table 6, Table 8) [[false-option-rejection]]
- Destroying visual evidence with a Gaussian-noise image collapses average overall
  accuracy to 27.58% (22.53% with forced-grounding); models fabricate answers
  from language priors (e.g. Qwen2.5-VL predicts the sky is "grey" on noise),
  revealing weak visual faithfulness. (Table 6, Section 3.3)
  [[language-priors-override-absent-visual-evidence]]
- Reasoning-oriented fine-tuning does not reliably improve hallucination
  robustness: R1-Onevision regresses below its base (67.06% vs 72.20%) while
  LLaVA-CoT improves over its base (67.70% vs 65.31%); the effect depends on
  training strategy and data. (Section 3.1)
  [[reasoning-finetuning-degrades-abstention]]
- Parameter count alone does not explain rankings and is confounded with
  architecture, data, encoders, and post-training: 4B Visionary-R1 beats larger
  reasoning models and 5B Phi-4 beats 12B Pixtral, so the authors decline a
  scaling-law reading. (Section 3.1) [[model-scale-confounded-with-design]]
