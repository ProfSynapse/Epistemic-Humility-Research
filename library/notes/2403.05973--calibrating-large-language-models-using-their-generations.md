## Abstract

As large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in user-facing applications, building trust and maintaining safety by accurately quantifying a model's confidence in its prediction becomes even more important. However, finding effective ways to calibrate LLMs - especially when the only interface to the models is their generated text - remains a challenge. We propose APRICOT (auxiliary prediction of confidence targets): A method to set confidence targets and train an additional model that predicts an LLM's confidence based on its textual input and output alone. This approach has several advantages: It is conceptually simple, does not require access to the target model beyond its output, does not interfere with the language generation, and has a multitude of potential usages, for instance by verbalizing the predicted confidence or adjusting the given answer based on the confidence. We show how our approach performs competitively in terms of calibration error for white-box and black-box LLMs on closed-book question-answering to detect incorrect LLM answers.

## Summary

APRICOT (Auxiliary Prediction of Confidence Targets; Ulmer et al., 2024) proposes training a lightweight auxiliary model on a target LLM's input questions and generated answer text to predict that LLM's confidence, requiring no access to internal states, token probabilities, or model weights. The core innovation is replacing binary correct/incorrect labels with cluster-derived calibration targets: questions are embedded with Sentence-BERT, normalized, and clustered via HDBSCAN, and the cluster-wise LLM accuracy then serves as a soft regression target for a DeBERTaV3 auxiliary calibrator trained by mean squared error. This pipeline is applicable to both white-box models (where sequence likelihood is also available) and black-box APIs (where only generated text is available). Evaluated on Vicuna v1.5 7B and GPT-3.5 on TriviaQA and CoQA, APRICOT achieves the highest misprediction AUROC in all four settings and competitive Brier scores. Clustering-derived targets outperform or match simple binary targets across all four model-dataset conditions, per Section 4.2 prose. Verbalized confidence baselines sometimes achieve lower ECE -- reaching 0.00 for GPT-3.5 Verbalized % (CoT) on TriviaQA -- but at the cost of near-random misprediction AUROC and, for 7B Vicuna, severe format inconsistency (19% success rate for qualitative expression). An input ablation shows the calibrator performs respectably from question text alone, implicating question-type difficulty as a learned latent signal.

- APRICOT auxiliary clustering misprediction AUROC 0.83 +/- 0.01 on Vicuna v1.5 TriviaQA; sequence-likelihood baseline AUROC 0.79 +/- 0.01 on the same setting (Table 3).
- APRICOT auxiliary clustering misprediction AUROC 0.82 +/- 0.01 on Vicuna v1.5 CoQA; 0.72 +/- 0.02 on GPT-3.5 TriviaQA; 0.81 +/- 0.01 on GPT-3.5 CoQA (Table 3).
- APRICOT auxiliary clustering Brier score 0.18 +/- 0.00 on Vicuna v1.5 TriviaQA; sequence-likelihood Brier 0.22 +/- 0.01 on the same setting (Table 3).
- Verbalized Qual. achieves ECE 0.02 and smECE 0.02 for Vicuna v1.5 TriviaQA but misprediction AUROC only 0.62 +/- 0.03 at 19% format-consistency (model fails to generate the desired confidence expression 81% of the time) (Table 3, Section 4.2).
- Within-cluster semantic cosine similarity 0.60 +/- 0.14 on TriviaQA and 0.70 +/- 0.17 on CoQA; unrelated random-pair baseline approximately 0.00 on both datasets (Table 2).
- Vicuna v1.5 7B task accuracy 58% on TriviaQA and 44% on CoQA; GPT-3.5 task accuracy 85% on TriviaQA and 55% on CoQA (Section 4.2).
- GPT-3.5 Verbalized % (CoT) achieves ECE 0.00 on TriviaQA, the actual calibration floor across all verbalized baselines, though its misprediction AUROC remains 0.63 +/- 0.02 (Table 3).

## Relevance to experiment

APRICOT is the closest published antecedent to the locked training-regimen auxiliary-head approach. Three connections bear on the experiment. First, the 0.83 AUROC for APRICOT on Vicuna TriviaQA provides a project-level reference point for external-readout approaches relying only on generated text (this is a team inference, not a paper claim about an absolute ceiling); a locked training-regimen aux-head engine targeting internal representations should approach or exceed this if the latent answerability axis carries richer signal than generated text, as Amendments O and P found (AUROC 0.997 and 0.983 respectively). Second, the finding that verbalized confidence achieves low ECE (as low as 0.00 for GPT-3.5) but near-random misprediction AUROC (0.62 for Vicuna, 0.48-0.63 for GPT-3.5 qualitative) concretely grounds the locked training-regimen argument that emitted confidence expressions are decoupled from the model's discriminative uncertainty: passing ECE targets while failing AUROC targets is exactly the failure mode the locked training-regimen probe-based readout avoids. Third, the question-text-only ablation (Section 4.3) shows that difficulty can be read from the question alone before any answer is generated, which matches the locked training-regimen hypothesis that answerability is encoded in the residual stream prior to generation and supports the amendment R token-faithfulness framing.

- Evidence label: experiment. Trained only on the target LLM's input questions and
  generated-answer text (no internals), APRICOT attains the highest misprediction
  AUROC in all settings and among the lowest Brier scores and calibration errors
  for both Vicuna v1.5 7B (white-box) and GPT-3.5 (black-box) on TriviaQA and CoQA.
  (library/pdfs/2403.05973.pdf, Sec 4.2, Table 3) [[auxiliary-model-predicts-llm-confidence-from-generations-alone]]
- Evidence label: ablation. Fine-grained clustering-derived calibration targets
  outperform simple binary correct/incorrect targets for both models on both
  datasets; the clusters are semantically coherent (within-cluster cosine .60/.70
  vs ~.00 for random pairs). (library/pdfs/2403.05973.pdf, Tables 2-3) [[clustering-question-embeddings-yields-graded-calibration-targets]]
- Evidence label: analysis. Verbalized-confidence baselines sometimes reach lower
  ECE but at the cost of higher worst-case calibration error, near-random
  misprediction AUROC, and low-diversity scores; APRICOT is more consistent and
  discriminative of wrong answers. (library/pdfs/2403.05973.pdf, Sec 4.2, Fig 4) [[verbalized-confidence]]
- Evidence label: analysis. An input-ablation study shows the calibrator already
  performs respectably from the question alone (inferring difficulty by question
  type), with further gains from adding the answer and chain-of-thought.
