## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across diverse tasks, yet they remain prone to generating hallucinations. Detecting these hallucinations is critical for deploying LLMs reliably in high-stakes applications. We present Grad Detect, a gradient-based approach for predicting hallucinations by analyzing layer-wise gradient patterns from a single forward-backward pass during inference. Our method shows that the internal gradient structure of a model carries rich information about the correctness of its output. This information is not accessible through output-level signals alone. We evaluate Grad Detect on several Q&A benchmarks across both hallucination detection and model abstention prediction, where it consistently outperforms confidence-based and sampling-based baselines. Through comprehensive layer ablation studies across all eleven models from four architectural families, we find that the final five layers concentrate over 97% of the discriminative gradient signal, enabling efficient deployment with minimal performance loss. Grad Detect provides a unified framework for predicting multiple dimensions of LLM reliability, offering strong predictive performance alongside interpretable insights into where and how model failures originate.

## Claims

- Evidence label: abstract-backed method. Grad Detect predicts hallucinations
  and model abstention from layer-wise gradient patterns captured in a single
  forward-backward pass during inference, a signal the authors argue is not
  accessible from output-level confidence alone.
- Evidence label: benchmark comparison. Across several Q&A benchmarks (TriviaQA,
  SciQ, PopQA, TruthfulQA), Grad Detect consistently outperforms confidence-based
  and sampling-based baselines on both hallucination detection and abstention
  prediction.
- Evidence label: layer ablation. Across all eleven instruction-tuned models
  from four families (Qwen 1.5B-7B, Falcon 1B-10B, Gemma 1B-12B, SmolLM3 3B), the
  final five layers concentrate over 97% of the discriminative gradient signal,
  enabling efficient deployment with minimal performance loss.

## Summary

Grad Detect treats the model's own backward pass as the signal. For a generated
answer it computes a loss, backpropagates once, and turns the per-layer gradient
statistics into features for a lightweight classifier that predicts two things:
whether the answer is a hallucination, and whether the model should abstain. The
claim is that gradients expose correctness structure that output-level signals
(token probability, sampled agreement, verbalized confidence) miss, and that the
signal is spatially concentrated: the last five layers hold over 97% of it, so
the detector stays cheap. Validation spans eleven instruction-tuned models from
four families (Qwen 1.5B-7B, Falcon 1B-10B, Gemma 1B-12B, SmolLM3 3B) on four
Q&A benchmarks (TriviaQA, SciQ, PopQA, TruthfulQA) at temperature zero.

## Relevance to experiment

### A detector to attach to the abstention training arms

Grad Detect predicts *model abstention*, which is exactly the behavior the Phase 1
SFT vs DPO vs KTO study trains. That makes it a candidate read-out, not just a
related result: after each training arm, a gradient probe could measure whether
the model's internal "should I abstain" signal sharpened or blurred, and whether
it still tracks ground-truth knowledge boundaries. It slots directly against the
output-side signals already in the graph ([[p-true]], [[verbalized-confidence]],
[[consistency-based-confidence]]), which is the baseline set it reports beating
by 3-8 AUC points.

### A second substrate for the Phase 3 mechanistic work

The Phase 3 mech-interp sweeps inventory hidden-state (activation) candidates.
Grad Detect adds a *gradient*-space candidate and a sharp, falsifiable
localization claim: correctness signal concentrates in the last five layers. If
the activation probes in the existing sweep localize the same way, that is
convergent evidence about where epistemic signal lives; if they do not, the
divergence is itself informative. See
[[final-layers-concentrate-discriminative-gradient-signal]].

### The caveat that makes it a research question, not a drop-in tool

The whole probe family shares a soft spot the graph already documents: does the
probe read *truth*, or familiarity and recall? See `2510.09033`
(probes-read-recall-not-truth), [[p-ik-ood-generalization-gap]], and
[[verbalized-prob-generalizes-logit-overfits-distribution-shift]]. Grad Detect's
evaluation is in-distribution Q&A with a trained classifier, so before treating
"gradients beat confidence" as a deployment-grade honesty signal, the open
question is whether the gradient signal survives distribution shift and
post-training behavior change. That question is what an exploratory experiment
here should target.
