## Abstract

We introduce methods for discovering and applying sparse feature circuits. These are causally implicated subnetworks of human-interpretable features for explaining language model behaviors. Circuits identified in prior work consist of polysemantic and difficult-to-interpret units like attention heads or neurons, rendering them unsuitable for many downstream applications. In contrast, sparse feature circuits enable detailed understanding of unanticipated mechanisms. Because they are based on fine-grained units, sparse feature circuits are useful for downstream tasks: We introduce SHIFT, where we improve the generalization of a classifier by ablating features that a human judges to be task-irrelevant. Finally, we demonstrate an entirely unsupervised and scalable interpretability pipeline by discovering thousands of sparse feature circuits for automatically discovered model behaviors.

## Claims

- Small sparse feature circuits explain the majority of Pythia-70M's subject-verb agreement performance with fewer than 100 nodes, while neuron circuits require approximately 1500 nodes to explain half the performance. (Figure 3, §3.2) [[circuit-faithfulness]]
- SHIFT raises worst-group profession accuracy from 24.4% to 76.0% (89.0% with retraining) on Bias in Bios, while reducing gender accuracy from 87.4% to 54.0%, all without access to disambiguating labeled data; the neuron skyline only achieves 41.5% worst-group accuracy. (Table 2, §4) [[shift-feature-trimming]]
- Human crowdworkers rate SAE sparse features as significantly more interpretable than neurons, and features participating in circuits are more interpretable than randomly sampled features. (§3.2, Appendix D) [[sparse-feature-circuits]]
- An unsupervised pipeline combining activation/gradient clustering with sparse feature circuit discovery automatically identifies thousands of LM behaviors and their underlying feature-level mechanisms, recovering known phenomena such as induction and succession at the feature level. (§5, Figure 5) [[sparse-feature-circuits]]
