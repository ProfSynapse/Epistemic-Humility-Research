## Abstract

We consider the problem of selective prediction (also known as reject option) in deep neural networks, and introduce SelectiveNet, a deep neural architecture with an integrated reject option. Existing rejection mechanisms are based mostly on a threshold over the prediction confidence of a pre-trained network. In contrast, SelectiveNet is trained to optimize both classification (or regression) and rejection simultaneously, end-to-end. The result is a deep neural network that is optimized over the covered domain. In our experiments, we show a consistently improved risk-coverage trade-off over several well-known classification and regression datasets, thus reaching new state-of-the-art results for deep selective classification.

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment. Training a selection head jointly with the predictor
  under a coverage-constrained selective loss achieves lower selective risk at
  fixed coverage than thresholding post-hoc confidence (softmax-response,
  MC-dropout) on CIFAR-10/SVHN and other benchmarks.
  (library/pdfs/1901.09192.pdf) [[joint-selection-head-training-lowers-selective-risk]]
- Evidence label: ablation. An auxiliary prediction head trained on the full data
  distribution prevents the shared representation from overfitting to the easy
  covered subset. (library/pdfs/1901.09192.pdf) [[auxiliary-head-prevents-covered-subset-overfit]]
- Evidence label: analysis. A model trained for its target coverage outperforms
  one trained once and thresholded to that coverage, evidencing coverage-specific
  capacity reallocation. (library/pdfs/1901.09192.pdf) [[coverage-specific-capacity-reallocation]]
