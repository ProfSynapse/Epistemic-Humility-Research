## Abstract

While large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong performance on factoid question answering, they are still prone to hallucination and untruthful responses, particularly when tasks demand information outside their parametric knowledge. Indeed, truthfulness requires more than accuracy -- models must also recognize uncertainty and abstain when unsure to avoid hallucinations. This presents a fundamental challenge for existing methods: approaches that optimize for accuracy often amplify hallucinations, while those that encourage abstention can become overly conservative, sacrificing correct answers. Both extremes ultimately compromise truthfulness. In this work, we present TruthRL, a general reinforcement learning (RL) framework that directly optimizes the truthfulness of LLMs. Specifically, we implement TruthRL using GRPO with a simple yet effective ternary reward that distinguishes correct answers, hallucinations, and abstentions. It incentivizes models to reduce hallucinations not only by providing correct responses, but also by enabling abstention when uncertain, thereby improving truthfulness. Extensive experiments across four knowledge-intensive benchmarks show that TruthRL significantly reduces hallucinations (e.g., 43.5% $\rightarrow$ 19.4%) and improves truthfulness (e.g., 5.3% $\rightarrow$ 37.2%), with consistent gains across various backbone models. Analysis shows that the improvement of TruthRL arises from enhanced capability of LLMs to recognize their knowledge boundary, hence avoiding being overly conservative as the baselines are.

## Summary

TruthRL frames hallucination reduction as a truthfulness optimization problem rather than an accuracy problem and solves it by replacing binary reward signals with a three-way ternary reward inside GRPO. The ternary reward assigns +1 to correct answers, 0 to abstentions, and -1 to hallucinations. Under GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation, a response that abstains accrues higher relative advantage than one that hallucinates, even when both lack a correct answer. This is the mechanism binary RL misses: binary reward collapses abstention and hallucination into the same -1 signal, giving the policy zero gradient to distinguish them. The paper demonstrates this on four knowledge-intensive benchmarks (CRAG, NQ, HotpotQA, MuSiQue) across five backbone sizes (3B to 32B), both with and without retrieval. TruthRL trains on CRAG and generalizes across all four datasets.

The paper's ablation structure makes three sharp design-choice contributions. First, the ternary reward alone outperforms knowledge-enhanced variants: adding out-of-knowledge labels to either binary or ternary reward hurts truthfulness on CRAG relative to the plain ternary baseline (Table 3: ternary T=37.2/H=19.4 vs. knowledge-enhanced ternary T=32.7/H=21.9). The benefit of explicit knowledge labeling is real but not additive with the ternary signal. Second, online RL via GRPO dominates iterative DPO (best at iteration 3: T=28.0/H=29.5) and single-pass DPO (T=6.8/H=43.7), with iterative DPO degenerating by iteration 4. Third, a string-match (rule-based) verifier causes training collapse into near-total abstention (T=-3.6/H=3.6), because exact-string mismatches misclassify correct responses as wrong, starving the policy of genuine reward signal. An LLM-based verifier that handles semantic equivalence is required.

Knowledge-boundary probing (sampling 256 responses per question, labeling a question out-of-knowledge if none is correct) is used to construct stronger SFT and RL baselines, but TruthRL without this labeling already outperforms all knowledge-enhanced variants, showing the ternary reward implicitly teaches boundary recognition. Analysis on difficult CRAG questions confirms this: TruthRL abstains on 84.5% of hard questions with only 15.5% hallucination, while SFT and TruthRL_Binary reach near-100% hallucination on the same subset (Section 4.3, Figure 3e).

## Relevance to experiment

TruthRL is the most direct prior art for the Phase 1 experiment matrix. Three connections are operative rather than speculative.

First, the binary-vs-ternary reward comparison (Table 3) is a controlled ablation of exactly the question Phase 1 asks about SFT, DPO, and KTO arms: do accuracy-oriented objectives suppress abstention? TruthRL_Binary (vanilla RL) reaches T=4.5/H=47.7 on the 4-benchmark With Retrieval average while TruthRL reaches T=25.6/H=18.8. This is direct evidence that a binary training signal systemically suppresses the model's capacity to abstain, which the existing mechanism `binary-grading-reinforces-hallucination` captures in a benchmark-evaluation framing and TruthRL now confirms in a training-signal framing.

Second, Table 4 shows online GRPO outperforming both single-pass DPO and iterative DPO on every benchmark, with iterative DPO degenerating after iteration 3. This is evidence for the existing mechanism `online-rl-outperforms-offline-rl` and provides an adversarial prediction for the Phase 1 DPO arm: even iteratively refreshed offline preference optimization is unlikely to match online RL for abstention-hallucination calibration.

Third, the R-Tuning comparison (Table 1) shows that the existing SFT-based abstention-training approach (knowledge boundary probing plus IDK relabeling) reaches T=15.2/H=33.1 on CRAG with retrieval versus TruthRL's T=37.2/H=19.4. This bounds the expected gain from the idk-sft / answer-relabeling arm of Phase 1 and gives a calibration target.

For mechanistic interpretability, the finding that TruthRL converts hard questions into abstentions (84.5% uncertainty rate on difficult questions) predicts a qualitative shift in the model's internal activation geometry on those questions. If truthfulness training sharpens the knowledge-boundary representation, activation probes should be more separable after TruthRL than after SFT or DPO, a testable prediction against the hidden-state candidate inventory.

The rule-based verifier collapse (Table 5) is also a practical guardrail: any Phase 1 reward or evaluation pipeline that uses string matching for answer verification risks the same training pathology.

## Claims

- A ternary reward (+1 correct, 0 abstain, -1 hallucinate) under GRPO allows the policy to distinguish abstention from hallucination via group-relative advantage, while a binary reward assigns both the same -1 signal and gives zero gradient for that distinction. (Section 3.2 (reward design); Table 3) [[ternary-reward-enables-abstention-over-hallucination]]
- TruthRL without knowledge-boundary labeling achieves higher truthfulness on CRAG (T=37.2) than every knowledge-enhanced variant (binary+KE T=27.4; ternary+KE T=32.7), showing the ternary reward implicitly learns boundary recognition without requiring OOK annotation. (Table 3) [[truthrl]]
- Online RL (TruthRL/GRPO) achieves CRAG T=37.2/H=19.4 versus Iterative DPO best-iteration (Iter 3) T=28.0/H=29.5 and single-pass DPO T=6.8/H=43.7, with iterative DPO degrading at Iter 4, confirming that repeated offline preference data refreshes cannot substitute for on-policy exploration. (Table 4; Section 4.4) [[online-rl-outperforms-offline-rl]]
- A string-match (rule-based) verifier during GRPO training produces T=-3.6/H=3.6, collapsing the model into near-total over-abstention because exact-string mismatches misclassify semantically correct responses as wrong and starve the policy of positive reward signal. (Table 5; Section 4.5) [[rule-based-verifier-causes-training-collapse]]
- On difficult CRAG questions where almost no method answers correctly, TruthRL produces 15.5% hallucination and 84.5% uncertainty, while SFT and TruthRL_Binary approach 100% hallucination, demonstrating that truthfulness-driven RL teaches genuine knowledge-boundary recognition rather than blanket conservatism. (Section 4.3; Figure 3e) [[knowledge-boundary]]
