## Abstract

Explicit reasoning models are trained to produce intermediate reasoning traces before final answers, but downstream fine-tuning is often performed on ordinary instruction-response data that contains no such traces. We show that this mismatch can induce reasoning-trace collapse: a fine-tuned model continues to produce plausible final answers while losing the structurally valid explicit reasoning traces that made it a reasoning model in the first place. We introduce a structural evaluation framework that separates answer correctness from reasoning-trace validity, measuring valid, empty, missing, and truncated reasoning alongside reasoning-conditioned task performance. Using this framework, we study four open-weight reasoning models and find that standard supervised fine-tuning can rapidly suppress valid reasoning traces, and that answer-only metrics can substantially obscure this failure: in several settings, performance conditional on valid reasoning remains high while the rate of valid reasoning falls sharply. We further show that simple loss-masking strategies can substantially mitigate collapse without requiring teacher-generated reasoning traces. These results suggest that evaluations of fine-tuned reasoning models should report structural reasoning reliability metrics in addition to final-answer performance, especially when adaptation data does not contain explicit reasoning traces.

## Summary

Twist et al. introduce reasoning-trace collapse, a structural failure mode in which explicit reasoning models lose the ability to produce complete, non-empty reasoning traces during downstream fine-tuning on ordinary instruction-response data, even when final-answer performance is partially preserved or continues to improve. The paper defines three types of invalid reasoning (empty, missing, truncated) and proposes a framework of structural metrics: valid reasoning rate (VR), the complementary rates ER/MR/TR, and reasoning-conditioned pass@1 (Rpass@1) that measures accuracy only over responses containing a valid trace. These metrics are implemented in ThinkPack, a lightweight open-source Python library providing model-agnostic chat construction, trace parsing, metric computation, and loss masking across HuggingFace transformers models.

Four small open-weight reasoning models are fine-tuned on the Chemistry L-3 subset of SciKnowEval (a domain with no explicit reasoning traces in the training data) and evaluated throughout training on Chemistry (in-domain), GSM8K (math), and EvalPlus (code). The central finding is that standard supervised fine-tuning can rapidly suppress valid reasoning traces while answer-only metrics continue to improve, and that the severity and form of collapse depend heavily on both the model and the format used to represent missing reasoning (empty-think vs. no-think). Qwen3-8B and Nemotron-7B collapse to 0% Chemistry VR under both formats while pass@1 rises to 50-57%; Llama-R1-8B collapses to 0% under no-think but preserves 71% VR under empty-think; OLMo-3-7B degrades more gradually to 58-59% Chemistry VR, primarily through truncation rather than empty or missing traces.

Two loss-masking strategies (masked-think, which excludes the empty reasoning region from the loss, and response-only, which masks both prompt and reasoning) can substantially preserve VR without requiring teacher-generated traces. A distillation baseline using GPT-5-mini-generated reasoning traces is highly effective for Qwen3-8B and Llama-R1-8B, but fails for OLMo-3-7B where final GSM8K VR collapses to 21% and Chemistry VR collapses to 22% despite teacher traces. The key normative conclusion is that evaluations of fine-tuned reasoning models must report structural reasoning reliability metrics alongside final-answer performance.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper is directly applicable to the locked training-regimen study. The locked training-regimen study fine-tunes Qwen3-class models on abstention or uncertainty data. The paper shows that fine-tuning Qwen3-8B on domain data without explicit reasoning traces collapses Chemistry VR to 0% even as pass@1 improves, and that GSM8K VR falls to 58% with Rpass@1 staying at 98%, meaning the model answers correctly when it reasons but reasons less and less often. That is the exact structural failure mode that would be invisible to the ECE and abstention metrics the locked training-regimen study currently tracks.

Three concrete actions follow. First, the ThinkPack VR metric should be logged alongside ECE and abstention rate in every locked training-regimen evaluation checkpoint. If the abstention training data lacks explicit reasoning traces, VR could collapse even as the model appears to improve on calibration benchmarks. Second, the masked-think and response-only masking strategies tested here provide a lightweight mitigation path that does not require teacher trace generation. If ThinkPack VR shows collapse in any locked training-regimen arm, masked-think is an immediate intervention that requires no new data. Third, OLMo-3-7B is the outlier where distillation fails and masking only partially helps, in contrast to Qwen3-8B and Llama-R1-8B where masking reliably preserves reasoning structure. If OLMo-style models enter future experiment arms, mitigation cannot be assumed to transfer.

For mechanism work (mechanistic interpretability), the paper adds a pre-measurement obligation: a model whose reasoning traces have already collapsed to 0% VR is structurally different from a model with intact traces, and the hidden-state distributions at inference time will reflect that difference. Any mechanism program probe comparing pre- and post-fine-tuning activations should check VR first, because a collapsed-trace model may produce systematically different activation patterns regardless of the knowledge boundary being probed.

## Claims

- Standard supervised fine-tuning on instruction-response data without explicit reasoning traces collapses valid reasoning rate (VR) to 0% in Qwen3-8B and Nemotron-7B on in-domain Chemistry while pass@1 improves to 50-57%, making answer-only metrics insufficient to detect the structural failure. (Section 5.2, Figure 1) [[sft-suppresses-honesty-expression]]
- The representation of missing reasoning in training data (empty-think vs. no-think format) is a primary driver of how much VR is preserved: Llama-R1-8B retains 71% Chemistry VR under empty-think but collapses to 0% under no-think, and model-default formatting is not reliably protective. (Section 5.2, Figure 1) [[format-induced-reasoning-trace-collapse]]
- Loss masking (masked-think or response-only) substantially preserves VR without teacher-generated traces, but effectiveness is model-dependent: masking works well for Qwen3-8B, Nemotron-7B, and Llama-R1-8B yet only partially for OLMo-3-7B, whose collapse is dominated by truncation rather than empty or missing reasoning. (Section 5.3, Figure 2) [[loss-masking-preserves-reasoning-traces]]
- Teacher distillation (GPT-5-mini traces) is highly effective for Qwen3-8B and Llama-R1-8B (near 100% VR) but fails for OLMo-3-7B, where final GSM8K VR collapses to 21% and Chemistry VR to 22%, showing that neither masking nor teacher traces guarantee preservation across all model families. (Section 5.3, Figure 2) [[loss-masking-preserves-reasoning-traces]]
- When VR collapses while Rpass@1 remains high (e.g., Qwen3-8B GSM8K: VR 58%, Rpass@1 98%), the model reasons effectively when it does produce a valid trace but increasingly fails to produce one; this is the characteristic signature distinguishing reasoning-trace collapse from general reasoning quality degradation. (Section 5.2, Section 3) [[reasoning-trace-collapse]]
