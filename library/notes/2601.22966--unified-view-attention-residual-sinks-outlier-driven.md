## Abstract

We investigate the functional role of emergent outliers in large language models, specifically attention sinks (a few tokens that consistently receive large attention logits) and residual sinks (a few fixed dimensions with persistently large activations across most tokens). We hypothesize that these outliers, in conjunction with the corresponding normalizations (\textit{e.g.}, softmax attention and RMSNorm), effectively rescale other non-outlier components. We term this phenomenon \textit{outlier-driven rescaling} and validate this hypothesis across different model architectures and training token counts. This view unifies the origin and mitigation of both sink types. Our main conclusions and observations include: (1) Outliers function jointly with normalization: removing normalization eliminates the corresponding outliers but degrades training stability and performance; directly clipping outliers while retaining normalization leads to degradation, indicating that outlier-driven rescaling contributes to training stability. (2) Outliers serve more as rescale factors rather than contributors, as the final contributions of attention and residual sinks are significantly smaller than those of non-outliers. (3) Outliers can be absorbed into learnable parameters or mitigated via explicit gated rescaling, leading to improved training performance (average gain of 2 points) and enhanced quantization robustness (1.2 points degradation under W4A4 quantization).

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment (ablation, Table 1 rows 1 & 6-9, Sec 3.1). Replacing
  RMSNorm with the pointwise Dynamic Tanh (DyT) function removes outliers (peak
  activation drops to 73) but breaks outlier-driven rescaling: DyT only
  converges at a much lower learning rate and its best final loss is +0.259
  worse than the RMSNorm baseline; adding an explicit gate after DyT
  (GatedDyT) narrows the gap to +0.018.
- Evidence label: experiment (ablation, Table 1 rows 10-14, Sec 3.2). Directly
  clipping residual activations while retaining normalization causes
  divergence at thresholds of 10-100 in the full baseline; once Gated
  Attention (GA) supplies explicit rescaling in the attention module, even
  aggressive clipping to 10 converges but still costs +0.003 loss, while
  clipping to 1000 with GA costs only +0.001, showing residual sinks
  contribute meaningfully to performance beyond attention-side rescaling.
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 2 / Sec 4, MoE-7B-A2B and MoE-24B-A3B
  models). GatedNorm reduces residual-sink magnitude while improving accuracy
  over the GA-only baseline by an average of about 2 points (+1.0 on
  knowledge tasks, >+2.0 on STEM/Code), and shows the smallest degradation
  under aggressive W4A4 quantization (-1.23 points) versus GA-only (-1.50) or
  PreAffine (-2.76), with MGSM degradation kept under 5 points versus ~10
  points for the other methods.
- Evidence label: observation (cross-model comparison, Sec 2 / Fig 1). In
  GPT-OSS, a learnable sink token eliminates both attention sinks and massive
  activations from real input tokens' residual stream, but fixed-dimension
  residual sinks persist regardless -- indicating residual sinks are a
  distinct outlier phenomenon from massive activations/attention sinks even
  though all three arise from the same outlier-driven-rescaling logic.
