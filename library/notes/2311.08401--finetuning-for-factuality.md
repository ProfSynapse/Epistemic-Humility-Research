## Abstract

The fluency and creativity of large pre-trained language models (LLMs) have led to their widespread use, sometimes even as a replacement for traditional search engines. Yet language models are prone to making convincing but factually inaccurate claims, often referred to as 'hallucinations.' These errors can inadvertently spread misinformation or harmfully perpetuate misconceptions. Further, manual fact-checking of model responses is a time-consuming process, making human factuality labels expensive to acquire. In this work, we fine-tune language models to be more factual, without human labeling and targeting more open-ended generation settings than past work. We leverage two key recent innovations in NLP to do so. First, several recent works have proposed methods for judging the factuality of open-ended text by measuring consistency with an external knowledge base or simply a large model's confidence scores. Second, the direct preference optimization algorithm enables straightforward fine-tuning of language models on objectives other than supervised imitation, using a preference ranking over possible model responses. We show that learning from automatically generated factuality preference rankings, generated either through existing retrieval systems or our novel retrieval-free approach, significantly improves the factuality (percent of generated claims that are correct) of Llama-2 on held-out topics compared with RLHF or decoding strategies targeted at factuality. At 7B scale, compared to Llama-2-chat, we observe 58% and 40% reduction in factual error rate when generating biographies and answering medical questions, respectively.

## Summary

Tian et al. (2023) demonstrate that language models can be fine-tuned for factuality without human annotation by coupling automated truthfulness scoring with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). The paper proposes two variants. FactTune-FS scores candidate completions using FactScore, a reference-based metric that decomposes text into atomic claims and checks each against Wikipedia. FactTune-MC is fully reference-free: it extracts atomic claims, rephrases each as a minimally ambiguous question, resamples 20 answers from Llama-1-7B (used as the confidence oracle regardless of which model is being tuned), bins answers by semantic equivalence using heuristic string matching, and uses the fraction of answers falling in the largest bin as the truthfulness score for each fact. This max-confidence metric is the paper's preferred and better-performing variant; the entropy over semantic bins is an alternative that underperforms max-confidence in the atomic-question setting on biographies (0.810 vs 0.840 % Correct). Given a set of unlabeled prompts, both variants sample multiple completions, rank them by truthfulness score, and feed the resulting preference pairs into DPO. At inference time no additional computation is required. Experiments on Llama-1/2-7B cover biography generation and medical question-answering. FactTune-FS achieves the largest gains and is the only method that simultaneously increases correct facts and decreases incorrect facts (a strict improvement in factuality). FactTune-MC achieves strong results without any retrieval system. The gains are complementary to decoding-time interventions (DOLA stacks on top of FactTune-FS to push further), and the method composes with RLHF-finetuned chat models. The main qualitative cost is a shift toward a more terse, less conversational generation style, which GPT-4 rates as noticeably different from the SFT baseline for the majority of samples.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper is directly pertinent to Phase 1 of the Epistemic Humility experiment in three ways. First, it is the clearest existence proof that DPO on automatically constructed factuality preference pairs -- rather than human-labeled data -- substantially improves factual accuracy at 7B scale, which is the same regime and training paradigm as the Phase 1 SFT/DPO/KTO comparison. The FactTune pipeline is essentially the reference-free arm of what Phase 1 could replicate or extend with abstention-targeted preference pairs. Second, the reference-free max-confidence scoring mechanism (resample 20 answers, bin by semantic equivalence, take fraction in largest bin as truthfulness) is a concrete instantiation of model confidence as a factuality proxy, which is the theoretical grounding the Phase 1 experiment uses when it treats self-consistency signals as epistemic surrogates. Third, the qualitative style cost (77.5% of generations rated less conversational) is the closest empirical analogue to the alignment-tax concern motivating the three-arm comparison: factuality gains come with a tone shift that users notice, even when no capability metrics are affected. The paper does not evaluate models above 7B, does not test Qwen3, and does not target abstention directly, so Phase 1 cannot treat these numbers as drop-in effect sizes. For Phase 3 mech-interp, FactTune-MC is mechanistically interesting because it encodes a model's own max-confidence signal into preference data -- after training, one could probe whether attention heads that track self-consistency sharpened, paralleling the ITI truth-direction analysis.

## Claims

- FactTune-FS on Llama-2-7B raises biography generation % Correct from 0.701 (SFT) to 0.895 and cuts incorrect facts from 5.19 to 2.00, a strict simultaneous improvement in both directions that no baseline achieves. (Table 2, Llama-2 section) [[facttune-fs]]
- The reference-free FactTune-MC variant, which builds preference pairs from max-confidence scoring over 20 resampled answers drawn from Llama-1-7B, achieves biography % Correct of 0.846 on Llama-2-7B without any retrieval system, matching or exceeding all RLHF and decoding baselines. (Table 2, Llama-2 section; Section 3.2) [[facttune-mc]]
- FactTune-FS composes additively with DOLA decoding: on Llama-1 biographies, stacking raises % Correct from 0.812 (FactTune-FS alone) to 0.864, suggesting the two methods act on distinct mechanisms. (Table 4, Llama-1 rows) [[facttune-fs]]
- In the atomic-question reference-free setting, max-confidence scoring (fraction in largest semantic bin = 0.840) outperforms entropy over semantic bins (0.810) on biographies, and atomic question extraction outperforms named-entity extraction across both tasks, establishing max-confidence as the preferred design choice. (Table 5, Atomic/Heuristic rows; Section 4.4) [[max-confidence-scoring]]
- Factuality tuning produces a measurable style cost: GPT-4 rates FactTune-FS as less conversational than the SFT baseline for 77.5% (n=40) of Llama-1 samples and 65.6% (n=32) of Llama-2 samples, an analogue to alignment tax that is qualitative rather than capability-metric-based. (Section 4.1 prose) [[factuality-style-cost]]
