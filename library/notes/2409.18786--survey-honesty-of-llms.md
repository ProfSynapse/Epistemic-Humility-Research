## Abstract

Honesty is a fundamental principle for aligning large language models (LLMs) with human values, requiring these models to recognize what they know and don't know and be able to faithfully express their knowledge. Despite promising, current LLMs still exhibit significant dishonest behaviors, such as confidently presenting wrong answers or failing to express what they know. In addition, research on the honesty of LLMs also faces challenges, including varying definitions of honesty, difficulties in distinguishing between known and unknown knowledge, and a lack of comprehensive understanding of related research. To address these issues, we provide a survey on the honesty of LLMs, covering its clarification, evaluation approaches, and strategies for improvement. Moreover, we offer insights for future research, aiming to inspire further exploration in this important area.

## Summary

Li et al. (2024) survey the landscape of honesty research in large language models through a dual-lens framework that decomposes honesty into self-knowledge (knowing what you know and do not know) and self-expression (faithfully conveying that knowledge in output). The paper introduces no new empirical results of its own; its contribution is a taxonomic synthesis that clarifies overlapping definitions, catalogs evaluation benchmarks, and organizes improvement strategies across these two dimensions. The evaluation section covers three paradigms: recognition of known versus unknown (model-agnostic benchmarks such as SelfAware, KUQ, UnknownBench, HoneSet, and BeHonest, plus the model-specific Idk approach), calibration (ECE, Brier score, verbalized confidence), and selective prediction (risk-coverage metrics including AURCC). Improvement strategies are divided by whether they require training: training-free approaches include predictive probability, prompting, and sampling-and-aggregation; training-based approaches include supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, and probing for self-knowledge, plus decoding-time intervention, self-aware fine-tuning, and self-supervised fine-tuning for self-expression. The survey closes with five open research directions: resolving the objective-versus-subjective framing of honesty, improving knowledge identification, extending evaluation to instruction-following, addressing honesty over in-context knowledge, and studying honesty in multimodal and compressed models.

- SelfAware benchmark size: 3,369 total questions, 31% unknown; unknown questions collected from Quora and HowStuffWorks across five categories (Table 1, §3.1)
- KUQ benchmark size: 6,884 total questions, 50% unknown; unknown questions annotated across six categories including future unknown, unsolved problem, and counterfactual (Table 1, §3.1)
- UnknownBench size: 13,319 total questions, 50% unknown; known questions sourced from FalseQA, NaturalQuestions, and template-generated data (Table 1, §3.1)
- HoneSet size: 930 questions, 100% unknown; generated by GPT-4 across five limitation categories and filtered by human annotators [note: the paper's own table description enumerates six items despite stating 'five categories', an internal inconsistency in the source] (Table 1, §3.1)
- BeHonest benchmark size: 12,227 total questions, 63% unknown; questions drawn from SelfAware and UnknownBench (Table 1, §3.1)
- Probing accuracy on hidden states for true-versus-false classification: average accuracy 71% to 83% across models (Azaria and Mitchell 2023), indicating internal states encode correctness even when generation is unfaithful (§4.2, Probing)
- Using 1,000 training examples with LoRA or a probe head yields promising performance for predicting whether the model's answer is correct (Kapoor et al. 2024; §4.2, Supervised Fine-tuning)

## Relevance to experiment

This survey maps directly onto the Phase 1 training-arm taxonomy. The self-knowledge versus self-expression decomposition provides a principled framing for assessing Phase 1 arms separately: SFT and RL arms primarily target self-knowledge (teaching the model to say "I don't know"), while decoding-time and self-supervised approaches target self-expression (reducing the gap between latent and expressed knowledge). The taxonomy of improvement methods covers exactly the arms under study: SFT via idk-sft and r-tuning, DPO-based RL, and probing-based diagnostics. The documented gap between probe accuracy (71-83%) and generation accuracy, explicitly raised in the Probing and Decoding-time Intervention sections citing Li et al. 2024a (the ITI paper), grounds the Phase 3 mech-interp hypothesis that trained abstention behavior may not fully express internally represented uncertainty. The survey also flags that calibration in verbalized confidence for long-form instruction-following remains underexplored, which is the precise gap the Phase 1 design targets with Qwen3 on open-ended tasks.

## Claims

- The dual-lens framework defines LLM honesty as requiring both self-knowledge (recognizing known and unknown information) and self-expression (faithfully conveying that internal knowledge), and uses this decomposition to structure all evaluation and improvement work in the survey. (Section 2.1, Figure 1) [[self-knowledge]]
- Model-agnostic benchmarks for known-unknown recognition range in size from 930 to 13,319 questions, with unknown proportions from 31% to 100%, reflecting substantial methodological diversity in how 'unknowability' is operationalized. (Table 1, §3.1) [[selfaware]]
- Probing on LLM hidden states can distinguish true from false statements at average accuracy 71% to 83% across models (Azaria and Mitchell 2023), suggesting that internal representations encode self-knowledge even when generation is unfaithful to that representation. (§4.2, Probing) [[generation-discrimination-gap]]
- The survey explicitly identifies a generation-discrimination gap in the decoding-time intervention section, citing Li et al. 2024a as evidence that LLMs 'know' more than they 'say', and this gap motivates activation-steering approaches to self-expression. (§5.1, Decoding-time Intervention) [[generation-discrimination-gap]]
- Using 1,000 training examples with LoRA or a probe head yields promising self-knowledge performance for predicting whether the model's answer is correct (Kapoor et al. 2024), demonstrating that data-efficient supervised approaches can install a correctness predictor on top of a frozen or lightly adapted LLM. (§4.2, Supervised Fine-tuning) [[linear-probe]]
