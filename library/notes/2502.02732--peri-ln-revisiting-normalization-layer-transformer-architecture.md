## Abstract

Selecting a layer normalization (LN) strategy that stabilizes training and speeds convergence in Transformers remains difficult, even for today's large language models (LLM). We present a comprehensive analytical foundation for understanding how different LN strategies influence training dynamics in large-scale Transformers. Until recently, Pre-LN and Post-LN have long dominated practices despite their limitations in large-scale training. However, several open-source models have recently begun silently adopting a third strategy without much explanation. This strategy places normalization layer peripherally around sublayers, a design we term Peri-LN. While Peri-LN has demonstrated promising performance, its precise mechanisms and benefits remain almost unexplored. Our in-depth analysis delineates the distinct behaviors of LN strategies, showing how each placement shapes activation variance and gradient propagation. To validate our theoretical insight, we conduct extensive experiments on Transformers up to $3.2$B parameters, showing that Peri-LN consistently achieves more balanced variance growth, steadier gradient flow, and convergence stability. Our results suggest that Peri-LN warrants broader consideration for large-scale Transformer architectures, providing renewed insights into the optimal placement of LN.

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment (Table 1). Across 400M/1.5B/3.2B-parameter models pre-trained on 30B tokens with 5 seeds each, Peri-LN achieves the highest average benchmark score and lowest C4 evaluation loss at every model size (e.g., at 3.2B: Avg. 58.56 for Peri-LN vs. 56.69 for Pre-LN vs. 46.45 for Post-LN; Loss 3.11 vs. 3.20 vs. 4.43), and more than halves the cross-seed standard deviation of benchmark scores relative to Pre-LN/Post-LN.
- Evidence label: theory (Proposition 3.1, Eqs. 5-8, Appendix C). Formally, a Pre-LN sub-layer's final-layer gradient norm is proportional to the hidden-unit magnitude ||h||, so a massive activation directly produces an exploding gradient, whereas Peri-LN's Output-LN introduces a damping factor 1/||a|| that keeps the gradient norm bounded even under the same massive activation.
- Evidence label: experiment (Figure 11, Section 7.2). Tracking the global top-100 absolute hidden-state values during pre-training of 3.2B-parameter models, Pre-LN activations exceed the FP16 representable maximum after roughly 0.5B training tokens, while Peri-LN activations remain within FP16 range throughout training, tying LN placement directly to numeric-precision requirements.
- Evidence label: experiment (Figure 10, Table 7, Section 6). Ablating weight decay and weight initialization shows Pre-LN's variance explosion (and an otherwise-divergent run) can be substantially curbed by a 10x increase in weight decay or a smaller initialization scale, but Peri-LN remains comparably robust across the same hyperparameter range and still outperforms even a tuned, stabilized Pre-LN.
