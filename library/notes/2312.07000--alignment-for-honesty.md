## Abstract

Recent research has made significant strides in aligning large language models (LLMs) with helpfulness and harmlessness. In this paper, we argue for the importance of alignment for \emph{honesty}, ensuring that LLMs proactively refuse to answer questions when they lack knowledge, while still not being overly conservative. However, a pivotal aspect of alignment for honesty involves discerning an LLM's knowledge boundaries, which demands comprehensive solutions in terms of metric development, benchmark creation, and training methodologies. We address these challenges by first establishing a precise problem definition and defining ``honesty'' inspired by the Analects of Confucius. This serves as a cornerstone for developing metrics that effectively measure an LLM's honesty by quantifying its progress post-alignment. Furthermore, we introduce a flexible training framework which is further instantiated by several efficient fine-tuning techniques that emphasize honesty without sacrificing performance on other tasks. Our extensive experiments reveal that these aligned models show a marked increase in honesty, as indicated by our proposed metrics. We open-source all relevant resources to facilitate future research at \url{https://github.com/GAIR-NLP/alignment-for-honesty}.

## Summary

Yang et al. (2023) introduce a systematic framework for alignment for honesty, operationalized as training LLMs to proactively refuse questions they cannot answer (producing "idk responses") while still answering questions they can. The paper's central contribution is definitional and methodological: it anchors the concept of honesty in Confucian epistemology ("say you know when you know, say you don't when you don't"), formalizes honesty as a measurable property via three evolutionary metrics (prudence score, over-conservativeness score, honesty score), and proposes a family of supervised fine-tuning methods that use model-estimated expected accuracy to generate training labels distinguishing known from unknown questions.

The training pipeline samples m=10 responses per question from the target model, uses that empirical accuracy estimate to assign known/unknown labels, and then replaces targets for unknown questions with idk responses. Three SFT variants are compared: Absolute (fixed threshold), Confidence (numeric or verbal confidence prefix), and Multisample (treats each individual sample as a training example, replacing wrong responses with idk). Experiments use LLaMA2-Chat at 7B, 13B, and 70B scales on TriviaQA (primary) and two purpose-built out-of-distribution sets: PUQA (1,000 questions about 2023 scientific papers, probing unknown-question abstention) and PKQA (1,000 model-generated known questions, probing that alignment does not suppress correct answers). On TriviaQA with LLaMA2-Chat-13B, Multisample achieves the highest honesty score (75.91) and prudence (67.72) at modest over-conservativeness (15.89), while Confidence-Verb preserves near-baseline accuracy (73.34 vs unaligned 73.71). Prompt-based methods produce lower honesty scores (60.64 at 13B); their over-conservativeness at 13B (12.50) is modest and comparable to some SFT methods, while at 7B it rises sharply (36.63 vs Confidence-Verb's 11.43), illustrating that the prompt method's stability degrades at smaller scale. Non-honesty SFT (Fine-tuned Baseline) causes accuracy to drop on PKQA from 100.00 to 87.70, confirming that training on gold answers for unknown questions teaches hallucination rather than abstention. Adding only 284 in-domain MMLU examples to Multisample's TriviaQA training set achieves honesty 67.17 on MMLU, supporting a domain-transfer strategy via minimal in-domain unknown examples. Alignment tax is near-zero: Confidence-Verb Auto-J helpfulness 5.54 vs unaligned 5.56, and GPT-4 helpfulness 8.61 vs 8.62.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper is the direct definitional prior for Phase 1. The honesty-score / prudence-score / over-conservativeness-score triad is the primary evaluation instrument the Phase 1 SFT-vs-DPO-vs-KTO study inherits, and the honesty-oriented SFT variants (Absolute, Confidence, Multisample) define the SFT comparison arm. Three specific connections follow.

First, the Multisample result (honesty 75.91, prudence 67.72, over-conservativeness 15.89 at 13B) sets the quantitative target that preference-optimization arms must match or exceed, and the Confidence-Verb result (near-baseline accuracy 73.34 vs 73.71) sets the accuracy floor below which a training method incurs unacceptable alignment tax. The alignment-tax experiment (Auto-J gap of 0.02, GPT-4 gap of 0.01 for Confidence-Verb) provides a concrete threshold for what "low tax" means in practice.

Second, the non-honesty SFT baseline (PKQA accuracy 87.70 vs 100.00) demonstrates the failure mode the study must avoid: training on gold answers for unknown questions systematically degrades accuracy on known questions. This is the empirical grounding for the hypothesis that DPO or KTO preference signals, which contrast desired abstention against undesired hallucination rather than providing gold answers, may sidestep this degradation more cleanly than standard SFT.

Third, the PUQA/PKQA generalization results (Multisample Non-AmbigQA honesty 70.18, PUQA prudence 86.20) quantify out-of-distribution generalization for the SFT arm, establishing the baseline against which Phase 1 preference-optimization arms should be measured on held-out domains. The MMLU domain-transfer result (adding 284 examples raises honesty from near-zero to 67.17) suggests a practical recipe for Phase 1 multi-domain evaluation: a small in-domain unknown-question set may be sufficient to transfer abstention behavior across task types.

## Claims

- Honesty-oriented SFT methods (Multisample, Confidence) substantially raise the prudence score (the fraction of unknown questions correctly refused) while preserving accuracy close to the unaligned baseline, yielding large gains in honesty score. (Table 5, §5.5.1) [[honesty-oriented-sft]]
- Prompt-only (training-free) methods raise prudence but cause high over-conservativeness (false abstention on known questions), resulting in inferior honesty scores compared to fine-tuning methods. (Table 5, §5.5.1) [[over-conservativeness-score]]
- Honesty-oriented fine-tuning methods trained on TriviaQA generalize to out-of-distribution datasets (Non-AmbigQA, PUQA), demonstrating that they learn to discriminate known from unknown questions rather than memorizing dataset-specific shortcuts. (§5.6, Table 7) [[honesty-oriented-sft]]
- Non-honesty SFT that forces answers to unknown questions teaches the model to hallucinate, reducing accuracy by ~10 points on PKQA relative to honesty-oriented methods (Table 8, §5.6). (§5.6, Table 8) [[knowledge-boundary]]
