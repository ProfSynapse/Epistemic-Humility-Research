## Abstract

Modern language models fail a fundamental requirement of trustworthy intelligence: knowing when not to answer. Despite achieving impressive accuracy on benchmarks, these models produce confident hallucinations, even when wrong answers carry catastrophic consequences. Our evaluations on GSM8K, MedQA and GPQA show frontier models almost never abstain despite explicit warnings of severe penalties, suggesting that prompts cannot override training that rewards any answer over no answer. As a remedy, we propose Reinforced Hesitation (RH): a modification to Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to use ternary rewards (+1 correct, 0 abstention, -$λ$ error) instead of binary. Controlled experiments on logic puzzles reveal that varying $λ$ produces distinct models along a Pareto frontier, where each training penalty yields the optimal model for its corresponding risk regime: low penalties produce aggressive answerers, high penalties conservative abstainers. We then introduce two inference strategies that exploit trained abstention as a coordination signal: cascading routes queries through models with decreasing risk tolerance, while self-cascading re-queries the same model on abstention. Both outperform majority voting with lower computational cost. These results establish abstention as a first-class training objective that transforms ``I don't know'' from failure into a coordination signal, enabling models to earn trust through calibrated honesty about their limits.

## Summary

Mohamadi et al. demonstrate that frontier language models are structurally incapable of calibrated abstention: evaluated on GSM8K, MedQA, and GPQA with explicit penalty warnings up to lambda=100, they abstain less than 1% of the time while sustaining error rates above 10%, and zero abstentions were observed across 11 models on MedQA's 1,273 questions. The finding is diagnosis, not solution: prompts cannot override gradient-driven priors from RLVR training that reward any answer over no answer.

The remedy is Reinforced Hesitation (RH), a minimal modification to RLVR that replaces the binary reward (+1 correct, 0 wrong) with a ternary structure (+1 correct, 0 abstain, -lambda wrong). The penalty parameter lambda is not a free hyperparameter but an interpretable domain knob: a rational agent abstains when expected utility falls below zero, so the natural confidence threshold above which answering is rational is 1/(1+lambda). Applied to Qwen3-1.7B trained with Dr.GRPO on 80,000 Knights and Knaves logic puzzles, varying lambda across {0, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20} produces a Pareto frontier: no single model dominates all evaluation conditions, and each training penalty is optimal for its matching risk regime. At lambda=1, the model selectively abstains on ~60% of hard problems and only ~10% of easy ones, collapsing the overall error rate from 15% to below 2% versus the lambda=0 baseline. At lambda=10, conditional accuracy (accuracy on answered questions) reaches over 99% while the wrong-answer rate falls below 1%. The lambda=0 baseline achieves 0.837 reward under zero-cost evaluation but plummets to -2.36 at evaluation penalty 20; the lambda=5 model maintains positive rewards from 0.659 to 0.547 across all conditions.

A secondary result is that the 4,096-token clipping constraint, combined with the lambda-scaled format penalty, incidentally teaches epistemic efficiency: all models start training with approximately 40% clipping rates but reach below 1% within 100 steps, with moderate penalties (lambda in {1,2,5}) compressing mean response length from 3,000+ to 1,200-2,200 tokens and delivering an unexpected 25-30% reduction in inference compute alongside the accuracy improvements.

The third contribution converts trained abstention from a coverage loss into a coordination signal. Cascading routes queries through a hierarchy of models with decreasing risk tolerance (lambda=10 to lambda=0); a five-tier cascade achieves 88.1% accuracy with only 2.2 average queries, outperforming majority voting. Self-cascading re-queries the same model when it abstains, exploiting inference nondeterminism: the lambda=1 model improves from 77.5% to 92.5% accuracy as budget grows from B=1 to B=64, while majority voting gains only 2 points (77.5% to 79.5%) at the same budget.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper is the most direct training-side evidence in the graph for the claim that RLVR's binary reward is the mechanistic root of abstention failure, not a missing capability. Its Section 2 evaluation of 11 frontier models replicates and extends the pattern documented in `rlvr-post-training-degrades-abstention` and `reasoning-finetuning-degrades-abstention`, providing the clearest controlled evidence that prompts cannot compensate for gradient-baked always-answer priors.

For the Phase 1 SFT/DPO/KTO abstention training experiment, RH establishes a concrete alternative: ternary reward shaping during RLVR as a training-time lever. Because Phase 1 uses Synaptic Tuner's RLVR infrastructure, the lambda-parameterized ternary reward is a candidate arm that requires only a reward function change. The Pareto frontier result directly informs arm selection logic: there is no single optimal lambda, and the right value must be matched to the deployment risk regime, which the experiment design should register before training.

The difficulty-calibrated abstention result (60% on hard, 10% on easy at lambda=1) is the first paper in this graph to show that a small RLVR model can learn to discriminate problem difficulty via abstention rather than confidence verbalization. This is mechanistically distinct from the probe-based approaches in `grad-detect` and `inference-time-intervention`, which read internal representations; RH achieves difficulty discrimination purely through behavioral shaping. Whether the Qwen3 hidden states after RH training show sharper truth-direction or knowledge-boundary signals is an open Phase 3 question.

The acknowledged limitations (single synthetic domain, 1.7B model only) mean these results are not ready to transfer to the medical or factual QA domains targeted by Phase 1 without replication. The cascading results suggest a test-time compute strategy that could complement the SFT/DPO/KTO comparison: a cascade of RH-trained models could serve as an oracle upper bound on what behavioral complementarity can deliver, providing a reference point for the single-model abstention metrics the experiment tracks.
