## Abstract

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) can lead to unintended out-of-distribution generalization. Standard approaches to this problem rely on modifying training data, for example by adding data that better specify the intended generalization. However, this is not always practical. We introduce Concept Ablation Fine-Tuning (CAFT), a technique that leverages interpretability tools to control how LLMs generalize from fine-tuning, without needing to modify the training data or otherwise use data from the target distribution. Given a set of directions in an LLM's latent space corresponding to undesired concepts, CAFT works by ablating these concepts with linear projections during fine-tuning, steering the model away from unintended generalizations. We successfully apply CAFT to three fine-tuning tasks, including emergent misalignment, a phenomenon where LLMs fine-tuned on a narrow task generalize to give egregiously misaligned responses to general questions. Without any changes to the fine-tuning data, CAFT reduces misaligned responses by 10x without degrading performance on the training distribution. Overall, CAFT represents a novel approach for steering LLM generalization without modifying training data.

## Summary

Concept Ablation Fine-Tuning projects residual activations away from selected
latent concepts throughout fine-tuning, while allowing gradients to update the
model through the projection. The projection is absent at inference. The
authors choose PCA directions from base-versus-fine-tuned activation
differences or sparse-autoencoder latents, then interpret and select directions
associated with an undesired generalization.

On insecure-code fine-tuning, PCA CAFT reduced broad emergent misalignment on
Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct and Mistral-Small-24B-Instruct-2501 while preserving
most insecure-code behavior. On synthetic spurious-correlation tasks with
Gemma-2-2B, SAE CAFT achieved above-chance out-of-distribution accuracy on more
tasks than PCA CAFT. Applying the same ablations only at test time was usually
less effective.

## Extracted numbers

- PCA CAFT reduced the reported coherent-misalignment rate from 7.00 percent to
  0.51 percent for Qwen and from 6.57 percent to 1.16 percent for Mistral
  (Section 4.1, Figure 3).
- Insecure-code vulnerability remained 84.8 percent for Qwen and 92.2 percent
  for Mistral, compared with 91.6 and 92.4 percent after ordinary insecure-code
  fine-tuning (Section 4.1, Figure 3).
- SAE CAFT produced misalignment rates of 2.38 percent for Qwen and 4.63 percent
  for Mistral (Section 4.1, Figure 3).
- On the synthetic tasks, SAE CAFT reached above-chance out-of-distribution
  accuracy on the gender-bias task and 10 of 16 double multiple-choice tasks.
  PCA CAFT did so on the gender task and 5 of 16 double multiple-choice tasks
  (Section 4.2, Figure 5).
- The emergent-misalignment evaluation used 4,500 insecure-code examples. The
  synthetic spurious-correlation results report five random seeds (Sections
  3.1 and 4.2).

## Relevance to experiment

CAFT directly changes model weights while imposing a representation-space
constraint during training. It therefore shows one way to remove a runtime
activation hook after training. The method suppresses a fixed set of selected
concept directions. It does not train generation to consult a changing latent
answerability readout or implement a hidden-state-dependent gate.

The result also depends on direction interpretation. PCA and SAE performance
varies by task and model. Some benchmark tasks are synthetic, and the
emergent-misalignment skyline shows that adding only 100 benign completions to
the 4,500-example training set can reduce misalignment to about 0.5 percent.
The paper does not use target-distribution examples for CAFT, but it uses
generic text and model completions to identify and interpret directions.

## Claims

- Evidence label: method. CAFT ablates selected latent directions during every
  fine-tuning forward pass and removes the projection at inference (Sections 2
  and 3).
- Evidence label: model comparison. PCA CAFT reduced coherent misalignment by
  more than fivefold on both large models while retaining most training-task
  behavior (Section 4.1, Figure 3).
- Evidence label: control comparison. Training-time concept ablation generally
  outperformed applying the same ablations only at inference and outperformed
  several random or uninterpreted direction controls (Sections 4.1 and 4.2).
- Evidence label: scope limitation. CAFT needs a reliably identified and
  interpreted direction set, and PCA versus SAE effectiveness varies across
  models and tasks (Sections 4.3, 6, and Appendix F).
