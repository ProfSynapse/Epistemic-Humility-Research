## Summary

Kuhn, Gal, and Farquhar (ICLR 2023) identify a fundamental flaw in applying standard entropy-based uncertainty to free-form natural language generation: because many distinct token sequences can express the same meaning, token-level predictive entropy conflates genuine semantic uncertainty with arbitrary lexical variation. Their fix is semantic entropy, computed by first sampling M generations from the model, clustering them into semantic equivalence classes via a bidirectional entailment algorithm (DeBERTa-large fine-tuned on MNLI), then computing entropy over the distribution of meanings rather than sequences. The method is unsupervised, requires no model modification, and works off the shelf with any autoregressive LLM. On free-form QA (TriviaQA closed-book, CoQA open-book conversational), semantic entropy achieves AUROC 0.85 vs 0.82 for length-normalised entropy and 0.80 for raw predictive entropy at the 30B OPT scale on TriviaQA, and the advantage widens with model size. The paper also establishes that the optimal sampling temperature for entropy-based uncertainty is 0.5 on both benchmarks, resolving a tension between generation diversity and accuracy, and that semantic entropy improves with additional samples more steeply than token-level baselines because it handles redundant paraphrase samples gracefully.

## Relevance to experiment

Semantic entropy is directly relevant to Phase 1 calibration and uncertainty measurement in three ways. First, it shows that token-level predictive entropy, the default uncertainty signal computed from logit sequences, is systematically biased upward on questions where a model generates paraphrase variations of a single correct answer. Any calibration metric that aggregates logit-level entropy across samples without semantic deduplication will conflate lexical uncertainty with semantic uncertainty, overstating model uncertainty on questions the model actually knows. This matters when comparing SFT, DPO, and KTO arms, since training can shift paraphrase diversity independently of genuine knowledge uncertainty. Second, the paper shows that semantic entropy's advantage over token-level baselines grows with model size (Figure 1b), which directly bears on scaling comparisons across 4B and 8B checkpoints in the experiment: the gap between token-level and semantic uncertainty measures will widen at 8B, making baseline metric choice a confound if not corrected. Third, semantic entropy outperforms p(True) in this paper's evaluation, which matters because p(True) is a standard comparison baseline already in the graph. The caveat that makes this a research question rather than a drop-in fix is that the method runs at inference via sampling and NLI classification, not as a training signal, so it can serve as an evaluation tool after training arms but not as a supervision signal during training. The paper reports no training-induced delta and does not populate effects.csv.

## Claims

- Semantic entropy AUROC of 0.85 outperforms length-normalised entropy (0.82) and predictive entropy (0.80) on TriviaQA with the 30B OPT model, because clustering semantically equivalent sequences before computing entropy removes lexical paraphrase inflation from the uncertainty estimate. (Figure 1a) [[semantic-entropy]]
- The advantage of semantic entropy over token-level entropy baselines increases monotonically with model size across the 2.7B to 30B OPT range, while p(True) does not improve at the same rate, making semantic entropy the better scaling uncertainty signal. (Figure 1b, Figure 2) [[lexical-entropy-overestimates-uncertainty-under-paraphrase]]
- Incorrectly answered questions have substantially more semantically distinct answer clusters than correctly answered ones (3.89 vs 1.89 for TriviaQA; 1.77 vs 1.27 for CoQA), providing empirical grounding for why semantic cluster count alone is a reasonable uncertainty proxy, though semantic entropy is better. (Table 2) [[semantic-equivalence]]
- Multinomial sampling with temperature 0.5 maximises AUROC for semantic entropy on both TriviaQA and CoQA, because it balances generation diversity against generation accuracy; multinomial beam-search sampling reduces AUROC from 0.758 to 0.735 by concentrating on high-probability sequences and suppressing diversity. (Section 6.2, Table 4, Figures 3b and 4) [[bidirectional-entailment-clustering]]
- The bidirectional entailment classifier (DeBERTa-large fine-tuned on MNLI) achieves 92.7% accuracy on TriviaQA and 95.3% accuracy on CoQA on 300 manually labelled pairs, validating the semantic clustering component; at temperatures above 1.0 classifier accuracy degrades to 61% as model generations become nonsensical. (Appendix B.2) [[bidirectional-entailment-clustering]]
