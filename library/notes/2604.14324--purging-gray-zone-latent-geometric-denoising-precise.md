## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) often exhibit hallucinations due to their inability to accurately perceive their own knowledge boundaries. Existing abstention fine-tuning methods typically partition datasets directly based on response accuracy, causing models to suffer from severe label noise near the decision boundaries and consequently exhibit high rates of abstentions or hallucinations. This paper adopts a latent space representation perspective, revealing a "gray zone" near the decision hyperplane where internal belief ambiguity constitutes the core performance bottleneck. Based on this insight, we propose the **GeoDe** (**Geo**metric **De**noising) framework for abstention fine-tuning. This method constructs a truth hyperplane using linear probes and performs "geometric denoising" by employing geometric distance as a confidence signal for abstention decisions. This approach filters out ambiguous boundary samples while retaining high-fidelity signals for fine-tuning. Experiments across multiple models (Llama3, Qwen3) and benchmark datasets (TriviaQA, NQ, SciQ, SimpleQA) demonstrate that GeoDe significantly enhances model truthfulness and demonstrates strong generalization in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. ## Summary

The paper proposes GeoDe, an abstention fine-tuning pipeline that uses distance from a linear truthfulness-probe hyperplane to filter ambiguous training examples. It builds the probe on TriviaQA hidden states, keeps the farthest known and unknown samples, and fine-tunes Llama3-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B on the denoised data. The evaluation balances correct answering and appropriate abstention through reliability F1, with tests on in-domain QA, out-of-domain QA, retrieval-augmented QA, and unanswerable datasets.

## Extracted numbers

- GeoDe TBG reached reliability F1 of 77.1 on Llama3-8B-Instruct and 77.6 on Qwen3-8B for TriviaQA. The corresponding R-Tuning values were 74.4 and 75.0 (Section 4.2, Table 2).
- On Qwen3-8B and Natural Questions, GeoDe TBG reached reliability F1 of 61.6, compared with 58.7 for R-Tuning and 59.4 for Probe-Tuning TBG (Section 4.2, Table 2).
- The farthest, middle, and nearest hidden-state tiers reached reliability F1 of 77.6, 74.6, and 73.2 on Qwen3-8B TriviaQA. The nearest-distance bin had probe AUROC below 0.6 (Section 5.1, Table 5 and Figure 3).
- Retaining 25% of examples produced the best TriviaQA and Natural Questions reliability F1 values, 77.6 and 61.6. Retaining 6.25% left too little data, while 75% reintroduced boundary noise (Section 5.1, Table 4).
- On Alcuna, GeoDe TBG produced a 94.5% abstention rate for Qwen3-8B, compared with 78.2% for R-Tuning (Section 5.3, Table 6).

## Relevance to experiment

The paper shows one way to use an internal answerability-like readout during data selection rather than at generation time. It supports distance-to-boundary as a confidence filter, but it does not show that the trained model consults the probe online. Its positive-to-negative analysis also preserves the core trade-off: more answer supervision raises both accuracy and hallucination, while too much abstention supervision causes over-abstention.

## Claims

- Evidence label: training comparison. Filtering fine-tuning examples by truthfulness-hyperplane distance improves reliability F1 over accuracy-only abstention baselines across the tested model and dataset panel (Section 4.2, Table 2).
- Evidence label: ablation. Samples far from the probe hyperplane yield better reliability than middle or near-boundary samples, and probe accuracy increases with distance (Section 5.1, Table 5 and Figure 3).
- Evidence label: robustness evaluation. GeoDe retains its reliability advantage in the paper's retrieval-augmented and out-of-domain evaluations (Sections 4.2 and 4.3, Tables 2 and 3).
- Evidence label: limitation. The study covers short-form QA and models up to 8B, relies on a linear truthfulness probe, and does not remove the over-abstention versus hallucination trade-off (Section 6, Limitations).
