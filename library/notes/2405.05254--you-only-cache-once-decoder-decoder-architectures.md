## Abstract

We introduce a decoder-decoder architecture, YOCO, for large language models, which only caches key-value pairs once. It consists of two components, i.e., a cross-decoder stacked upon a self-decoder. The self-decoder efficiently encodes global key-value (KV) caches that are reused by the cross-decoder via cross-attention. The overall model behaves like a decoder-only Transformer, although YOCO only caches once. The design substantially reduces GPU memory demands, yet retains global attention capability. Additionally, the computation flow enables prefilling to early exit without changing the final output, thereby significantly speeding up the prefill stage. Experimental results demonstrate that YOCO achieves favorable performance compared to Transformer in various settings of scaling up model size and number of training tokens. We also extend YOCO to 1M context length with near-perfect needle retrieval accuracy. The profiling results show that YOCO improves inference memory, prefill latency, and throughput by orders of magnitude across context lengths and model sizes. Code is available at https://aka.ms/YOCO.

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment. At 1.6T training tokens, YOCO-3B (gated-retention self-decoder) matches or slightly exceeds StableLM-3B-4E1T and OpenLLaMA-v2-3B on the LM Eval Harness downstream-task average (0.634-0.636 vs ~0.612-0.619 for the baselines), and a length-extended variant YOCO-3B-1M further raises the average to 0.645 (Table 4).
- Evidence label: experiment. Scaling curves from 160M to 13B parameters show YOCO with gated retention achieves lower validation loss than a Llama-style Transformer baseline and than YOCO with sliding-window attention, which the authors attribute to complementary inductive biases between attention and retention (Figure 3, Section 4.2).
- Evidence label: experiment. Extended to 1M context length, YOCO-3B-1M achieves near-perfect single-needle retrieval and competitive multi-needle retrieval accuracy (0.98/0.98/0.84/0.56 for N=1/2/4/8 needles at 128K length) versus larger 6-7B-parameter Transformer-based long-context models (Figure 4, Table 5).
- Evidence label: experiment. Compared to an inference-optimized Transformer of the same 3B size, YOCO reduces inference GPU memory (12.4GB vs 9.4x more at 1M context), prefilling latency (from ~180s to under 6s at 512K context, and 2.87x faster even at 32K), and throughput (4.5 to 43.1 tokens/s at 512K, a 9.6x gain) (Section 4.4, Figures 6, 8, 9).
