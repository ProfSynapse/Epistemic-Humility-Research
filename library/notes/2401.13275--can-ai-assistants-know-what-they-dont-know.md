## Abstract

Recently, AI assistants based on large language models (LLMs) show surprising performance in many tasks, such as dialogue, solving math problems, writing code, and using tools. Although LLMs possess intensive world knowledge, they still make factual errors when facing some knowledge intensive tasks, like open-domain question answering. These untruthful responses from the AI assistant may cause significant risks in practical applications. We believe that an AI assistant's refusal to answer questions it does not know is a crucial method for reducing hallucinations and making the assistant truthful. Therefore, in this paper, we ask the question "Can AI assistants know what they don't know and express them through natural language?" To answer this question, we construct a model-specific "I don't know" (Idk) dataset for an assistant, which contains its known and unknown questions, based on existing open-domain question answering datasets. Then we align the assistant with its corresponding Idk dataset and observe whether it can refuse to answer its unknown questions after alignment. Experimental results show that after alignment with Idk datasets, the assistant can refuse to answer most its unknown questions. For questions they attempt to answer, the accuracy is significantly higher than before the alignment.

## Summary

Cheng et al. build model-specific IDK datasets from TriviaQA by sampling model
answers, grading correctness, and labeling questions as known/unknown under an
Ik threshold. They compare prompting, Idk-SFT, and preference-aware methods such
as DPO, PPO, BoN, and HIR. The key qualitative claim for our locked training-regimen work is
that SFT teaches refusal but can become overly conservative, while preference
optimization can reduce that over-refusal when built on the IDK/SFT setup.

## Relevance to experiment

locked training-regimen reuses Cheng's model-specific known/unknown dataset idea and quadrant
metrics, but the current Qwen3 recipes are not a bit-for-bit reproduction of
Cheng's training stack.

Verified against the official OpenMOSS/Say-I-Dont-Know README on 2026-06-14:
Idk-SFT is launched through `llama_recipes/finetuning.py --enable_fsdp` with
`--num_epochs 10`, `--lr 2e-5`, `--batch_size_training 4`, and
`--gradient_accumulation_steps 2`. Idk-DPO is initialized from the SFT result
model and uses `loss.beta=0.1`, `loss.sft_coef_when_dpo=0.01`, batch size 64,
gradient accumulation 4, and `FSDPTrainer`.

Our locked training-regimen Qwen3 local runs use Unsloth QLoRA/LoRA for feasible single-GPU
local execution, with matched LoRA capacity across arms. Therefore DPO/KTO from
base being weak should not be read as a direct contradiction of Cheng's
sequential SFT-warmed DPO result. It strengthens the case for explicit
`SFT -> DPO` and `SFT -> KTO` follow-up cells, plus later sensitivity on epochs
and LoRA rank/alpha.

## Claims

- After alignment with model-specific Idk datasets, Llama-2-7b-chat achieves a Truthful rate of 78.96% (Idk-BoN), correctly identifying known and unknown questions for up to 78.96% of test questions. (Table 1) [[knowledge-quadrant-metric]]
- Idk-SFT raises Truthful rate to 74.75% but at the cost of an 'alignment tax': Ik-Ik rate decreases as the model incorrectly refuses questions it actually knows; preference optimization (DPO 77.89%, BoN 78.96%, PPO 76.47%) mitigates this trade-off. (Table 1, Figure 2) [[idk-sft]]
- Abstention behavior trained on TriviaQA generalizes to out-of-distribution data: aligned models show high Truthful rates on Natural Questions and high Ik-Idk rates on ALCUNA (which contains only unknown questions). (Table 1, Section 4.3) [[idk-dataset]]
- Higher Ik thresholds produce more conservative (refusal-heavy) models; using Ik threshold = 1.0 maximizes the total Ik-Ik + Ik-Idk count, yielding the most truthful assistants in ablations. (Section 4, ablation results) [[ik-threshold]]
