## Abstract

Transformers have been recently adapted for large scale image classification, achieving high scores shaking up the long supremacy of convolutional neural networks. However the optimization of image transformers has been little studied so far. In this work, we build and optimize deeper transformer networks for image classification. In particular, we investigate the interplay of architecture and optimization of such dedicated transformers. We make two transformers architecture changes that significantly improve the accuracy of deep transformers. This leads us to produce models whose performance does not saturate early with more depth, for instance we obtain 86.5% top-1 accuracy on Imagenet when training with no external data, we thus attain the current SOTA with less FLOPs and parameters. Moreover, our best model establishes the new state of the art on Imagenet with Reassessed labels and Imagenet-V2 / match frequency, in the setting with no additional training data. We share our code and models.

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment (Table 1, Fig. 3). LayerScale (a per-channel learnable diagonal scaling applied to each residual-block output, initialized near zero) stabilizes and improves training of deep image transformers, reaching 82.9% top-1 at 36 layers and outperforming adapted ReZero/Fixup/T-Fixup variants at every tested depth, while the unmodified DeiT-S baseline fails to converge at 24 and 36 layers.
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 2). Dedicated class-attention layers -- which read patch-embedding content into the class embedding late in the network without writing back into the patch stream -- outperform DeiT's practice of inserting and jointly updating a class token from layer 0, with the best configurations reaching 80.6-80.8% top-1 versus the 79.9% DeiT-S baseline at a matched 12-13 layer budget.
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 5). Combining LayerScale and class-attention layers into the CaiT architecture reaches 86.5% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k-val with no external training data (CaiT-M48↑448 with distillation), matching prior SOTA (NFNet-F6+SAM, 86.5%) with fewer FLOPs (329.6B vs 377.3B) and fewer parameters (356M vs 438M), and sets a new SOTA on ImageNet-Real and ImageNet-V2 matched-frequency among no-external-data models.
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 7). CaiT models transfer well to downstream classification tasks (iNaturalist 2018/2019, Flowers-102, Stanford Cars, CIFAR-100/10) without distillation, achieving overall better performance than EfficientNet-B7 across the transfer datasets evaluated.
