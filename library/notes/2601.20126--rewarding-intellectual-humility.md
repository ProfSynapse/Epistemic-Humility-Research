## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) often produce hallucinated or unverifiable content, undermining their reliability in factual domains. This work investigates Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) as a training paradigm that explicitly rewards abstention ("I don't know") alongside correctness to promote intellectual humility. We fine-tune and evaluate Granite-3.3-2B-Instruct and Qwen-3-4B-Instruct on the MedMCQA and Hendrycks Math benchmarks using a ternary reward structure ($-1$, r_abs, 1) under varying abstention reward structures. We further study the effect of combining RLVR with supervised fine-tuning strategies that teach abstention prior to reinforcement learning. Our results show that moderate abstention rewards (r_abs $\approx -0.25$ to 0.3) consistently reduce incorrect responses without severe accuracy degradation on multiple-choice tasks, with larger models exhibiting greater robustness to abstention incentives. On open-ended question answering, we observe limitations due to insufficient exploration, which can be partially mitigated through supervised abstention training. Overall, these findings demonstrate the feasibility and flexibility of verifiable reward design as a practical approach for hallucination mitigation in language models. Reproducible code for our abstention training framework is available here https://github.com/Mystic-Slice/rl-abstention.

## Summary

Jha et al. study whether Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) can be extended with an explicit abstention reward to make LLMs say "I don't know" when uncertain, rather than hallucinate. The paper introduces a ternary reward structure (correct = +1, IDK = r_abs, incorrect = -1) and sweeps r_abs across three training regimes: RL-only (GRPO from the base model), RL-SFT-Random (random 30% of ground truths relabeled as IDK before RL), and RL-RTuning (base-model-incorrect examples relabeled as IDK before RL). Experiments use Granite-3.3-2B-Instruct and Qwen-3-4B-Instruct on MedMCQA (multiple-choice medical QA) and Hendrycks MATH (open-ended mathematical reasoning). Key findings: on MCQ tasks, moderate abstention rewards (r_abs approximately -0.25 to 0.3) reduce incorrect responses substantially without severe accuracy loss, and larger models (Qwen 4B) show greater robustness across the reward sweep. On open-ended QA, RL-only fails because the base model almost never outputs IDK spontaneously, starving the RL algorithm of exploration signal; a supervised abstention warm-up partially overcomes this. RL-RTuning unexpectedly underperforms RL-SFT-Random because RTuning's SFT phase produces approximately 60% IDK responses (vs approximately 30% for Random), leaving the model in a capability-collapsed state that RL cannot recover. The paper defines three evaluation metrics (Accuracy, Adjusted Accuracy, Abstention Recall per Equation 4) and plots all three across methods in Figures 1-2. Code is released at github.com/Mystic-Slice/rl-abstention.

## Extracted numbers

Extracted numbers sourced from library/fulltext/2601.20126.html, Tables 1-6 and Section 3.6; verified against the source on 2026-06-24. Numbers populate an abstention-vs-hallucination tradeoff format (correct/incorrect/IDK distribution); the paper reports Accuracy, Adjusted Accuracy, and Abstention Recall (Equation 4, Figures 1-2) but not ECE or Brier score, so these rows do not directly fit the effects.csv calibration-delta schema.

- Granite-3.3-2B-Instruct base model (no IDK option): 46.8% correct, 53.2% incorrect on MedMCQA (Table 1).
- Granite-3.3-2B-Instruct base model with IDK option: 44.8% correct, 48.6% incorrect, 6.6% IDK on MedMCQA (Table 1).
- Granite-3.3-2B RL-only (r_abs = -0.25) on MedMCQA: 46.4% correct, 46.4% incorrect, 7.0% IDK (Table 1, Table 3).
- Granite-3.3-2B RL-only (r_abs = 0) on MedMCQA: 15.0% correct, 6.0% incorrect, 78.0% IDK (Table 3).
- Granite-3.3-2B RL-only (r_abs = 0.3) on MedMCQA: 0% correct, 0% incorrect, 100% IDK; complete abstention collapse at positive reward (Table 3).
- Qwen-3-4B-Instruct base model: 67.5% correct, 32.5% incorrect on MedMCQA (Table 1).
- Qwen-3-4B RL-only (r_abs = 0.3) on MedMCQA: 48.0% correct, 10.3% incorrect, 41.0% IDK; hallucination rate drops from 32.5% to 10.3% (Table 1, Table 3, Section 4.1).
- Granite-3.3-2B RL-SFT-Random (r_abs = -0.25) on MedMCQA: 39.0% correct, 40.0% incorrect, 21.0% IDK (Table 1, Table 4).
- Granite-3.3-2B RL-RTuning (r_abs = -0.25) on MedMCQA: 47.0% correct, 45.0% incorrect, 8.0% IDK (Table 1, Table 5).
- RTuning SFT phase yields approximately 60% IDK dominance vs approximately 30% for SFT-Random; paper proposes this excess IDK causes capability collapse that RL cannot recover (Section 4.2).
- Granite-3.3-2B base model on Hendrycks MATH: 49.59% correct, 50.41% incorrect; base model with IDK option barely abstains (0.03% IDK) (Table 2).
- Granite-3.3-2B RL-only (r_abs = -0.25) on MATH: 66.0% correct, 34.0% incorrect, 0.0% IDK; RL-only does not introduce abstention on open-ended QA (Table 2).
- Granite-3.3-2B RL-SFT-Random (r_abs = -0.5) on MATH: 35.0% correct, 26.0% incorrect, 39.0% IDK; incorrect answers cut from 50.4% to 26.0% (~48% relative reduction) (Table 2).
- Granite-3.3-2B RL-RTuning (r_abs = -0.5) on MATH: 21.3% correct, 33.7% incorrect, 44.4% IDK (Table 2).
- RL-SFT-Random Granite MedMCQA reward sweep: r_abs = -0.5 yields 3% IDK; r_abs = -0.25 yields 21% IDK; r_abs = 0.0 yields 73% IDK; monotonic trade-off confirmed (Table 4).
- RL-RTuning Granite MATH reward sweep: r_abs = -0.95 yields 5.4% IDK; r_abs = -0.50 yields 44.4% IDK; r_abs = 0.25 yields 98.2% IDK (Table 6).
- Training configuration: GRPO via HuggingFace TRL GRPOTrainer; learning rate 2e-5; gradient accumulation 64; 8 generations per prompt; 500 max steps; LoRA fine-tuning; bfloat16; vLLM colocated generation with 0.6 GPU memory cap; approximately 8k training samples; approximately 100 held-out test samples (Section 3.6).

## Relevance to experiment

This paper is directly relevant to the Phase 1 experiment on abstention training with verifiable rewards. It is the most closely matched prior art to the project's SFT-vs-RL abstention comparison: it trains on MedMCQA and MATH with GRPO, sweeps the abstention reward hyperparameter, and reports correct/incorrect/IDK distributions alongside Abstention Recall (Equation 4, Figures 1-2), which is one of the project's primary evaluation axes. The exploration-failure result on open-ended QA (RL-only gains zero abstention on MATH because the base model almost never outputs IDK without prior SFT) is a direct empirical caution for any RLVR-only abstention arm in the project's experiment design. The RTuning collapse result (excess IDK in the SFT phase blocks RL recovery) quantifies the risk of over-reliance on model-error-based relabeling as the abstention curriculum, directly informing data mixture choices. The scale contrast (Qwen 4B retains 48% accuracy at 41% IDK where Granite 2B collapses to 0% at r_abs = 0.3) is relevant to the 4B-vs-8B robustness question in Phase 1, though the model families differ and the comparison should be treated as suggestive rather than controlled. The paper does not use ECE or Brier score, so it does not populate the effects.csv calibration-delta schema directly, but it does use Abstention Recall and Adjusted Accuracy as defined evaluation metrics.

## Claims

- Moderate RLVR abstention rewards (r_abs approximately -0.25 to 0.3) consistently reduce hallucination rates on MCQ tasks without severe accuracy degradation, and larger models tolerate higher abstention rewards before accuracy collapse. (Table 1, Table 3, Section 4.1) [[ternary-reward-enables-abstention-over-hallucination]]
- RL-only training fails to induce abstention on open-ended QA because the base model almost never emits IDK responses, providing insufficient exploration for the RL algorithm to discover the abstention action. (Table 2, Section 4.1) [[rl-insufficient-exploration-blocks-open-ended-abstention]]
- RL-RTuning underperforms RL-SFT-Random despite its theoretically superior data curation, because the RTuning SFT phase dominates outputs with approximately 60% IDK (vs approximately 30% for Random), collapsing model capability that RL cannot recover. (Table 1, Section 4.2) [[excess-sft-idk-causes-capability-collapse]]
- Qwen-3-4B-Instruct with RL-only (r_abs = 0.3) reduces its incorrect response rate from 32.5% to 10.3% while achieving 41.0% abstention, demonstrating that a larger, higher-capacity model can sustain meaningful accuracy under aggressive abstention incentives. (Table 1, Table 3) [[larger-model-better-abstention]]
- Abstention Recall is defined as incorrect answers converted to IDK divided by total incorrect answers, and RL-only generally maximizes Adjusted Accuracy while RL-SFT variants generally maximize Abstention Recall, with no single method dominating all three metrics simultaneously. (Equation 4, Figure 1, Figure 2, Section 4.5) [[abstention-recall]]
