## Abstract

Neural networks are trained primarily based on their inputs and outputs, without regard for their internal mechanisms. These neglected mechanisms determine properties that are critical for safety, like (i) transparency; (ii) the absence of sensitive information or harmful capabilities; and (iii) reliable generalization of goals beyond the training distribution. To address this shortcoming, we introduce gradient routing, a training method that isolates capabilities to specific subregions of a neural network. Gradient routing applies data-dependent, weighted masks to gradients during backpropagation. These masks are supplied by the user in order to configure which parameters are updated by which data points. We show that gradient routing can be used to (1) learn representations which are partitioned in an interpretable way; (2) enable robust unlearning via ablation of a pre-specified network subregion; and (3) achieve scalable oversight of a reinforcement learner by localizing modules responsible for different behaviors. Throughout, we find that gradient routing localizes capabilities even when applied to a limited, ad-hoc subset of the data. We conclude that the approach holds promise for challenging, real-world applications where quality data are scarce.

## Summary

The paper introduces gradient routing, a modification of backpropagation that multiplies per-example gradient paths by user-specified weights while leaving the forward pass unchanged. The experiments route learning signals to selected activation dimensions, neurons, or mixture-of-experts modules. The method partitions MNIST and CIFAR-100 representations, localizes California-related language-model features to one residual-stream coordinate, supports later capability removal through Expand, Route, Ablate, and separates Diamond-seeking from Ghost-seeking policy updates under partial oversight. The paper calls the broader localization of semantically related but unrouted data absorption.

## Extracted numbers

- The MNIST autoencoder has a 32-dimensional encoding split into two 16-dimensional halves for digits 0 to 4 and 5 to 9 (Section 4.1).
- The steering-scalar language model has 303 million parameters and 20 layers. Gradients for positions predicting `_California` are restricted to residual dimension 0 at layers 6 through 18 (Section 4.2.1).
- The TinyStories unlearning experiment uses a 28-million-parameter transformer. Stories containing forest, tree, or woodland terms form 20% of training data, and robustness is tested by retraining on 64 forget stories (Section 4.2.2, Appendix C).
- The larger unlearning experiment uses a 0.7-billion-parameter transformer, routes 20 virology-related tokens to 80 MLP dimensions in layers 0 through 7, applies 32 retain-repair steps, and then retrains for 20 steps on two WMDP-bio examples (Section 4.2.3).
- After retraining, WMDP-bio loss remains 0.182 above baseline, or 0.171 when the 20 routed tokens are excluded, while FineWeb-Edu loss is 0.032 above baseline (Section 4.2.3, Table 1).
- The gridworld is 5 by 5, ends after 32 steps, and trains for 20,000 algorithm steps or 328 million environment steps (Section 4.3).
- The routed Diamond expert has nontrivial performance at 1% oversight. At 5% oversight, it matches comparison methods trained with six times more oversight (Section 4.3, Figure 6).

## Relevance to experiment

Gradient routing is direct evidence that a training rule can constrain where selected computations are learned. It therefore supplies a concrete weight-level localization method. However, the routes are supplied by the user from labels or token membership during backpropagation. The paper does not train generation to consult a model-derived answerability readout, and its inference-time examples use ablation or a fixed steering coordinate rather than an endogenous readout gate.

## Claims

- Evidence label: construction. Data-dependent masks multiply gradients along chosen backward-pass paths without changing the forward computation or behavioral loss (Section 3, Figures 1 and 2).
- Evidence label: representation intervention. Label-dependent routing partitions MNIST and CIFAR-100 representations, and token-dependent routing makes one residual coordinate steer California-related generation (Sections 4.1 and 4.2.1, Figures 3 and 11).
- Evidence label: training and ablation. Expand, Route, Ablate localizes designated TinyStories and virology capabilities so later ablation remains partly resistant to small forget-set retraining (Sections 4.2.2 and 4.2.3, Figures 4 and 5, Table 1).
- Evidence label: reinforcement-learning intervention. Outcome-conditioned gradient routing specializes Diamond and Ghost experts, and selecting the Diamond expert outperforms feasible behavioral-supervision baselines at low oversight (Section 4.3, Figure 6).
- Evidence label: limitation. Performance depends on route data, target regions, and mask weights. Language-model routing is token-local, ERA degrades retain performance, and the scalable-oversight environment is a small gridworld whose ablated policy remains coherent by construction (Section 5, Appendix A).
