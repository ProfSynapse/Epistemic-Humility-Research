## Abstract

Reliable uncertainty communication is critical to the trustworthiness of LLMs, yet faithful calibration (FC)--the alignment between models' intrinsic and (linguistically) expressed confidence--is a persistent failure mode. This challenge is key for large reasoning models (LRMs), whose extended reasoning traces are often interpreted by users as evidence of deliberation, competence, and confidence. Despite the importance of FC and wide usage of LRMs, the extent to which LRMs can faithfully express their confidence remains poorly understood. Moreover, the prevailing paradigm to measure FC does not generalize well to the long chain-of-thought outputs generated by LRMs, which tend to lack clear step boundaries, involve inconsistent step structure, and encode complex conditional dependencies throughout the trace--complicating estimation of intrinsic confidence. To address this challenge, we introduce a novel framework to systematically quantify FC of LRMs. Our framework analyzes linguistic decisiveness relative to three sources of internal uncertainty, based on token probabilities, hidden states, and sampled response consistency. We also devise a prefix-conditioned sampling approach to control for conditional and structural variation across traces. Applying our framework to a diverse suite of leading models, datasets, and prompts, we find that faithful confidence expression is a significant challenge for LRMs. Reasoning behaviors do not automatically translate to improved FC, and prompt interventions for non-reasoning models do not improve faithfulness in the reasoning setting. Different confidence estimators further produce divergent assessments of the same traces, revealing fragility in prior evaluation methodologies. Taken together, our work establishes FC as a distinct reliability and alignment target for LRMs, particularly as such systems are increasingly deployed in high-stakes contexts.

## Summary

Gani et al. introduce a framework to quantify faithful calibration (FC) in large reasoning models (LRMs): the alignment between a model's intrinsic confidence (what token probabilities, hidden states, or sampled response consistency reveal) and its linguistically expressed confidence (how hedged or decisive its final answer is). The paper's central argument is that the long chain-of-thought outputs produced by LRMs break the assumptions underlying standard FC measurement, because traces lack clear step boundaries, have inconsistent structure, and encode complex conditional dependencies that confound any naively-applied intrinsic confidence estimate.

The framework has two main technical contributions. First, a linguistic decisiveness scorer, implemented as an LLM-as-judge (Gemini-2.5-Flash), rates how hedged or decisive each model response is. The scorer achieves Pearson r=0.884 and Spearman r=0.869 against human ratings on a 300-example short-form validation set (Table 6, §A.2.2). Second, a prefix-conditioned sampling approach samples k=10 continuations of up to 200 tokens at each reasoning step, retaining the first and last step and uniformly subsampling up to 20 steps per trace (§3.1), to estimate how much the model's intrinsic confidence shifts over the course of the trace. Three internal confidence estimators are computed: RCC (token-probability-based), DeepConf (hidden-state-based), and Sampling Consistency. The compound metric cMFG* combines decisiveness with each estimator's internal confidence into a single faithful calibration score.

Applying this framework to leading LRMs across six benchmarks (AIME, HLE, SGPQA, MuSR, CalibratedMath, Math-500) reveals three empirical findings that challenge conventional assumptions. First, estimators produce strikingly divergent assessments of identical traces: at the trace level, RCC correlates with linguistic decisiveness at Spearman 0.081, DeepConf at 0.631, and Sampling Consistency at 0.104 (Table 2, §5.1). This divergence means that which estimator a researcher picks governs the conclusions drawn. Second, reasoning fine-tuning decouples verbal decisiveness from internal confidence in ways that lower cMFG*: after Synthetic-1 training, Llama-3.1-8B decisiveness drops from 0.793 to 0.634 on AIME while its RCC internal confidence stays nearly flat (0.882 to 0.881), and cMFG* falls from 0.819 to 0.694 (Table 3, §5.2). A parallel teacher-student comparison shows DeepSeek-R1-671B is less decisive (0.553-0.697 across datasets) but also lower internal confidence (0.699-0.775 DeepConf), while its Qwen3-8B distilled student is more decisive (0.680-0.741) and higher internal confidence (0.726-0.843), yet both achieve comparable cMFG* (Tables 4-5, §5.2). Third, prompt interventions that raise factual accuracy (Perception, MetSens+Hedge) produce approximately zero change in cMFG* under all three estimators (Figure 4, §5.3), showing that accuracy improvements do not automatically carry over to faithful confidence expression.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper matters to the Epistemic-Humility experiment in three concrete ways.

First, the finding that reasoning fine-tuning (Synthetic-1 SFT, knowledge distillation) can drop verbal decisiveness while leaving token-probability-based internal confidence nearly unchanged is direct motivation to add internal-confidence probes to the Phase 1 output-level abstention metrics. The Phase 1 arms (SFT, DPO, KTO) all modify a Qwen3 reasoning model, and the paper shows that changes in verbal hedging behavior after such training can dissociate from what confidence estimators reveal internally. Reporting only output-level abstention rates may therefore miss or mischaracterize the training effect.

Second, the estimator-divergence result is a hard methodological warning for mechanistic interpretability: at the trace level, three principled estimators produce Spearman correlations with verbal decisiveness spanning 0.081 to 0.631. Any mech-interp sweep that trains a single hidden-state probe and treats it as a faithful calibration signal is building on fragile ground. The mechanism program candidate inventory should include both token-probability and hidden-state estimators and report whether their rankings agree, rather than treating any one probe's verdict as definitive.

Third, the prefix-conditioned sampling design addresses a real confound for the Phase 1 evaluation setup: Qwen3's chain-of-thought outputs encode strong conditional dependencies between steps, which means naively measuring token probabilities at the final token of a long trace mixes intrinsic uncertainty with autoregressive conditioning. The paper's approach (sampling continuations from each step position, not just the end) is a replicable design pattern. If Phase 1 or mechanism program evaluations use token-level confidence signals, this method for controlling for CoT-structure effects should be adopted or explicitly justified as unnecessary.

Limitation: the paper does not report a training-before-after humility delta on a calibration metric, so it does not populate effects.csv directly.
