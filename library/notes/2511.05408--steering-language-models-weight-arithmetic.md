## Abstract

Providing high-quality feedback to Large Language Models (LLMs) on a diverse training distribution can be difficult and expensive, and providing feedback only on a narrow distribution can result in unintended generalizations. To better leverage narrow training data, we propose contrastive weight steering, a simple post-training method that edits the model parameters using weight arithmetic. We isolate a behavior direction in weight-space by subtracting the weight deltas from two small fine-tunes -- one that induces the desired behavior and another that induces its opposite -- and then add or remove this direction to modify the model's weights. We apply this technique to mitigate sycophancy and induce misalignment, and find that weight steering often generalizes further than activation steering, achieving stronger out-of-distribution behavioral control before degrading general capabilities. We also show that, in the context of task-specific fine-tuning, weight steering can partially mitigate undesired behavioral drift: it can reduce sycophancy and under-refusals introduced during fine-tuning while preserving task performance gains. Finally, we provide preliminary evidence that emergent misalignment can be detected by measuring the similarity between fine-tuning updates and an "evil" weight direction, suggesting that it may be possible to monitor the evolution of weights during training and detect rare misaligned behaviors that never manifest during training or evaluations.

## Summary

Fierro and Roger introduce contrastive weight steering, a post-training method that derives a behavioral parameter direction from the difference between positive and negative fine-tunes. The paper applies scaled directions to pretrained and task-fine-tuned models, then compares the edits with fine-tuning and activation steering on out-of-distribution sycophancy, evil multiple-choice behavior, and refusal restoration. The tested weight edits often provide stronger behavioral control before the paper's narrow capability checks degrade. A final study asks whether cosine similarity with an evil contrastive direction can monitor emergent misalignment in fine-tuning updates, but the authors describe this result as preliminary.

## Extracted numbers

- The vector-construction protocol starts from 40 behavior questions, 5 positive system prompts, 5 negative system prompts, and 10 sampled responses per question-prompt pair. Half the questions are reserved for evaluation, and filtering leaves 500 to 900 examples per behavior set (Section 3).
- The LoRA fine-tunes update all modules with rank 32, alpha 16, no dropout, Adam weight decay 0.01, and 5 warm-up steps. The authors reserve 5% for validation and usually train steering-vector fine-tunes for about one epoch (Section 3 and Appendix B).
- The GCD-sycophancy dataset contains 7.62k training examples, divided equally between query-only and correct-solution formats, plus 1k test examples in each of three formats (Section 4.1.2).
- The bad-advice fine-tunes used in monitoring show 11% to 31% emergent misalignment and 80% to 88% coherence. The original model has 0% emergent misalignment and 90% coherence (Section 5, Figure 33).

## Relevance to experiment

The paper gives direct evidence that a behavior direction derived from opposing fine-tunes can be added to weights and can generalize beyond the vector-construction prompts. It therefore supplies a concrete weights-level internalization method and a comparison with online activation steering. The edit is unconditional: no learned gate or internal answerability readout controls when it acts. Its monitoring result concerns similarity between training updates and behavioral directions, not generation-time consultation of a model's own answerability signal.

## Claims

- Evidence label: method. Contrastive weight steering defines a behavior direction as the difference between positive and negative fine-tuned weights and adds a scaled direction to pretrained or task-fine-tuned weights (Section 3, Equation 1).
- Evidence label: intervention. On OOD factual, arithmetic, and evil-MCQA evaluations, contrastive weight steering usually achieves stronger behavioral control before the tested capability checks degrade than the paper's activation-steering baseline (Sections 4.1 and 4.2, Figures 2, 3, and 5).
- Evidence label: mitigation. Weight steering reduces sycophancy and restores refusal behavior induced or eroded by task-specific fine-tuning while retaining more task performance than the tested activation variants (Sections 4.1.2 and 4.3, Figures 3 and 6).
- Evidence label: preliminary monitoring. Bad-advice fine-tuning updates have higher cosine similarity with contrastive evil directions than good-advice and control updates, but the paper does not establish practical precision or realistic coverage (Section 5, Figures 8 and 9; Section 7).
