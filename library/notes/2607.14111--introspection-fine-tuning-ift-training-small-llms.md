## Abstract

Can small language models detect and report on perturbations their own internal activations? We investigate this question through the lens of activation steering: injecting concept vectors into a model's residual stream and measuring whether the model can accurately report on the perturbation. We first show that the binary detection paradigm used in prior work -- prompting the model to answer Yes'' or No'' to whether it detects an injected thought -- is confounded in small models, as steering biases the model toward affirmative responses regardless of the question content. We therefore propose two confound-free evaluation paradigms: sentence localization (identifying which of $N$ sentences was perturbed, chance $= 1/N$) and strength comparison (identifying which of two sentences received a stronger injection, chance $= 50\%$). Evaluating across six models from two families (Llama-3.2 and Gemma-4), we find that models as small as 2B parameters introspect reliably well above chance, and that introspective ability generally increases with scale. Llama-1B, however, performs at or below chance. We then introduce \emph{Introspection Fine-Tuning} (IFT): supervised fine-tuning on sentence-localization examples constructed from the model's own perturbed forward passes. IFT raises Llama-1B sentence-localization accuracy from $9.6\%$ to $60.6\%$ (a $6\times$ improvement), with gains generalizing zero-shot to the held-out strength-comparison task ($30.2\% \to 52.2\%$). IFT also improves introspection for 3B and 8B models, while inducing negligible degradation on standard capability benchmarks. Our results suggest that introspective ability is not fixed by scale alone: it can be directly trained, and doing so unlocks latent self-monitoring capacity with implications for AI transparency and alignment. Our code is \href{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/IFT-introspection-2092/README.md}{here}.

## Summary

The paper studies whether small open-weight language models can identify
controlled concept-vector injections into their own residual streams. It shows
that binary yes-or-no detection is confounded by a content-independent
affirmative-logit shift, then evaluates two relative tasks that avoid this
confound: sentence localization and injection-strength comparison. Across Llama
and Gemma models, above-chance performance generally grows with model size. The
paper then introduces Introspection Fine-Tuning, supervised training on
localization examples produced from the model's perturbed forward passes, and
reports large gains for Llama 1B with transfer to the untrained strength task.

## Extracted numbers

- Binary detection control: introspection-question and factual-no logit shifts
  correlate at r = 0.999 across 40 layer-strength configurations, with mean net
  signal -0.006 +/- 0.033 logits (Figure 1; Section 2.3.1).
- Scale sweep: Llama localization peaks at approximately 20 percent or lower for
  1B, approximately 65 percent for 3B, and approximately 88 percent for 8B;
  Gemma complex-dataset strength comparison rises from approximately 85 percent
  at 2B to approximately 95 percent at 26B A4B (Figure 2; Section 3).
- Llama 1B random-layer semantic IFT raises average localization from 9.6 percent
  to 60.6 percent; semantic-plus-reasoning IFT raises held-out strength
  comparison from 30.2 percent to 52.2 percent (Table 2; Section 4.3).
- Random-layer semantic localization exceeds fixed-layer semantic localization
  at 1B (60.6 vs. 28.2 percent), 3B (34.7 vs. 22.0 percent), and 8B (28.3 vs.
  22.7 percent) (Table 5; Section 4.3).
- Source-quality note: Appendix B repeats r = 0.999 and -0.006 +/- 0.033 but its
  final sentence says these values confirm genuine sensitivity. That
  interpretation contradicts the abstract, Section 2.3.1, Figure 1, and the
  immediately preceding Appendix B discussion, all of which identify an
  affirmative-response confound.

## Relevance to experiment

The relative evaluation designs offer useful controls for causal self-monitoring
tests because uniform answer bias cannot determine which position or strength is
selected. The random-layer training result also cautions against treating one
residual-stream depth as a portable monitoring location. For this research
program, the paper motivates separating readout quality from generic response
bias, counterbalancing position, and evaluating transfer across layers and
perturbation strengths.

## Claims

- Evidence label: causal diagnostic. Binary yes-or-no introspection detection is
  confounded in the tested small-model setting because the same affirmative
  logit shift appears on factual-no controls (Figure 1; Section 2.3.1).
- Evidence label: observational scaling result. Controlled activation
  introspection is reliably above chance above 1B parameters and generally
  improves with model scale, although the trend is not strictly monotonic
  (Figure 2; Section 3).
- Evidence label: fine-tuning intervention. Random-layer semantic IFT raises
  Llama 1B average sentence-localization accuracy from 9.6 percent to 60.6
  percent (Table 2; Section 4.3).
- Evidence label: held-out transfer. Training only on sentence localization can
  improve strength-comparison accuracy, with Llama 1B rising from 30.2 percent
  to 52.2 percent in the semantic-plus-reasoning condition (Table 2; Sections
  4.1 and 4.3).
