## Abstract

While reasoning in LLMs plays a natural role in math, code generation, and multi-hop factual questions, its effect on simple, single-hop factual questions remains unclear. Such questions do not require step-by-step logical decomposition, making the utility of reasoning highly counterintuitive. Nevertheless, we find that enabling reasoning substantially expands the capability boundary of the model's parametric knowledge recall, unlocking correct answers that are otherwise effectively unreachable. Why does reasoning aid parametric knowledge recall when there are no complex reasoning steps to be done? To answer this, we design a series of hypothesis-driven controlled experiments, and identify two key driving mechanisms: (1) a computational buffer effect, where the model uses the generated reasoning tokens to perform latent computation independent of their semantic content; and (2) factual priming, where generating topically related facts acts as a semantic bridge that facilitates correct answer retrieval. Importantly, this latter generative self-retrieval mechanism carries inherent risks: we demonstrate that hallucinating intermediate facts during reasoning increases the likelihood of hallucinations in the final answer. Finally, we show that our insights can be harnessed to directly improve model accuracy by prioritizing reasoning trajectories that contain hallucination-free factual statements.

## Summary

Gekhman et al. ask why turning on reasoning helps a model answer simple, single-hop factual questions, where there is nothing to decompose. Using hybrid models that toggle reasoning ON or OFF (so parametric knowledge is held fixed), they measure parametric recall with pass@k coverage and find reasoning ON consistently lifts pass@k over OFF across Gemini-2.5-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and Qwen3-32B on SimpleQA-Verified and EntityQuestions, sometimes nearly doubling it. A summary metric Omega (a k-weighted average of the relative ON-vs-OFF pass@k gain) shows the effect shrinks as base capability grows: weaker models hold more hidden knowledge that reasoning unlocks. Question-complexity metadata shows the gain is not about decomposing complex questions, since simple and complex subsets benefit about equally.

Two controlled mechanisms explain the gain. First, a content-independent computational buffer: overriding the trace with meaningless filler ("Let me think." repeated to length) still raises accuracy, and a single-token dummy does not, isolating extra computation as the cause. This effect is bounded and non-monotonic in length. Second, a content-dependent factual priming effect: extracting the related facts a trace recalls and re-prompting on just those facts recovers most of the reasoning gain even with reasoning disabled, evidence that generative self-retrieval surfaces latent knowledge. The priming mechanism is fragile: a large search-grounded audit shows traces with hallucinated intermediate facts are much less likely to yield correct final answers, even within the same question. Finally, a test-time selection strategy that keeps only traces recalling verified, hallucination-free facts improves accuracy.

- Setup: hybrid reasoning models Gemini-2.5-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen3-32B; datasets SimpleQA-Verified (1,000 examples) and EntityQuestions (4 relations, 250 each, 1,000 total); pass@k with up to N=100 samples (Section 2)
- Reasoning ON raises pass@k over OFF across all 3 models and both datasets, nearly doubling for Qwen3-32B on SimpleQA-Verified (Figure 1, Section 3)
- Reasoning-effectiveness Omega decreases as base model capability increases, and is consistently higher on SimpleQA than EntityQuestions (Figure 2, Section 3)
- Question complexity is a poor predictor: 903 of 1,000 SimpleQA-Verified questions are single-hop; Omega for Complex vs Simple subsets has overlapping 95% CIs, and for Gemini-2.5-Pro the Complex-subset CI crosses zero (Figure 3, Section 4)
- Computational buffer: a meaningless dummy trace raises pass@1 from 0.206 to 0.262 on SimpleQA-Verified and from 0.457 to 0.554 on EntityQuestions (Figure 4, Section 5.1)
- Dummy-length scaling is non-monotonic: pass@k improves up to roughly 2048 tokens then declines at 4096, 8192, and 16384 tokens on SimpleQA-Verified (Figure 5, Section 5.1)
- Factual priming: ON Facts and OFF Facts both beat dummy-length-matched controls; OFF Facts recovers most of the reasoning gain with reasoning disabled, and ON Facts matches full reasoning ON on EntityQuestions at far lower compute (Figure 6, Section 5.2)
- Hallucination audit: clean traces yield correct final answers 41.4% of the time vs 26.4% for hallucinated traces on SimpleQA-Verified, and 71.1% vs 32.2% on EntityQuestions (Section 5.3)
- Within-question control: fitted clean-vs-hallucinated regression slope is below 1 (0.84 on SimpleQA-Verified, 0.86 on EntityQuestions), so the gap persists after controlling for question difficulty (Figure 7, Section 5.3)
- Fact verifier (search-enabled Gemini-2.5-Flash, abstaining when undeterminable) reaches an estimated ~100% verification accuracy on human-checked correct/incorrect items (Section 5.3)
- Test-time selection (Table 1, expected accuracy): Regular 27.9 / 56.9; Only Facts 30.2 (+8.2%) / 58.4 (+2.6%); Only Correct Facts 31.3 (+12.2%) / 59.8 (+5.1%) on SimpleQA-Verified / EntityQuestions (Table 1, Section 5.4)

## Relevance to experiment

This paper sharpens two assumptions the Epistemic Humility study leans on. First, it is direct evidence for a generation-discrimination gap that reasoning can partly close: a model can hold a fact it cannot generate, and reasoning ON exposes it (pass@k often nearly doubling for weaker models). For Phase 1 this reframes what a calibration or abstention intervention is acting on: if SFT, DPO, or KTO changes a reasoning model, the relevant question is whether training widens or narrows the recall boundary that reasoning opens, not just whether top-1 accuracy moves. The Omega metric and the ON/OFF toggle give a concrete instrument for measuring that boundary before and after each training arm.

Second, it complicates reading a chain of thought as faithful. The computational-buffer result shows part of the recall gain is mechanical (meaningless filler tokens help), so a trace's apparent reasoning is not a trustworthy account of how the answer was reached. This is a caution for any mechanistic-interpretability work that treats reasoning text as a window into the computation: the fact-extraction and dummy-trace probes here are reusable templates for testing whether trace content is causally informative versus incidental.

Third, the hallucination-propagation finding (hallucinated intermediate facts make wrong final answers much more likely, even within a question) is a calibration target. It suggests an abstention or process-reward signal keyed on intermediate-fact correctness, which connects to the project's interest in keying confidence on internal signals rather than output-level certainty alone. The test-time selection result (up to +12.2% relative accuracy by keeping only verified-fact traces) is a cheap inference-time baseline the experiment could compare against a trained abstention arm.

## Claims

- Evidence label: behavioral measurement. Reasoning consistently increases pass@k parametric recall over reasoning OFF across three hybrid models and two closed-book QA datasets, nearly doubling for Qwen3-32B on SimpleQA-Verified, with weaker models benefiting most (Figure 1, Figure 2) [[reasoning-expands-parametric-knowledge-boundary]]
- Evidence label: controlled experiment. A semantically meaningless dummy reasoning trace raises pass@1 from 0.206 to 0.262 (SimpleQA-Verified) and 0.457 to 0.554 (EntityQuestions), and a single-token dummy does not, isolating a content-independent computational buffer that is bounded and non-monotonic in length (Figure 4, Figure 5) [[computational-buffer-effect]]
- Evidence label: controlled experiment. Conditioning only on facts extracted from a reasoning trace recovers most of the pass@k gain even with reasoning disabled and beats dummy-length controls, showing factual priming via generative self-retrieval drives the majority of reasoning gains (Figure 6) [[factual-priming]]
- Evidence label: large-scale audit. Reasoning traces containing a hallucinated intermediate fact yield correct final answers far less often (41.4% vs 26.4% on SimpleQA-Verified; 71.1% vs 32.2% on EntityQuestions), a gap that survives within-question difficulty control (regression slope 0.84 and 0.86) (Section 5.3, Figure 7) [[reasoning-hallucinations-cause-answer-hallucinations]]
- Evidence label: test-time selection. Keeping only traces that recall verified, hallucination-free facts raises expected accuracy by +12.2% on SimpleQA-Verified and +5.1% on EntityQuestions over the regular baseline (Table 1) [[factual-priming-improves-recall]]
