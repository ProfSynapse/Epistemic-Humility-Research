## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have been shown to possess impressive capabilities, while also raising crucial concerns about the faithfulness of their responses. A primary issue arising in this context is the management of (un)answerable queries by LLMs, which often results in hallucinatory behavior due to overconfidence. In this paper, we explore the behavior of LLMs when presented with (un)answerable queries. We ask: do models represent the fact that the question is (un)answerable when generating a hallucinatory answer? Our results show strong indications that such models encode the answerability of an input query, with the representation of the first decoded token often being a strong indicator. These findings shed new light on the spatial organization within the latent representations of LLMs, unveiling previously unexplored facets of these models. Moreover, they pave the way for the development of improved decoding techniques with better adherence to factual generation, particularly in scenarios where query (un)answerability is a concern.

## Summary

Slobodkin et al. investigate whether instruction-finetuned LLMs internally encode the answerability of queries they nonetheless answer with hallucinated responses. Three models -- Flan-T5xxl (11B), Flan-UL2 (20B), and OPT-IML (30B) -- are tested on three reading-comprehension QA benchmarks (SQuAD 2.0, Natural Questions, MuSiQue) across three experimental prongs: prompt manipulation that adds a natural-language hint about the possibility of unanswerability; beam relaxation, which widens decoding beam search and checks whether any non-top beam explicitly acknowledges unanswerability; and linear probing of the last hidden layer's first-token embedding, followed by LEACE-based causal erasure of the identified answerability subspace.

The core finding is a sharp internal-behavior dissociation. Models hallucinate answers to unanswerable questions at high rates under standard prompting, yet a logistic-regression probe trained on 800 balanced examples of last-layer first-token embeddings achieves above 75% F1 for all nine model-dataset pairings, including 89.9% for Flan-T5xxl on SQuAD and 87.3% for Flan-UL2 on NQ. The probe transfers across datasets, indicating an abstract subspace rather than dataset-specific features. LEACE erasure of that subspace causally degrades unanswerability classification from 50.1% to 31.2% F1 on Flan-UL2/SQuAD (regular beam), confirming the subspace is behaviorally active. The generation-discrimination gap appears at the prompt level too: adding a unanswerability hint raises zero-shot detection F1 by up to 79.9 points, while beam relaxation reveals that unanswerability-acknowledging responses are already present in lower-ranked beams, confirming the model encodes the information it fails to surface through greedy decoding.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper provides the direct empirical foundation for the Amendment O/P probe design used in this project. Amendment P confirmed near-identical transfer (AUROC 0.983) from KUQ to SelfAware, replicating Figure 6 of this paper at a different model family -- an independent replication of the cross-dataset transfer finding. The LEACE erasure result (unanswerability F1 crashing from 50.1 to 31.2 after subspace erasure) motivates Amendment R: if the answerability subspace is causally active, training the model to express it through the output channel rather than encoding it silently should produce measurable gains in output-level abstention, which is precisely what the axis-distillation approach targets.

The prompt-hint result (up to 79.9 F1 points zero-shot) establishes a no-training bar for unanswerability detection but simultaneously illustrates the core tradeoff the locked training-regimen arms are designed to resolve: prompt-based surfacing of the latent answerability signal costs 8.3% average F1 on answerable questions, whereas training-based approaches aim to recover the signal without that answerable-question penalty. The beam relaxation result adds a third line of evidence -- alongside the probe and the prompt-hint -- that the greedy decoding policy, not the model's internal representation, is the primary bottleneck for expressing known unanswerability, reinforcing the design logic of the confidence-head engine in Amendment Q.
