## Abstract

In this work, we consider the problem of uncertainty estimation for Transformer-based models. We investigate the applicability of uncertainty estimates based on dropout usage at the inference stage (Monte Carlo dropout). The series of experiments on natural language understanding tasks shows that the resulting uncertainty estimates improve the quality of detection of error-prone instances.
Special attention is paid to the construction of computationally inexpensive estimates via Monte Carlo dropout and Determinantal Point Processes.

## Summary

Shelmanov et al. evaluate uncertainty estimation for transformer-based natural language understanding models using inference-time dropout and cheaper approximations based on Determinantal Point Processes. The paper is an early NLP reference for transformer uncertainty estimates as an error-detection signal.

## Relevance to experiment

Relevant as a pre-LLM transformer uncertainty baseline. It informs whether uncertainty signals derived from stochastic inference or diversity can detect likely errors without directly training abstention behavior.

## Claims

- Monte Carlo dropout at inference time can produce uncertainty estimates that improve detection of error-prone natural language understanding instances. (Abstract) [[calibration]]
- Determinantal Point Processes are explored as a computationally cheaper route to uncertainty estimates. (Abstract) [[consistency-based-confidence]]
- The paper frames uncertainty estimation as an operational detection problem for transformer errors rather than only as post-hoc confidence reporting. (Abstract; Introduction) [[expected-calibration-error]]
