## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) have impressive capabilities, but are prone to outputting falsehoods. Recent work has developed techniques for inferring whether a LLM is telling the truth by training probes on the LLM's internal activations. However, this line of work is controversial, with some authors pointing out failures of these probes to generalize in basic ways, among other conceptual issues. In this work, we use high-quality datasets of simple true/false statements to study in detail the structure of LLM representations of truth, drawing on three lines of evidence: 1. Visualizations of LLM true/false statement representations, which reveal clear linear structure. 2. Transfer experiments in which probes trained on one dataset generalize to different datasets. 3. Causal evidence obtained by surgically intervening in a LLM's forward pass, causing it to treat false statements as true and vice versa. Overall, we present evidence that at sufficient scale, LLMs linearly represent the truth or falsehood of factual statements. We also show that simple difference-in-mean probes generalize as well as other probing techniques while identifying directions which are more causally implicated in model outputs.

## Summary

Marks and Tegmark ask whether large language models contain a single linear truth direction in their residual-stream representations, or whether observed linear separability of true and false statements is an artifact of dataset-specific correlations. They study this question on LLaMA-13B and LLaMA-2-13B across eleven curated and uncurated datasets using three converging lines of evidence: PCA visualizations, cross-dataset generalization of linear probes, and causal interventions that surgically shift activations along candidate truth directions.

The PCA evidence is striking: true and false statements separate cleanly in the top principal components of residual-stream activations at the end-of-clause token (layer 13 for LLaMA-13B, layer 14 for LLaMA-2-13B). Linear structure emerges rapidly in early-to-middle layers and arrives later for structurally complex statements such as conjunctions. However, naive truth directions do not always align across datasets: the cities and neg_cities directions are approximately orthogonal, and the larger_than and smaller_than directions are approximately antipodal. The paper argues this is best explained by misalignment from correlational inconsistency (MCI): the model linearly represents both truth and non-truth features that correlate with truth on narrow distributions, and these correlations are inconsistent across datasets, so training on both a dataset and its negations produces a better-calibrated truth direction.

The transfer generalization experiments confirm this picture. No matter which probing technique is used, probes trained only on numerical-comparison datasets achieve over 90% accuracy on the Spanish-English translation dataset. Training on cities plus neg_cities consistently beats training on cities alone across all techniques.

On method comparison, the paper introduces mass-mean probing: compute the vector from the mean of false-statement activations to the mean of true-statement activations, normalize it, and use it directly as the probe direction. This is optimization-free (for the base direction theta_mm, though the IID variant applies a covariance-inverse transform). It generalizes better than logistic regression and identifies directions more causally implicated in model outputs. The causal advantage matters because the paper also measures normalized indirect effect (NIE): how much does adding a truth vector to the relevant hidden states move the model probability from false toward true (or vice versa), relative to the natural gap between true and false statement probabilities? The best intervention, using the mass-mean probe trained on cities plus neg_cities on LLaMA-13B, achieves NIE 0.95 in the false-to-true direction and NIE 1.41 in the true-to-false direction, the latter exceeding 1.0 by overshooting the natural true-statement confidence level. The best true-to-false intervention swings the model from predicting TRUE at 77% to FALSE at 92%.

The plausibility-confound control is the likely dataset: statements whose final token is either the most likely or the 100th most likely completion by LLaMA-13B. The logistic-regression probe trained on likely achieves NIE 0.01 to 0.10 on LLaMA-13B, near-zero. However, the mass-mean probe on likely achieves NIE 0.61 and 0.60 (false-to-true and true-to-false on LLaMA-13B), substantially lower than the best truth-trained probes but not negligible. The paper concludes that the model represents truth-relevant information beyond text plausibility, but the mass-mean likely result shows that plausibility directions are also causally non-trivial.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper provides three things the Epistemic Humility experiment needs: a causal (not merely correlational) benchmark for truth-direction geometry, a method comparison that motivates using mass-mean probing over logistic regression for mechinterp sweeps, and a confound-control design (the likely dataset) that the experiment can replicate to check whether training shifts plausibility directions rather than truth directions.

The NIE metric is a direct complement to activation-addition and logit-diagnostic approaches already planned in mechanism program. If a Qwen3 base and a Qwen3 DPO-abstention model have different NIE profiles on a held-out factual dataset, that is mechanistic evidence that training moved the truth direction, not just the output behavior. The paper's finding that training on statements plus their negations stabilizes the direction (the MCI result) suggests that a diverse probe training set is essential for any mechanism program probe to be trusted as a truth-direction readout rather than a plausibility-direction readout.

The MCI finding also connects directly to the existing mechanism estimator-divergence-invalidates-single-probe-faithfulness: both results say that the choice of probe training distribution mediates what the probe actually reads. For the locked training-regimen arms, this means that a probe run after SFT or DPO training should be evaluated with a diverse, negation-augmented training set, otherwise the probe may be capturing the training distribution's surface correlates rather than the model's truth representation. The paper does not report a training before/after delta and does not populate effects.csv directly, but it establishes the geometric substrate that motivates comparing probe geometry before and after each locked training-regimen arm.
