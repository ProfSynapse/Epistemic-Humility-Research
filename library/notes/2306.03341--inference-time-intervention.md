## Abstract

We introduce Inference-Time Intervention (ITI), a technique designed to enhance the "truthfulness" of large language models (LLMs). ITI operates by shifting model activations during inference, following a set of directions across a limited number of attention heads. This intervention significantly improves the performance of LLaMA models on the TruthfulQA benchmark. On an instruction-finetuned LLaMA called Alpaca, ITI improves its truthfulness from 32.5% to 65.1%. We identify a tradeoff between truthfulness and helpfulness and demonstrate how to balance it by tuning the intervention strength. ITI is minimally invasive and computationally inexpensive. Moreover, the technique is data efficient: while approaches like RLHF require extensive annotations, ITI locates truthful directions using only few hundred examples. Our findings suggest that LLMs may have an internal representation of the likelihood of something being true, even as they produce falsehoods on the surface.

## Summary

Li et al. introduce Inference-Time Intervention (ITI), which improves LLM
truthfulness by shifting attention-head activations along directions correlated
with factually correct statements during decoding. The motivation is a roughly
40-point gap on LLaMA-7B between how well a linear probe reads truth off the
model's activations and how truthfully the model actually generates, which the
paper operationalizes as the generation-discrimination gap (a term it credits to
Saunders et al.): the intermediate representations encode truth-relevant
information the output does not express. ITI first finds a sparse set of
attention heads with high probe accuracy for truthfulness, then adds a scaled
mass-mean shift vector to those head outputs at every decoding step. The finding
that matters for epistemic-humility work is that an interpretable truth direction
lives in a subset of early-to-middle-layer heads, and that causally pushing
activations along it raises TruthfulQA scores at near-zero compute, using only a
few hundred labeled examples rather than the annotation cost of RLHF. The
intervention strength alpha exposes a tunable truthfulness-helpfulness tradeoff.

## Extracted numbers

Provenance: arXiv HTML render (`library/fulltext/2306.03341.html`), Tables 1-4
and §3-5; verified against the source on 2026-06-24. This paper reports steering
and probe quality, not a training before/after humility delta, so it does not map
onto the `effects.csv` intervention schema (see Relevance).

- TruthfulQA True*Informative, LLaMA-7B: baseline 30.5% to Baseline + ITI 43.5%,
  a 13-point gain (Table 1).
- TruthfulQA True (generation track), LLaMA-7B: baseline 31.6% to ITI 49.1%
  (Table 1).
- ITI beats supervised finetuning on True*Informative (43.5% vs SFT 36.1%) but at
  a higher cross-entropy cost (CE 2.48 vs SFT 2.10): a truthfulness gain with some
  fluency penalty (Table 1).
- Few-shot prompting True*Informative: 49.5% to +ITI 51.4% (Table 1).
- Instruction-tuned bases: Alpaca 32.5% to +ITI 65.1% (+32.6 points); Vicuna
  51.5% to +ITI 74.0% (Table 2).
- Direction-estimator ablation: mass-mean shift 42.3% True*Informative vs
  probe-weight direction 34.8% vs CCS direction 33.4% (Table 3, 2-fold CV).
- Probe accuracy for truthfulness: best single head 83.3% (layer 14, head 18),
  all-head concatenated 84.4% (§3.2, §5.5); against ~31.6% generation True, this
  is the ~40-point generation-discrimination gap the paper reports (§1).
- Out-of-distribution, zero-shot (no new directions learned): MMLU 35.71% to
  40.16%; Natural Questions 46.6% to 51.3%; TriviaQA 89.6% to 91.1% (Table 4).
- Tuned hyperparameters: K=48 heads, alpha=15, swept on 10% of TruthfulQA (81
  questions) (§4.3, Figure 4).

## Relevance to experiment

ITI bears on locked training-regimen and mechanism program in three ways. First, it shows a small
supervised set (under 100 labeled examples, as few as 40) is enough to locate
truth-correlated directions in attention heads, which raises the question of
whether Qwen3 after SFT or DPO abstention training has sharper or more
distributed truth directions than the base model. A probe run before and after
each locked training-regimen arm could test this. Second, the evidence that probe accuracy
(83-84%) far exceeds generation accuracy (~40%) on the same model is the
empirical grounding for the mechinterp hypothesis: trained abstention
behavior may not fully express the model's internally represented uncertainty.
Third, ITI's truthfulness-helpfulness tradeoff (tunable via alpha, with more
intervention producing more "I have no comment" responses and lower informative
scores) maps onto the over-abstention / alignment-tax concern that motivates
comparing SFT, DPO, and KTO arms, but reaches it through an activation-steering
knob rather than a loss function, giving a mechanistic contrast case. The paper
reports no training delta on a calibration or abstention metric, so it does not
populate `effects.csv` directly, but it backs the hypothesis that activation-space
signal and output-level humility metrics can diverge after training.

## Claims

- ITI raises LLaMA-7B TruthfulQA True*Informative from 30.5% to 43.5% by shifting a sparse set of attention-head activations along a mass-mean truth direction at inference, with no weight update. (Table 1) [[inference-time-intervention]]
- A linear probe reads truthfulness off LLaMA-7B activations at up to 84.4% accuracy while the model generates truthfully only about 31.6% of the time, a roughly 40-point generation-discrimination gap. (Section 3.2, Section 1) [[generation-discrimination-gap]]
- The mass-mean shift direction (42.3% True*Informative) outperforms the probe-weight direction (34.8%) and the CCS direction (33.4%), and the gains transfer zero-shot to MMLU, Natural Questions, and TriviaQA. (Tables 3-4) [[mass-mean-probing]]
- Intervention strength alpha trades truthfulness against informativeness: stronger steering raises True but lowers Informative, so their product peaks at an intermediate alpha. (Figure 4) [[truthfulness-helpfulness-tradeoff-under-activation-steering]]
