## Abstract

Alignment is a standard procedure to fine-tune pre-trained large language models (LLMs) to follow natural language instructions and serve as helpful AI assistants. We have observed, however, that the conventional alignment process fails to enhance the factual accuracy of LLMs, and often leads to the generation of more false facts (i.e. hallucination). In this paper, we study how to make the LLM alignment process more factual, by first identifying factors that lead to hallucination in both alignment steps:\ supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). In particular, we find that training the LLM on new knowledge or unfamiliar texts can encourage hallucination. This makes SFT less factual as it trains on human labeled data that may be novel to the LLM. Furthermore, reward functions used in standard RL can also encourage hallucination, because it guides the LLM to provide more helpful responses on a diverse set of instructions, often preferring longer and more detailed responses. Based on these observations, we propose factuality-aware alignment, comprised of factuality-aware SFT and factuality-aware RL through direct preference optimization. Experiments show that our proposed factuality-aware alignment guides LLMs to output more factual responses while maintaining instruction-following capability.

## Summary

Lin et al. diagnose two ways the standard alignment pipeline degrades factuality and propose FLAME (Factuality-Aware Alignment for Large Language Models) to fix both. In the SFT stage the problem is training-data provenance: fine-tuning on human-written or retrieval-augmented responses introduces facts the base model has never seen, nudging it to confabulate when it encounters similar prompts. In the DPO stage the problem is reward scope: a single instruction-following scalar rewards length and detail, which correlates with more false claims. FLAME addresses each stage separately. Factuality-aware SFT (SFT^flame) first classifies every instruction as fact-requiring or not; for fact-requiring instructions it replaces human-authored targets with responses sampled from the pre-trained model itself via few-shot prompting, keeping human responses only for non-fact instructions. Factuality-aware DPO (DPO^flame) adds a second reward track alongside instruction-following: a retrieval-augmented factuality reward model built on atomic-fact decomposition and claim verification selects factuality preference pairs, which are combined with instruction-following pairs during DPO. Both components require the fact-based instruction classifier; ablation shows removing it drops Alpaca Eval win rate by 3.6 points. Experiments on Llama-2 70B show the full FLAME pipeline achieves Bio FActScore 47.9 versus 42.3 for standard SFT+DPO, a +5.6-point gain, while maintaining a 51.2% Alpaca Eval win rate against the SFT+DPO reference at 50.0%.

## Relevance to experiment

FLAME is directly relevant to the experiment on two dimensions. First, the SFT analysis confirms the mechanism that already appears in the graph as sft-unknown-examples-drive-hallucination and sft-beyond-knowledge-boundary-hallucination: the pilot study shows this is not a theoretical concern but a measurable effect large enough to fully negate the quality advantage of RAG-augmented training data. Any Phase 1 SFT arm that uses external or human-authored targets on knowledge-intensive prompts should treat this as a live risk and track FActScore alongside calibration metrics. Second, the DPO analysis shows that instruction-following preference optimization degrades factuality by increasing response length and false-fact density, a pattern complementary to rlhf-helpfulness-bias-suppresses-refusal and reward-model-overestimation-undermines-rl-factuality. The FLAME fix, adding a second factuality reward track and routing it only to fact-requiring instructions, offers a recipe that could be adapted to the DPO arm in Phase 1: factuality preference pairs constructed from the model's own generations, not human or retrieval-augmented targets. The factuality reward model built on atomic-fact decomposition and retrieval-augmented verification (Kendall tau 0.34 with FAVA annotations) is a concrete proxy signal that could substitute for or complement FActScore-based selection without requiring a separate oracle. The length increase under standard DPO (1221 to 1494 tokens in biography) relative to FLAME's more moderate lengths is a confound to watch in any generation-length-sensitive evaluation. The sentence-level fact classifier ablation (Table 5, row 5 vs 4) is also informative: finer-grained factuality routing can backfire when the sub-classifier is noisy, suggesting the Phase 1 pipeline should avoid over-segmenting the reward signal without validating the classifier quality first.

## Claims

- Fine-tuning a pre-trained LLM on RAG-augmented responses that are more factual than its own outputs nonetheless decreases FActScore (37.9 to 35.7 in SFT, 41.6 to 23.5 in DPO) because the external knowledge is unfamiliar to the model and induces confabulation. (Table 1, rows 3-6) [[sft-unknown-examples-drive-hallucination]]
- FLAME's factuality-aware SFT, which routes fact-based instructions to PT's own few-shot generations and non-fact instructions to human responses, raises Bio FActScore from 44.7 to 49.5 on Llama-2 70B while maintaining a 51.2% Alpaca Eval win rate. (Table 2, rows 1 and 3) [[flame-factuality-aware-alignment]]
- Standard instruction-following DPO degrades Bio FActScore from 44.7 to 42.3 while increasing the error count from 26.8 to 35.0 per response, and it inflates average generation length from 1221 to 1494 tokens, confirming that the helpfulness reward drives both verbosity and hallucination. (Table 3 rows 1-2; Table 7 rows 1-2) [[rlhf-helpfulness-bias-suppresses-refusal]]
- Full FLAME (SFT^flame + DPO^flame) achieves Bio FActScore 47.9, Alpaca Fact FActScore 48.7, and FAVA FActScore 58.9, versus SFT+DPO baselines of 42.3, 41.6, and 52.9 respectively, at equivalent 51.2% Alpaca Eval win rate, showing factuality gains propagate across knowledge-intensive domains without an instruction-following penalty. (Table 3, rows 2 and 7) [[flame-factuality-aware-alignment]]
- Fact-based instruction classification is necessary for FLAME's gains: removing it drops Alpaca Eval win rate by 3.6 points (51.2 to 47.6) and Bio FActScore by 1.1 points (49.5 to 48.4), because without routing the system cannot apply self-generation only where it helps. (Table 5, rows 1 and 2) [[flame-fact-instruction-routing]]
