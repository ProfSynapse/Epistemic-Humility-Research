## Abstract

Large language models are prone to hallucinating factually incorrect statements. A key source of these errors is exposure to new factual information through supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which can increase hallucinations w.r.t. knowledge acquired during pre-training. In this work, we explore whether SFT-induced hallucinations can be mitigated using established tools from the continual learning literature, since they arise as a by-product of knowledge degradation during training. We propose a self-distillation-based SFT method that facilitates effective factual learning while minimizing hallucinations w.r.t. pre-existing knowledge by regularizing output-distribution drift. We also show that, in settings where new knowledge acquisition is unnecessary, suppressing factual plasticity by freezing parameter groups, can preserve task performance while reducing hallucinations. Lastly, we investigate the mechanism behind SFT-induced hallucinations through three hypotheses: capacity limitations, behavior cloning, and localized interference. Our experiments show that a main driver is interference among overlapping semantic representations, and that self-distillation succeeds by mitigating this interference.

## Summary

Kaplan et al. reframe SFT-induced hallucinations as factual forgetting arising from continual learning dynamics rather than from inference-time confabulation or pre-training gaps. Using the SLiCK framework on EntityQuestions with Qwen2.5-1.5B as the primary model (replicated on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B), they establish a clean three-way split: HighlyKnown training examples for task-format learning, Unknown examples for factual plasticity, and a held-out HighlyKnown set as a forgetting probe. Standard SFT on the mixed set produces a characteristic two-stage pattern: rapid task-format acquisition then progressive degradation of held-out accuracy by approximately 15 percentage points, onset tightly coupled to when Unknown-fact acquisition begins. When Unknown examples are excluded, held-out accuracy stays flat throughout training, confirming that factual plasticity drives forgetting. Selective parameter freezing reveals the FFN layer as the primary locus of new-fact storage: FFN-only training closely tracks standard SFT in both plasticity (D_U = 0.941) and forgetting (held-out stability = 0.782), while attention-only training suppresses plasticity to near chance (D_U = 0.010) and preserves stability (0.931), approaching the Only-Known upper bound. Self-distillation, a continual-learning technique that regularizes the student model to stay near a frozen teacher's output distribution, reduces held-out forgetting from approximately 15% to approximately 3% while preserving full factual plasticity. Mechanistic analysis using synthetic entity names varying in surface-form similarity to real entities shows that UUID-style identifiers with no lexical overlap with existing entities produce near-zero forgetting (0-4%) at all scales from 10 to 1M new facts, whereas name-like entities that share token substrings with real locations produce sharply increasing forgetting with scale. Hidden-state cosine drift at layer 14 of Qwen2.5 rises to approximately 5% in all conditions during task-format learning, then continues to approximately 11% only under SFT on semantically overlapping keys; both UUID-SFT and self-distillation stabilize near 5%. L2 weight regularization matched to self-distillation in gradient magnitude leaves forgetting near 10 percentage points, ruling out generic weight-drift suppression as the mechanism. These converging results support a structural interference account: gradient updates for new semantically similar entities propagate through shared representational regions and corrupt nearby held-out entity representations, and self-distillation works because its output-level constraint targets precisely that propagation pathway. MaybeKnown facts, whose encodings are less robust, show amplified forgetting of approximately 18-19 percentage points under standard SFT for the larger models, with self-distillation mitigating approximately 90% of that drop.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper bears directly on Phase 1, Phase 3, and the dataset composition decisions in Synaptic Tuner.

For Phase 1 training arm design, the plasticity-stability framing gives a principled baseline for predicting which SFT configurations induce hallucinations on pre-training knowledge. The ~15% degradation under standard SFT with Unknown-fact mixing is a concrete reference point against which the abstention-arm training recipes (SFT, DPO, KTO) should be benchmarked: any arm that mixes training examples outside the model's SLiCK HighlyKnown tier is exposing itself to the same interference dynamic. Self-distillation is a plug-in mitigation that the Phase 1 SFT arm could adopt to limit hallucination cost while retaining factual plasticity. The paper's self-distillation formulation (one-epoch teacher snapshot, lambda=1, tau=0.5) is specified at implementation level.

For dataset curation and the abstention training signal, the SLiCK-based split into HighlyKnown/MaybeKnown/Unknown is directly reusable as a pre-processing step before any Phase 1 arm to predict and control hallucination risk from training mix. The MaybeKnown forgetting result (18-19pp drop, amplified relative to HighlyKnown) implies that the boundary-case knowledge tier is the highest-risk region for inadvertent hallucination, though the paper does not prescribe a specific evaluation protocol for this; the implication for Phase 1 is that held-out MaybeKnown accuracy is a more sensitive probe of representational interference than HighlyKnown alone.

For Phase 3 mech-interp, the layer-14 cosine drift trajectory is an actionable diagnostic: it distinguishes task-format adaptation (shared 5% drift plateau) from representational interference (additional ~6% drift under semantic-key SFT). This is a concrete probe that can be layered on top of the existing Phase 3 hidden-state candidate inventory to attribute forgetting to specific layers. The attention-vs-FFN and single-projection granularity findings provide an explicit parameter-group hypothesis for where to look for interference effects in the mechanistic sweep. The L2 null result rules out a generic weight-magnitude explanation, narrowing the causal claim to output-distribution-level interference.
