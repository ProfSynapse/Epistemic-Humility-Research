## Abstract

Abstention Ability (AA) is a critical aspect of Large Language Model (LLM) reliability, referring to an LLM's capability to withhold responses when uncertain or lacking a definitive answer, without compromising performance. Although previous studies have attempted to improve AA, they lack a standardised evaluation method and remain unsuitable for black-box models where token prediction probabilities are inaccessible. This makes comparative analysis challenging, especially for state-of-the-art closed-source commercial LLMs. This paper bridges this gap by introducing a black-box evaluation approach and a new dataset, Abstain-QA, crafted to rigorously assess AA across varied question types (answerable and unanswerable), domains (well-represented and under-represented), and task types (fact centric and reasoning). We also propose a new confusion matrix, the ''Answerable-Unanswerable Confusion Matrix'' (AUCM) which serves as the basis for evaluating AA, by offering a structured and precise approach for assessment. Finally, we explore the impact of three prompting strategies-Strict Prompting, Verbal Confidence Thresholding, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT)-on improving AA. Our results indicate that even powerful models like GPT-4, Mixtral 8x22b encounter difficulties with abstention; however, strategic approaches such as Strict prompting and CoT can enhance this capability.

## Summary

Madhusudhan et al. introduce Abstain-QA, a black-box evaluation benchmark for LLM abstention ability, and the Answerable-Unanswerable Confusion Matrix (AUCM) as its evaluation framework. The benchmark collects 2900 zero-shot MCQA samples from three sources: Pop-QA (1000 questions, 10 relationships x 100), MMLU (1000 questions, 10 subjects x 100), and Carnatic-QA (900 questions across 9 task templates x 100). Each sub-dataset provides an equal split of answerable and unanswerable questions; unanswerable items are created by replacing the correct option with a plausible distractor, with IDK/None-of-the-Above as the designated target. The AUCM formalizes four outcomes (TP, FP, TN, FN) and derives Answerable Accuracy (AAC), Unanswerable Accuracy (UAC), and Abstention Rate alongside standard Precision. Five state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-4-32K, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Mixtral 8x7b, Mixtral 8x22b) are evaluated across three abstain clauses (Standard, Abstain, Extreme-Abstain) and three task-prompt regimes (Base, Verbal Confidence at threshold 3, Chain-of-Thought). Key findings: CoT dramatically raises UAC on CQA under-represented domain questions but consistently reduces AAC, indicating over-abstention on answerable queries in unfamiliar domains; on well-represented factoid domains (PopQA), CoT and Abstain clauses improve UAC while preserving high AAC for capable models; verbal confidence thresholding does not consistently outperform the Base setting across models, with only GPT-4-Turbo showing slight reliable gains; GPT-3.5-Turbo nearly fails to abstain on CQA under Standard/Base (UAC 0.6%), while structured prompting raises UAC dramatically; and abstention ability is both domain-sensitive and capability-sensitive, with gains from stricter clauses consistent for larger models but inconsistent or harmful for smaller ones.

## Relevance to experiment

Abstain-QA directly instantiates the answerable/unanswerable decomposition that the Phase 1 training study must track. The AUCM's four-cell structure separates over-abstention (FN on answerable) from under-abstention (FP on unanswerable), providing the exact two-axis view the SFT-vs-DPO-vs-KTO comparison needs to avoid trading one failure mode for the other. The CQA finding that CoT sharply raises UAC at the cost of AAC is a prompting-level precursor to the same tradeoff the training arms aim to navigate: a method that maximizes abstention on unknowns but penalizes answers on knowns may look good on UAC in isolation while degrading overall reliability. The verbal confidence failure (quantized self-report does not reliably track correctness) complements the existing mechanism node on verbalized-prob-generalizes-logit-overfits-distribution-shift: even at the prompting level, verbal confidence is unreliable for smaller models. The paper reports no training deltas, so it does not populate effects.csv, but it supplies the vocabulary (AAC, UAC, AUCM) and domain-contrast design (well-represented PopQA vs under-represented CQA) that the experiment's evaluation layer should adopt.

## Claims

- Abstain-QA uses the AUCM four-cell matrix (TP, FP, TN, FN based on answerable/unanswerable question type and model answer/abstain response) to derive AAC, UAC, Abstention Rate, and Precision as complementary abstention metrics, enabling black-box evaluation without access to token probabilities. (Section 3.3) [[answerable-unanswerable-confusion-matrix]]
- On CQA (under-represented domain), GPT-4-Turbo with CoT and the Standard clause achieves UAC 67.1% versus 32.8% under Base/Standard, a 34-point gain, but CoT combined with the Abstain clause drops AAC from 48.0% to 32.8%, demonstrating that step-by-step reasoning causes systematic over-abstention on answerable queries in low-familiarity domains. (Table 1, CQA section) [[over-abstention]]
- Verbal confidence thresholding (threshold=3) does not consistently improve over the Base setting for most models, indicating that models struggle to quantify uncertainty via verbal confidence outputs; GPT-4-Turbo is a partial exception with slight reliable gains. (Section 4, Comparison of Task Prompt Types) [[verbalized-confidence]]
- GPT-3.5-Turbo Standard/Base UAC on CQA is 0.6%, illustrating near-total failure to abstain on unanswerable questions from an under-represented domain without any structured prompting, confirming that abstention ability is heavily dependent on model capability and domain familiarity. (Table 1, CQA section) [[abstention]]
- Carnatic-QA is an expert-validated under-represented domain dataset of 900 MCQA questions built from 272 curated ragas across 9 task templates, providing a controlled test bed for knowledge-boundary abstention where LLMs lack pre-training coverage. (Section 2) [[carnatic-qa]]
