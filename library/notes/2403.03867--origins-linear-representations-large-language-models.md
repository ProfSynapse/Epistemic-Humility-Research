## Abstract

Recent works have argued that high-level semantic concepts are encoded "linearly" in the representation space of large language models. In this work, we study the origins of such linear representations. To that end, we introduce a simple latent variable model to abstract and formalize the concept dynamics of the next token prediction. We use this formalism to show that the next token prediction objective (softmax with cross-entropy) and the implicit bias of gradient descent together promote the linear representation of concepts. Experiments show that linear representations emerge when learning from data matching the latent variable model, confirming that this simple structure already suffices to yield linear representations. We additionally confirm some predictions of the theory using the LLaMA-2 large language model, giving evidence that the simplified model yields generalizable insights.

## Claims

- Theorem 3: under the log-odds matching condition, concept steering vectors for a fixed concept are mutually parallel in the LLM representation space regardless of other concept values; this holds unconditionally for jointly independent concepts. (Theorem 3, Section 3.1) [[linear-representation-hypothesis]]
- Theorems 4–5: gradient descent on the NTP exponential loss subproblem converges to cos(Δ_{c1,i}, Δ_{c2,i}) = 1 in the limit; with joint embedding/unembedding training the two spaces also align. Simulation experiments confirm cosine similarities of 0.97–0.997 across concept counts 3–7. (Theorems 4–5, Section 3.2; Table in Section 5.1) [[implicit-bias-gradient-descent]]
- Corollary 8: under the latent conditional model, steering vectors for statistically independent (graph-separated) concepts are exactly orthogonal when representations are matched; LLaMA-2 experiments show same-concept vectors are more aligned than cross-concept vectors (Fig. 3). (Corollary 8, Section 4; Figure 3, Section 5.2) [[concept-orthogonality]]
- LLaMA-2-7B embedding and unembedding steering vectors show nontrivial alignment for matching language-translation and Winograd concepts, while unrelated concepts are represented nearly orthogonally, confirming the latent conditional model's predictions for real LLMs. (Figures 3–4, Section 5.2) [[llama-2]]
