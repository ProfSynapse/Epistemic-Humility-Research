## Abstract

Reasoning language models have set state-of-the-art (SOTA) records on many challenging benchmarks, enabled by multi-step reasoning induced using reinforcement learning. However, like previous language models, reasoning models are prone to generating confident, plausible responses that are incorrect (hallucinations). Knowing when and how much to trust these models is critical to the safe deployment of reasoning models in real-world applications. To this end, we explore uncertainty quantification of reasoning models in this work. Specifically, we ask three fundamental questions: First, are reasoning models well-calibrated? Second, does deeper reasoning improve model calibration? Finally, inspired by humans' innate ability to double-check their thought processes to verify the validity of their answers and their confidence, we ask: can reasoning models improve their calibration by explicitly reasoning about their chain-of-thought traces? We introduce introspective uncertainty quantification (UQ) to explore this direction. In extensive evaluations on SOTA reasoning models across a broad range of benchmarks, we find that reasoning models: (i) are typically overconfident, with self-verbalized confidence estimates often greater than 85% particularly for incorrect responses, (ii) become even more overconfident with deeper reasoning, and (iii) can become better calibrated through introspection (e.g., o3-Mini and DeepSeek R1) but not uniformly (e.g., Claude 3.7 Sonnet becomes more poorly calibrated). Lastly, we conclude with important research directions to design necessary UQ benchmarks and improve the calibration of reasoning models.

## Summary

Mei et al. (Princeton, 2025) ask three questions about reasoning models and uncertainty: are they well-calibrated, does deeper reasoning improve calibration, and can introspection improve calibration? They evaluate four state-of-the-art reasoning models (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, DeepSeek R1, Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking, o3-Mini) across five QA benchmarks (ARC-Challenge, MMLU, GPQA, StrategyQA, SimpleQA) using self-verbalized confidence under four zero-shot prompt strategies (basic, chain-of-thought, multi-step, Top-K) plus a two-stage introspective UQ procedure.

The central finding is that reasoning models are typically overconfident, not underconfident. On the three challenging benchmarks (StrategyQA, GPQA, SimpleQA), confidence estimates cluster in the 85%-100% range even on incorrect responses. Calibration is better on older benchmarks where models already achieve near-perfect accuracy (ARC-Challenge, MMLU), which the authors attribute to benchmark saturation rather than genuine calibration competence. Claude 3.7 Sonnet is the best-calibrated model by ECE overall, with DeepSeek achieving the best calibration on StrategyQA and GPQA specifically. Gemini and o3-Mini are most overconfident.

Standard prompt strategies (CoT, multi-step, Top-K) do not improve calibration over basic prompting for reasoning models. The explanation is that reasoning models already generate internal reasoning traces by default, so CoT-style prompts add no new reasoning signal that would affect confidence expression.

On inference-time scaling: the authors study three reasoning budget levels (RE-Low, RE-Medium, RE-High) for Claude and o3-Mini, the two models with controllable reasoning budgets. On GPQA, where accuracy rises substantially with depth, calibration also improves. But where accuracy saturates, increasing depth worsens overconfidence: more tokens reinforce confident but incorrect reasoning traces. On questions the models answer wrongly at all reasoning depths, both models show higher ECE at higher compute, with o3-Mini suffering a 7-percentage-point increase versus 2 percentage points for Claude.

Introspective UQ runs a two-stage procedure: a first model instance answers and reports confidence; a second fresh instance reads the first model's chain-of-thought and provides an updated confidence estimate without changing the answer. Three conservativeness levels are tested (IUQ-Low, IUQ-Medium, IUQ-High). IUQ-Low shifts confidence rightward into high bins, worsening overconfidence. IUQ-Medium and IUQ-High decrease ECE across all datasets except StrategyQA for IUQ-High. The results split by model: DeepSeek and o3-Mini benefit from introspection, while Claude's calibration consistently worsens, with its 0.9-0.95 confidence bin density nearly doubling under any IUQ level. The authors suggest Claude's closed-source implementation details may explain this anomaly.

## Relevance to experiment

Three connections to the Epistemic Humility Phase 1 experiment are direct.

First, the finding that RL-induced reasoning training produces systematically overconfident models whose calibration worsens rather than improves with more compute (on wrong-answer questions) is the clearest empirical anchor for Phase 1's SFT vs. DPO vs. KTO comparison. If the base reasoning model is already overconfident, each arm's effectiveness should be measured against that baseline ECE, not against a pre-training calibration baseline. The accuracy-ECE negative correlation (strong across three models, weak for Claude) suggests that any abstention arm that raises accuracy without a calibration objective will paradoxically appear better calibrated by ECE without actually being more honest.

Second, introspective UQ as a zero-shot post-hoc diagnostic is directly applicable as a read-out for Phase 1. After each fine-tuning arm, running IUQ-Medium on the same evaluation sets would expose whether the trained model can self-correct its confidence via reasoning, or whether it is more "Claude-like" in becoming more overconfident under reflection. The divergence between DeepSeek/o3-Mini (improved by IUQ) and Claude (degraded by IUQ) suggests that calibration training history or RLHF-style post-training shape the tractability of introspective correction in ways that matter for choosing which Phase 1 arm to build on.

Third, the accuracy-ECE correlation result provides a calibration design criterion: a Phase 1 model that achieves good ECE only where it already achieves high accuracy should not be considered well-calibrated for deployment. The paper's call for benchmarks that remain informative at high accuracy maps onto the Phase 1 protocol's use of challenging reasoning and factual QA tasks rather than saturated benchmarks.

Speculative but testable: DPO or KTO arms that explicitly target calibration (rather than only correct-answer preference) might produce models closer to Claude's ECE profile (relatively robust to accuracy level) rather than o3-Mini's (ECE tightly coupled to accuracy). This hypothesis could be tested by measuring the accuracy-ECE slope before and after each training arm, using the same five benchmarks studied here.

## Claims

- Reasoning models are systematically overconfident across challenging benchmarks: on StrategyQA, GPQA, and SimpleQA their self-verbalized confidence estimates cluster above 85%, particularly on incorrect responses, while calibration on saturated benchmarks (ARC-Challenge, MMLU) masks this failure. (Figure 4, Section 5.2) [[overconfidence]]
- Increasing inference-time reasoning depth improves accuracy and ECE when accuracy is still rising (e.g., 10.8% accuracy gain with 5.5% ECE decrease on GPQA), but worsens overconfidence once accuracy saturates, because extended reasoning traces reinforce confidence in incorrect answers without changing the answers. (Figure 6, Section 5.4) [[deeper-reasoning-increases-overconfidence]]
- Standard prompt strategies (CoT, multi-step, Top-K) do not improve calibration or accuracy over basic prompting for reasoning models, because these models already generate reasoning traces by default, making explicit CoT elicitation redundant. (Figure 14, Section 5.6) [[verbalized-confidence]]
- Introspective UQ with conservative prompting (IUQ-Medium, IUQ-High) decreases ECE across all tested datasets except StrategyQA under IUQ-High, improving calibration for DeepSeek and o3-Mini but worsening it for Claude, whose 0.9-0.95 confidence bin density nearly doubles under introspection. (Figures 12-13, Section 5.5) [[introspective-uncertainty-quantification]]
- ECE and accuracy are strongly negatively correlated across all four reasoning models: Gemini shows a perfect negative correlation, DeepSeek and o3-Mini near-perfect, while Claude's correlation is 28% smaller than DeepSeek's, reflecting its relatively better calibration across difficulty levels. (Figure 5, Section 5.3) [[expected-calibration-error]]
