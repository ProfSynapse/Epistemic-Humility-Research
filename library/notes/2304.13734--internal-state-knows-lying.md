## Abstract

While Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown exceptional performance in various tasks, one of their most prominent drawbacks is generating inaccurate or false information with a confident tone. In this paper, we provide evidence that the LLM's internal state can be used to reveal the truthfulness of statements. This includes both statements provided to the LLM, and statements that the LLM itself generates. Our approach is to train a classifier that outputs the probability that a statement is truthful, based on the hidden layer activations of the LLM as it reads or generates the statement. Experiments demonstrate that given a set of test sentences, of which half are true and half false, our trained classifier achieves an average of 71\% to 83\% accuracy labeling which sentences are true versus false, depending on the LLM base model. Furthermore, we explore the relationship between our classifier's performance and approaches based on the probability assigned to the sentence by the LLM. We show that while LLM-assigned sentence probability is related to sentence truthfulness, this probability is also dependent on sentence length and the frequencies of words in the sentence, resulting in our trained classifier providing a more reliable approach to detecting truthfulness, highlighting its potential to enhance the reliability of LLM-generated content and its practical applicability in real-world scenarios.

## Summary

Azaria and Mitchell train SAPLMA (Statement Accuracy Prediction based on Language Model Activations), a lightweight feedforward classifier that reads truthfulness from a single hidden-layer snapshot of the LLM as it processes a statement. The architecture is deliberately simple: three hidden layers (256/128/64 units, ReLU), trained on activations from five candidate layers (last, 28th, 24th, 20th, and middle/16th out of 32) across six topics in a custom true-false dataset, then tested on held-out topics it was never trained on. That out-of-distribution evaluation is the paper's strongest methodological choice: it forces the classifier to recover a general truthfulness representation rather than topic-specific surface patterns. The result is that middle-to-penultimate hidden layers carry a stable truthfulness signal that far exceeds what few-shot prompting or BERT embeddings can recover from the same content. For OPT-6.7b the 20th layer is optimal at 71.0% average cross-topic accuracy; for LLaMA2-7b the 16th (middle) layer is optimal at 83.0% average accuracy. Both beat few-shot baselines (53.7%) by roughly 17-29 points. On LLM-generated sentences the signal weakens but remains detectable: with a tuned threshold the 28th-layer probe reaches 71.3% accuracy, while the AUC of 76.1% at the default 0.5 threshold confirms the discriminative structure is real. Crucially, GPT-4 queried on the same generated-sentence set achieves 84.4% (Cohen's Kappa 0.6883 with ground truth), making it a useful upper-bound comparison. The paper also shows that training accuracy on randomly permuted labels drops to 62.5% versus 86.4% on genuine labels, confirming the classifier exploits real structure rather than overfitting. The main confound the paper raises is topic-level variation in probe accuracy: the pattern of high accuracy on Cities/Companies and lower accuracy on Chemical Elements/Animals is consistent with the hypothesis that the probe partially reads memorization depth rather than a pure truth-vs-falsehood distinction.

## Relevance to experiment

SAPLMA is a direct antecedent to the probing line the Phase 3 mech-interp sweeps are exploring: it establishes that middle-to-penultimate hidden layers encode a general truthfulness signal detectable by a lightweight out-of-distribution classifier. The Phase 3 work should treat this as a baseline and attempt replication on the Qwen3 models before and after Phase 1 training arms. Three points connect directly to the experiment design. First, the topic-level variation (Cities/Companies 81% vs. Animals 61% for OPT-6.7b) suggests the probe partly reads memorization depth, not pure truth-vs-falsehood. This is the same confound flagged by p-ik-ood-generalization-gap and verbalized-prob-generalizes-logit-overfits-distribution-shift: any activation probe's in-distribution success needs an OOD stress test before treating it as a truth detector. Second, the 71% vs. 63.6% gap between tuned-threshold and default-threshold performance on LLM-generated sentences shows that probe calibration matters as much as probe discrimination, which is directly relevant to the metrics used in Phase 1 evaluation. Third, GPT-4 at 84.4% on the same sentences serves as a practical ceiling: if Phase 3 probes approach this ceiling, that is meaningful; if they fall near the 63-71% range, the gap is the research question. The paper does not provide a training delta on a calibration or abstention metric, so it does not populate effects.csv directly, but it provides the key baseline numbers for any probe quality comparison.

## Claims

- SAPLMA classifying externally generated statements from a held-out topic (OPT-6.7b, 20th layer) achieves 71.0% average accuracy across six topics, versus 53.7% for few-shot prompting and 54.3% for BERT, establishing that a lightweight out-of-distribution hidden-state probe substantially outperforms output-level and embedding-level baselines for truthfulness detection. (Table 1) [[saplma]]
- For LLaMA2-7b the optimal layer shifts to the middle (16th) layer at 83.0% average accuracy, while for OPT-6.7b the optimal is the 20th layer at 71.0%, showing that the best probe layer is model-dependent and not uniformly the final layer. (Table 1, Table 2) [[saplma]]
- Training accuracy on genuine labels (86.4%) far exceeds training accuracy on randomly permuted labels (62.5%), ruling out capacity-driven overfitting and supporting the claim that the hidden-state structure genuinely encodes statement truthfulness rather than spurious correlates. (Section 5) [[hidden-state-encodes-statement-truthfulness]]
- LLM-assigned sentence probability is confounded by token frequency and sentence length, making it an unreliable truth signal without normalization; SAPLMA's hidden-state classifier achieves 71.3% accuracy on LLM-generated sentences with a tuned threshold against a BERT baseline of 57.1%. (Table 4, Section 6) [[sentence-probability-confounds-truth-detection]]
- Topic-level accuracy for OPT-6.7b ranges from 60.6% (Animals) to 81.3% (Cities) on the 20th layer, and the paper attributes this variation to training-data familiarity, raising the concern that the probe may partially read memorization depth rather than a pure internal truth representation. (Table 1, Section 5) [[hidden-state-encodes-statement-truthfulness]]
