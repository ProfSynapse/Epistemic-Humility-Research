## Abstract

Large Language Models deployed as question answering tools require robust calibration to avoid overconfidence. We systematically evaluate how reasoning capabilities and budget affect confidence assessment accuracy, using the ClimateX dataset (Lacombe et al., 2023) and expanding it to human and planetary health. Our key finding challenges the "test-time scaling" paradigm: while recent reasoning LLMs achieve 48.7% accuracy in assessing expert confidence, increasing reasoning budgets consistently impairs rather than improves calibration. Extended reasoning leads to systematic overconfidence that worsens with longer thinking budgets, producing diminishing and negative returns beyond modest computational investments. Conversely, search-augmented generation dramatically outperforms pure reasoning, achieving 89.3% accuracy by retrieving relevant evidence. Our results suggest that information access, rather than reasoning depth or inference budget, may be the critical bottleneck for improved confidence calibration of knowledge-intensive tasks.

## Summary

Lacombe, Wu, and Dilworth (ICML 2025 Workshop) ask whether the test-time scaling paradigm, which allocates more reasoning tokens to improve performance, also improves LLM confidence calibration to human expert labels. They evaluate a wide range of reasoning and non-reasoning models on the ClimateX dataset (8,094 IPCC AR6 statements labeled with a 5-level confidence scale) and on a newly introduced IARC carcinogenicity dataset (1,053 agents with 5-level hazard classification), using a masked-label classification framing: each model sees a statement with its confidence label removed and must reconstruct the label.

The main finding is a direct challenge to test-time scaling. Gemini 2.5 Pro, the top-performing reasoning model, reaches only 48.7% accuracy (Cohen's kappa 31.6%) on ClimateX, barely above RoBERTa-Large fine-tuned on the training set (53.7%, kappa 38.3%) and well above the non-expert human baseline (36.2%, kappa 14.9%). The thinking-budget sweep, conducted on Gemini 2.5 Flash, shows that a modest budget (64-192 tokens) lifts accuracy from 41.7% to 45.0% on the IPCC dataset, but accuracy then collapses to 35.7% at 768 tokens and only partially recovers to 41.7% at the 24,576-token maximum. For the IARC dataset, any reasoning at all is immediately harmful: accuracy drops from 66.9% to 62.4% at the first nonzero budget. On the overconfidence dimension (average predicted confidence versus ground truth), the pattern is monotonically bad: Gemini 2.5 Flash grows from +6% to +21.3% overconfident on IPCC as budget grows from 0 to 384 tokens, and from +15.6% to +35.6% overconfident on IARC at 320 tokens.

In contrast, search-augmented generation (RAG via web search) nearly solves the task: Gemini 2.5 Pro with search reaches 89.3% accuracy and kappa 85.7%, up from 48.7% and 31.6% without search. The authors interpret this as evidence that calibration on knowledge-intensive tasks is bottlenecked by evidence access rather than reasoning depth. A notable emergent behavior: OpenAI o3, when faced with the 300-statement batch, refused to label directly and instead generated a Python heuristic script that achieved 40.7% accuracy, a form of latent program synthesis the authors flag for future investigation.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper extends the test-time-scaling-worsens-abstention pattern already in the graph into a closely related but distinct failure mode: calibration to human expert confidence levels. Three connection points stand out for the experiment. First, the thinking-budget sweep on Gemini 2.5 Flash directly parallels the abstention-budget sweep in AbstentionBench (2506.09038): both show that more reasoning tokens do not monotonically improve the target epistemic behavior and can actively harm it. The overconfidence dimension here is especially relevant because the locked training-regimen SFT / DPO / KTO comparison trains on abstention signals, and the reward function implicitly shapes confidence calibration as a side effect. The Flash sweep result shows that even without any training intervention, extended chain-of-thought already degrades calibration, making baseline calibration measurement before training a non-negotiable step. Second, the large gap between search-augmented (89.3%) and pure-reasoning (48.7%) performance on a knowledge-intensive task sets an evidence-access ceiling that training alone cannot close, which is directly relevant to whether the locked training-regimen intervention will generalize to domain-specialist queries. Third, ClimateX and the IARC dataset are clean, held-out, expert-labeled calibration probes that do not appear in the current evaluation suite; adding either as a zero-shot calibration checkpoint could provide an externally valid, training-contamination-free signal alongside the existing TriviaQA/SciQ/PopQA benchmark set.

- Search-augmented generation raises Gemini 2.5 Pro accuracy from 48.7% to 89.3% (kappa 31.6% to 85.7%) on ClimateX, while pure reasoning with more tokens fails to close even a fraction of that gap, implicating evidence access rather than reasoning depth as the primary bottleneck for calibration on knowledge-intensive tasks. (§5.2.3) [[evidence-access-bottlenecks-expert-calibration]]
- RoBERTa-Large fine-tuned on the ClimateX train set (355M parameters) achieves 53.7% accuracy and kappa 38.3%, outperforming every zero-shot LLM tested including frontier reasoning models with orders of magnitude more parameters, demonstrating that task-specific supervised training on expert-labeled data remains a competitive baseline for calibration classification. (Table 1; §4.3.2) [[climatex]]
- Llama-3-8B-Chat (41.1% accuracy, kappa 21.5%) outperforms Llama-3-70B-Chat (36.2%, kappa 14.9%) on ClimateX because the larger model exhibits excessive overconfidence (+0.444 bias vs near-zero), showing that within a model family, overconfidence can more than offset the gains from scale. (Table 1; §5.1.2; §5.1.3) [[overconfidence]]
- OpenAI o3, when unable to directly label 300 statements in one prompt, autonomously generated a Python heuristic script that achieved 40.7% accuracy unprompted, exceeding the non-expert human baseline (36.2%) via latent program synthesis. This emergent fallback behavior is unrequested by the task prompt and warrants further investigation. (Table 1; §5.2.2) [[masked-label-calibration-probe]]
