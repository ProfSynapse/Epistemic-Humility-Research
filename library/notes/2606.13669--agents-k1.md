## Summary

Agents-K1 is an end-to-end pipeline that converts raw documents into agent-native multimodal scientific knowledge graphs. The system integrates three layers: a KG layer built around a MinerU-based offline parser and a five-module extraction schema (metadata, explicit entity mentions, implicit abstractions, citation intent, and fine-grained inter-entity relations); an LLM layer consisting of a 4B-parameter information-extraction backbone trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and a rule-based reward that jointly supervises format compliance, JSON validity, and task-conditioned F1 on NER, relation extraction, and long-form structured extraction; and an agent CLI layer (GraphAnything CLI) that fuses real-time web search, multimodal graph retrieval, and cross-document knowledge-network traversal into a tri-source interface supporting iterative idea generation, method specification, and code synthesis. The pipeline is instantiated on 2.46 million scientific papers across six disciplines (computer science, chemistry, biology, earth science, physics, and materials) to produce Scholar-KG, with a one-million-paper subset released publicly. A schema-adaptive General-KG variant extends the same parser and backbone to arbitrary document corpora without per-domain engineering. The GRPO-trained 4B backbone surpasses its own Qwen3-4B base and the Qwen3-8B base on eight of ten IE benchmarks; on NER regimes it exceeds the Qwen3-32B base (roughly eight times larger) while trailing on relation extraction. Graph-augmented retrieval via Agents-K1 lifts parametric-only LLM accuracy by large margins on specialized scientific benchmarks, with the gains concentrated on research questions that require cross-document synthesis rather than single-hop recall.

## Relevance to experiment

Agents-K1 is the ingestion infrastructure directly powering the vault's own knowledge graph. Every paper note in `library/` is a product of the same pipeline design this paper describes, so the method section is practically prescriptive for graph maintenance here. Three further connections matter for the epistemic-humility research program. First, the FrontierScience-Research and geoscience results provide controlled empirical evidence for the parametric-knowledge versus structured-access tradeoff: the largest gains appear on research questions (cross-document synthesis) rather than knowledgeable questions (single-hop recall), directly relevant to understanding when LLMs rely on uncertain memorized knowledge versus when external grounding substitutes for it. The GPT-5.2 physics gap (9.0% to 46.7%) is a particularly stark instance. Second, the GRPO backbone result, a 4B model exceeding Qwen3-32B on NER through task-conditioned RL without RLHF-style preference data, provides a comparison point for the Phase 1 RL training arms. A rule-based F1 reward applied via GRPO recovers most of the scaling benefit for structured extraction, raising the question of whether a similarly structured signal on calibration or abstention tasks could produce analogous parameter-count compression. Third, the backbone's regime asymmetry (strong NER generalization, persistent RE gap) illustrates that RL on a composite task-conditioned reward shapes cross-domain generalization in uneven ways, a concrete analog of the alignment-tax and over-abstention concerns the experiment directly studies.
