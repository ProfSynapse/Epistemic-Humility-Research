## Abstract

How does a language model's dependence on query-routing information and target knowledge change as it answers a question? We study this question through layerwise interventions on the hidden state at the end of the question. Across Qwen, Llama, and Gemma, we compare country-continent questions with noun, adjective, and code answers while keeping several fitted measurements distinct. A pair-conditioned request direction describes which country is queried in natural single-country questions; a global request direction describes first- versus second-country requests in paired questions; separate selection candidates test control among contents already available in the hidden state. A diagnostic reanalysis of frozen Qwen natural-question states shows that the pair-conditioned direction grows stronger before interventions on it begin to alter later fitted knowledge, with this causal window opening while answer-supporting content is still forming. The paired three-model trajectories are not uniform: Gemma shows a partially overlapping mid-layer routing-content profile, whereas Llama has no sustained routing-effect window under the same gates. In the paired protocol, dependence on the global request direction decreases from fixed earlier to later layer sets while dependence on fitted content persists. A matched Qwen comparison shows that the pair-conditioned direction retains a late effect, so this operational handoff concerns the global fitted direction rather than all request information. These results separate early readability, natural strength, causal steering, and later content dependence.

## Summary

The paper separates three functions in question-end hidden states: a [[parameter-retrieval-routing]] candidate that helps determine which parametric fact later computation forms, [[object-selection-routing]] that chooses among contents already represented in the state, and answer-supporting factual content itself. It fits linear directions or subspaces for each function, deletes or reverses them at individual layers, compares the edits with eight equal-length random controls, and measures both answer-margin damage and changes to fitted knowledge in later hidden states.

The natural single-country Qwen experiment shows that a pair-conditioned request direction can be readable long before an intervention on it affects the answer. Its natural coefficient strengthens while fitted fact content is still forming, and reversal at layer 32 changes both the answer margin and the layer-36 fitted knowledge score. Deletion changes the answer margin, but its fixed layer-32-to-36 knowledge interval crosses zero, so the paper does not claim deletion-based necessity.

The paired-country protocol yields an operational [[routing-content-handoff]] for a global first-versus-second request direction: global-route deletion matters less in fixed later layer sets, while fitted-content deletion remains consequential. This result is deliberately representation-specific. In the matched Qwen comparison, pair-conditioned deletion retains a strong late effect, and the pair-conditioned intervention is much longer than the global intervention. Selection evidence is also conditional: the strict two-continuation test succeeds in one Qwen2.5-3B base capital task, while the instruction-model tests stop at earlier gates.

## Extracted numbers

- In natural single-country Qwen questions, the pair-conditioned direction points toward the requested country on every held-out question at every layer, while deletion and reversal effects on the answer have 95% intervals above zero only at layers 28-36 and peak at layer 32 (Section 3.5, Figure 1).
- At the fixed Qwen layer-32-to-36 comparison, deletion changes the answer margin by 2.418 [1.721, 2.976] but gives a later-knowledge effect of 1102.517 [-62.431, 2133.854]; reversal changes the answer margin by 7.200 [5.422, 8.634] and later knowledge by 6270.843 [3408.236, 8771.132] (Section 3.5, Table 1).
- On 24 new country pairs, the global-route earlier-minus-later contrast is 0.669 [0.560, 0.758] for Qwen, 3.428 [3.031, 3.846] for Gemma, and 0.112 [0.075, 0.144] for Llama. Later fitted-content deletion exceeds later global-route deletion by 6.190 [5.738, 6.599], 13.418 [12.518, 14.065], and 8.291 [8.066, 8.467], respectively (Section 6.2; Appendix C.1, Table 5).
- In the matched late-layer Qwen comparison, global request deletion with the fit center has effect -0.195 [-0.251, -0.160], while pair-conditioned deletion with the same center has effect 3.092 [2.464, 3.624]. Mean actual deletion lengths are 16.38 and 80.47, so orientation and intervention size remain confounded (Section 6.4, Table 3; Appendix F.2).
- A noun-fitted content change transfers to adjective answers in all three models, with mean random-corrected answer-margin shifts of 15.72 for Qwen, 19.78 for Llama, and 46.09 for Gemma. Arbitrary-code transfer passes only 1/5 baseline-qualified Qwen maps, 0/4 Llama maps, and 0/2 Gemma maps (Section 5.3; Appendix D.1).
- In the Qwen2.5-3B base capital task, two continuations from the same layer-26 selection-modified state jointly recover the two candidate capitals at layers 27-29 on 25/40, 28/40, and 24/40 questions in validation A and 34/44, 34/44, and 32/44 in validation B. No instruction model reaches this restoration test (Sections 4.3-4.5; Appendix F.6-F.7).

## Relevance to experiment

The paper directly motivates keeping read and write evidence separate in Synaptic Tuner cells. A direction can be readable from the first layer yet causally consequential only much later, so probe accuracy or sign alignment should not be treated as a causal gate. Its equal-length random controls, explicit verification of the written vector, downstream knowledge readout, and frozen held-out layer choices are useful defaults for future routing or knowledge-boundary interventions.

The representation-specific late result is especially important for experiment design: changing a direction from global to pair-conditioned changes both its orientation and natural intervention length. Future comparisons should report direction construction, centering, actual write norm, precision, and matched controls, and should not port layer windows across Qwen, Llama, and Gemma families. This paragraph states design implications only and does not assert a result from any prior project experiment.

## Claims

- Evidence label: causal timing. In natural single-country Qwen questions, perfect early readability of a pair-conditioned request distinction precedes a late causal answer-effect window; the route coefficient strengthens while fitted continent content is still forming (Sections 3.5-3.6, Figure 1).
- Evidence label: bounded causal result. Reversing the layer-32 pair-conditioned request component changes both answers and layer-36 fitted knowledge, but deletion does not establish necessity because its later-knowledge interval crosses zero under the registered and pooled-control analyses (Sections 3.5 and 3.7, Table 1; Appendix F.3, Table 7).
- Evidence label: cross-model held-out comparison. For a fitted global request direction, all three instruction models show less deletion sensitivity in fixed later layer sets while remaining more sensitive to fitted-content deletion on 24 new country pairs (Section 6.2; Appendix C.1, Table 5).
- Evidence label: representation-specific boundary. Under a matched late-layer Qwen protocol, pair-conditioned request deletion remains consequential while global-direction deletion does not, but the five-fold difference in mean write length prevents attributing the contrast to orientation alone (Section 6.4, Table 3; Appendix F.2).
