## Abstract

Large-scale pretrained language models are surprisingly good at recalling factual knowledge presented in the training corpus. In this paper, we present preliminary studies on how factual knowledge is stored in pretrained Transformers by introducing the concept of knowledge neurons. Specifically, we examine the fill-in-the-blank cloze task for BERT. Given a relational fact, we propose a knowledge attribution method to identify the neurons that express the fact. We find that the activation of such knowledge neurons is positively correlated to the expression of their corresponding facts. In our case studies, we attempt to leverage knowledge neurons to edit (such as update, and erase) specific factual knowledge without fine-tuning. Our results shed light on understanding the storage of knowledge within pretrained Transformers. The code is available at https://github.com/Hunter-DDM/knowledge-neurons.

## Claims

- Suppressing knowledge neurons identified by integrated-gradient attribution decreases the model's correct-answer probability by 29.03% on average, while suppressing baseline (activation-value) neurons causes only a 1.47% decrease, demonstrating the causal specificity of the proposed attribution method. (Figure 4, Section 4.5) [[knowledge-attribution]]
- Knowledge neurons for relational facts are predominantly concentrated in the upper (topmost) FFN layers of BERT-base-cased, with very few distributed in lower layers. (Figure 3, Section 4.4) [[knowledge-neurons]]
- Knowledge neurons are selectively activated by knowledge-expressing prompts (avg activation 0.485 for head+tail prompts on BingRel) compared to head-only (0.019) or random prompts (-0.018), and this selectivity generalizes to open-domain web text unseen during training. (Table 4, Section 4.6) [[knowledge-neurons]]
- Directly modifying the FFN value-slot weights of ~4 knowledge neurons per fact (knowledge surgery) achieves a 34.4% fact-update success rate without any fine-tuning, and erasing a relation's top-20 shared knowledge neurons increases perplexity on that relation by over 36% while increasing perplexity on other relations by only 1-10%. (Table 5, Table 6, Section 5) [[knowledge-surgery]]
