## Abstract

Post-trained language models produce less varied outputs than their base counterparts. This output diversity collapse undermines inference-time scaling methods that rely on varied samples, and risks homogenizing model outputs on creative and value-laden tasks. Prior work attributes collapse to specific post-training methods, without separating the role of training data composition from the method, or the generation format from the model weights. We trace output diversity through three parallel post-training lineages of Olmo 3, Think (chain-of-thought distillation), Instruct (broad multi-source data), and RL-Zero, across 15 tasks and four text diversity metrics. We find that the location of collapse co-varies with data composition: the Think lineage loses most semantic diversity at supervised fine-tuning, and the effect of DPO is larger in Instruct than in Think. Suppressing chain-of-thought reasoning at inference in Think models drops accuracy on hard tasks, yet leaves answer-level diversity unchanged, showing that the collapse is embedded in the model weights by training data, not imposed by the generation format. Decomposing diversity loss on six verifiable tasks into a quality-control component (removal of incorrect outputs) and a residual component (genuine narrowing among correct outputs) reveals that the split is task-dependent, and Think models retain more correct-answer diversity than Instruct despite collapsing more in aggregate. Our results indicate that diversity collapse is determined during training by data composition and cannot be addressed at inference time alone.

## Summary

Karouzos et al. trace output diversity through three parallel post-training lineages of OLMo 3 at 7B to answer two questions: does diversity collapse co-vary with the post-training method or with training data composition, and does the chain-of-thought (CoT) generation format constrain diversity at inference or is the collapse encoded in the weights? The study evaluates 13 checkpoints (Base, Think-SFT, Think-DPO, Think, Instruct-SFT, Instruct-DPO, Instruct, and six RL-Zero variants) across 15 tasks covering summarization, reasoning, code, instruction following, creative writing, and value pluralism, using four diversity metrics: SBERT (semantic), EAD (lexical), NLI (logical), and Vendi Score (effective modes).

The central finding is that collapse location co-varies with data composition, not the training method. Think-SFT, trained on 2.3M synthetic CoT traces from two teachers (QwQ-32B and DeepSeek-R1), loses 62% of Base SBERT diversity on average, 24 percentage points more than Instruct-SFT (38%), which initializes from Think-SFT but trains on broader multi-source data. DPO erases more diversity in Instruct than in Think, because Think has already collapsed at SFT, leaving little residual spread for DPO to remove. RL-Zero, which applies GRPO without a KL penalty directly to Base and bypasses SFT and DPO, retains at least 71% and a median 94% of Base diversity (Table 1 shows 93% aggregate), while both supervised lineages converge to 34-38% retained.

Suppressing CoT at inference (Think-not-thinking) does not recover diversity on any task-stage combination, while accuracy costs reach -32% on hard tasks. This confirms that collapse is embedded in the learned weights by training data, not imposed by the generation format. A quality-filtered decomposition on six verifiable tasks separates diversity loss into a quality-control component (removal of incorrect outputs) and a genuine-narrowing component (homogenization among correct outputs). The split is task-dependent: on IFEval 83.4% of the drop is genuine narrowing, on MBPP 38%, on HumanEval less than 10%, and on math reasoning 57-64%. Collapsed correct-answer diversity directly limits majority-voting gains: Think gains only +0.4% on GSM8K from 16 samples, while Base gains +24% and RL-Zero +22-26%. TruthfulQA is the limiting case where majority voting hurts all models, because the dominant mode converges onto the misconception the question was designed to test.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper provides the most direct causal evidence in the graph that DPO's diversity cost is upstream-state-dependent rather than intrinsic to the DPO objective. The -23% Instruct-DPO vs -4% Think-DPO asymmetry, with both lineages using the same DPO data pool, shows that the DPO mechanism documented in `dpo-choice-induces-severe-answer-uncertainty-shift` operates on the residual spread the SFT stage left behind. This is locked training-regimen-actionable: the relative diversity impact of DPO in the Qwen3 abstention arms will depend on how much spread the SFT stage preserved, making SFT data breadth a confound to measure before attributing locked training-regimen effects to the DPO arm.

The Think-not-thinking finding is directly relevant to mechanistic interpretation. Diversity collapse that survives CoT suppression at inference is definitionally encoded in weight space, not in the generation routine. This motivates a weight-space probe as the primary mechanistic target: the activation or gradient signal the mechinterp sweep is inventorying should differ between Think-collapsed and RL-Zero-diverse models even when generation format is held constant. The result also bounds what inference-time interventions can achieve: activation steering or CoT-mode switching cannot recover a training-induced diversity floor.

The quality-filtered decomposition (D_all vs D_correct) gives locked training-regimen a concrete measurement discipline. Aggregate diversity reductions conflate quality control (removing wrong answers) with genuine homogenization among correct answers. Measuring only D_all would overstate the harm of higher-accuracy arms. locked training-regimen should track D_correct and majority-vote@K gains separately from accuracy@1 to avoid this confound.

The RL-Zero result shows that removing the KL penalty from GRPO allows partial diversity recovery, connecting to `rlvr-post-training-degrades-abstention` which finds the opposite direction (RLVR degrades abstention). The two papers together suggest a regime where reward specificity (code vs instruction vs math) modulates whether RL tightens or loosens the output distribution, a dimension the locked training-regimen RL arm should track.
