## Abstract

Metacognition is a critical component of intelligence that describes the ability to monitor and regulate one's own cognitive processes. Yet LLMs exhibit systemic deficiencies in key metacognitive faculties: they hallucinate with high confidence, fail to recognize knowledge boundaries, and misrepresent their internal uncertainty--undermining trustworthiness and reliability. Since monitoring task performance and adapting behavior accordingly are central to metacognition, we posit that models capable of accurately judging their own performance are better positioned to improve it. We operationalize this idea via two novel mechanisms: reinforcement learning with metacognitive feedback (RLMF), a paradigm to refine completion rankings during preference optimization based on the quality of a model's self-judgments of performance, and metacognitive data selection, which uses similar self-judgments to identify high-value training examples, outperforming naive active learning. We apply these innovations to the problem of faithful calibration (FC), a task that is itself fundamentally metacognitive: the goal is to align expressed with intrinsic uncertainty, difficult even for frontier LLMs. We adopt a two-stage, decoupled approach, first using these methods to calibrate the faithfulness of models' self-reported confidence scores, then mapping to natural, context-adaptable linguistic uncertainty via targeted output editing. Extensive experiments show RLMF achieves generalizable, state-of-the-art FC on diverse tasks while preserving accuracy. Further, RLMF surpasses standard RL by up to 63% while enhancing models' ability to assess and express their own capability limits. This positions RLMF as a promising paradigm to enhance LLM metacognition toward improved abilities and alignment, and suggests metacognitive performance as an effective RL signal to overcome limits of prior intrinsic feedback methods.

## Summary

Liu, Caciularu, Yona, Szpektor, and Cohan introduce reinforcement learning with metacognitive feedback (RLMF), a GRPO-based post-training method that scales the learning signal for strong completions by how accurately the model judges its own faithful-calibration performance. They pair it with metacognitive data selection, which chooses high- and low-self-assessed training examples, and with a second-stage rewriting protocol that maps sentence-level numerical confidence into more natural linguistic hedging.

The paper applies this to faithful calibration: aligning expressed uncertainty with estimated intrinsic confidence rather than only with empirical correctness. Across 10 datasets and several open models, RLMF improves faithful calibration over base, prompting, SFT-style FUT, and standard RL baselines while preserving task accuracy and Brier-score factual calibration. The strongest evidence is the standard-RL ablation: without metacognitive advantage scaling, gains are weaker and less generalizable.

- Table 1: On Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, average `cMFG*` rises from 0.60 base to 0.77 with standard RL, 0.84 with RLMF, and 0.82 with RLMF plus rewriting; corresponding accuracy is 0.31 base, 0.40 RL, and 0.41 for both RLMF rows, while Brier Score is 0.33 base, 0.20 RL, and 0.26 for RLMF.
- Table 1: On Qwen3-8B, average `cMFG*` rises from 0.54 base to 0.51 with standard RL, 0.83 with RLMF, and 0.83 with RLMF plus rewriting; accuracy is 0.55 base, 0.59 RL, and 0.57 for RLMF; Brier Score is 0.31 base, 0.26 RL, and 0.19 for RLMF.
- Section 5/Table 1: The authors report that RLMF outperforms standard RL by up to 63% in the numerical faithful-calibration setting.
- Table 2/Table 26: Training on PopQA, SelfAware, HaluEval, or UMWP yields average `cMFG*` around 0.80-0.84 for Llama3.1-8B-Instruct and 0.81-0.83 for Qwen3-8B, supporting cross-training-task robustness.
- Table 3/Table 27: Metacognitive data selection improves average `cMFG*` over random and active-learning selection: Llama3.1-8B-Instruct 0.84 vs 0.80/0.79; Qwen3-8B 0.83 vs 0.76/0.72.
- Appendix E: Human evaluation over 120 examples reports Krippendorff's alpha 0.93 and RLMF+rewriting win rates of 98%, 98%, 95%, and 96% for diversity, naturalness, helpfulness, and context suitability versus FUT.

## Relevance to experiment

This paper is directly relevant to the Phase 1/mechanism program abstention and calibration line because it treats uncertainty expression as a trainable metacognitive behavior and explicitly separates faithful calibration from factual calibration. The RLMF design is a plausible future training arm for Synaptic Tuner once the locked confirmatory matrix is complete: it suggests a GRPO-style online objective where abstention or confidence alignment is not just rewarded directly, but reweighted by model self-assessment quality.

For current claims, this is exploratory support rather than a source of headline Phase 1 evidence. It does, however, sharpen a mechanistic hypothesis worth testing later: metacognitive self-judgment may stabilize calibration training by prioritizing examples and completions where the model can assess the reliability of its own uncertainty signal.

## Claims

- Intervention result: RLMF improves average faithful calibration over base and standard-RL baselines while largely preserving accuracy and Brier-score factual calibration; the main 8B averages are in Table 1.
- Mechanism evidence: Scaling above-average faithful completions by self-judgment accuracy improves cross-task faithful calibration more robustly than standard RL, with the authors reporting up to 63% improvement over standard RL and rising metacognitive-performance traces in Figure 25.
- Data-selection result: Metacognitive selection of high- and low-self-assessed examples outperforms both random and active-learning-style selection on average `cMFG*` in Table 3/Table 27.
- Human-evaluation result: The two-stage numerical-to-linguistic pipeline is strongly preferred over FUT in human annotation, with 98%, 98%, 95%, and 96% win rates across four criteria and Krippendorff's alpha 0.93 in Appendix E.
