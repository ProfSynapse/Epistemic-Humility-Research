## Abstract

The alignment tuning process of large language models (LLMs) typically involves instruction learning through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and preference tuning via reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). A recent study, LIMA (Zhou et al. 2023), shows that using merely 1K examples for SFT can achieve significant alignment performance as well, suggesting that the effect of alignment tuning might be "superficial." This raises questions about how exactly the alignment tuning transforms a base LLM. We analyze the effect of alignment tuning by examining the token distribution shift between base LLMs and their aligned counterpart. Our findings reveal that base LLMs and their alignment-tuned versions perform nearly identically in decoding on the majority of token positions. Most distribution shifts occur with stylistic tokens. These direct evidence strongly supports the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis suggested by LIMA. Based on these findings, we rethink the alignment of LLMs by posing the research question: how effectively can we align base LLMs without SFT or RLHF? To address this, we introduce a simple, tuning-free alignment method, URIAL. URIAL achieves effective alignment purely through in-context learning (ICL) with base LLMs, requiring as few as three constant stylistic examples and a system prompt. We conduct a fine-grained and interpretable evaluation on a diverse set of examples, named JUST-EVAL-INSTRUCT. Results demonstrate that base LLMs with URIAL can match or even surpass the performance of LLMs aligned with SFT or SFT+RLHF. We show that the gap between tuning-free and tuning-based alignment methods can be significantly reduced through strategic prompting and ICL. Our findings on the superficial nature of alignment tuning and results with URIAL suggest that deeper analysis and theoretical understanding of alignment is crucial to future LLM research.

## Summary

Lin et al. (ICLR 2024) analyze what alignment tuning actually changes by comparing next-token distributions between base LLMs and their SFT- or RLHF-aligned counterparts, position by position, over 1,000 test examples. Most positions are unshifted: base and aligned models agree on the top token 77.7% of the time, rising to 92.2% including near-ties, and knowledge-bearing content (the actual factual answer) sits almost entirely at unshifted positions. Across three base-vs-aligned pairs at the 7B scale, the shifted-token ratio is a consistent 5-7%, concentrated in stylistic tokens (discourse markers, greetings, list-structuring words) and safety/refusal tokens, with substantial overlap in which specific tokens shift across model families. This is offered as direct token-level evidence for LIMA's Superficial Alignment Hypothesis. The paper then asks how far alignment can be reproduced purely by prompting, and introduces URIAL (Untuned LLMs with Restyled In-context ALignment): a fixed system prompt plus as few as K=3 constant, restyled in-context demonstrations, with zero gradient updates. On the just-eval-instruct benchmark, URIAL on Llama-2-7B (score 4.33) is comparable to that model's own SFT/RLHF-tuned versions and well ahead of zero-shot prompting (3.05-3.14) or vanilla K=3 ICL (3.18). On stronger base models URIAL exceeds the official tuned model: 4.63 vs. 4.44 for Mistral-7B-Instruct (SFT), and 4.74 vs. 4.67 for Llama-2-70B-Chat (RLHF), the latter approaching ChatGPT (4.75) and GPT-4 (4.8). A human pairwise evaluation corroborates the automatic scores. The authors conclude that when a base model is well-pretrained, SFT and RLHF may not be as necessary for alignment quality as previously believed.

## Relevance to experiment

URIAL is the sharpest available prompt-only counterfactual for the SFT vs. DPO vs. KTO training comparison: it shows that most of what alignment tuning changes at the token level is stylistic surface behavior reachable by K=3 in-context examples and zero weight updates, with only a 5-7% token-position shift concentrated on discourse markers and refusal/safety tokens. This sets the baseline expectation for how much of trained abstention behavior should be reproducible by prompting alone (relevant to [[prompt-cannot-override-rlvr-abstention-deficit]]): if general instruction-following alignment closes almost entirely via ICL, any abstention-specific residual that training adds but prompting cannot reach is the more theoretically interesting finding, and the token-shift methodology itself (base-vs-aligned distributional comparison) is directly reusable for isolating which tokens change under SFT/DPO/KTO in the locked training-regimen runs.
