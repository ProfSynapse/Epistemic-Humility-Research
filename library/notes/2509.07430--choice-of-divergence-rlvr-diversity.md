## Abstract

A central paradox in fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) is the frequent degradation of multi-attempt performance (Pass@k) despite improvements in single-attempt accuracy (Pass@1). This is often accompanied by catastrophic forgetting, where models lose previously acquired skills. While various methods have been proposed, the choice and function of the divergence term have been surprisingly unexamined as a proactive solution. We argue that standard RLVR objectives -- both those using the mode-seeking reverse KL-divergence and those forgoing a divergence term entirely -- lack a crucial mechanism for knowledge retention. The reverse-KL actively accelerates this decay by narrowing the policy, while its absence provides no safeguard against the model drifting from its diverse knowledge base. We propose a fundamental shift in perspective: using the divergence term itself as the solution. Our framework, Diversity-Preserving Hybrid RL (DPH-RL), leverages mass-covering f-divergences (like forward-KL and JS-divergence) to function as a rehearsal mechanism. By continuously referencing the initial policy, this approach forces the model to maintain broad solution coverage. Extensive experiments on math and SQL generation demonstrate that DPH-RL not only resolves the Pass@k degradation but improves both Pass@1 and Pass@k in- and out-of-domain. Additionally, DPH-RL is more training-efficient because it computes f-divergence using generator functions, requiring only sampling from the initial policy and no online reference model. Our work highlights a crucial, overlooked axis for improving RLVR, demonstrating that the proper selection of a divergence measure is a powerful tool for building more general and diverse reasoning models.

## Summary

Li et al. identify the choice of KL-divergence direction as the overlooked driver of two linked failure modes in RLVR fine-tuning: Pass@k degradation and catastrophic forgetting. Standard GRPO and DAPO use reverse-KL (mode-seeking), which narrows the policy toward a single high-probability solution path; methods that omit a KL term entirely offer no regularization against policy drift. Both failure modes appear together: after training, GRPO and DAPO solve only about 85% of queries the base model previously handled correctly, and their Pass@k scores fall below the base model even as Pass@1 rises.

The paper proposes Diversity-Preserving Hybrid RL (DPH-RL), which replaces the reverse-KL with a mass-covering f-divergence (forward-KL or Jensen-Shannon divergence). The key mechanism is a rehearsal loop: before online training the reference policy is used to pre-sample a static "near-perfect" dataset; during training, the f-divergence penalty is applied only to those easy queries, penalizing the updated policy for losing probability mass on any solution mode the reference policy covers. Challenging queries receive unrestricted PPO-clip exploration. Because the divergence is computed against a static pre-sampled dataset, the Generator-based implementation requires no online reference-model inference during the training loop, reducing compute relative to approaches that maintain a live reference policy. The Divergence Definition variant of DPH-JS does still require a reference model (Appendix C), so the compute saving applies specifically to the Generator implementation.

Experiments use Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (not the Instruct variant) on SQL (Bird, Spider) and six math benchmarks (AIME24, AMC23, Math500, Olympiad, Minerva, College Math). DPH-RL consistently improves both in-domain greedy accuracy and Pass@k, and its OOD math averages after SQL-only training nearly match the base model while GRPO and DAPO lose roughly 8 percentage points. A style-diversity experiment (Appendix A) shows that reverse-KL training collapses outputs to a single solution style, while forward-KL training produces three or more distinct styles in 60% of 32-sample draws.

## Relevance to experiment

DPH-RL is relevant to the Epistemic-Humility research program along three axes.

First, the catastrophic-forgetting / OOD generalization findings extend the evidence base around RLVR post-training harms. The graph already contains the mechanisms `rlvr-post-training-degrades-abstention` and `reasoning-finetuning-degrades-abstention`, which document that RLVR narrows the policy's abstention behavior. DPH-RL provides a complementary mechanistic account at the diversity level: the divergence choice, not just the reward signal, determines how much pre-training coverage the model retains. That framing links divergence selection to the self-knowledge and knowledge-boundary concerns central to Phase 1.

Second, the DPH-RL rehearsal mechanism is structurally analogous to what the graph calls `self-distillation-suppresses-representational-drift`: forcing the policy to keep probability mass on base-model outputs acts as an anchor against post-training interference, the same role played by KL-toward-teacher in SFT self-distillation. If Phase 1 training arms (SFT, DPO, KTO) could swap in a mass-covering f-divergence constraint, the OOD abstention-recall gap between training-domain and cross-domain abstention queries (documented in `sft-abstention-overfits-indomain` and `instruction-tuning-hurts-abstention-generalization`) might narrow, because the policy would be penalized for dropping coverage of the base model's hedging behaviors.

Third, the compute efficiency claim (Generator-based DPH-RL eliminates the online reference-model forward pass) is directly actionable for the Synaptic Tuner experiment design: the GPU budget freed by dropping the live reference model could be reallocated to larger batch sizes or longer training runs on the 4B and 8B arms, without changing the reward structure.
