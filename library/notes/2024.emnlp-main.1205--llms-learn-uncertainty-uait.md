## Abstract

Large language models often express excessive, random, or uninformative
uncertainty. Liu et al. propose uncertainty-aware instruction tuning (UaIT), a
self-training method intended to align a model's expressed uncertainty with the
probabilistic uncertainty of its generation. They evaluate LLaMA2 and Mistral
on free-form QA tasks and report a 45.2% improvement in uncertainty-expression
effectiveness, with out-of-domain generalization and downstream use cases such
as retrieval or routing to stronger models.

## Relevance to experiment

UaIT is the direct lineage source for treating stated confidence as a generated
answer attribute rather than only a post-hoc logit statistic. For the
Epistemic Humility study, it supports the Amendment B design choice to require
models to emit both an answer and a numeric confidence, then score whether that
confidence tracks either the model-specific known/unknown boundary or factual
answer correctness.

## Claims

- Uncertainty-aware instruction tuning aligns expressed uncertainty with
  probabilistic generation uncertainty, rather than treating uncertainty as a
  generic hedge phrase. (Abstract) [[verbalized-confidence]]
- UaIT improves uncertainty-expression effectiveness by 45.2% in the reported
  free-form QA experiments, with reasonably good out-of-domain generalization.
  (Abstract) [[calibration]]
- Expressed uncertainty can be used as an online decision signal for retrieval
  or routing to a stronger model. (Abstract) [[confidence-elicitation]]
