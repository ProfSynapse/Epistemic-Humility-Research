## Abstract

We show that a GPT-3 model can learn to express uncertainty about its own answers in natural language -- without use of model logits. When given a question, the model generates both an answer and a level of confidence (e.g. "90% confidence" or "high confidence"). These levels map to probabilities that are well calibrated. The model also remains moderately calibrated under distribution shift, and is sensitive to uncertainty in its own answers, rather than imitating human examples. To our knowledge, this is the first time a model has been shown to express calibrated uncertainty about its own answers in natural language. For testing calibration, we introduce the CalibratedMath suite of tasks. We compare the calibration of uncertainty expressed in words ("verbalized probability") to uncertainty extracted from model logits. Both kinds of uncertainty are capable of generalizing calibration under distribution shift. We also provide evidence that GPT-3's ability to generalize calibration depends on pre-trained latent representations that correlate with epistemic uncertainty over its answers.

## Claims

- Finetuned verbalized probability achieves MSE=22.0 on Multi-answer (distribution shift) vs. MSE=37.4 for answer logit and MSE=33.7 for indirect logit, outperforming both logit baselines under distribution shift. (Table 1) [[verbalized-confidence]]
- 50-shot in-context verbalized probability already approaches the calibration of the finetuned model trained on over 2,500 examples, suggesting GPT-3 leverages pre-existing latent representations rather than learning from scratch. (Section 3.3, Figure 6) [[verbalized-confidence]]
- A linear probe on GPT-3 embeddings achieves MSE=14.0 on Multiply-divide without calibration-specific training, providing evidence that GPT-3 pre-training encodes features correlated with epistemic uncertainty. (Table 2, Section 3.4) [[calibration]]
- Verbalized probability performance cannot be fully explained by learning to output logits or simple heuristics: a logistic regression with hand-crafted difficulty features (digit count, operator, number format) performs worse than verbalized probability on both evaluation sets. (Table 2, Section 3.4) [[verbalized-confidence]]
