## Abstract

An LLM's factuality and refusal training can be compromised by simple changes to a prompt. Models often adopt user beliefs (sycophancy) or satisfy inappropriate requests which are wrapped within special text (jailbreaking). We explore \emph{consistency training}, a self-supervised paradigm that teaches a model to be invariant to certain irrelevant cues in the prompt. Instead of teaching the model what exact response to give on a particular prompt, we aim to teach the model to behave identically across prompt data augmentations (like adding leading questions or jailbreak text). We try enforcing this invariance in two ways: over the model's external outputs (\emph{Bias-augmented Consistency Training} (BCT) from Chua et al. [2025]) and over its internal activations (\emph{Activation Consistency Training} (ACT), a method we introduce). Both methods reduce Gemini 2.5 Flash's susceptibility to irrelevant cues. Because consistency training uses responses from the model itself as training data, it avoids issues that arise from stale training data, such as degrading model capabilities or enforcing outdated response guidelines. While BCT and ACT reduce sycophancy equally well, BCT does better at jailbreak reduction. We think that BCT can simplify training pipelines by removing reliance on static datasets. We argue that some alignment problems are better viewed not in terms of optimal responses, but rather as consistency issues.

## Summary

The paper treats sycophancy and jailbreak susceptibility as failures of invariance to irrelevant prompt cues. Bias-Augmented Consistency Training generates a clean-prompt completion from the model being trained, then uses supervised fine-tuning to reproduce that completion from a wrapped prompt. The proposed Activation Consistency Training instead minimizes squared residual-stream distance between matching clean and wrapped prompt suffixes across layers. Across four Gemma models and Gemini 2.5 Flash, both methods reduce sycophancy. BCT reduces jailbreak attack success much more than ACT, while ACT usually preserves benign answering better. Training-loss analysis indicates that token-level and activation-level consistency produce distinct internal updates.

## Extracted numbers

- Models are Gemma 2 2B, Gemma 2 27B, Gemma 3 4B, Gemma 3 27B, and Gemini 2.5 Flash dated 20 May 2025 with thinking disabled (Section 4.1).
- Inference-time all-layer activation patching raises Gemma 2 2B non-sycophancy from 49% to 86%. Patching only layer 20 reaches 65% (Section 3.1.2).
- ACT uses loss weight `1e-4` and averages squared activation distance across all layers at shared suffix token positions. BCT uses loss weight 1 (Sections 3.1.2 and 4.1).
- Jailbreak training sets contain 830 to 1,330 filtered examples depending on the initial model's refusal rate (Section 4.3).
- On Gemini 2.5 Flash, BCT changes ClearHarm attack success from 67.8% to 2.9%. ACT reaches 52.2%, and DPO reaches 3.5% (Section 4.3.1, Table 6).
- On Gemini 2.5 Flash sycophancy evaluation, BCT raises non-sycophancy from 86.0% to 89.2% with MMLU changing from 90.3% to 89.1%. ACT reaches 86.7% non-sycophancy and 87.8% MMLU (Table 5).
- On Gemma 2 27B, ACT reaches 82.4% non-sycophancy and 77.7% MMLU, while BCT reaches 80.0% and 78.7% (Table 5).
- Combining ACT and BCT on Gemini 2.5 Flash gives 6.6% jailbreak attack success and 75.7% benign answering, close to BCT alone at 5.7% and 75.5% (Section 5).
- Restricting Gemma 2 2B ACT updates to the final half of layers lowers the harmonic-mean score from 0.612 to 0.600 (Section 5, Table 3).

## Relevance to experiment

ACT is a direct representation-space training objective that changes weights so wrapped prompts reproduce clean-prompt residual states. It is relevant as an example of internal-state supervision. It does not use an answerability readout or train generation to condition on a model-derived scalar. The finding that BCT is stronger on jailbreaks also warns that a more internal objective is not automatically more effective than token-level supervision.

## Claims

- Evidence label: intervention. Replacing wrapped-prompt activations with clean-prompt activations across all layers sharply reduces Gemma 2 2B sycophancy, motivating the ACT training objective (Section 3.1.2).
- Evidence label: training. ACT and BCT reduce sycophancy across the tested Gemma and Gemini models, with similar validation tradeoffs and model-dependent differences (Section 4.2, Figure 2, Table 5).
- Evidence label: comparison. BCT reduces jailbreak attack success substantially more than ACT, while ACT usually incurs less benign-refusal cost (Section 4.3, Figure 3, Table 6).
- Evidence label: mechanism diagnostic. BCT increases clean-versus-wrapped activation distance, while ACT does not meaningfully optimize the response-token cross-entropy target, indicating distinct update paths (Section 5, Figure 4).
- Evidence label: limitation. Consistency training assumes the clean-prompt behavior is desirable, requires curated prompt transformations and filtering, can make unsafe behavior consistent, and may teach the model to ignore relevant details. Jailbreak helpfulness measures refusal rather than answer quality (Sections 4.3 and 6).
