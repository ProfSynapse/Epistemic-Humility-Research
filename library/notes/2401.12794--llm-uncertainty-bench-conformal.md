## Abstract

The proliferation of open-source Large Language Models (LLMs) from various institutions has highlighted the urgent need for comprehensive evaluation methods. However, current evaluation platforms, such as the widely recognized HuggingFace open LLM leaderboard, neglect a crucial aspect -- uncertainty, which is vital for thoroughly assessing LLMs. To bridge this gap, we introduce a new benchmarking approach for LLMs that integrates uncertainty quantification. Our examination involves nine LLMs (LLM series) spanning five representative natural language processing tasks. Our findings reveal that: I) LLMs with higher accuracy may exhibit lower certainty; II) Larger-scale LLMs may display greater uncertainty compared to their smaller counterparts; and III) Instruction-finetuning tends to increase the uncertainty of LLMs. These results underscore the significance of incorporating uncertainty in the evaluation of LLMs.

## Summary

Ye et al. introduce a benchmarking framework for open-source LLMs that folds conformal prediction into the standard accuracy evaluation, producing two new signals: prediction set size (SS), a distribution-free upper bound on per-instance uncertainty, and Uncertainty-aware Accuracy (UAcc), a composite metric that rewards certainty and penalizes hedging. The paper evaluates eight LLM series across five NLP tasks reformulated as multiple-choice questions (QA on MMLU, reading comprehension on CosmosQA, commonsense inference on HellaSwag, dialogue response selection on HaluDial, document summarization on HaluSum), each with 10,000 instances standardized to six options. With error rate alpha=0.1, the conformal coverage guarantee is met: all models achieve average coverage above 90%. Three headline findings emerge. First, higher accuracy does not imply lower uncertainty: models that outperform peers on accuracy can show larger conformal sets, and UAcc can reverse the Acc rank ordering of two models (e.g., Llama-2-13B outranks Mistral-7B on UAcc on the DS task despite lower accuracy). Second, scaling is non-monotonic for uncertainty: the general trend of shrinking SS from 1.8B to 14B breaks at the 7B level for Qwen on QA, and scaling beyond 14B to 72B increases SS on some tasks despite accuracy gains. Third, instruction fine-tuning consistently increases SS and lowers UAcc relative to the base model, with Chat-V1 (instruction-format prompting) causing the sharpest degradation; Chat-V2 (base-format prompting on the chat checkpoint) also increases SS but less severely. Calibration set size has negligible effect on SS, coverage, and UAcc from 10% to 50%, confirming robustness of the conformal estimate.

## Extracted numbers

Numbers verified against the arXiv HTML render at library/fulltext/2401.12794.html, Table 1 (main 6B-14B results), Table 2 (large-model ablation), and §6.3-6.6 prose; verified against the source on 2026-06-24. Key tables: Table 1 reports Coverage Rate, Acc, SS, UAcc for ten individual checkpoints from eight LLM series across five tasks; Table 2 reports average Acc/SS/UAcc for Yi-34B and Llama-2-70B with and without demonstrations. This is a benchmarking paper comparing pretrained vs instruction-tuned checkpoints, not a controlled fine-tuning experiment with a pre/post intervention delta, so the numbers do not map onto the effects.csv intervention schema (no treatment group, no effect size over a baseline training condition). They are observational correlates of instruction tuning and scale, suitable as prior observations for the locked training-regimen study hypothesis framing.

- Conformal coverage guarantee met: all eight LLM series achieve average coverage rate above 90% across five tasks; the single lowest individual coverage is Qwen-7B on the Document Summarization task at 89.56% (Table 1, Coverage Rate columns; §6.3 prose).
- Accuracy-uncertainty inversion on DRS task: InternLM-7B accuracy 43.72% vs MPT-7B 24.38% (19.34 absolute percentage point gap in favor of InternLM-7B), yet InternLM-7B shows higher uncertainty (SS 3.63 vs 3.59) (Table 1, DRS columns; §6.3 prose).
- UAcc metric reverses model ranking on DS task: Llama-2-13B accuracy 60.05% (rank 3) vs Mistral-7B 62.16% (rank 2), but Llama-2-13B UAcc 66.02 (rank 2) vs Mistral-7B UAcc 64.25 (rank 3) (Table 1, DS columns; §6.3 prose).
- Yi-34B with demonstrations: average Acc 81.46%, SS 1.93, UAcc 121.09; without demonstrations (Yi-34B*): Acc 80.10%, SS 1.88, UAcc 119.99 — removing demonstrations slightly decreases SS for Yi-34B (Table 2).
- Llama-2-70B with demonstrations: average Acc 72.24%, SS 2.16, UAcc 94.62; without demonstrations (Llama-2-70B*): Acc 63.55%, SS 2.72, UAcc 63.67 — removing demonstrations substantially increases SS for Llama-2-70B (Table 2).
- Instruction fine-tuning and uncertainty: Chat-V1 (instruction-format prompting on Llama-2 chat model) consistently lowers accuracy and increases SS relative to base across all three sizes (7B, 13B, 70B); Chat-V2 (base-format prompting on chat model) also increases SS vs base, though less severely; both prompt variants yield lower UAcc than the base model (Figure 4; §6.5 prose).
- Scale and uncertainty in Qwen series: SS generally decreases from 1.8B to 14B, with the exception that Qwen-7B shows higher uncertainty than Qwen-1.8B on the QA task; scaling from 14B to 72B shows non-monotonic uncertainty, with Qwen-72B having higher SS than Qwen-14B on RC and DRS despite higher accuracy on those tasks (Figure 3; §6.4 prose).
- Calibration robustness: varying calibration data proportion from 10% to 50% produces no significant changes in coverage rate, SS, or UAcc for any Llama-2 variant (Figure 5; §6.6 prose).
- UAcc amplifies relative accuracy advantage: on the QA task, Qwen-14B shows a relative accuracy gain of 11.60% over Yi-6B (64.25% vs 57.57% absolute), and the relative improvement in UAcc widens to 28% (57.83 vs 45.18); these figures are relative percentage lifts, not absolute point differences (§6.3 prose, Table 1 QA columns).

## Relevance to experiment

This paper provides two direct inputs for the locked training-regimen study. First, it establishes that instruction fine-tuning increases conformal prediction set size (SS), a distribution-free uncertainty signal, across multiple model families without a corresponding accuracy gain; Chat-V1 in the Llama-2 series shows the most severe degradation. This is a prior observation that the locked training-regimen SFT arms can be compared against: does abstention-oriented SFT push SS up or down relative to the base model, and how does that compare to the direction documented here for general instruction tuning? Second, the UAcc metric operationalizes the accuracy-certainty tradeoff in a statistically grounded way; it is a candidate composite metric for tracking whether locked training-regimen arms trade accuracy for certainty or achieve aligned gains. On the mechanistic side, the paper complements Grad Detect (2606.24790): conformal SS is an output-level uncertainty estimate, while gradient-space signals are internal; comparing how the two co-vary after each training arm would test whether internal and external uncertainty signals track together. The paper's data are observational, not interventional, so it does not contribute effect sizes to effects.csv directly, but it motivates specific hypotheses about the sign and magnitude of the instruction-tuning uncertainty effect and suggests that scale alone cannot guarantee lower SS.

## Claims

- Instruction fine-tuning consistently increases conformal prediction set size (uncertainty) in the Llama-2 series under both Chat-V1 and Chat-V2 prompting, and both variants yield lower UAcc than the base model, with Chat-V1 also reducing accuracy. (Figure 4; §6.5) [[instruction-tuning-increases-conformal-uncertainty]]
- Higher task accuracy does not reliably predict lower conformal set size: on the DRS task, InternLM-7B beats MPT-7B by 19.34 absolute accuracy points yet carries higher uncertainty (SS 3.63 vs 3.59), and the same inversion appears on QA, CI, and DS tasks. (Table 1; §6.3) [[conformal-prediction-for-llm-uncertainty]]
- The UAcc metric can reverse the rank order of two models: Llama-2-13B has lower accuracy than Mistral-7B on the DS task (60.05% vs 62.16%) but higher UAcc (66.02 vs 64.25), showing that certainty-weighted evaluation changes relative standings. (Table 1; §6.3) [[uncertainty-aware-accuracy]]
- Scaling Qwen from 14B to 72B increases conformal set size on RC and DRS tasks despite accuracy gains, demonstrating non-monotonic uncertainty with scale and supporting the case for measuring SS independently of accuracy. (Figure 3; §6.4) [[instruction-tuning-increases-conformal-uncertainty]]
- Conformal prediction set size is robust to calibration data proportion from 10% to 50% in the Llama-2 series: no significant variation in coverage rate, SS, or UAcc, making it a practical uncertainty estimate for small held-out sets. (Figure 5; §6.6) [[conformal-prediction-for-llm-uncertainty]]
