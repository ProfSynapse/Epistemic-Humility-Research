## Abstract

Fine-tuning large pre-trained models has become the de facto strategy for developing both task-specific and general-purpose machine learning systems, including developing models that are safe to deploy. Despite its clear importance, there has been minimal work that explains how fine-tuning alters the underlying capabilities learned by a model during pretraining: does fine-tuning yield entirely novel capabilities or does it just modulate existing ones? We address this question empirically in synthetic, controlled settings where we can use mechanistic interpretability tools (e.g., network pruning and probing) to understand how the model's underlying capabilities are changing. We perform an extensive analysis of the effects of fine-tuning in these settings, and show that: (i) fine-tuning rarely alters the underlying model capabilities; (ii) a minimal transformation, which we call a 'wrapper', is typically learned on top of the underlying model capabilities, creating the illusion that they have been modified; and (iii) further fine-tuning on a task where such hidden capabilities are relevant leads to sample-efficient 'revival' of the capability, i.e., the model begins reusing these capability after only a few gradient steps. This indicates that practitioners can unintentionally remove a model's safety wrapper merely by fine-tuning it on a, e.g., superficially unrelated, downstream task. We additionally perform analysis on language models trained on the TinyStories dataset to support our claims in a more realistic setup.

## Summary

The paper studies how continued fine-tuning changes capabilities acquired during pretraining. It uses compiled Tracr transformers, minGPT models trained on probabilistic context-free grammars, and 91-million-parameter language models trained on TinyStories-Instruct. Across these controlled settings, fine-tuning often changes behavior through a small transformation that the authors call a wrapper, while representations needed for the earlier capability remain linearly recoverable. Pruning selected weights or neurons can remove the wrapper, and reverse fine-tuning on pretraining-like examples restores the earlier behavior faster than learning it from an initialization without that capability.

## Extracted numbers

- The PCFG experiments use six-block minGPT models and operand priors of `(0.999, 0.001, 0.000)`, `(0.9, 0.1, 0.0)`, and `(0.5, 0.3, 0.2)` to vary capability relevance (Sections 4 and 5).
- Fine-tuning uses learning rates one or two orders of magnitude below the average pretraining learning rate (Section 5).
- The TinyStories model has 91 million parameters, hidden size 768, 12 layers, 12 attention heads, and vocabulary size 8,192. It is pretrained for 30,000 gradient steps (Appendix F.1).
- The GPT-3.5 classifier used to score generated story twists achieves 92% held-out accuracy (Appendix F.2).
- In the TinyStories reverse-fine-tuning table, the proportion of generated stories with twists rises from 44% to 82% for filtering at the medium learning rate and from 12% to 81% for the no-twist pretraining control after 3,000 iterations (Table 1).

## Relevance to experiment

The results warn that changed output behavior does not establish deletion or replacement of an internal capability. A localized transformation can suppress an earlier behavior while leaving relevant intermediate information intact. The paper does not train a model to consult an answerability readout, but its wrapper hypothesis motivates tests that distinguish behavior-level suppression from changes to the computation that produces a capability.

## Claims

- Evidence label: intervention. Gradient-weight pruning of a small number of selected neurons or weights restores pretraining-task behavior in the controlled PCFG and Tracr settings (Section 5.1, Figure 7, Appendix G).
- Evidence label: probe. Linear probes recover information needed for the earlier task from intermediate activations after fine-tuning, especially at smaller fine-tuning learning rates (Section 5.1, Figure 8).
- Evidence label: training dynamics. Reverse fine-tuning restores earlier PCFG and TinyStories behavior more sample-efficiently than comparison models that did not retain the relevant pretrained capability (Section 5.1, Section 5.2, Figures 9 and 11, Table 1).
- Evidence label: limitation. Most mechanistic evidence comes from synthetic or small controlled models. The TinyStories extension tests story-feature generation in a 91-million-parameter model and uses a 92%-accurate learned classifier for one behavioral measure (Sections 4 to 6, Appendix F).
