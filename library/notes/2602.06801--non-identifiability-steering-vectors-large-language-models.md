## Abstract

Activation steering methods are widely used to control large language model (LLM) behavior and are often interpreted as revealing meaningful internal representations. This interpretation assumes that steering directions are identifiable and uniquely recoverable from input-output behavior. We show that, under white-box single-layer access, steering vectors are fundamentally non-identifiable due to large equivalence classes of behaviorally indistinguishable interventions. Empirically, we find that orthogonal perturbations achieve near-equivalent efficacy with negligible effect sizes across multiple models and traits, with pre-trained semantic classifiers confirming equivalence at the output level. We estimate null-space dimensionality via SVD of activation covariance matrices and validate that equivalence holds robustly throughout the operationally relevant steering range. Critically, we show that non-identifiability is a robust geometric property that persists across diverse prompt distributions. These findings reveal fundamental interpretability limits and highlight the need for structural constraints beyond behavioral testing to enable reliable alignment interventions.

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment (Table 1, Section 6.2). Orthogonal perturbation test: across five semantic traits (formality, politeness, sentiment, truthfulness, agreeableness) and two models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct), adding a random unit vector orthogonal to the steering vector produces behavioral effects statistically indistinguishable from steering with the original vector alone -- mean Cohen's d = 0.119 (Llama) and 0.131 (Qwen) at n=10 seeds, all trait-model pairs below the |d|<0.2 negligible-effect threshold, with effect sizes converging between n=5 and n=10 seeds.
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 2, Figure 2, Section 6.3). Steering vectors extracted by two geometrically distinct methods -- contrastive mean-activation difference (v1) and the first principal component of activation variance (v2) -- produce behaviorally equivalent effects (all Cohen's d < 0.27) despite cosine similarities between v1 and v2 ranging from -0.54 to +0.32, i.e. often near-orthogonal or anti-aligned; the Llama sentiment pair (cos=-0.54, |d|=0.19) shows nearly opposite directions producing statistically equivalent behavior.
- Evidence label: analysis (Table 4, Appendix A). SVD of the activation covariance matrix at three depths (L/4, L/2, 3L/4) in each model shows a large, depth-stable null-space fraction for the activation-to-logit map: ~94% (effective rank 57-63 of 4096) for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and ~86% (effective rank 116-139 of 2048) for Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, with a sharp singular-value cliff after the first 50-140 dimensions (Figure 6).
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 3 / Section 6.5; Figure 5 / Section 6.6; Table 5 / Appendix B). The non-identifiability equivalence class is high-dimensional and robust rather than a narrow or artifact-driven region: 50 independently sampled orthogonal directions per trait mostly stay below the equivalence threshold (Table 3); equivalence persists across three prompt environments (in-distribution, topic-shift, genre-shift, Figure 5) and throughout the operational steering range alpha in [0,3]; and a raw logit-level test (Appendix B, Table 5) confirms orthogonal perturbations shift output logits 27-53% less than norm-matched random directions (R=0.47-0.73 across all model-trait conditions), showing the equivalence is not an artifact of the semantic classifiers used elsewhere.
