## Abstract

One of the core pillars of efficient deep learning methods is architectural improvements such as the residual/skip connection, which has led to significantly better model convergence and quality. Since then the residual connection has become ubiquitous in not just convolutional neural networks but also transformer-based architectures, the backbone of LLMs. In this paper we introduce Learned Augmented Residual Layer (LAuReL) -- a novel generalization of the canonical residual connection -- with the goal to be an in-situ replacement of the latter while outperforming on both model quality and footprint metrics. Our experiments show that using LAuReL can help boost performance for both vision and language models. For example, on the ResNet-50, ImageNet 1K task, it achieves 60% of the gains from adding an extra layer, while only adding 0.003% more parameters, and matches it while adding 2.6 times fewer parameters. Similarly, when pre-training 1B and 4B parameter LLMs, LAuReL improves performance on a variety of challenging downstream evaluation tasks by 2.54% to 20.05%, while adding only 0.012% and 0.1% additional parameters, respectively.

## Claims

- Evidence label: experiment (Table 1, Sec. 3.1). On ResNet-50/ImageNet-1K, LAuReL-RW+LR matches the accuracy of naively adding an extra layer (75.20% vs. a 74.95% baseline) while adding 2.6x fewer new parameters (1.68% vs 4.37%); LAuReL-RW+LR+PA improves further to 75.25% using 1.82x fewer added parameters than the 1-extra-layer baseline.
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 2, Sec. 3.2.1). Pre-training a 1B-parameter decoder-only LLM with LAuReL-RW+LR improved performance on every evaluated Q&A/reasoning/math/code benchmark except MBPP (neutral), e.g. BoolQ +13.07% and GSM8K-CoT +5.39%, while adding only 0.012% additional parameters and near-zero latency.
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 3, Sec. 3.2.2). On a 4B-parameter multimodal/multilingual LLM, LAuReL-RW+LR (r=64) improved 8 of 10 downstream evaluation tasks (e.g. BookQA +20.05%, MGSM +15.45%) while adding ~0.1% extra parameters and ~1-2% extra latency, and was neutral on WMT23 and DocVQA.
- Evidence label: experiment (Table 6, Table 7, Sec. 4.5). In a controlled comparison at 4B parameters, a naive extra layer (Baseline-41) cost +3.63% parameters, whereas LAuReL-40 added only +0.1% parameters yet outperformed Baseline-41 on all 10 downstream tasks, achieving 2%-21% relative improvement over the non-LAuReL Baseline-40.
