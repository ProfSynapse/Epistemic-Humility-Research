## Claims

- Evidence label: intervention. CAA is reported to steer alignment-relevant
  behaviors, including sycophancy-style behavior, during evaluation or
  generation.

## Summary

Panickssery et al. introduce Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA), a method for steering large language models by adding mean-difference vectors computed from paired positive/negative behavior examples into the residual stream at inference time. Applied to Llama 2 7B Chat and 13B Chat across seven alignment-relevant behaviors (Sycophancy, Hallucination, Refusal, Corrigibility, AI Coordination, Myopic Reward, Survival Instinct), CAA consistently shifts model behavior in the intended direction on both multiple-choice evaluations (50 held-out questions per behavior) and GPT-4-rated open-ended generation tasks. Optimal steering layers cluster around layer 13 in the 7B model and layers 14-15 in the 13B model, coinciding with where PCA projections of residual-stream activations first show behavioral clustering (for refusal in 7B Chat, this transition occurs between layers 9 and 10). Compared to supervised finetuning, CAA is far cheaper (under 5 minutes on a single GPU via forward passes only, versus about 10 minutes on two GPUs for finetuning on 1000 examples), and it generalizes to open-ended generation for all seven behaviors, whereas finetuning on multiple-choice A/B data for sycophancy collapses to a GPT-4 score of 0.00 on open-ended prompts. CAA can be layered additively on top of system prompts and finetuning. Capability cost is modest: MMLU correct-answer probability ranges from 0.57 to 0.65 across all seven behaviors and both multiplier signs (baseline 0.63), with the true minimum at 0.57 for Hallucination at the -1 multiplier (Table 5). Subtracting the sycophancy steering vector raises TruthfulQA accuracy from 0.60 to 0.62 in 13B Chat (layer 14). Analysis of steering vector geometry reveals: (i) cosine similarity between Llama 2 7B Chat and 7B base model steering vectors peaks between layers 7 and 15, indicating RLHF has a smaller representational effect in that range; (ii) base-model vectors transfer significantly to the chat model between layers 10 and 15; (iii) cross-layer transfer within the same model shows a steep drop-off in effect size around layer 17.

## Extracted numbers

Provenance: arXiv HTML render at library/fulltext/2312.06681.html, cross-checked against paper text and tables; Tables 3, 4, 5, 9, 12 and Figures 2, 3, 8, 9, 10 and Appendices E, H, J verified against source on 2026-06-24. This paper reports steering effect sizes (behavior score shifts), not training intervention deltas against a humility calibration metric, so it does not map directly onto the effects.csv intervention schema. The sycophancy-to-TruthfulQA numbers (Table 12, Appendix H) are the closest analog to a calibration effect and could be logged as a mechanistic probe result rather than a training arm delta.

- CAA steers all seven alignment behaviors in the expected direction on 50 held-out multiple-choice questions; optimal steering layer in Llama 2 7B Chat is layer 13 and adjacent layers, and in 13B Chat is usually layer 14 or 15 (§4.1, Figure 3)
- Hallucination multiple-choice score, Llama 2 13B Chat (layer 13): no-system-prompt baseline 0.54, +1 multiplier 0.78; positive system prompt + +1 multiplier 0.87; negative system prompt + -1 multiplier 0.42 (Table 3, §5)
- Refusal multiple-choice score, Llama 2 13B Chat (layer 13): no-system-prompt baseline 0.78, +1 multiplier 0.86; positive system prompt + 0 multiplier 0.95; no-system-prompt -1 multiplier 0.56; negative system prompt + -1 multiplier 0.41 [note: 0.41 is the negative-system-prompt + -1 condition, not the no-system-prompt -1 condition which is 0.56; conditions span distinct columns in Table 3] (Table 3, §5)
- Hallucination open-ended GPT-4 score (scale 1-10), Llama 2 7B Chat (layer 13): no-finetuning baseline 3.28; positive finetuning + +1 multiplier 5.80; negative finetuning + -1 multiplier 3.02 (Table 4, §6)
- Sycophancy open-ended GPT-4 score, Llama 2 7B Chat: positive finetuning at 0 multiplier 0.00, positive finetuning at +1 multiplier 0.00 (both lowest for that behavior), showing finetuning on A/B multiple-choice sycophancy data fails to generalize to open-ended generation; CAA alone (no finetuning) achieves non-zero scores across all seven behaviors (Table 4, §6)
- Finetuning on 1000 examples for a single behavior takes approximately 10 minutes on 2 NVIDIA L40 GPUs; generating a CAA steering vector requires less than 5 minutes on a single GPU using forward passes only (§6, Appendix J)
- MMLU correct-answer probability, Llama 2 13B Chat (layer 14), all seven behaviors at +1 and -1 multipliers versus baseline of 0.63: range is 0.57-0.65 [corrected from a prior draft; the minimum is 0.57 for Hallucination at -1 multiplier, not 0.59] (Table 5, §7)
- Sycophancy steering on TruthfulQA, Llama 2 13B Chat (layer 14): subtracting sycophancy vector raises average accuracy from 0.60 to 0.62 (+0.02); adding it lowers accuracy to 0.57 (-0.03) (Table 12, Appendix H)
- Sycophancy steering on TruthfulQA, Llama 2 7B Chat: subtracting the sycophancy vector improves accuracy by 0.01; adding it worsens accuracy by 0.05 (Appendix H prose)
- Behavioral clustering in PCA projections of residual-stream activations emerges suddenly around one-third of the way through the layers; for the refusal dataset in Llama 2 7B Chat the transition occurs between layers 9 and 10 (§3.2, Figure 2)
- Cosine similarity between Llama 2 7B Chat and 7B base model steering vectors peaks in layers 7-15, indicating RLHF has a smaller effect on representations in that range (§8.3, Figure 9)
- Cross-layer transfer of steering vectors within Llama 2 7B Chat shows a steep drop-off in effect size around layer 17; base-model vectors transfer significantly to the chat model between layers 10 and 15 (§8.2, §8.3, Figures 8 and 10)
- Contrastive dataset sizes: Hallucination 1000 pairs, Sycophancy 1000 pairs, Myopic Reward 950 pairs, Survival Instinct 903 pairs, Refusal 408 pairs, AI Coordination 360 pairs, Corrigibility 290 pairs; all test sets 50 questions (Table 9, Appendix E)

## Relevance to experiment

Three connections to the Epistemic Humility experiment are direct. First, CAA is a candidate post-training probe for Phase 1: because CAA can amplify or suppress hallucination and sycophancy behavior via residual-stream vectors, it provides a lightweight read-out for whether a training arm has sharpened or diffused a behavior direction, without requiring additional annotation. The hallucination multiple-choice and open-ended scores in Tables 3 and 4 suggest what effect sizes to expect when a steering vector is applied to a chat model. Second, the finding that behavioral clustering and peak steering effect both localize to layers 13-15 in 7B and 13B Chat narrows the candidate space for the mechanism program causal-pilot sweep: the mid-layer window (roughly one-third through the network) is where residual-stream activations first encode high-level behavioral distinctions, and this aligns with the mechanism program hypothesis about where epistemic signal lives. Third, the sycophancy-to-TruthfulQA mechanistic link (Table 12, Appendix H) provides a held-out check: if a Phase 1 SFT arm reduces sycophancy, the sycophancy steering vector should produce a smaller effect and subtracting it should improve TruthfulQA less, providing cross-validation. One risk to flag for the SFT arm: finetuning on multiple-choice A/B sycophancy data produces 0.00 open-ended GPT-4 scores (Table 4), a complete generalization failure. Any SFT intervention using similar narrow-format data should be validated on open-ended prompts before assuming it has changed the underlying behavior.
