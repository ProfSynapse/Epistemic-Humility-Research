## Abstract

Large Language Models' safety-aligned behaviors, such as refusing harmful queries, can be represented by linear directions in activation space. Previous research modeled safety behavior with a single direction, limiting mechanistic understanding to an isolated safety feature. In this work, we discover that safety-aligned behavior is jointly controlled by multi-dimensional directions. Namely, we study the vector space of representation shifts during safety fine-tuning on Llama 3 8B for refusing jailbreaks. By studying orthogonal directions in the space, we first find that a dominant direction governs the model's refusal behavior, while multiple smaller directions represent distinct and interpretable features like hypothetical narrative and role-playing. We then measure how different directions promote or suppress the dominant direction, showing the important role of secondary directions in shaping the model's refusal representation. Finally, we demonstrate that removing certain trigger tokens in harmful queries can mitigate these directions to bypass the learned safety capability, providing new insights on understanding safety alignment vulnerability from a multi-dimensional perspective. Code and artifacts are available at https://github.com/BMPixel/safety-residual-space.

## Claims

- The safety residual space is low-rank linear: for SSFT, effective rank stays at 1 in the first 10 layers and peaks near layer 20, confirming that safety fine-tuning encodes behavior along a dominant singular direction rather than a diffuse high-dimensional change. (Figure 2 (effective rank by layer)) [[safety-residual-space]]
- The dominant refusal direction achieves refusal prediction accuracy on par with a separately trained probe vector in later layers (plateau after layer 15), establishing that the largest singular vector of the residual space alone is sufficient to decode aligned behavior without supervision. (Figure 3 (refusal prediction accuracy by layer)) [[dominant-refusal-direction]]
- The trigger removal attack retains an attack pass rate of 0.30 at 160-shot fine-tuning exposure, while PAIR drops to 0.05 at 80-shot and 0.05 at 160-shot, demonstrating that token-level trigger avoidance is systematically resilient to safety fine-tuning in a way that surface-pattern attacks are not. (Table 3 (Attack Pass Rate under n-shot exposure)) [[trigger-removal-attack]]
- Non-dominant directions are interpretable (L14-C2: creative writing/storytelling; L14-C3: hypothetical narrative; L14-C5: role-playing) and directional intervention to suppress them reduces refusal rate, showing that secondary orthogonal components in the safety residual space act as amplifiers of the dominant refusal signal. (Table 2 (top-10 tokens per direction), Figure 6 (refusal rate under non-dominant suppression)) [[dominant-refusal-direction]]
