## Abstract

We report a mixture of expert strategy to create fine-tuned large language models using a deep layer-wise token-level approach based on low-rank adaptation (LoRA). Starting with a set of pre-trained LoRA adapters, our gating strategy uses the hidden states to dynamically mix adapted layers, allowing the resulting X-LoRA model to draw upon different capabilities and create never-before-used deep layer-wise combinations to solve tasks. The design is inspired by the biological principles of universality and diversity, where neural network building blocks are reused in different hierarchical manifestations. Hence, the X-LoRA model can be easily implemented for any existing large language model (LLM) without a need for modifications of the underlying structure. We develop a tailored X-LoRA model that offers scientific capabilities including forward/inverse analysis tasks and enhanced reasoning capability, focused on biomaterial analysis, protein mechanics and design. The impact of this work include access to readily expandable and adaptable models with strong domain knowledge and the capability to integrate across areas of knowledge. Featuring experts in biology, mathematics, reasoning, bio-inspired materials, mechanics and materials, chemistry, protein biophysics, mechanics and quantum-mechanics based molecular properties, we conduct a series of physics-focused case studies. We examine knowledge recall, protein mechanics forward/inverse tasks, protein design, adversarial agentic modeling including ontological knowledge graph construction, as well as molecular design. The model is capable not only of making quantitative predictions of nanomechanical properties of proteins or quantum mechanical molecular properties, but also reasons over the results and correctly predicts likely mechanisms that explain distinct molecular behaviors.

## Summary

The paper introduces X-LoRA, a mixture of frozen low-rank adapter experts controlled by a trainable scaling head. A first model pass produces hidden states, and the scaling head converts them into token-by-layer-by-adapter coefficients. A second pass applies those coefficients to mix the adapters. The paper trains Zephyr-7B-beta and Gemma-7B-it variants for scientific tasks in protein mechanics, materials, chemistry, and molecular design.

## Extracted numbers

- The main X-LoRA-Zephyr model mixes nine adapter experts covering bioinspired materials, reasoning, chemistry, mathematics, physics, biology, mechanics and materials, logic, and protein mechanics (Section 2).
- X-LoRA-Gemma mixes four adapters and reports R-squared of 0.87 for protein unfolding-force and unfolding-energy prediction and 0.96 for QM9 molecular-property prediction (Section 2.5, Figure 15).
- On the paper's STEM subset of MMLU, X-LoRA-Zephyr improved over Zephyr-7B-beta from 54.6% to 56.6%, and X-LoRA-Gemma improved over Gemma-7B-it from 40.6% to 52.4% (Section 2.5.1, Figure 18).
- Across the quantitative scientific prediction tasks, the paper reports R-squared values from 0.85 to 0.96, compared with 0.78 for a cited protein-unfolding-force baseline (Section 3).
- The scaling tensor has shape sequence length by layer count by adapter count, and softmax normalization makes expert coefficients sum to one (Section 4.1).

## Relevance to experiment

X-LoRA is an implemented hidden-state-gated adapter architecture. The trainable scaling head reads the model's own hidden states and routes layer-specific adapter writes at token granularity. The gate is trained on task data and is not constrained to recover a separately validated answerability variable, so this paper supplies architectural scaffolding rather than evidence of epistemic self-consultation.

## Claims

- Evidence label: architecture. X-LoRA uses a trainable hidden-state scaling head to mix frozen LoRA experts at token and layer granularity (Sections 1.1 and 4.1).
- Evidence label: process. In the reported implementation, a scaling pass computes routing coefficients and a second forward pass applies them, with separate cache handling required to avoid cross-pass contamination (Sections 3 and 4.2.1).
- Evidence label: benchmark. X-LoRA improves the paper's STEM MMLU subset over both Zephyr-7B-beta and Gemma-7B-it base models (Section 2.5.1, Figure 18).
- Evidence label: scientific task. X-LoRA-Gemma reports strong R-squared values on protein-mechanics and QM9 molecular-property tasks (Section 2.5, Figure 15).
