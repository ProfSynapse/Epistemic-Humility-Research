## Abstract

When using large language models (LLMs) in high-stakes applications, we need to know when we can trust their predictions. Some works argue that prompting high-performance LLMs is sufficient to produce calibrated uncertainties, while others introduce sampling methods that can be prohibitively expensive. In this work, we first argue that prompting on its own is insufficient to achieve good calibration and then show that fine-tuning on a small dataset of correct and incorrect answers can create an uncertainty estimate with good generalization and small computational overhead. We show that a thousand graded examples are sufficient to outperform baseline methods and that training through the features of a model is necessary for good performance and tractable for large open-source models when using LoRA. We also investigate the mechanisms that enable reliable LLM uncertainty estimation, finding that many models can be used as general-purpose uncertainty estimators, applicable not just to their own uncertainties but also the uncertainty of other models. Lastly, we show that uncertainty estimates inform human use of LLMs in human-AI collaborative settings through a user study.

## Summary

Kapoor et al. (2024) argue that prompting alone is structurally insufficient for reliable uncertainty calibration in open-ended LLM generation, and that supervised fine-tuning on a small graded dataset of correct and incorrect answers is both necessary and tractable. The paper introduces calibration tuning (CT): LoRA adapters trained on approximately 20,000 correctness-labeled examples from 16 benchmarks, with a JSD regularizer (labeled "KL" in Table 1, clarified as Jensen-Shannon Divergence in Appendix C.1) to keep the fine-tuned model close to the base. Three parameterizations are compared: a frozen linear probe on last-layer features (Probe), LoRA alone, and LoRA with a language prompt framing correctness as a binary token choice (LoRA + Prompt). The paper introduces an open-ended (OE) variant of MMLU where no answer choices are provided and correctness is graded by an auxiliary LLM, and shows that multiple-choice and open-ended settings behave fundamentally differently. Perplexity fails as an uncertainty signal in OE; prompting methods (zero-shot classifier, verbalized confidence) do not improve with model capability on OE; and all prompting approaches fall well below even the weakest fine-tuned model. Section 5 shows that frozen probes suffice for multiple-choice MMLU but LoRA + Prompt is required for open-ended MMLU, and that approximately 1000 labeled examples is a practical threshold for outperforming baselines. Section 6 tests generalization across subject-matter shifts, format shifts (MC-to-OE and OE-to-MC transfer), and solvability shifts (SelfAware unanswerable questions), finding that LoRA + Prompt generalizes across all three where Probe does not. The cross-model experiment (Section 6.1) shows that a Mistral 7B calibration tuner applied to LLaMA-2 7B generations achieves better AUROC for LLaMA-2 7B uncertainty than LLaMA-2 7B applied to its own generations, suggesting that calibration-tuned models function as general-purpose correctness estimators rather than self-knowledge probes. This framing is the paper's own (Section 6.1, Figure 5 Center); the paper does not state that Mistral was trained specifically on LLaMA-2 7B outputs. Section 7 closes with a user study (N=181) confirming that calibrated confidence scores modulate human reliance on LLM predictions, while random confidence scores produce no discernible effect.

- JSD regularization during calibration tuning reduces ECE from 29.9% to 10.8% while AUROC rises from 70.2% to 71.6%, both mean over six base models (Table 1, Section 5). Note: Table 1 labels the regularizer 'KL'; Appendix C.1 clarifies it is Jensen-Shannon Divergence.
- Approximately 1000 labeled graded examples suffices to outperform zero-shot classifier and sampling baselines on open-ended MMLU; gains continue to approximately 5000 examples before marginal benefit decreases (Figure 3 Right, Section 5).
- Prompting-based uncertainty methods fail on open-ended MMLU: ECE does not improve with model capability, and AUROC shows only small gains for the best models, all remaining below the worst fine-tuned model (LLaMA-2 7B LoRA), shown as a dotted line (Figure 2 Right, Section 4).
- Frozen linear probe (Probe) suffices to outperform baselines on multiple-choice MMLU but is substantially worse than LoRA + Prompt on open-ended MMLU; LoRA + Prompt trained on MC data outperforms Probe trained directly on OE data in MC-to-OE transfer (Figure 3 Left and Figure 4 Center, Sections 5-6).
- A model trained on incorrect-answer ablation data generalizes surprisingly well (on par with Probe) but is significantly worse than LoRA + Prompt trained on original answers, showing pure question-error-rate correlation is insufficient to explain full performance (Figure 5 Left, Section 6.1).
- Mistral 7B calibration tuner applied to LLaMA-2 7B generations achieves higher AUROC estimating LLaMA-2 7B uncertainty than LLaMA-2 7B achieves on its own generations (uncertain: the paper confirms the AUROC comparison but does not state that Mistral was calibration-tuned specifically on LLaMA-2 7B outputs; it describes cross-model application of a Mistral calibration tuner to LLaMA-2 generations) (Figure 5 Center, Section 6.1).
- LoRA fine-tuning uses rank r=8, alpha=32, dropout 0.1, with 8-bit quantization; each run takes approximately 1-3 GPU days on 4 NVIDIA RTX 8000 (48GB) GPUs (Section 5, Training and regularization).
- User study with N=181 participants shows calibrated confidence scores modulate human reliance on LLM predictions; random confidence scores produce no discernible modulation (Section 7, Figure 6).

## Relevance to experiment

This paper bears directly on locked training-regimen and mechanism program. For the locked training-regimen study, calibration tuning (CT) with LoRA is structurally analogous to the SFT abstention arm: both fine-tune on a supervised correctness or abstention signal with parameter-efficient adapters. The JSD regularization result (Table 1) is a concrete demonstration that divergence-based regularization is necessary to prevent ECE blow-up during calibration fine-tuning, which is a design constraint the SFT arm of locked training-regimen should account for. The data-efficiency finding (1000 labeled examples) bounds how much graded data a locked training-regimen calibration arm would need before outperforming prompting baselines. For mechanism work, the cross-model generalization result raises a falsifiable prediction: if LLM calibration estimators are general-purpose correctness detectors rather than self-knowledge probes, then the mechanistic signal driving correctness estimation should be domain-general and visible in activation space regardless of which model generated the query. The finding that LoRA + Prompt is required for OE but Probe suffices for MC also points to a hypothesis for the mechanism program hidden-state inventory: if frozen features fail on OE, it suggests the relevant correctness signal for open-ended generation is not fully linearly readable from the final layer at inference, which is a different picture from the mechanism program assumption that hidden states carry rich abstention signal. The paper does not report a before/after abstention delta in the format used by effects.csv, so it does not populate that table directly.

## Claims

- Calibration tuning with LoRA + Prompt on a graded dataset of correct and incorrect answers creates reliable uncertainty estimates with good generalization on both multiple-choice and open-ended MMLU, outperforming all prompting and sampling baselines while requiring only approximately 1000 labeled examples to exceed baselines. (Figure 3, Section 5) [[calibration-tuning]]
- JSD regularization during calibration tuning is necessary to prevent ECE blow-up: without it ECE is 29.9%, with it ECE drops to 10.8%, with only marginal AUROC cost, averaged over six base models. (Table 1, Section 5) [[calibration-tuning]]
- Frozen linear features (Probe) are sufficient for multiple-choice MMLU but insufficient for open-ended MMLU; training through the model features with a language prompt (LoRA + Prompt) is required for open-ended settings and also generalizes MC-to-OE better than a Probe trained directly on OE data. (Figure 3 Left and Figure 4 Center, Sections 5-6) [[linear-probe]]
- A calibration tuner trained on one model's generations can estimate the uncertainty of a different model more accurately than that model estimates its own: Mistral 7B applied as a cross-model estimator achieves better AUROC on LLaMA-2 7B generations than LLaMA-2 7B applied to its own generations, suggesting calibration tuners function as general-purpose correctness estimators. (Figure 5 Center, Section 6.1) [[calibration-tuning-generalizes-across-models]]
- Calibrated confidence scores from a fine-tuned uncertainty estimator modulate human reliance on LLM predictions in a collaborative setting (N=181 participants), while random confidence scores produce no discernible modulation. (Section 7, Figure 6) [[calibration-tuning]]
