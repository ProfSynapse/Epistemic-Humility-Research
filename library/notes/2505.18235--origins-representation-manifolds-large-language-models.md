## Abstract

There is a large ongoing scientific effort in mechanistic interpretability to map embeddings and internal representations of AI systems into human-understandable concepts. A key element of this effort is the linear representation hypothesis, which posits that neural representations are sparse linear combinations of `almost-orthogonal' direction vectors, reflecting the presence or absence of different features. This model underpins the use of sparse autoencoders to recover features from representations. Moving towards a fuller model of features, in which neural representations could encode not just the presence but also a potentially continuous and multidimensional value for a feature, has been a subject of intense recent discourse. We describe why and how a feature might be represented as a manifold, demonstrating in particular that cosine similarity in representation space may encode the intrinsic geometry of a feature through shortest, on-manifold paths, potentially answering the question of how distance in representation space and relatedness in concept space could be connected. The critical assumptions and predictions of the theory are validated on text embeddings and token activations of large language models.

## Summary

Modell, Rubin-Delanchy & Whiteley formalize a feature as a metric space and prove that, under a continuous-correspondence assumption, its representation lives on a manifold homeomorphic to that space (an interval maps to a curve, a circle to a loop). They argue cosine similarity locally decreases with squared feature distance, so on-manifold geodesic distance recovers intrinsic feature geometry up to a scale constant. Manifolds are hypothesized to arise because bending a feature into a higher-dimensional subspace makes a richer class of functions of its value linearly readable by the next layer. Validated on text embeddings (colors, dates) and SAE-extracted token activations (years on a log scale).

## Claims

- Evidence label: theoretical. Under a continuous-correspondence assumption, the map from a feature's metric space to representation directions is a homeomorphism, so representations live on a manifold topologically identical to the feature space (Prop 1; Sec 2.1-2.3). [[representation-manifold]]
- Evidence label: theoretical. Combining continuous correspondence with "cosine similarity decreases with squared distance", geodesic distance on the representation manifold equals intrinsic feature-value distance up to one scale constant (Theorem 1; Sec 3). [[representation-manifold]]
- Evidence label: empirical. On text-embedding-3-large and GPT2-small SAE token activations, homeomorphism holds broadly (rank correlation ~1) and isometry holds with caveats (years Pearson 0.99 after log re-scaling, dates 0.97); GPT2-small encodes 20th-century years on a logarithmic scale (Sec 3.1; Figs 3-4).
