## Abstract

This ICML 2026 Position Track paper argues that frontier language models still hallucinate in factoid question answering and that many factuality gains expand the model's knowledge boundary without making the model more aware of where that boundary lies. The authors frame hallucinations as confident errors and argue that eliminating hallucinations while preserving utility may face a tradeoff if the only choices are answering or abstaining. They propose a third path: faithful uncertainty, where a model's linguistic uncertainty aligns with its intrinsic uncertainty. On this view, metacognition is needed both for direct user interaction and for agentic control over when to search and what information to trust.

## Summary

The paper is useful as conceptual framing rather than as an intervention result. It connects hallucination, trust, and metacognition by treating the core failure as a mismatch between what the model internally should be uncertain about and how confidently it speaks. Its most relevant move for this project is the shift from "answer versus abstain" to "answer, abstain, or express calibrated uncertainty," which matches the synthesis's coherence/faithfulness axis.

## Relevance to experiment

This supports the experiment program's focus on whether training changes a model's epistemic state or only its outward policy. For Phase 1 it does not change the locked protocol, hypotheses, or run matrix. For later phases, it strengthens the rationale for measuring whether stated uncertainty, token-level confidence, hidden-state probes, and search/retrieval decisions move together rather than treating abstention alone as the endpoint.
