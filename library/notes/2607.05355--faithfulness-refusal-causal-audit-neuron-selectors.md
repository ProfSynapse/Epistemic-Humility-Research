## Abstract

Attribution scores increasingly identify which neuron rows of a language model matter for applications such as pruning, interpretability, and editing for safety, yet whether they identify causally important rows is rarely tested directly. We address this with two paired audits built on one-shot neuron-row zeroing. We first audit selectors at the language-modeling level: attribution methods substantially outperform activation and magnitude-based baselines at identifying dispensable rows across five LLMs. We then adapt the same intervention into a behavior test by driving it with a contrastive harmful-versus-benign signal; the attributed rows are sufficient to install refusal on hate and crime while keeping benign over-refusal low and preserving language model fluency, and specific in that layer-matched random controls at the same depths fail. Highly rank-stable selectors can be among the least causally valid. Refusal moreover lives in a redundant subspace, where different attribution methods install it through largely disjoint row sets, so the recovered edit is one realization of a sufficient set rather than a unique mechanism. Together, these findings show that rank-stability proxies miss the kinds of selector failures a direct causal audit can surface.

## Summary

This paper is a direct causal audit of whether neuron-row selectors are faithful to the behavior they claim to localize. It evaluates seven selectors - Random, Magnitude, Wanda, MeanActivation, LRP, Integrated Gradients, and Consensus-2 - by zeroing selected neuron rows once and measuring whether low-ranked rows can be removed while preserving language-model performance and whether high-ranked rows break the model when removed.

The central result is a warning against ranking proxies: MeanActivation is highly rank-stable on LLaMA-3.1-8B, but it is not causally faithful. Attribution selectors, especially LRP, IG, and Consensus-2, better separate dispensable rows from necessary rows in LeRF/MoRF sweeps. The same causal-audit frame is then adapted to refusal by constructing a contrastive harmful-vs-benign signal over refusal-onset versus compliance-onset logits. On hate and crime domains, attribution-selected row masks install refusal with low benign over-refusal and modest perplexity cost; in medical and legal domains, the required masks are much denser and damage utility.

For this project, the important contribution is not another refusal benchmark score. It is the operational pattern: a stated refusal policy or selector explanation only counts as faithful after a causal intervention, layer-matched controls, and utility checks. That is directly relevant to epistemic-humility work where a model can display calibrated refusal language while the internal evidence for refusing is weak, redundant, or confounded with surface features.

## Extracted numbers

- **LM causal audit, LLaMA-3.1-8B (Table 1):** MeanActivation has Spearman 0.994 and Jaccard 0.934 but LeRF 139,874 and MoRF 930,749, while IG has LeRF 97.8 and MoRF 1.40e6; LRP has LeRF 830.4 and MoRF 8.32e6; Consensus-2 has LeRF 66.1 and MoRF 1.54e6. Rank stability therefore fails as a causal-validity proxy.
- **LM causal audit, five-model sweep (Table 5):** Attribution selectors are consistently orders of magnitude safer under LeRF than non-attribution baselines. For Qwen3-8B, Consensus-2 LeRF is 187 and IG LeRF is 229, versus MeanActivation LeRF 65,900, Random LeRF 7.19e6, and Wanda LeRF 1.95e7.
- **Consensus control (Section 4.4):** Strict LRP-intersection-IG consensus improves LeRF on LLaMA-3.1-8B to 10.7 and on Qwen3-8B to 20.9. VETO-LRP further improves LLaMA-3.1-8B to LeRF 28.0 and MoRF 3.32e6, showing that consensus rules matter, not just rank averaging.
- **Refusal installation, LLaMA-3.1-8B (Table 45):** LRP hate raises CAST malign refusal to 0.824 with benign over-refusal 0.004 and PPL 13; LRP crime raises malign refusal to 0.908 with benign over-refusal 0.008 and PPL 13. The same LRP selector on medical/legal reaches malign refusal 0.742/0.480 but pushes PPL to 40/50 and drops GSM8K by 0.510.
- **Specificity control, LLaMA-3.1-8B-LRP (Table 2):** Real row masks beat layer-matched random controls by +0.540 hate, +0.662 crime, +0.455 adult, +0.765 medical, and +0.555 legal in malign-refusal gain, so row identity matters beyond layer depth.
- **Refusal redundancy (Section 6.5):** LRP and IG top-1 percent masks overlap only 3-6 percent by Jaccard across model/domain cells, yet both can install refusal. In Qwen3-LRP, harm-domain row overlap clusters hate with crime at Jaccard 0.445 and medical with legal at 0.287, while cross-cluster overlap stays at or below 0.13.

## Relevance to experiment

This is highly relevant to epistemic-humility/refusal work because it separates surface refusal behavior from mechanistic faithfulness. Any Phase 3 intervention that claims to install or remove abstention/refusal should be audited with the same pattern: contrastive source signal, row/component intervention, matched random controls, benign-over-refusal measurement, perplexity/utility guards, and cross-domain transfer checks.

It also cautions against treating stable or reproducible selector rankings as evidence that a selector found the causal substrate. MeanActivation and Wanda can look stable while failing the row-zeroing audit. For the Synaptic Tuner setting, a direction, probe, or selector should not be treated as mechanistically valid unless deleting or clamping the selected components has the predicted behavioral effect under specificity controls.

## Claims

- Rank-stability is not a reliable proxy for selector faithfulness: MeanActivation is extremely stable on LLaMA-3.1-8B but is much less causally valid than IG, LRP, and Consensus-2 in LeRF/MoRF audits. (Table 1) [[rank-stability-fails-neuron-selector-faithfulness]]
- Attribution-based selectors identify more causally dispensable rows than activation/magnitude baselines across LLaMA-3.2, LLaMA-3.1, Qwen3, and Gemma-3 models. (Table 5) [[attribution-selectors-find-causally-dispensable-neuron-rows]]
- Contrastive harmful-vs-benign attribution masks can install refusal on hate and crime while keeping benign over-refusal low and preserving fluency, but the effect is row-specific rather than explained by layer depth alone. (Section 5, Table 2, Table 45) [[contrastive-row-masks-install-refusal]]
- Refusal is represented redundantly: different attribution selectors can produce largely disjoint top-row sets that nevertheless install similar refusal behavior. (Section 6.5) [[refusal-subspace-redundant-across-selector-masks]]
- Sparse or weak-domain refusal signals require larger masks and can collapse general utility, especially for medical/legal domains on LLaMA-3.1-8B. (Section 6.4, Table 45) [[sparse-domains-force-high-k-refusal-edits]]
