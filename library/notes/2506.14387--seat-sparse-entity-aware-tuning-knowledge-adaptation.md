## Abstract

Adapting LLMs with new knowledge is increasingly important, but standard fine-tuning often erodes aligned epistemic abstention: the ability to acknowledge when the model does not know. This failure mode is especially concerning in high-stakes settings, where abstention is a critical safeguard against hallucination. We present SEAT, a preventive fine-tuning method that preserves epistemic abstention while maintaining strong knowledge acquisition. SEAT combines sparse tuning, which constrains global activation drift, with entity-perturbed KL regularization, which sharpens local epistemic boundaries and prevents spillover to neighboring knowledge. Crucially, SEAT requires no alignment data, explicit boundary probing, or post-hoc re-alignment, making it attractive for lightweight and privacy-sensitive adaptation. Across models and datasets, SEAT improves human-evaluated abstention on unknown queries by 18%-101% over the strongest baseline while retaining near-perfect target knowledge acquisition, and produces coherent, context-aware abstentions after tuning. Further analyses show that both components are essential, that SEAT more cleanly separates known from unknown queries in representation space, and that it preserves downstream utility. These results identify preservation of epistemic abstention as a core objective for safe knowledge adaptation.

## Summary

SEAT is a preventive knowledge-adaptation method that combines sparse parameter
updates with a KL penalty on entity-perturbed versions of the training inputs.
The sparse mask limits global representation drift. The perturbation replaces a
training subject with a fictitious entity, and the KL term constrains the
adapted model to match the frozen base model on that neighboring input.

Across Llama3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, conventional full and LoRA
fine-tuning learned the new facts but often replaced prior abstention on unseen
queries with fabricated answers. SEAT retained near-perfect target recall and
high human-judged abstention on synthetic and post-cutoff knowledge updates.
The ablations show that sparse updates and entity perturbation contribute
complementary effects.

## Extracted numbers

- SEAT's fine-tuning score ranged from 0.987 to 1.000 across two models and
  three update datasets (Section 4.2, Table 2).
- Human-judged IDK scores for SEAT ranged from 0.954 to 1.000. Full fine-tuning
  scored 0.000 in all six model-dataset settings (Section 4.2, Table 2).
- Compared with experience replay, the strongest baseline in Table 2, SEAT
  improved human-judged abstention by approximately 18 percent to 101 percent,
  depending on model and update dataset (Abstract and Table 2).
- In the Llama PISTOL ablation, full fine-tuning plus entity perturbation scored
  0.630, sparse fine-tuning scored 0.806, and SEAT scored 0.954 on human-judged
  abstention (Section 4.3, Table 3b).
- Across random, magnitude-based, and importance-based sparse masks, adding
  entity perturbation produced human-judged IDK scores from 0.941 to 0.954
  (Section 4.3, Table 4).
- Cross-dataset human-judged IDK scores ranged from 0.910 to 0.960, while mean
  recall on supported factual questions was 0.991 (Section 4.2, Table 3a).
- All experiments were repeated three times. Human assessment used 20
  participants (Appendix B.3 and B.4).

## Relevance to experiment

SEAT directly updates weights and preserves a pre-existing abstention policy
without abstention examples, explicit boundary probes, or a post-hoc alignment
stage. It is strong evidence that training-data neighborhood regularization can
protect behavior associated with an epistemic boundary.

The method does not implement weights-level consultation of an internal
answerability readout. Its training signal is output-distribution agreement
with the base model on synthetically perturbed inputs. Its representation
analysis is diagnostic only. The authors explicitly note that the model lacks
an answerability scalar head and evaluate abstention through generated text.

The evidence is limited to two instruction-tuned models, three knowledge-update
datasets, and output-defined unknown-query behavior. Dataset provenance is used
to designate unsupported questions, rather than a per-item latent answerability
test. The PCA results establish association, not a causal abstention circuit.
The importance-based masking ablation estimates Fisher importance on the same
Unverifiable set used for its reported abstention score, while the main SEAT
results use random masks and do not require that set during training.

## Claims

- Evidence label: method. SEAT combines masked parameter updates with KL
  matching to the base model on one fictitious subject replacement per training
  example (Section 3, Equations 3 to 5).
- Evidence label: behavioral comparison. SEAT retained human-judged abstention
  from 0.954 to 1.000 while full fine-tuning reached zero in every main setting,
  with similar target recall (Section 4.2, Table 2).
- Evidence label: component ablation. Sparse updates and entity perturbation
  each helped, and their combination outperformed either component alone
  (Section 4.3, Tables 3b and 4).
- Evidence label: representation analysis. Sparse training reduced activation
  drift for unseen inputs while allowing target-input representations to move,
  but the paper does not identify a causal circuit (Section 4.3, Figure 3).
