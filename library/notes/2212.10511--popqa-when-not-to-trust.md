## Abstract

Despite their impressive performance on diverse tasks, large language models (LMs) still struggle with tasks requiring rich world knowledge, implying the limitations of relying solely on their parameters to encode a wealth of world knowledge. This paper aims to understand LMs' strengths and limitations in memorizing factual knowledge, by conducting large-scale knowledge probing experiments of 10 models and 4 augmentation methods on PopQA, our new open-domain QA dataset with 14k questions. We find that LMs struggle with less popular factual knowledge, and that scaling fails to appreciably improve memorization of factual knowledge in the long tail. We then show that retrieval-augmented LMs largely outperform orders of magnitude larger LMs, while unassisted LMs remain competitive in questions about high-popularity entities. Based on those findings, we devise a simple, yet effective, method for powerful and efficient retrieval-augmented LMs, which retrieves non-parametric memories only when necessary. Experimental results show that this significantly improves models' performance while reducing the inference costs.

## Summary

Mallen et al. introduce PopQA, a 14k-question open-domain QA dataset constructed by converting Wikidata triples into natural-language questions and annotating each question with the subject entity's Wikipedia page-view popularity. The dataset is designed to expose the long-tail regime that popular benchmarks like Natural Questions underrepresent. Across ten LMs (GPT-Neo 1.3B to 20B, OPT 1.3B to 13B, GPT-3 davinci-002 and davinci-003) evaluated in zero-shot or 15-shot closed-book prompting, two findings stand out. First, entity popularity is a strong predictor of factual memorization: the Pearson correlation between log-popularity and per-question accuracy is roughly 0.4 for GPT-3 davinci-003 and roughly 0.1 for GPT-Neo 1.3B, holding across nearly all 16 relationship types. Second, scaling provides almost no benefit on the low-popularity tail: for the 4,000 least-popular questions, GPT-Neo 6B achieves 15%, GPT-Neo 20B achieves 16%, and GPT-3 davinci-003 achieves only 19% accuracy. The paper then investigates retrieval augmentation (BM25, Contriever, GenRead) and finds that Contriever adds 7 percentage points on top of GPT-3 davinci-003 overall, but that for 10% of questions retrieval is harmful, returning misleading context with recall@1 of only 0.14 compared to 0.42 overall. Conversely, for 17% of questions retrieval rescues the LM, with recall@1 of 0.88 on those items. Building on this complementary-memory picture, the authors propose Adaptive Retrieval: use entity-popularity and relationship-type thresholds to decide per query whether to retrieve. The best configuration (GPT-3 davinci-003 with GenRead and Contriever combined) reaches 46.5% accuracy on PopQA, 5.3 percentage points above the best non-adaptive method. Under Adaptive Retrieval with BM25, GPT-3 davinci-003 retrieves for only 40% of questions and GPT-NeoX 20B retrieves for fewer than 20%, reducing latency by up to 9% and cutting API costs by 15% on EntityQuestions while maintaining equivalent accuracy.

## Relevance to experiment

PopQA bears on the Epistemic-Humility experiment in three ways. First, entity popularity operationalizes the knowledge boundary in a way that is both measurable from input features and predictive of LM failure: a question about a low-popularity entity is exactly the case where a calibrated model should hedge or abstain. The finding that the popularity-accuracy correlation is stronger for larger LMs (roughly 0.4 vs 0.1) means that models with more parametric knowledge have a sharper internal separation between what they know and do not know, but still fall to 19% on the hardest tail. This motivates using PopQA as a testbed for abstention training: the threshold at which parametric knowledge becomes unreliable is measurable and provides a natural supervision signal. Second, the scaling-fails-on-long-tail result (Figure 5) directly constrains expectations for Phase 1: a move from 4B to 8B parameters is unlikely to substantially shift the knowledge boundary on tail entities, so improvements in abstention behavior after SFT, DPO, or KTO training cannot be attributed to incidental knowledge gain. Third, Adaptive Retrieval demonstrates a deployment-time complement to training-based abstention: instead of teaching the model to recognize its ignorance, a routing rule (popularity threshold) decides when to invoke external memory. The paper does not report calibration or abstention metrics (ECE, AUROC, abstention rate) and does not involve a training intervention, so it does not populate `effects.csv` directly. Its contribution is the evidence base for why the knowledge boundary matters at scale and why retrieval augmentation is complementary rather than uniformly superior to parametric knowledge.

## Claims

- Subject entity popularity (Wikipedia page views) is a reliable predictor of LM factual memorization, with log-popularity / accuracy Pearson correlation of roughly 0.4 for GPT-3 davinci-003 and roughly 0.1 for GPT-Neo 1.3B across relationship types, enabling boundary prediction from input features alone. (Section 4.2, Figure 4) [[entity-popularity-predicts-parametric-memorization]]
- Scaling provides negligible benefit on the long tail: for the 4,000 least-popular PopQA questions, GPT-Neo 6B (15%), GPT-Neo 20B (16%), and GPT-3 davinci-003 (19%) are nearly indistinguishable, even though scale helps substantially on high-popularity questions. (Section 4.2, Figure 5) [[scaling-fails-on-long-tail-knowledge]]
- Retrieval augmentation is complementary rather than uniformly superior to parametric memory: Contriever adds 7 percentage points overall but is harmful for 10% of questions (recall@1 0.14 vs 0.42 overall) and beneficial for 17% (recall@1 0.88), because popular entities are already memorized and low-quality retrieval misleads the LM. (Table 1, Section 5.2) [[adaptive-retrieval]]
- Adaptive Retrieval using entity-popularity thresholds achieves 46.5% accuracy on PopQA with GPT-3 davinci-003 (5.3 percentage points above the best non-adaptive method) and reduces retrieval calls to 40% of queries, cutting latency by up to 9%. (Section 6.2) [[adaptive-retrieval]]
- The popularity threshold at which parametric memory outperforms retrieval shifts with model scale: GPT-3 davinci-003 retrieves for only 40% of questions under BM25, while smaller models retrieve for nearly all questions, showing that larger parametric memories extend reliable coverage further down the popularity distribution. (Section 6.2, Figure 10) [[entity-popularity-predicts-parametric-memorization]]
