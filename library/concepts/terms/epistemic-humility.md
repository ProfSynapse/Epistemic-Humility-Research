Epistemic humility is a model's capacity to recognize and act on the limits of
what it can justify, declining to commit to an answer when the available evidence
does not support any of the candidate responses. In multiple-choice settings it
surfaces as the willingness to select "None of the above" rather than picking a
plausible but unsupported option; in open-ended settings it overlaps with
[[abstention]] and well-calibrated [[uncertainty-quantification]]. It is the
positive capability whose absence produces [[hallucination]] and overconfident
commitment.

**Why it matters here:** Epistemic humility is the organizing construct for this
research program: calibration, abstention, and uncertainty reporting are all
facets of a model knowing when not to answer. HumbleBench
([[2509.09658--humblebench-epistemic-humility-multimodal]]) measures it directly
in the multimodal setting, and the Phase 1 training-method study targets it in the
text-only setting via the abstention/over-abstention tradeoff.

**Lineage:** realized as [[abstention]] under forced choice; opposed to
[[hallucination]] and overconfident commitment; measured by [[humblebench]].
