Epistemic humility is a model's capacity to recognize and act on the limits of
what it can justify, declining to commit to an answer when the available evidence
does not support any of the candidate responses. It spans several facets:
calibrated uncertainty, principled refusal, and, narrowly, the rejection of
unsupported options. It is the positive capability whose absence produces
[[hallucination]] and overconfident commitment.

The construct is broad, so concrete benchmarks usually target one facet. HumbleBench
([[2509.09658--humblebench-epistemic-humility-multimodal]]) is explicit that it
measures only [[false-option-rejection]], the directly observable behavior of
rejecting all offered options when none is supported, and not calibrated
uncertainty or refusal in their full generality. Treat "measuring epistemic
humility" claims as facet-specific unless stated otherwise.

**Why it matters here:** Epistemic humility is the organizing construct for this
research program: calibration, [[abstention]], and uncertainty reporting are all
facets of a model knowing when not to answer. The Phase 1 training-method study
targets the abstention facet in the text-only setting via the
abstention/over-abstention tradeoff, while HumbleBench targets the
false-option-rejection facet in the multimodal setting.

**Lineage:** realized as [[abstention]] and [[false-option-rejection]] under
forced choice; opposed to [[hallucination]] and overconfident commitment; a facet
measured by [[humblebench]].
