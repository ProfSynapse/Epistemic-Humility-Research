Cognitive access is the functional availability of a representation for flexible reasoning, action control, and report. In the workspace commentary bundle, it is treated as the conceptually cleaner name for what the Anthropic paper calls conscious access, because it identifies the information-processing property without settling whether phenomenal consciousness is present.

**Why it matters here:** epistemic-humility work often depends on whether internal uncertainty, doubt, or knowledge-boundary information is available to the model's own downstream computations. Cognitive access names that availability question without requiring a claim about subjective experience.

**Lineage:** the commentaries connect this term to Block's access consciousness distinction and to global workspace theory; in the language-model setting it is operationalized through [[global-workspace]] and [[jacobian-lens]] readouts.
