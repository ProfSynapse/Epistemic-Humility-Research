Phenomenal consciousness is subjective experience: the question of whether there is something it is like to be in a mental or computational state. The workspace commentaries separate this from [[cognitive-access]], emphasizing that evidence for reportable, globally usable representations does not by itself settle whether a language model has experience.

**Why it matters here:** the research program should not conflate mechanistic access to uncertainty or self-monitoring with claims about welfare or subjective experience. This atom keeps the philosophical and moral-status claim separate from the mechanistic J-space claim.

**Lineage:** the term is used in the Butlin/Shiller/Plunkett/Long commentary to distinguish Block-style access consciousness from the harder phenomenal-consciousness question.
