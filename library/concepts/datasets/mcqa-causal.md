A paired multiple-choice question-answering benchmark for four-way causal relation classification (direct causal, conditional causal, correlational, no relationship) built from PubMed abstracts. MCQA contains 1,434 sentences sourced from The Pile corpus (filtered from Yu et al. 2019 with total variation distance 0.026 from original label distribution: 234 direct, 113 conditional, 489 correlational, 598 no relationship). MCQA-newer contains a matched-distribution extension of post-2024 PubMed sentences labeled by a BERT classifier (F1 = 0.97) and subsampled to match MCQA class proportions. Each sentence is augmented with five GPT-4o-mini paraphrases, one negation, and two probe questions, yielding 18,366 total items. Designed to disentangle pretraining-data exposure from causal understanding by comparing Pile-trained models on seen (MCQA) vs unseen (MCQA-newer) text.

**Why it matters here:** Enables controlled separation of verbatim recall from genuine causal reasoning by pairing semantically matched seen and unseen sentences. The null result (no accuracy advantage on seen text) provides a calibration-agnostic baseline showing that pretraining exposure does not drive causal understanding, which constrains interpretations of uncertainty probing and intervention experiments.

**Lineage:** Built by Lithgow-Serrano, Kanjirangat, Antonucci (2025) from Yu et al. (2019) annotations + PubMed abstracts; BERT classifier trained on the Yu annotations; paraphrases generated by GPT-4o-mini.
