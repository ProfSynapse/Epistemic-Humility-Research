The Dyck language is the formal language of balanced bracket sequences (e.g.
`([{}])`), generated by matching opening and closing symbols under a
last-in-first-out (stack) discipline. Because well-formedness requires
unbounded-depth recursive nesting, it is a standard synthetic testbed for
whether sequence models learn hierarchical/context-free structure rather than
shallow surface statistics, and it admits an explicit, unambiguous ground
truth for depth, distance-to-match, and top-of-stack identity at every
position.

**Why it matters here:** it is the training and evaluation distribution used
in arXiv:2604.22128 to dissociate what is linearly decodable (depth,
distance, top-of-stack) from what is causally used by the model when
producing bracket-closing predictions, including out-of-distribution splits
on sequence length and nesting depth.
