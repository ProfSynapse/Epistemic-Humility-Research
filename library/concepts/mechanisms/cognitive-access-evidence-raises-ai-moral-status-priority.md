Butlin et al. argue that the workspace evidence should prompt a modest increase in credence that LLMs could be phenomenally conscious, while preserving high uncertainty because substrate, embodiment, interoception, action, emotion, and other background conditions may matter. Even if phenomenal consciousness is absent, they argue that cognitive access and agency could themselves be morally significant, making empirical AI-welfare research more urgent.
