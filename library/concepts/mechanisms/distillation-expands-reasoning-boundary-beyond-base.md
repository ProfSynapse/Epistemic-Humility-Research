The one training signal in Yue et al.'s comparison that is NOT bounded by
the base model's own reachable-solution distribution is distillation:
DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, trained on long chain-of-thought traces
generated by a stronger teacher (DeepSeek-R1), shows a pass@k curve
consistently and significantly above its own base model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B)
across every k tested, unlike its RL-trained counterpart
(Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Oat-Zero), which stays bounded by the base. The
distinction the paper draws is that distillation supplies external
information (the teacher's reasoning traces) that the base model's own
sampling distribution does not contain, whereas RLVR can only reweight
what is already there.

**Why it matters here:** this is the explicit positive contrast to
[[rlvr-reasoning-bounded-by-base-model]], establishing that "training
cannot exceed the base" is not a universal law but specifically a property
of reward-only reweighting objectives (RL) versus objectives that import
new signal from outside the base's own distribution (distillation,
and this program's SFT arm, which trains directly on IDK-labeled targets
rather than reweighting the base's own rollouts).

**Lineage:** established in
[[2504.13837--does-reinforcement-learning-really-incentivize-reasoning-capacity]]
(Yue et al. 2025), Section 4.2.
